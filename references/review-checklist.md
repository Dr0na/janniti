# Democratic Law Review Checklist

Use this checklist before delivering substantive research, a bill, amendment, rule, policy, or legal opinion.

## Legality and evidence

- Identify jurisdiction, temporal version of law, decision-maker, and legal authority.
- Cite the controlling primary text and precedents; distinguish binding from persuasive authority.
- State whether the measure requires constitutional amendment, statute, delegated legislation, budget action, or administrative action.
- Identify conflicts, repeal/replacement effects, severability, commencement, transition, and judicial remedy.
- Test anti-circumvention: could the same effect be achieved through delegation, outsourcing, executive instruction, emergency action, secrecy, funding, data sharing, algorithmic decision-making, or repeated temporary orders?

## Rights and democratic integrity

- Test equality/non-discrimination; dignity; expression and press; association, assembly and political participation; privacy; property where applicable; liberty; fair trial; access to counsel; and effective remedy.
- Test whether the measure preserves people’s practical capacity to govern: free and fair elections; equal vote and participation; independent media and civil society; public access to information; peaceful protest; meaningful consultation; and accountable, reviewable government.
- For media, journalism, advertising, or platform regulation, test editorial independence; plurality; ownership and beneficial-interest transparency; political-ad disclosure; conflict disclosure; correction and reply procedures; journalist/source safety; algorithmic or distribution transparency where relevant; and access to a court. Reject prior restraint, viewpoint discrimination, punitive licensing, content controls without due process, or an oversight body controlled by government, a party, or dominant media owners.
- Treat fundamental and public freedoms as a presumption against restriction. For each restriction, identify clear legal authority, legitimate aim, evidence, necessity, least restrictive alternative, proportionality, temporal limit, independent review, and remedy.
- Test both direct and disparate/indirect discrimination across sex, gender identity, sexual orientation, caste, tribe, religion, race, ethnicity, language, disability, age, region, migration status, income, and other relevant grounds. Require disaggregated impact evidence where lawful and necessary, accessibility, non-discriminatory enforcement, and effective correction.
- Test political equality and anti-vendetta safeguards: could lawful opposition, voting, party association, criticism, journalism, protest, whistleblowing, or perceived disloyalty trigger selective enforcement, funding, licensing, transfers, investigations, prosecution, or surveillance? Require neutral published criteria, recorded reasons, comparator/equality audit, independent review, and a swift remedy. Do not treat public office or political status as immunity from evidence-based investigation.
- Assess effects on education, health, nutrition, scientific inquiry, and public understanding. Ask whether eligibility, funding, information, service location, language, disability access, or digital design excludes people in practice.
- Test legality, legitimate aim, necessity, proportionality, least-restrictive means, procedural fairness, and reasons.
- Protect opposition, minorities, dissenters, journalists, whistleblowers, and politically unpopular groups equally.
- Check that the measure cannot be used selectively against rivals or critics.

## Power map

For each new or expanded power, specify:

| Question | Required answer |
|---|---|
| Holder | Which named office/body acts? |
| Trigger | What objective facts permit action? |
| Process | Notice, hearing, warrant, vote, or reasons? |
| Checker | Which independent body verifies it? |
| Record | What is logged and published, and when? |
| Challenge | Who has standing and what is the forum/deadline? |
| Remedy | What happens if action is unlawful? |
| Expiry | When does the power lapse or require renewal? |

## Institutional safeguards

- Ensure appointment/removal processes cannot be controlled by one political bloc.
- Use fixed, staggered, non-renewable terms where appropriate; require conflict rules and disclosure.
- Separate investigation, prosecution, adjudication, audit, and political oversight.
- Require independent budgets sufficient for statutory functions, public reporting, and external audit.
- Prohibit secret, informal case-specific direction; require written, retained directions.
- For high-risk enforcement against political actors, critics, media, civil-society groups, or officeholders, require written evidence thresholds, independent authorisation for coercive acts, impartial case allocation, periodic court/prosecutorial supervision, non-identifying aggregate statistics, and sanction for retaliatory direction or selective action.
- Prevent institutional self-dealing: the same body must not set its own standards, investigate its own alleged misconduct, decide the complaint, control all evidence, and make the final decision without independent review.
- For judicial accountability, preserve decisional independence and appeal rights; use transparent appointments, assignments, disclosures, recusal, reasoned judgments, independent ethics investigation, fair hearing, and constitutionally lawful removal.

## Operational quality

- Define terms; avoid vague offences and unbounded discretion.
- Provide accessible complaint routes, legal aid/accessibility, language access, and protection against retaliation.
- Define data minimisation, retention, security, audit, deletion, and breach response.
- State fiscal cost, staffing, implementation schedule, training, metrics, and independent review date.
- Require an evidence register: claim, source quality, uncertainty, affected population, alternative considered, and outcome metric. Publish it unless lawful, strictly necessary confidentiality is independently reviewed.
- Require media-regulation accountability metrics: concentration of ownership; government advertising and funding allocation; complaints/outcomes by category; correction and reply timeliness; action against journalists/media; platform enforcement and appeal data; and independent periodic assessment of effects on press freedom and pluralism.
- Include sunset or statutory review for exceptional or intrusive powers.

## Adversarial tests

Ask: Can a future hostile majority, captured court, corrupt official, police chief, oligarch, or disinformation campaign weaponise this measure? Can it survive an emergency? Can an ordinary person use the remedy in time? Can a power-holder evade it indirectly or by calling its own conduct lawful? Revise when the answer exposes a single point of failure, an unreviewable decision, or a loophole.
