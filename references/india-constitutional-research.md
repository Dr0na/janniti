# India Constitutional Research Module

## Source order

Use the latest official text from India Code or the Gazette for the Constitution and statutes; official Supreme Court/High Court material for judgments; and the Election Commission, CAG, Parliament, relevant ministry, or statutory regulator for institutional facts. Record version/date and link directly to the authority.

For a topic that may be governed by State or Union Territory law, search the relevant State/UT legislature, law department, and official Gazette in addition to India Code. Record each jurisdiction searched, repository URL, search terms, result, and limitation. Never infer that a State/UT has no relevant law from a Central search or an incomplete India Code result.

Read `knowledge-base/README.md` and `knowledge-base/sources.yaml` before substantive Indian research. Retrieve each required primary source for the issue; never infer legislative history from a secondary summary when a debate, bill, committee report, or Gazette record is available.

## Mandatory issues to test

1. **Legislative competence:** Union List, State List, Concurrent List, residuary power, and effects on States/local government.
2. **Fundamental rights, public freedoms, and equality:** Articles 14–18, 19, 20, 21, 21A, 22, 25–30 and constitutional remedies, as applicable; apply legality, legitimate aim, proportionality, due process, non-arbitrariness, substantive equality, non-discrimination, and meaningful democratic participation.
3. **Institutional structure:** parliamentary responsibility, federalism, separation of powers, judicial review, independence of the judiciary, free and fair elections, and constitutional accountability.
4. **Amendments:** Article 368 procedure and whether a proposal risks the basic structure. Do not claim Parliament can make an unlimited constitutional amendment.
5. **Emergency and enforcement:** Articles 123, 213, 352–360; arrest/remand, surveillance, criminal procedure, police governance and prosecution must receive heightened scrutiny.
6. **Electoral integrity:** Article 324, Representation of the People Acts, political finance, candidate disclosure, equal opportunity, and freedom of political communication.
7. **Judicial accountability:** distinguish a challenge to a judgment (appeal/review/curative process as law permits) from conduct allegations. Preserve judicial independence while testing appointment, roster/case allocation, recusal, reasons, disclosure, discipline, removal, and public confidence safeguards.
8. **Education, health, and scientific temper:** examine Articles 21A, 39A, 41, 45–47 and 51A(h), as applicable. Assess equal access, nutrition, accessibility, public-health consequences, evidence quality, scientific inquiry, and whether a measure perpetuates social exclusion.
9. **Political equality and impartial enforcement:** assess Articles 14, 19, 20, 21 and 22, as applicable. Distinguish lawful investigation on objective evidence from retaliation or selective enforcement based on political opinion, party association, criticism, journalism, protest, or whistleblowing; require equal standards, recorded reasons, independent review, and remedy without creating immunity for public officials.
10. **Popular sovereignty:** protect free and fair elections, political participation, public information, independent media, peaceful dissent, and constitutional remedies. Treat democratic accountability as government answerability to people under the Constitution, not unlimited authority of a majority over equal rights.
11. **Media freedom and integrity:** assess Articles 14, 19 and 21, as applicable, alongside the legal framework governing press, broadcasting, advertising, digital platforms, access to information, defamation, and source protection. Protect editorial independence and pluralism; test ownership concentration, government advertising, paid political content, conflicts, correction/reply, retaliation against journalists, and any censorship or prior-restraint risk.

## Drafting directions

- For a constitutional amendment, identify every Article/Schedule amended, insertion, deletion, commencement, transitional provision, State-ratification issue, and consequential statute/rule.
- For a Union statute, state the constitutional entry and whether State concurrence or a model law is more appropriate.
- For a State measure, state the relevant State/Concurrent List basis and Union-law interaction.
- Never use “national interest,” public order, security, morality, or emergency as a conclusory justification; define the harm, evidence threshold, decision-maker, time limit, hearing, review, and remedy.
- Add an anti-evasion clause where appropriate: executive, legislative, judicial, regulatory, or private-delegate action cannot achieve indirectly what the Constitution or statute forbids directly; all such action remains subject to constitutional remedies.

## Starting authorities

- Constitution of India: official India Code text.
- *Kesavananda Bharati v State of Kerala* (1973): basic-structure doctrine.
- *Minerva Mills v Union of India* (1980): limited amending power and constitutional balance.
- *S.R. Bommai v Union of India* (1994): federalism and review of Article 356 action.
- *Puttaswamy v Union of India* (2017): privacy and proportionality.

Verify every authority’s current relevance and exact holding before relying on it.
