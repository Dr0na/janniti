# THE CITIZEN ALGORITHMIC RIGHTS, BIOMETRIC SAFEGUARDS, AND AI TRANSPARENCY BILL, 2026

### A

### BILL

to protect citizens against arbitrary algorithmic discrimination, mass facial recognition surveillance, and automated biometric exclusion; to mandate open-source algorithmic audits for public sector software and artificial intelligence deployments; to establish the statutory right to human explainability, review, and opt-out from automated profiling; to create the Algorithmic Rights and AI Transparency Commission; and for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto.

BE it enacted by Parliament in the Seventy-Seventh Year of the Republic of India as follows:—

### 1. Short title, extent and commencement

(1) This Act may be called the Citizen Algorithmic Rights, Biometric Safeguards, and AI Transparency Act, 2026.

(2) It extends to the whole of India.

(3) It shall come into force on such date as the Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, appoint.

### 2. Definitions

In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires,—

(a) "Algorithmic System" means any computational, statistical, or machine learning model, automated decision-making tool, or artificial intelligence application used to process data or make decisions affecting individuals;

(b) "Biometric Welfare Exclusion" means the denial, suspension, or delay of any public entitlement, pension, ration, or benefit due to biometric authentication failure or digital record mismatch;

(c) "Mass Facial Recognition Surveillance" means the continuous, remote, or real-time automated identification of individuals in public spaces using biometric image matching without a specific judicial warrant; and

(d) "National AI Commission" means the Algorithmic Rights and AI Transparency Commission established under section 5.

### 3. Prohibition of Mass Biometric Surveillance and Welfare Exclusion

(1) Mass Facial Recognition Surveillance in public spaces by state or private entities is prohibited.

(2) No public entitlement, welfare benefit, or essential service shall be denied or delayed solely due to biometric authentication failure, biometric mismatch, or technical connectivity loss.

(3) In every case of biometric authentication failure, physical alternative verification methods shall be accepted immediately.

### 4. Right to Human Explainability and Algorithmic Audit

(1) Every person subject to an automated decision by a public authority or essential service provider shall have the statutory right to receive a plain-language explanation of the decision logic, inputs, and parameters.

(2) Every public sector entity deploying an Algorithmic System shall publish an open-source algorithmic impact assessment and source code audit prior to deployment.

(3) Individuals shall have the right to request human review and override of any automated decision within thirty days.

### 5. Algorithmic Rights and AI Transparency Commission

(1) There shall be established an autonomous body to be called the Algorithmic Rights and AI Transparency Commission.

(2) The Commission shall consist of cybersecurity experts, computer scientists, privacy judges, and civil liberties advocates.

(3) The Commission shall have power to audit AI systems, issue binding cease-and-desist orders against biased algorithms, and award compensation for algorithmic discrimination.

### 6. Offences and Penalties for Illegal Surveillance and Exclusion

(1) Whoever deploys unapproved Mass Facial Recognition Surveillance shall be punishable with imprisonment up to five years and fine up to fifty lakh rupees.

(2) Any public official denying essential welfare benefits due to biometric failure shall face administrative disciplinary action and fine under section 7.

### 7. Power to Make Rules

(1) The Central Government may make rules to carry out the provisions of this Act.

(2) Every rule made under this section shall be laid before Parliament.

---

## STATEMENT OF OBJECTS AND REASONS

1. Unchecked AI deployments, mass facial recognition surveillance, and automated biometric welfare exclusions infringe upon Article 21 privacy rights and Article 14 equality rights (*Puttaswamy* judgment).

2. The Citizen Algorithmic Rights, Biometric Safeguards, and AI Transparency Bill, 2026 bans mass biometric surveillance, prohibits biometric welfare exclusion, mandates open-source AI audits, and establishes the right to human explainability.

---

## FINANCIAL MEMORANDUM

1. Clause 5 establishes the Algorithmic Rights and AI Transparency Commission.

2. Recurring annual operational expenditure is estimated at rupees three hundred crores.
