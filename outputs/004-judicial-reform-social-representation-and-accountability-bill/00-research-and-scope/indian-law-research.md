## 2. Executive finding and reliance limits

The existing judicial administration framework in India faces structural challenges:
1. **The Collegium System Deficit**: Established via judicial interpretation in the *Second Judges Case* (1993) and *Third Judges Case* (1998), the collegium system allows judges to appoint judges without statutory transparency, leading to criticisms of social homogeneity, lack of representation for SC/ST/OBC/Minority communities, and accusations of nepotism.
2. **The NJAC Precedent**: The 99th Constitutional Amendment and NJAC Act 2014 were struck down by the Supreme Court in 2015 (*SCAORA v. Union of India* 2015 5 SCC 1) for violating judicial independence due to executive veto powers. A new NJARC must balance judicial independence with social diversity and transparent merit criteria.
3. **Judicial Accountability & Misconduct**: Impeachment under Article 124(4) / 217(1)(b) is politically cumbersome and rarely completed (*V. Ramaswami* 1993). An independent oversight body (NJOCC) is needed for investigation while preserving protection against frivolous complaints.
4. **Open Courts & PIL Safeguards**: *Swapnil Tripathi v. Supreme Court of India* (2018 10 SCC 639) affirmed live streaming as part of Article 21 and open justice, but implementation remains patchy.

A Constitutional Amendment under Article 368 is legally required to replace the collegium, establish the NJARC, enforce live streaming, and create the NJOCC.

## 5. State and Union Territory coverage

Applies uniformly across the Supreme Court of India, all 25 High Courts, and the subordinate judiciary in all 28 States and 8 Union Territories. Reform of Article 124 and 217 under Article 368 requires ratification by at least 50% of State Assemblies.

## 6. Bills, amendments, ordinances, history, and Gazette

- Judicial Standards and Accountability Bill, 2012 (passed Lok Sabha, lapsed in Rajya Sabha) proposed an independent Judicial Oversight Committee.
- Law Commission of India Report No. 121 (1987) & Report No. 214 (2008) recommended a broad-based National Judicial Commission.

## 7. Delegated law and implementation

Rules for live streaming (Supreme Court Model Rules for Live Streaming 2021) currently exist as guidelines. Proposed legislation elevates live streaming to a mandatory statutory requirement with narrow exceptions for sexual offense trials.

## 9. Rights, equality, federalism, and public-interest assessment

- **Social Representation & Equal Protection**: Proportional representation across SC/ST/OBC and Minority communities ensures judicial diversity under Articles 14, 15, and 16.
- **Decisional Independence vs. Accountability**: Judges are protected from penal liability for honest legal errors, while corrupt acts (bribery, bad-faith collusion to stifle fundamental rights) attract 7-14 years imprisonment upon prior 5-Judge SC Bench authorization.
- **Open Courts & Public Scrutiny**: Universal live streaming guarantees transparency and deters misconduct.
