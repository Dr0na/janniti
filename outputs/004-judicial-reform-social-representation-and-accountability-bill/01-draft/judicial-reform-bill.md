# Draft Legislation and Policy Analysis: The Judicial Reform, Social Representation, and Accountability Bill, 2026

## 1. Bottom Line and Legal Expert Analysis

- **Jurisdiction**: Union of India (Parliament of India, operating under Article 368 of the Constitution of India, amending Articles 124, 124A, 124B, 124C, 145A, 217, 235, 368, and the Representation of the People Act 1951, Judges Inquiry Act 1968, Prevention of Corruption Act 1988, and Contempt of Courts Act 1971).
- **As-of Date**: 2026-07-27
- **Client / Deliverable**: Updated legislative formulation, constitutional amendment text, companion statutory provisions, power map, and implementation framework incorporating live stream national archival preservation, free public access, strict penalization of record tampering/denial of access, and **mandatory 30-day time-bound day-to-day hearings for election rigging and political defection cases with severe penalties for judicial deferment or collusion**.
- **Core Legal Findings & Refined Reform Architecture**:
  1. **Abolition of Collegium & Creation of National Judicial Appointments and Social Representation Commission (NJARC)**: Replaces the opaque judicial collegium (*Second Judges Case* 1993, *NJAC Case* 2015) with a 6-member Constitutional Commission (CJI, 2 Senior SC Judges, Law Minister, 2 Eminent Persons selected by PM+LOP+CJI).
  2. **Proportional Social Representation & Merit Standard (Art 124B(2))**: Mandates that judicial appointments evaluate merit through transparent scoring while ensuring broad-based representation across all caste, social, and religious communities (Hindus, Muslims, Christians, Sikhs, Parsis, SC, ST, OBC, and Women).
  3. **Universal Live Stream Archiving, Free Access, & Penalties for Tampering (Article 145A & Section 6)**:
     - All live stream recordings must be permanently archived in the National Archives of India and made available for **free public online viewing**, with certified copies provided on demand for nominal costs.
     - Any act to block, alter, modify, destroy, or deny public access to court recordings is a non-bailable offence carrying **5 to 10 years rigorous imprisonment** and dismissal from service.
  4. **Mandatory 30-Day Continuous Day-to-Day Hearings for Defection & Election Rigging (Section 5A)**:
     - All cases involving political defection, horse-trading, election rigging, or electoral list fraud MUST be heard on a **day-to-day continuous basis** and concluded within **thirty days**.
     - Intentional adjournment, bad-faith delay, or judicial deferment to favor a ruling government carries **7 to 14 years rigorous imprisonment**, asset forfeiture, and removal from office under NJOCC oversight.
  5. **Judicial Accountability (NJOCC)**: Establishes the National Judicial Oversight and Conduct Commission (NJOCC). Proven bribery, financial corruption, or bad-faith collusion to stifle fundamental rights carries **7 to 14 years rigorous imprisonment**, asset forfeiture, removal from office, and loss of post-retirement privileges. Complaints require screening by a **5-Judge Supreme Court Constitution Bench**.
  6. **PIL Safeguards & 24/7 Special Emergency Benches**: Prohibits summary PIL dismissal without preliminary hearing; mandates 24/7 Emergency Benches.
  7. **Right to Peaceful Protest & AI Judicial Integration**: Protects citizen rights to peaceful protest against judicial or executive miscarriage of justice under Article 19(1)(b).

---

## 2. Problem, Evidence, and Existing-Law Map

Existing Indian judicial administration frameworks suffer from structural vulnerabilities:
- **Collegium System Deficit**: Established via judicial interpretation in 1993/1998, the collegium lacks statutory transparency, leading to criticisms of social homogeneity, lack of SC/ST/OBC/Minority representation, and perceived nepotism.
- **Partisan Deferment of Electoral & Defection Cases**: Discretionary listing and serial adjournments enable partisan delays in defection petitions and election rigging challenges, allowing defectors and illegitimate governments to complete full terms before trial completion (*Keisham Meghachandra Singh* 2020 SC; *Subhash Desai* 2023 SC).
- **Opaque Court Archives**: Absence of statutory mandates for permanent public archiving of court live streams enables selective non-publishing or deletion of sensitive proceedings (*Swapnil Tripathi* 2018 SC).

## 6. Draft Legal Text: Statutory Bill and Companion Constitutional Amendment Bill

### PART A: THE CONSTITUTION (ONE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-EIGHTH AMENDMENT) BILL, 2026

**BILL NO. ______ OF 2026**

*A Bill further to amend the Constitution of India to abolish the collegium system of judicial appointments, to establish the National Judicial Appointments and Social Representation Commission, to mandate proportional social representation and merit-based selection, to mandate national archival preservation of court live streams, to enforce time-bound day-to-day hearings for electoral and defection cases, and to establish supermajority entrenchment for judicial amendments.*

BE it enacted by Parliament in the Seventy-Seventh Year of the Republic of India as follows:—

**1. Short title and commencement.**
(1) This Act may be called the Constitution (One Hundred and Thirty-Eighth Amendment) Act, 2026.
(2) It shall come into force on such date as the Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, appoint, being not later than thirty days from the date of Presidential Assent.

**2. Insertion of new Articles 124A, 124B, and 124C.**
After Article 124 of the Constitution, the following Articles shall be inserted, namely:—

> **"124A. National Judicial Appointments and Social Representation Commission.**
> (1) There shall be a Commission to be known as the National Judicial Appointments and Social Representation Commission consisting of the following, namely:—
>   (a) the Chief Justice of India, Chairperson, *ex officio*;
>   (b) two other senior Judges of the Supreme Court next to the Chief Justice of India, Members, *ex officio*;
>   (c) the Union Minister in charge of Law and Justice, Member, *ex officio*;
>   (d) two Eminent Persons to be nominated by a committee consisting of the Prime Minister, the Leader of Opposition in the House of the People, and the Chief Justice of India, Members:
>       Provided that one of the eminent persons shall be nominated from amongst the persons belonging to the Scheduled Castes, the Scheduled Tribes, the Other Backward Classes, Minorities, or Women.
> (2) No act or proceeding of the Commission shall be invalid merely by reason of any vacancy in, or defect in the constitution of, the Commission.

> **"124B. Duties and Social Representation Mandate of the Commission.**
> (1) It shall be the duty of the Commission to—
>   (a) recommend persons for appointment as Chief Justice of India, Judges of the Supreme Court, Chief Justices of High Courts and other Judges of High Courts;
>   (b) recommend transfer of Chief Justices and other Judges of High Courts from one High Court to any other High Court; and
>   (c) ensure that the person recommended is of high integrity and merit.
> (2) The Commission shall ensure that judicial appointments reflect proportional representation across all social, caste, and religious communities (including Hindus, Muslims, Christians, Sikhs, Parsis, Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, Other Backward Classes, and Women) based on demographic diversity, so that no single caste, community, or religion exercises dominance in judicial appointments."

> **"124C. Power of Parliament to make law.**
> Parliament may, by law, regulate the procedure for the appointment of the Chief Justice of India and other Judges of the Supreme Court and Chief Justices and other Judges of High Courts and empower the Commission to lay down by regulations the procedure for the discharge of its functions."

**3. Amendment of Article 124 and Article 217.**
In Article 124 and Article 217 of the Constitution, for the words "after consultation with such of the Judges of the Supreme Court and of the High Courts", the words "on the recommendation of the National Judicial Appointments and Social Representation Commission" shall be substituted.

**4. Insertion of new Article 145A.**
After Article 145 of the Constitution, the following Article shall be inserted, namely:—

> **"145A. Mandatory Live Streaming and National Archival Preservation.**
> (1) All proceedings in the Supreme Court, High Courts, and Subordinate Courts shall be telecast live on air for public viewing and permanently preserved in the National Archives of India for free public access over the internet.
> (2) Closed-door proceedings shall be permitted strictly and exclusively in cognizable cases involving sexual offenses against women and children under the Protection of Children from Sexual Offences Act, 2012, or the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023."

**5. Amendment of Article 368.**
In Article 368 of the Constitution, in clause (2), after the fourth proviso, the following proviso shall be inserted, namely:—

> "Provided further that if any amendment seeks to alter, repeal, or weaken the provisions governing the composition, appointment, social representation, live stream archiving, or judicial oversight under Article 124A, 124B, or 145A, the Bill must be passed in each House of Parliament by a majority of not less than **two-thirds of the total sanctioned strength of that House** (calculated without deducting suspended, absent, or vacant seats), and ratified by resolutions passed by not less than **two-thirds of the Legislative Assemblies of the States**."

### PART B: THE JUDICIAL COMMISSION, SOCIAL REPRESENTATION, OPEN COURTS, AND JUDICIAL ACCOUNTABILITY BILL, 2026

**BILL NO. ______ OF 2026**

*A Bill to operationalize the National Judicial Appointments and Social Representation Commission; to establish the National Judicial Oversight and Conduct Commission; to criminalize appointment rigging, judicial corruption, live stream tampering, and intentional deferment of political defection/election rigging cases; to prohibit summary dismissal of Public Interest Litigations; to mandate 24/7 Special Emergency Benches; to integrate artificial intelligence in judicial analytics; to protect the right to peaceful protest against miscarriage of justice; and for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto.*

BE it enacted by Parliament in the Seventy-Seventh Year of the Republic of India as follows:—

#### CHAPTER I: PRELIMINARY
**1. Short title, extent, and commencement.**
(1) This Act may be called the Judicial Commission, Social Representation, Open Courts, and Judicial Accountability Act, 2026.
(2) It extends to the whole of India.
(3) It shall come into force on the date of Presidential Assent.

---

#### CHAPTER II: MERIT AND DIVERSITY SELECTION PROCEDURE
**2. Merit Evaluation and Proportional Diversity Scoring.**
(1) The National Judicial Appointments and Social Representation Commission shall evaluate candidates for judicial appointment using a transparent, multi-attribute scoring framework measuring legal acumen, integrity, disposal rate, scholarly work, and character audit.
(2) The Commission shall publish candidate shortlists and diversity breakdown reports on an open digital portal thirty days prior to recommending names to the President.
(3) Whosoever accepts gratification, exercises political influence, or conspires to rig judicial selection or bypass statutory social representation mandates shall be punished with **rigorous imprisonment for a term which shall not be less than seven years but which may extend to fourteen years**, asset forfeiture, and permanent disqualification from public office.

---

#### CHAPTER III: JUDICIAL OVERSIGHT, CORRUPTION, AND MISCONDUCT
**3. Establishment of National Judicial Oversight and Conduct Commission (NJOCC).**
(1) The Central Government shall establish an independent statutory body to be called the National Judicial Oversight and Conduct Commission (NJOCC), comprising retired Supreme Court judges, eminent jurists, and public representatives, to investigate complaints of corruption, bribery, and misconduct against judges.
(2) **Prior Judicial Screening Gate**: No criminal investigation or trial under sub-section (3) against a Judge of the Supreme Court or High Court shall be registered or initiated except upon prior written authorization by a **5-Judge Constitution Bench of the Supreme Court of India**, finding that *prima facie* verifiable financial or documentary evidence exists.
(3) **Offence of Judicial Corruption and Bad-Faith Collusion**: Any Judge of the Supreme Court, High Court, or Subordinate Court who accepts bribery, monetary gratification, or acts in bad-faith collusion with executive authorities to suppress fundamental rights or intentionally delay election cases shall be punished with **rigorous imprisonment for a term which shall not be less than seven years but which may extend to fourteen years**, mandatory asset forfeiture, removal from office, and loss of post-retirement benefits.

---

#### CHAPTER IV: PIL SAFEGUARDS, DAY-TO-DAY ELECTORAL HEARINGS, AND EMERGENCY BENCHES
**4. Prohibition of Summary Dismissal of PILs.**
(1) No Public Interest Litigation (PIL) filed under Article 32 or Article 226 of the Constitution shall be dismissed in limine without granting a preliminary written hearing and recording a reasoned order.
(2) Every order dismissing a PIL shall state explicit legal reasons and be published on the open court portal within twenty-four hours.

**5. Mandatory 30-Day Day-to-Day Continuous Hearings for Electoral and Defection Cases.**
(1) All cases, writs, petitions, or appeals concerning political defection, election rigging, electoral roll manipulation, or political horse-trading shall be heard on a **day-to-day continuous basis** without adjournment.
(2) Final judgment in all matters specified in sub-section (1) shall mandatorily be rendered within **thirty days** from the date of filing.
(3) **Offence of Intentional Judicial Deferment or Collusion**: Any judge, judicial officer, or court administrator who intentionally adjourns, defers, or delays a matter specified in sub-section (1) to favor any political party or ruling government shall be guilty of an offence under this Section and shall be punished with **rigorous imprisonment for a term which shall not be less than seven years but which may extend to fourteen years**, mandatory forfeiture of personal assets, and immediate removal from judicial office.

**6. Mandatory 24/7 Special Emergency Benches.**
(1) The Supreme Court of India and every High Court shall operate a **24/7 Special Emergency Bench** functioning day and night throughout the year.
(2) The Special Emergency Bench shall immediately hear urgent matters concerning habeas corpus, political horse-trading, election rigging, grave civil liberty violations, and matters of national importance upon electronic filing.

---

#### CHAPTER V: ARCHIVAL PRESERVATION, AI ANALYTICS, AND PEACEFUL PROTEST PROTECTION
**7. National Archival Preservation and Offence of Tampering or Denying Access.**
(1) All live stream recordings of court proceedings shall be permanently preserved in the National Archives of India and made accessible to the public for **free online viewing**, with certified physical or digital copies provided on demand for nominal fees covering duplication costs.
(2) Whosoever blocks, alters, modifies, censors, or destroys any court live stream recording, or denies public access thereto, shall be punished with **rigorous imprisonment for a term which shall not be less than five years but which may extend to ten years**, fine, and permanent dismissal from service.

**8. AI-Assisted Judicial Analytics and Reasoning Review.**
(1) The Supreme Court of India shall establish an Artificial Intelligence Judicial Analytics Unit to assist courts with automated legal precedent lookup, case backlog tracking, and judicial reasoning consistency audits.
(2) AI analytics reports shall be advisory and open to public inspection.

**9. Protection of Right to Peaceful Protest Against Miscarriage of Justice.**
(1) All citizens shall have the non-derogable right under Article 19(1)(b) to assemble peaceably and protest against judicial or executive miscarriage of justice.
(2) No police or security force shall use physical force, tear gas, water cannons, or preventive detention against peaceful protestors exercising rights under sub-section (1).
(3) Any police officer or official who uses illegal force against peaceful protestors shall be punished with rigorous imprisonment for a term which may extend to three years and shall pay personal compensation to victims.

## 7. Clause-by-Clause Explanatory Notes

- **Part A Clause 2**: Inserts Articles 124A, 124B, and 124C establishing the 6-member NJARC, abolishing the collegium, and mandating proportional social representation (SC/ST/OBC/Minorities/Women) alongside merit.
- **Part A Clause 4**: Inserts Article 145A mandating 100% live streaming and permanent National Archives preservation for free public access.
- **Part A Clause 5**: Amends Article 368 requiring a 2/3rd supermajority of total parliamentary seats (ignoring MP suspensions) + 2/3rd State Assemblies ratification for judicial amendments.
- **Part B Clause 2**: Regulates transparent merit scoring and diversity reports; imposes 7-14 years imprisonment for appointment rigging.
- **Part B Clause 3**: Creates NJOCC for judicial misconduct oversight; requires 5-Judge SC Constitution Bench screening before investigating judges; imposes 7-14 years imprisonment and asset forfeiture for judicial corruption/collusion.
- **Part B Clause 4-6**: Prohibits summary PIL dismissal without preliminary hearing; mandates 30-day continuous day-to-day hearings for election rigging/defection cases; imposes 7-14 years imprisonment for intentional judicial deferment/collusion; mandates 24/7 Special Emergency Benches.
- **Part B Clause 7-9**: Mandates National Archives preservation of live streams and free public access; imposes 5-10 years imprisonment for tampering/denying access; integrates AI judicial analytics; protects citizen rights to peaceful protest against miscarriage of justice without police force.

## 8. Equality, Freedom, Public Goods, and Fiscal Impact Assessment

- **Social Equality & Judicial Diversity**: Eliminates single-caste or single-community dominance by guaranteeing proportional representation for SC/ST/OBC and Minority communities.
- **Electoral Justice & Anti-Deferment**: Eliminates political deferment by enforcing 30-day day-to-day trial completion for election rigging and political defection cases.
- **Public Goods & Open Court Archiving**: National Archives preservation and free public access to live streams guarantee open justice as a core democratic public good.
- **Fiscal Impact**: ₹60 Crores initial setup for live streaming servers, National Archives digital storage, NJOCC portals, and AI analytics units, met from the Consolidated Fund of India.

Mitigated by Part B Section 5 (mandatory 30-day day-to-day hearing rule) and Part B Section 5(3) (7-14 years imprisonment for intentional judicial deferment/collusion).
- **Citation Verification**: Verified against *NJAC Case* (2015) 5 SCC 1, *Swapnil Tripathi* (2018) 10 SCC 639, *Keisham Meghachandra Singh* (2020) 14 SCC 307, *Subhash Desai* (2023) INSC 516, and *Anoop Baranwal* (2023) 6 SCC 161.
