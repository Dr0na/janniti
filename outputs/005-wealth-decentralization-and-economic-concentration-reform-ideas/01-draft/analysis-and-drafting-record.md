# Analytical and Drafting Record: National Wealth and Human Capital Decentralization Bill, 2026

## 1. Executive Summary & Problem Definition

### 1.1 The Public Problem
Economic growth in India over recent decades has been accompanied by a historical concentration of wealth in a small number of conglomerate enterprises and ultra-high-net-worth individuals, alongside a hyper-concentration of human capital and economic opportunities in a handful of Tier-1 metropolitan agglomerations (e.g., Delhi-NCR, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Chennai).

This dual concentration presents major national security, democratic, and economic threats:
1. **Oligopolistic Capital Capture**: Market dominance in critical infrastructure (ports, airports, telecommunications, energy, digital media) distorts public policy, stifles entry of small and medium enterprises (MSMEs), and creates "too-big-to-fail" systemic economic risks.
2. **Geographic and Human Capital Inequality**: Concentration of top-tier educational institutions, corporate headquarters, and high-paying jobs in a few urban hubs forces mass economic migration, creates acute urban infrastructure stress (housing shortages, pollution, water crises), and drains talent and capital from Tier-2/3 cities and rural districts.
3. **Vulnerability to Political Repeal & Corruption**: Ordinary statutory anti-monopoly or progressive tax measures passed by one administration are easily diluted or repealed by subsequent business-backed or capitalist administrations through simple parliamentary majorities. Furthermore, large public dividend funds and asset valuation mechanisms risk corruption, valuation fraud, and fund siphoning unless protected by stringent statutory integrity frameworks.

### 1.2 Legislative Objective
To formulate a comprehensive, rights-respecting, democratically entrenched statute—**The National Wealth and Human Capital Decentralization Bill, 2026**—that:
1. **Entrenches Wealth Decentralization & Anti-Corruption**: Establishes an independent, multi-stakeholder National Wealth Dividend Trust and Citizen Dividend Fund, protected through statutory entrenchment mechanisms (supermajority amendment thresholds, joint Union-State federal compacts under Concurrent List Entry 20, and arm's-length trust asset insulation from executive expropriation), reinforced by severe anti-corruption criminal sanctions (10 years to life imprisonment for fund misappropriation, 300% asset forfeiture, and independent Special Anti-Corruption Courts).
2. **De-concentrates Conglomerate Capital**: Enforces cross-sectoral antitrust limits, structural unbundling of essential infrastructure, progressive capital wealth levies above high net-worth thresholds, and statutory worker ownership options.
3. **Decentralizes Human Capital via Democratic Means**: Incentivizes and enforces geographic distribution of human capital **without violating fundamental rights of free movement (Article 19(1)(d), 19(1)(e))**, through distributed public university/research footprints, mandatory Union CPSE/agency headquarter decentralization to non-metropolitan districts, regional remote-work wage rights, and equalizing civic/healthcare infrastructure spending across districts.

## 3. Entrenchment & Anti-Corruption Architecture

### 3.1 Entrenchment Against Unilateral Political Repeal
1. **Statutory Trust & Arm's-Length Insulation**: Assets of the *National Citizen Wealth Dividend Fund* are held in an independent statutory trust governed by an autonomous Board of Trustees (comprising representatives nominated by the Chief Justice of India, Inter-State Council, recognized labor unions, and regional enterprise associations). The assets do not form part of the Consolidated Fund of India and cannot be diverted or liquidated by executive notification or budget appropriation.
2. **Special Parliamentary Special-Majority Amendment Requirement**: Section 30 of the Bill specifies that any amendment to dilute the core asset distribution, antitrust thresholds, or citizen dividend provisions requires a resolution passed by a two-thirds majority of members present and voting in each House of Parliament, following formal consultation with the Inter-State Council.
3. **Federal Multi-State Compact Framework**: Core human capital and regional infrastructure allocation provisions are enacted under Concurrent List Entry 20, establishing a Union-State Compact where State Legislatures adopt mirror provisions. Under constitutional federalism principles, Union statutory changes cannot unilaterally dismantle State-level decentralized trust holdings.

### 3.2 Anti-Corruption & Anti-Misappropriation Framework
1. **Special Anti-Corruption Directorate & Ombudsperson**: An autonomous investigative body headed by a Special Integrity Ombudsperson appointed by a consensus panel (CJI, Prime Minister, Leader of Opposition) with independent powers to investigate and prosecute corruption, valuation fraud, or fund diversion.
2. **Mandatory Annual Asset & Conflict Disclosures**: Statutory duty for all Trustees, officers, valuation assessors, and auditors to publicly disclose global personal assets and financial interests annually on an open digital portal.
3. **Severe Criminal Penalties**:
   * *Misappropriation of Citizen Wealth Funds*: Minimum 10 years extending up to Life Imprisonment, 300% financial penalty on misappropriated funds, and immediate civil forfeiture of all direct/indirect proceeds of crime.
   * *Collusive Tax Evasion & Valuation Fraud*: Minimum 7 years extending to 14 years Imprisonment, 300% tax penalty, personal director liability, and permanent corporate directorship disqualification.
   * *Regulatory Corruption in Antitrust Unbundling*: Minimum 7 years Imprisonment and corporate license revocation.
4. **Whistleblower Protection & Financial Rewards**: Statutory protection against retaliation and a 10% financial reward (capped at ₹5 Crore) from recovered proceeds for reporting verified valuation fraud or fund misappropriation.
5. **Special Courts & Day-to-Day Trial**: Exclusive Special Courts mandated to conduct trials on a day-to-day basis and complete proceedings within 12 months.

## 4. Operational Strategy for Human Capital Decentralization

Coercive restrictions on human movement are unconstitutional under Article 19(1)(d) & 19(1)(e). The Bill achieves human capital decentralization through four positive economic drivers:

1. **Decentralized Higher Education & National Research Laboratories**:
   * Mandates that all premier Union higher education and research institutions (IITs, IIMs, AIIMS, CSIR labs, Central Universities) allocate at least 40% of new capital expansion and satellite research centers to Tier-2, Tier-3, and rural districts.
   * Establishes a *Regional Innovation & Research Fund* offering 100% matched grants for research R&D centers located outside Tier-1 urban agglomerations.

2. **Public Sector Agency & Corporate Headquarter Relocation**:
   * Directs Union Ministries, Central Public Sector Enterprises (CPSEs), statutory regulatory bodies, and public sector banks to relocate non-capital operational headquarters and technological divisions out of metropolitan areas (Delhi-NCR, Mumbai, Bengaluru) to identified non-metropolitan district hubs within 5 years.

3. **Regional Remote Work & High-Value Job Distribution Rights**:
   * Enacts statutory rights for digital, technology, and corporate employees to perform remote work from non-metropolitan districts without territorial pay penalties.
   * Offers a 150% weighted tax deduction under direct tax laws for corporate payroll expenses paid to employees residing and working in designated non-metropolitan economic development zones.

4. **Civic & Healthcare Quality Equalization Mandate**:
   * Requires the Union and State governments to achieve per-capita public health, primary/secondary education, and broadband infrastructure parity across all non-metropolitan districts within 7 years, removing the push factors driving forced urban migration.

## 6. Detailed Clause Breakdown of Revised Bill

* **Clauses 1–3**: Short title, extent (All India), staged commencement, definitions, declaration of Article 39(b/c) constitutional purpose, and fundamental rights compliance.
* **Clauses 4–6**: Establishment of National Citizen Wealth Dividend Trust, Citizen Wealth Dividend Fund (protected from executive appropriation), and transparent equal citizen cash dividend payments.
* **Clauses 7–8**: Conglomerate antitrust limits (25% cross-sector infrastructure market share cap) and structural separation of platform/infrastructure operators from downstream commercial trading.
* **Clauses 9–11**: Progressive net wealth tax on assets > ₹50 Cr (1–3% rates with exemptions for primary homes/small farms), Worker Ownership Trusts, and mandatory non-managerial workforce representation on corporate boards.
* **Clauses 12–15**: Human capital decentralization framework: public agency headquarter relocation, regional university expansion, remote work wage parity rights, and district-level civic/health infrastructure parity mandates.
* **Clauses 16–25**: **Chapter IV: Integrity, Anti-Corruption, Whistleblower Protection and Offences**:
  * Clause 16: Mandatory public asset and conflict of interest disclosures for Trustees and officers.
  * Clause 17: Independent Special Anti-Corruption Directorate and Ombudsperson.
  * Clause 18: Zero-trust cryptographic audit trail and annual CAG Special Audit.
  * Clause 19: Offence of criminal misappropriation of Citizen Wealth Funds (mandatory 10 years to life imprisonment, 300% fine, and 100% asset forfeiture).
  * Clause 20: Offence of collusive valuation fraud and wealth tax evasion (7 to 14 years imprisonment, 300% tax penalty, personal director liability).
  * Clause 21: Offence of regulatory corruption in antitrust unbundling (7 years imprisonment).
  * Clause 22: Establishment of Special Anti-Corruption Courts and 12-month day-to-day trial mandate.
  * Clause 23: Elimination of prior executive sanction requirements for corruption prosecutions under the Act.
  * Clause 24: Whistleblower protection framework and 10% financial reward on recovered proceeds.
  * Clause 25: Permanent disqualification of convicted persons from public office, statutory trusts, or corporate directorships.
* **Clauses 26–32**: Entrenchment safeguard (2/3 parliamentary special majority + Inter-State Council concurrence), annual democratic impact reporting, rulemaking limits, saving, and transition.
