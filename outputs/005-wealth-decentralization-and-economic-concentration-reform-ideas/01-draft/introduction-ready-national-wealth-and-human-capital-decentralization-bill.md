THE NATIONAL WEALTH AND HUMAN CAPITAL DECENTRALIZATION BILL, 2026

A

BILL

to give effect to the Directive Principles of State Policy under clauses (b) and (c) of Article 39 of the Constitution of India; to establish a statutory framework for the progressive decentralization of national economic wealth; to curb conglomerate market concentration and cross-sectoral economic monopolies; to establish an independent National Citizen Wealth Dividend Trust; to promote employee ownership and democratic enterprise governance; to mandate the affirmative decentralization of human capital, public institutions, and economic opportunities across non-metropolitan districts; to establish an independent anti-corruption oversight framework, Special Anti-Corruption Courts, severe criminal penalties, asset forfeiture, and whistleblower protections against fund misappropriation and collusive valuation fraud; and for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto.

BE it enacted by Parliament in the Seventy-Seventh Year of the Republic of India as follows:—

### 1. Short title, extent and commencement

1. This Act may be called the National Wealth and Human Capital Decentralization Act, 2026.

2. It extends to the whole of India.

3. It shall come into force on such date as the Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, appoint:

Provided that different dates may be appointed for different provisions of this Act, and any reference in any such provision to the commencement of this Act shall be construed as a reference to the coming into force of that provision.

### 2. Definitions

In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires,—

(a) "Board of Trustees" means the independent Board of Trustees of the National Citizen Wealth Dividend Trust established under section 4;

(b) "Citizen Wealth Dividend Fund" means the statutory trust fund established under section 5;

(c) "Commission" means the Competition Commission of India established under section 7 of the Competition Act, 2002;

(d) "Conglomerate Group" means an interconnected group of enterprise entities under common ownership, control, or promoter management operating across two or more distinct key economic sectors including infrastructure, telecommunications, energy, digital media, logistics, or financial services;

(e) "Essential National Infrastructure" means ports, airports, power transmission grids, national highway networks, telecommunications spectrum, public transport networks, and municipal water utilities;

(f) "Human Capital Development Hub" means a designated Tier-2 city, Tier-3 city, or rural district headquarters identified under section 12 for targeted educational, industrial, and public sector decentralization;

(g) "Net High-Value Capital Asset" means the total valuation of global capital assets, corporate equity, real estate, and financial instruments held by an individual, Hindu undivided family, or private trust exceeding the threshold specified under section 9, excluding primary personal residential property and active agricultural holdings;

(h) "Non-Metropolitan District" means any revenue district situated outside the territorial limits of a Tier-1 urban agglomeration as classified by the Census of India;

(i) "Prescribed" means prescribed by rules made under this Act;

(j) "Proceeds of Crime" means any property, asset, equity, or financial gain derived or obtained, directly or indirectly, by any person as a result of criminal misappropriation, bribery, valuation fraud, or tax evasion under this Act;

(k) "Special Court" means a Special Court designated under section 22 for the trial of offences under this Act;

(l) "Special Integrity Ombudsperson" means the head of the Special Anti-Corruption Directorate appointed under section 17; and

(m) "Worker Ownership Trust" means an employee-controlled trust established under section 10 for holding corporate equity on behalf of enterprise employees.

### 3. Declaration of statutory purpose and basic democratic principles

1. It is hereby declared that this Act is for giving effect to the policy of the State towards securing the principles specified in clauses (b) and (c) of Article 39 of the Constitution of India.

2. The decentralization of economic wealth and human capital under this Act shall be carried out strictly through non-coercive affirmative incentives, public capital distribution, structural regulatory measures, and institutional expansion, preserving at all times the fundamental freedoms of movement, residence, trade, and personal liberty guaranteed under Articles 19 and 21 of the Constitution.

### 4. Establishment of National Citizen Wealth Dividend Trust

1. The Central Government shall, within six months of the commencement of this Act, establish an independent statutory body to be known as the National Citizen Wealth Dividend Trust.

2. The Trust shall be a body corporate by the name aforesaid, having perpetual succession and a common seal, with power to acquire, hold, and dispose of property, and to contract, and shall by the said name sue or be sued.

3. The head office of the Trust shall be located in a designated Non-Metropolitan District Hub outside Tier-1 metropolitan areas.

4. The Trust shall be governed by an independent Board of Trustees consisting of eleven members, namely:—

  (a) a Chairperson, who shall be a retired judge of the Supreme Court of India or a High Court, appointed by the President of India on the recommendation of a selection committee comprising the Prime Minister, the Leader of the Opposition in the House of the People, and the Chief Justice of India;

  (b) two representatives nominated by the Inter-State Council representing State Governments;

  (c) two representatives from recognized national labor unions and worker co-operatives;

  (d) two independent economists specializing in public finance and regional development;

  (e) two representatives from micro, small, and medium enterprise associations; and

  (f) two ex-officio members, being the Secretary to the Government of India in the Department of Economic Affairs and the Governor of the Reserve Bank of India or their nominee.

### 5. Citizen Wealth Dividend Fund and anti-expropriation safeguards

1. The Trust shall manage a dedicated statutory fund to be called the Citizen Wealth Dividend Fund.

2. There shall be credited to the Citizen Wealth Dividend Fund—

  (a) proceeds collected from the progressive net wealth levy under section 9;

  (b) twenty-five per cent of all revenue proceeds derived from public auctions of natural resources, telecommunication spectrum, and mining leases;

  (c) dividends accruing from state-held equity shares transferred to the Trust; and

  (d) returns on investments made by the Trust in approved public infrastructure projects.

3. The capital assets and cash balances of the Citizen Wealth Dividend Fund shall vest exclusively in the Board of Trustees in trust for the citizens of India.

4. The assets of the Fund shall not form part of the Consolidated Fund of India or the Consolidated Fund of any State, and shall not be subject to executive appropriation, budget diversion, or attachment by any authority.

### 6. Annual Citizen Wealth Dividend distribution

1. The Board of Trustees shall, after meeting operational reserves and capital reinvestment allocations, distribute the net annual income of the Citizen Wealth Dividend Fund as an equal annual cash dividend to every citizen of India.

2. The dividend shall be credited directly into the verified bank account of each eligible citizen through transparent digital payment channels.

3. No Citizen Wealth Dividend paid under this section shall be subject to income tax or set-off against existing social welfare entitlements under any law for the time being in force.

### 7. Conglomerate cross-sector antitrust limits

1. No single Conglomerate Group shall hold a market share exceeding twenty-five per cent across more than two distinct sectors of Essential National Infrastructure.

2. The Competition Commission of India shall conduct an annual market concentration audit of all Conglomerate Groups operating in India.

3. Where the Commission determines that a Conglomerate Group exercises dominant cross-sectoral control creating systemic economic vulnerability or market foreclosure, it shall issue a structural unbundling order directing the group to divest its equity interest or operational control in specified subsidiary entities within two years.

### 8. Structural separation of infrastructure and commercial operations

1. No enterprise entity that owns, operates, or manages Essential National Infrastructure or a dominant digital marketplace platform shall engage in downstream commercial trading of goods or services in direct competition with independent enterprise users of such infrastructure or platform.

2. Any contract, agreement, or arrangement entered into in violation of sub-section (1) shall be void ab initio.

### 9. Levy of progressive net wealth tax on ultra-high-net-worth assets

1. There shall be charged, levied, and paid for every assessment year a progressive net wealth tax on the net high-value capital assets of every individual, Hindu undivided family, and private trust whose total global capital assets exceed the threshold of fifty crore rupees.

2. The net wealth tax shall be levied at the following rates, namely:—

  (a) on net assets exceeding fifty crore rupees but not exceeding two hundred crore rupees, one per cent per annum;

  (b) on net assets exceeding two hundred crore rupees but not exceeding one thousand crore rupees, two per cent per annum; and

  (c) on net assets exceeding one thousand crore rupees, three per cent per annum.

3. In computing net high-value capital assets under sub-section (1), the following assets shall be excluded, namely:—

  (a) one primary residential house property occupied by the assessee;

  (b) active agricultural land situated in India below statutory State ceiling limits; and

  (c) equity held in micro, small, and medium enterprises or registered worker-owned co-operatives.

### 10. Worker Ownership Trusts and tax incentives

1. Any public or private corporate enterprise registered under the Companies Act, 2013 may establish a Worker Ownership Trust for acquiring and holding equity shares of the enterprise on behalf of its employees.

2. Where an enterprise transfers not less than twenty per cent of its voting share capital to a Worker Ownership Trust,—

  (a) fifty per cent of the dividend income received by the Trust shall be exempt from direct tax; and

  (b) the enterprise shall be eligible for a corporate income tax credit equal to ten per cent of its annual tax liability for five consecutive assessment years.

### 11. Employee representation on corporate boards

1. Every public listed company having a net worth exceeding five hundred crore rupees or an annual turnover exceeding one thousand crore rupees shall ensure that not less than two members of its Board of Directors are elected directly by the non-managerial workforce of the company.

2. Directors elected under sub-section (1) shall enjoy equal voting rights, duties, and privileges as other directors on the Board.

### 12. Affirmative decentralization of Union public institutions and headquarters

1. The Central Government shall, within twelve months of the commencement of this Act, formulate and notify a phased National Public Institutional Relocation Plan.

2. The Plan shall mandate the relocation of operational headquarters, research wings, and technical divisions of non-capital Union Ministries, Central Public Sector Enterprises, statutory regulatory commissions, and public financial institutions out of Tier-1 metropolitan agglomerations to designated Human Capital Development Hubs in Non-Metropolitan Districts.

3. The relocation required under sub-section (2) shall be completed within five years from the date of notification of the Plan.

### 13. Decentralized footprint of national higher education and research institutes

1. Every national institute of higher education or advanced research established or funded by the Central Government, including Indian Institutes of Technology, Indian Institutes of Management, All India Institutes of Medical Sciences, and Central Universities, shall allocate not less than forty per cent of its capital expansion budget to setting up campuses, research labs, and incubator centers in Non-Metropolitan Districts.

2. The University Grants Commission and Central educational authorities shall disburse additional matching research grants to universities establishing regional research clusters in Tier-2 and Tier-3 districts.

### 14. Statutory remote work rights and regional payroll tax credits

1. Every employee engaged in technology, corporate, or knowledge-based services shall have the right to request remote work from a Non-Metropolitan District, subject to objective operational feasibility determined by an independent grievance redressal tribunal.

2. No employer shall reduce the base compensation or benefits of an employee solely on the ground that the employee resides and performs work in a Non-Metropolitan District.

3. Any corporate employer that maintains not less than thirty per cent of its total employee workforce in Non-Metropolitan Districts shall be entitled to a weighted tax deduction equal to one hundred and fifty per cent of the payroll expenses incurred on such employees under direct tax laws.

### 15. District civic and health infrastructure parity mandate

1. The Central Government, in consultation with State Governments through the Inter-State Council, shall establish mandatory per-capita public expenditure benchmarks for health, primary education, sanitation, clean drinking water, and high-speed digital connectivity across all Non-Metropolitan Districts.

2. The benchmarks established under sub-section (1) shall ensure that by the end of seven years from the commencement of this Act, public infrastructure quality in Non-Metropolitan Districts achieves parity with Tier-1 urban agglomeration standards.

### 16. Mandatory annual public asset and conflict of interest disclosures

1. Every Trustee, officer, tax valuation assessor, and auditor appointed under this Act shall, within thirty days of assuming office and annually thereafter, submit a complete digital declaration of global personal assets, liabilities, corporate shareholdings, and potential financial conflicts of interest.

2. Declarations submitted under sub-section (1) shall be published openly on a searchable public digital portal managed by the Special Anti-Corruption Directorate.

3. Any person who fails to submit an asset declaration under sub-section (1) or submits false, misleading, or incomplete asset details shall be subject to immediate suspension from office and prosecution under section 20.

### 17. Special Anti-Corruption Directorate and Ombudsperson

1. There shall be established an autonomous investigative wing within the Trust to be known as the Special Anti-Corruption Directorate.

2. The Directorate shall be headed by a Special Integrity Ombudsperson, appointed by the President of India on the recommendation of a selection committee comprising the Chief Justice of India, the Prime Minister, and the Leader of the Opposition in the House of the People.

3. The Special Integrity Ombudsperson shall exercise independent powers of investigation, search, seizure, asset attachment, and prosecution in respect of any offence of corruption, bribery, valuation fraud, or fund misappropriation under this Act.

### 18. Zero-trust digital audit trail and CAG Special Audit Cell

1. All financial transactions, wealth tax assessments, asset valuations, and dividend disbursements under this Act shall be executed through an open, zero-trust digital platform incorporating cryptographic transaction logging and real-time public audit capabilities.

2. The Comptroller and Auditor General of India shall establish a dedicated Special Audit Cell to conduct continuous real-time forensic audits of the Citizen Wealth Dividend Fund and submit quarterly audit reports directly to Parliament and the Special Anti-Corruption Directorate.

### 19. Offence of criminal misappropriation of Citizen Wealth Funds

1. Whosoever, being a Trustee, public servant, officer, banking agent, or digital system administrator, criminally misappropriates, siphons, or diverts any capital asset or monetary sum belonging to the Citizen Wealth Dividend Fund, or conspires to do so, shall be punishable with rigorous imprisonment for a term which shall not be less than ten years but which may extend to imprisonment for life, and shall also be liable to a fine equal to three hundred per cent of the value of the misappropriated funds.

2. All property, equity, bank accounts, and financial assets acquired through the proceeds of crime under sub-section (1) shall be subject to immediate statutory attachment and civil forfeiture to the Citizen Wealth Dividend Fund.

### 20. Offence of collusive valuation fraud and wealth tax evasion

1. Any assessee, corporate officer, tax practitioner, or valuation assessor who willfully falsifies, conceals, under-reports, or misrepresents net high-value capital assets, offshore holdings, or corporate equity to evade the net wealth levy under section 9, or who gives or accepts any bribe for such purpose, shall be punishable with rigorous imprisonment for a term which shall not be less than seven years but which may extend to fourteen years, and shall also be liable to a fine equal to three hundred per cent of the tax evaded or benefit sought.

2. Where an offence under sub-section (1) is committed by a corporate entity or private trust, every promoter, director, key managerial personnel, and professional assessor who was in charge of, or responsible for, the conduct of the business shall be jointly and severally liable to the criminal penalties and financial fines specified under sub-section (1).

### 21. Offence of regulatory corruption in antitrust unbundling

1. Any public official or member of the Competition Commission of India who solicits, accepts, or agrees to accept any gratification, bribe, or financial favor to delay, suppress, modify, or refrain from issuing a structural unbundling order under section 7 or 8 shall be punishable with rigorous imprisonment for a term which shall not be less than seven years but which may extend to fourteen years, and fine.

2. Any corporate entity or Conglomerate Group that offers, gives, or promises any gratification or bribe referred to in sub-section (1) shall be liable to a fine extending up to ten per cent of its global annual turnover, and its operating licenses in Essential National Infrastructure shall be subject to immediate statutory revocation.

### 22. Special Anti-Corruption Courts and time-bound trial mandate

1. The State Government, in consultation with the Chief Justice of the High Court, shall designate one or more Special Courts of Sessions in each State for the exclusive trial of offences under this Act.

2. A Special Court shall conduct trial proceedings under this Act on a day-to-day basis and shall complete the trial within twelve months from the date of taking cognizance of the offence.

### 23. Elimination of prior executive sanction for corruption prosecutions

1. Notwithstanding anything contained in section 197 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 or section 17A of the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988, no prior executive sanction or permission shall be required for initiating investigation, search, seizure, attachment, or prosecution against any Trustee, public servant, or corporate officer accused of an offence under this Act.

### 24. Whistleblower protection framework and financial rewards

1. Any person who provides verified information or evidence leading to the detection, investigation, or recovery of proceeds of crime, valuation fraud, or fund misappropriation under this Act shall be granted statutory protection of identity, physical security, and employment immunity.

2. A whistleblower whose information leads to successful recovery of misappropriated funds or evaded wealth taxes under sub-section (1) shall be entitled to a statutory financial reward equal to ten per cent of the recovered amount, subject to a maximum cap of five crore rupees, paid directly out of the recovered proceeds.

### 25. Permanent disqualification from public office and corporate directorships

1. Any person convicted of an offence under section 19, 20, or 21 shall, from the date of conviction, stand permanently disqualified from—

  (a) contesting any election to Parliament, State Legislature, or local self-government body;

  (b) holding any public office or appointment in any statutory trust, university, or government authority; and

  (c) holding any position as a Director, Key Managerial Personnel, or officer in any public or private company registered under the Companies Act, 2013.

### 26. Entrenchment safeguard against statutory dilution

1. Notwithstanding anything contained in any other law for the time being in force, no bill seeking to repeal, amend, or dilute the provisions of sections 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 16, 17, 19, 20, 22, or this section shall be passed in either House of Parliament unless it is passed by a majority of the total membership of that House and by a majority of not less than two-thirds of the members of that House present and voting.

2. Before any bill referred to in sub-section (1) is introduced in Parliament, the Central Government shall obtain a formal resolution of concurrence passed by the Inter-State Council.

### 27. Annual Democratic Impact Audit and reporting

1. The Board of Trustees shall submit an annual Democratic Economic Impact Report to Parliament within three months of the close of each financial year.

2. The Report shall contain comprehensive statistical details regarding—

  (a) net wealth concentration trends across top wealth deciles in India;

  (b) total capital levies collected, dividend disbursements, and anti-corruption audit findings;

  (c) market concentration ratios in essential infrastructure sectors;

  (d) progress of human capital and public institutional relocation to Non-Metropolitan Districts; and

  (e) independent evaluation of public infrastructure parity across revenue districts.

3. The Report shall be laid before both Houses of Parliament and published openly on a public digital portal.

### 28. Power to make rules

1. The Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, make rules for carrying out the provisions of this Act.

2. In particular, and without prejudice to the generality of the foregoing power, such rules may provide for all or any of the following matters, namely:—

  (a) the procedure for appointment and terms of service of Trustees under section 4;

  (b) the digital asset declaration formats and verification procedures under section 16;

  (c) the manner of verification of citizen accounts and digital disbursement of Citizen Wealth Dividends under section 6;

  (d) the valuation rules for net high-value capital assets under section 9;

  (e) the regulatory guidelines for Worker Ownership Trusts under section 10; and

  (f) any other matter which is required to be, or may be, prescribed.

3. Every rule made under this Act shall be laid, as soon as may be after it is made, before each House of Parliament, while it is in session, for a total period of thirty days which may be comprised in one session or in two or more successive sessions, and if, before the expiry of the session immediately following the session or the successive sessions aforesaid, both Houses agree in making any modification in the rule or both Houses agree that the rule should not be made, the rule shall thereafter have effect only in such modified form or be of no effect, as the case may be; so, however, that any such modification or annulment shall be without prejudice to the validity of anything previously done under that rule.

## STATEMENT OF OBJECTS AND REASONS

Over the past three decades, India's economic expansion has been accompanied by a sharp concentration of national wealth in a small number of corporate conglomerates and ultra-high-net-worth individuals. Simultaneously, economic opportunities, high-value corporate employment, and educational infrastructure have hyper-concentrated in a small group of Tier-1 metropolitan agglomerations. This dual concentration exacerbates socio-economic inequality, threatens competitive market dynamics, distorts public policy, and forces mass economic migration that severely strains urban infrastructure while draining talent from rural and semi-urban India.

The Constitution of India, through clauses (b) and (c) of Article 39 of the Directive Principles of State Policy, casts a positive duty on the State to ensure that ownership and control of material resources are distributed to subserve the common good and that the economic system does not result in the concentration of wealth and means of production to the common detriment.

To fulfill these constitutional mandates in a rights-respecting, democratically durable, and corruption-free manner, this Bill provides for:

1. The establishment of an independent National Citizen Wealth Dividend Trust and Fund to collect a progressive net wealth tax on ultra-high-net-worth assets exceeding fifty crore rupees and redistribute proceeds directly to all citizens as an equal annual Citizen Wealth Dividend.

2. Conglomerate antitrust reform empowering the Competition Commission of India to issue structural unbundling orders against cross-sectoral infrastructure monopolies.

3. Promotion of democratic enterprise governance through Worker Ownership Trusts and employee representation on corporate boards.

4. Affirmative decentralization of human capital through the phased relocation of Union public sector agency headquarters, regional expansion of national higher education institutes, protection of remote work rights, and public infrastructure spending parity across Non-Metropolitan Districts.

5. Stringent anti-corruption and integrity oversight, including mandatory asset disclosures, an independent Special Anti-Corruption Directorate, zero-trust digital audit trails, Special Courts bound to complete trials in 12 months, severe criminal penalties (10 years to life imprisonment for fund misappropriation; 300% fines), 100% asset forfeiture, whistleblower protections, and permanent disqualification from public office or corporate directorships for convicted persons.

6. Statutory entrenchment safeguards requiring a special two-thirds parliamentary majority and Inter-State Council concurrence for any future dilution of core wealth-decentralization and anti-corruption frameworks.

The Bill seeks to achieve these objectives without violating fundamental freedoms of movement, residence, or trade guaranteed under Articles 19 and 21 of the Constitution.

## FINANCIAL MEMORANDUM

1. Clause 4 provides for the establishment of the National Citizen Wealth Dividend Trust. The initial non-recurring expenditure for establishing the Trust, setting up its head office, digital dividend distribution infrastructure, and administrative machinery is estimated at two hundred crore rupees.

2. Clause 12 provides for the phased relocation of operational headquarters of non-capital Union public institutions and CPSEs to Non-Metropolitan District Hubs over five years. The non-recurring capital expenditure for institutional relocation is estimated at two thousand five hundred crore rupees, spread over five financial years.

3. Clause 13 provides for matching capital research grants for higher education expansion in Non-Metropolitan Districts, with an estimated recurring annual expenditure of one thousand crore rupees from the Consolidated Fund of India.

4. Clause 17 and Clause 22 provide for the establishment of the Special Anti-Corruption Directorate and Special Courts. The non-recurring establishment expenditure is estimated at one hundred and fifty crore rupees, with an annual recurring operational expenditure of fifty crore rupees from the Consolidated Fund of India.

5. The recurring operational expenses of the Citizen Wealth Dividend Fund under Clause 5 and disbursement of citizen dividends under Clause 6 shall be met entirely out of the statutory proceeds of the progressive net wealth levy and natural resource revenues credited to the Fund, incurring no net recurring burden on the Consolidated Fund of India.

## MEMORANDUM REGARDING DELEGATED LEGISLATION

Clause 28 of the Bill empowers the Central Government to make rules for carrying out the provisions of the Act. The matters with respect to which rules may be made pertain to procedural and administrative details regarding:

1. The terms of service and administrative procedures of the Board of Trustees under Clause 4;

2. Digital asset declaration formats and verification procedures under Clause 16;

3. Digital account verification and dividend payment mechanisms under Clause 6;

4. Asset valuation rules and tax reporting forms under Clause 9; and

5. Regulatory guidelines for registration of Worker Ownership Trusts under Clause 10.

The delegated powers are administrative in nature and strictly bounded by statutory criteria. All rules made under the Act are subject to mandatory parliamentary scrutiny and laying requirements under sub-clause (3) of Clause 28. The delegation of legislative power is, therefore, of a normal and routine character.
