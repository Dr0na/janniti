# Indian Law Landscape Research Pack: Wealth Decentralization & Anti-Concentration Measures

## 1. Scope and search record

- Matter ID: 005
- Topic and objective: Public policy, constitutional frameworks, statutory interventions, and economic mechanisms to prevent concentration of wealth/means of production and achieve wealth decentralization in India.
- Jurisdiction(s): Union of India and State/UT jurisdictions (Schedule VII competent domains).
- As-of date: 2026-07-27
- Search terms and synonyms: Concentration of wealth, Article 39(b), Article 39(c), wealth tax, estate duty, anti-trust, competition law, conglomerate dominance, employee ownership, co-operatives, public wealth fund, land ceiling, financial inclusion, priority sector lending.
- Requested legal question: How can government policies, statutory mechanisms, and constitutional frameworks effectively decentralize wealth, curb extreme capital concentration, and distribute economic power in India while respecting fundamental rights and due process?
- Sources/repositories searched and retrieval dates: Constitution of India (Legislative Department, Ministry of Law & Justice), India Code (indiacode.nic.in), Competition Commission of India (CCI), Reserve Bank of India (RBI), Ministry of Finance, Supreme Court of India reports (SCC/SCR).

## 2. Executive finding and reliance limits

Current Indian law contains explicit constitutional directives under Directive Principles of State Policy (Articles 39(b) and 39(c)) mandating that the ownership and control of material resources be distributed to subserve the common good and that the economic system not cause concentration of wealth to common detriment. However, statutory tools have become fragmented following the repeal of the Estate Duty Act 1953 (in 1985), repeal of the Wealth Tax Act 1957 (in 2015), and the replacement of the MRTP Act 1969 by the Competition Act 2002 (which focuses primarily on market abuse rather than conglomerate economic size/dominance across sectors). Decentralization can be achieved through a multi-dimensional reform package across fiscal policy, corporate governance, anti-trust regulation, public financial institutions, labor shareholding, and decentralization of land and natural resource rights.

## 9. Rights, equality, federalism, and public-interest assessment

- Fundamental Rights & Due Process: Any tax or regulatory restriction on wealth must comply with Article 14 (non-arbitrariness), Article 19(1)(g) (proportionality of trade restriction), and Article 300A (lawfulness and non-confiscatory due process).
- Federalism: Taxing powers over non-agricultural wealth belong to Parliament (List I Entry 86), while agricultural land and agricultural income belong to State Legislatures (List II Entries 18, 46, 49). Cooperative federalism is essential for joint policy initiatives (e.g., land banking, local economic decentralization).
- Substantive Equality: Wealth concentration limits access to capital, stifles entry of small entrepreneurs, distorts public policy, and deepens socio-economic disparities. Measures targeting wealth decentralization promote substantive equality.
