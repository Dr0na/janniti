# Independent Review Brief — Clean Water Bodies and Single-Use Plastic Prohibition Bill

## Review question

Can the proposed Union Bill create a stringent national prohibition on waste disposal into protected water receptors and a listed single-use-plastic ban while remaining within Parliament's competence, preserving State/local implementation, protecting equality, religious freedom, livelihoods, accessibility, due process and effective remedies, and delivering technically credible water protection?

## Why independent review is mandatory

The proposal creates criminal offences, affects waste services and livelihoods, regulates conduct associated with religious/cultural observance, and has material environmental, health, fiscal, federal and local-government effects. It must not proceed as cleared or introduction-ready until all Critical and High matters below have a recorded response.

## Review packets and reviewer criteria

| Review | Required independent expertise | Material question |
|---|---|---|
| Constitutional/public law | Indian public-law counsel with no material sponsor, industry or enforcement conflict | Exact legislative competence, Articles 14/19/21/25, federalism, Article 254 interaction, remedies and criminal-procedure compatibility. |
| Water science | Hydrogeologist, freshwater ecologist and water-quality scientist | Definitions, protected receptors, pathways, monitoring, causation, groundwater maps, standards and restoration feasibility. |
| Waste/public health | Waste-engineering, sanitation and public-health experts | Treatment capacity, sewage/septage, biomedical and hazardous waste interaction, emergency response and health impacts. |
| Equality/accessibility/labour | Disability, informal-worker/waste-picker, gender, tribal/community and livelihood specialists | Product exceptions, accessible alternatives, transition support, displacement and enforcement burdens. |
| Religious/community participation | Plural community and rights representatives, selected through published criteria | Content-neutral waste stewardship, practical alternatives and protection against selective enforcement. |
| Criminal justice/audit | Criminal-procedure, anti-corruption, election/public-service-law and constitutional-law counsel, forensic laboratory and public-finance/audit experts | Mens rea, evidence, penalties, bail/cognizability proposal, institutional separation, intentional-or-knowing circumvention by political officeholders and entities, public-service discipline, corruption and audit design. |

## Critical and High issues requiring resolution

| Severity | Issue | Required resolution |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | Exact constitutional entry/route and interaction with State water, groundwater, municipal/local-body law is unverified. | Produce a source-backed competence and savings/repugnancy opinion after State/UT mapping. |
| Critical | Schedule I is intentionally uncompleted; a total “all plastic” ban cannot be filed without evidence-backed product definitions and exceptions. | Verify current plastic rules, assess alternatives, publish schedule and transition plan. |
| High | Current rules, consents and statutes are not yet mapped to an exact amendment/repeal/savings schedule. | Verify all current official texts and draft exact consequential language. |
| High | Cost, treatment capacity and fair transition evidence is absent. | Cost a national/State/UT plan; test adverse impact on sanitation, informal workers, small enterprise, disability access and health. |
| High | The proposed prohibition on water commodification and shift to cost-recovery services has not been tested against current water-service, groundwater, public-finance, contract, labour and State/UT law. | Map existing service arrangements, assess essential-access and affordability impacts, cost the transition, and draft verified savings/transition provisions. |
| High | Scarcity priorities, ecological-flow protection, large-withdrawal controls and stressed-aquifer phase-outs need basin-specific hydrological and livelihood evidence. | Obtain independent hydrology, ecology, agriculture, fisheries, tribal/community and labour review; publish catchment/aquifer criteria and costed transition plans before commencement. |
| High | Criminal offence and investigation clauses need current criminal-procedure/NGT review. | Confirm classification, jurisdiction, evidentiary standards, penalties and remedies against current primary law. |
| High | The intentional-or-knowing circumvention offence must not duplicate, conflict with or bypass current anti-corruption, legislative-privilege, election/public-service or constitutional-removal safeguards. | Map it against current anti-corruption, criminal-procedure, legislative-privilege, electoral/disqualification, service-discipline, procurement and constitutional-removal rules; retain independent investigation and judicial review. |
| High | Court/tribunal access and local remedial capacity may be unaffordable or delayed. | Specify accessible local complaints, legal aid/referral and judicial/NGT relationship. |

## Conflict protocol and decision log

Reviewers must disclose funding, employment, political, personal, professional and litigation conflicts. A reviewer with a material conflict may provide factual evidence but shall not be sole reviewer of the disputed conclusion. Dissent, adverse authority and unresolved scientific uncertainty must be recorded and published in an appropriate redacted form.
