# Proposed FSS Evidence and Food Claims Amendment

**Draft text; not enacted.** This instrument is a proposed insertion in the Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006. It complements, and does not replace, the Food Safety and Standards (Advertising and Claims) Regulations, 2018.

## 1. Insertion of section 24A

After section 24 of the Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006, insert:—

**“24A. Evidence, transparency and correction for prescribed food claims.**—

(1) A food business operator shall not make, authorise, procure or materially benefit from a prescribed nutrition, health, safety, environmental, origin, composition or performance claim unless the claim—

(a) is specific, comprehensible and capable of independent verification;

(b) is supported by evidence proportionate to the claim, including the relevant method, limitations and uncertainty;

(c) identifies the responsible food business operator and any material sponsor; and

(d) complies with regulations made under this Act.

(2) A person shall not represent sponsored, selectively reported, ghost-written, manipulated or non-reproducible research as independent evidence.

(3) Regulations under this section may require a public evidence register for prescribed claims. The register shall disclose the claim, protocol, method, material funding, sponsor role, conflict of interest, limitations, adverse findings, data-access status and replication status, subject only to necessary protection of personal data, trade secrets and legitimate safety information.

(4) A publisher, broadcaster, platform, agency or influencer that receives material consideration for a prescribed claim and has knowledge or control of its publication shall clearly disclose that consideration and the responsible sponsor. A neutral intermediary without knowledge, control or material benefit is not liable merely for transmission.

(5) Where a claim contravenes this section, the competent authority may direct correction, withdrawal, consumer notice, preservation of evidence or a proportionate recall, after notice, access to the material evidence, a reasoned decision and an effective review route. An urgent temporary direction requires recorded objective grounds, minimum necessary scope and prompt independent review.

(6) This section shall not be used to suppress lawful scientific inquiry, consumer criticism, journalism, fair comment, academic discussion or truthful comparative information. No action may be taken on the ground of political belief, lawful criticism, association, protected characteristic or commercial rivalry.

## 2. Rule-making limits

Every regulation made under section 24A shall specify the claim category, evidence threshold, disclosure format, correction route, review body, record-retention period, privacy/trade-secret limits, enforcement standard and appeal. It shall be published, laid before Parliament and periodically reviewed.

## Source and limitation

FSS Act section 24 and FSSAI’s official regulations index are the verified route. The full current consolidated Advertising and Claims Regulations text remains subject to the source-acceptance gate; this proposed statutory insertion therefore avoids claiming an unverified current regulation number or wording.
