# BNS Food-Adulteration Minimum-Sentence Amendment Schedule

## Status and scope

**Draft text; not enacted.** This is a targeted Union amendment to section 274 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS), with the necessary matching amendment to its BNSS First Schedule classification. It implements the requested minimum custodial sentence of two years for the intentional adulteration offence. It does not amend BNS section 275 (knowing sale of noxious food or drink) or the Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006 (FSS Act).

## Proposed operative amendment

### THE BHARATIYA NYAYA SANHITA (AMENDMENT) BILL, 2026

### A BILL

to amend the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023.

**BE it enacted by Parliament in the Seventy-seventh Year of the Republic of India as follows:—**

### 1. Short title and commencement

(1) This Act may be called the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (Amendment) Act, 2026.

(2) It shall come into force on such date as the Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, appoint.

### 2. Amendment of section 274

In section 274 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, for the words “imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to six months, or with fine which may extend to five thousand rupees, or with both”, substitute the following:—

“imprisonment of either description for a term which shall not be less than two years but which may extend to five years, and shall also be liable to fine.”

### 3. Consequential amendment of the First Schedule to the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023

In the First Schedule to the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023, in the entry relating to section 274 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, substitute the following entry:—

| Section | Offence | Punishment | Cognizable or non-cognizable | Bailable or non-bailable | By what Court triable |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 274 | Adulterating food or drink intended for sale, so as to make the same noxious. | Imprisonment for not less than 2 years but which may extend to 5 years, and fine. | Non-cognizable. | Bailable. | Chief Judicial Magistrate. |

## Effect and limits

The amendment preserves every existing element of the offence: the prosecution must still prove adulteration that makes the article noxious and the required intent or knowledge. It does not create strict liability, reverse the burden of proof, retrospectively increase punishment, or displace a court’s ordinary fair-trial and sentencing duties. The matching BNSS amendment keeps the offence non-cognizable and bailable, and assigns trial to the Chief Judicial Magistrate because a Magistrate of the first class may not impose more than three years but a Chief Judicial Magistrate may impose up to seven years. A greater sentence may be available only where another applicable offence is proved on its own elements.

## FSS Act interface and non-duplication rule

The FSS Act section 59 unsafe-food offence remains intact. Its outcome-based penalty ladder is not amended by this schedule. An investigation may examine all applicable law, but no person may be punished twice for the same offence. Charging and sentencing must identify the distinct statutory elements, act, mental state, harm, and remedy relied on; any overlap must be addressed by the competent court according to constitutional and criminal-law principles.

Food-safety inspection, sampling, analyst reports, referral testing and prosecution decisions remain governed by the FSS Act, including sections 41 to 47 and 59, and applicable BNSS procedure. The existing FSS Act provides four-part sampling, an accredited-laboratory request, referral testing on a material variance, analyst reporting, and a prosecution route. This Bill does not dilute those safeguards.

## Constitutional and legislative route

This is a proposed ordinary Union criminal-law amendment. Parliament’s criminal-law and criminal-procedure competence is principally engaged under Concurrent List Entry 1, subject to Articles 14, 20(1) and 21 and any applicable State-law interaction under Article 254. It should apply prospectively only, after publication and commencement.

## Reasoned design choice

The two-year statutory minimum meets the requested increase while retaining a five-year maximum and a required fine. Retaining the existing mens rea and noxiousness threshold is essential: a minimum sentence should not be imposed for an innocent error, unproven contamination, or a mere technical non-compliance. The separate Food Safety and Standards Act penalty framework and any offence involving injury or death require an independently verified, fact-specific charging assessment; this narrow amendment does not merge or override them.

## Formal-readiness limits

This schedule is an exact textual redline against accepted official BNS and BNSS snapshots, but it is **not ready for parliamentary introduction**. Before a filing package can be labelled ready, the matter must record an immediately current official-text recheck, current official parliamentary-format guidance, legislative-secretariat review, and the applicable formal memoranda. The remaining larger-food-Bill source, competence, State/UT and fiscal gates are separately recorded and do not prevent this narrow schedule from being used as a standalone amendment draft.
