# Model State/UT Food, Feed and Local Integrity Act

**Model draft; not enacted.** This instrument is for State/UT adaptation. It does not purport to amend the FSS Act, BNS, BNSS, DPDP Act, Consumer Protection Act, Legal Metrology Act or Environment (Protection) Act.

## 1. Purpose and scope

This Act provides for locally lawful food-feed hygiene, animal-feed traceability, local-market sanitation, complaint handling, waste collection, public warning, assistance and review, consistently with Union law and constitutional rights.

## 2. Duties of regulated persons

(1) A person who manufactures, stores, transports, sells, serves, delivers or controls animal feed or a prescribed local food/feed activity shall maintain reasonable source, batch, storage, expiry, recipient, recall and disposal records.

(2) No person shall knowingly sell, distribute or conceal unsafe, adulterated, expired, falsely labelled or materially substituted feed or animal product.

(3) A regulated person shall maintain proportionate hygiene, pest-control, water, temperature, segregation, worker-safety and waste-management measures, with accessible compliance support for small, traditional, informal and community operators.

## 3. Institutional separation

(1) The State shall keep licensing, inspection, sampling/laboratory analysis, investigation, prosecution recommendation, prosecution decision, adjudication and appeal functionally separate.

(2) A local authority may provide assistance, receive complaints and implement published sanitation/waste services, but may not exercise criminal-prosecution or final-adjudication power unless separately authorised by law.

## 4. Inspection, sampling and emergency action

(1) An inspection requires recorded objective grounds or a published risk-based allocation method. An officer shall identify themselves, give a receipt for anything taken, preserve relevant evidence and provide a written record.

(2) Sampling must use documented chain of custody, independent accredited testing where applicable, split-sample or equivalent challenge opportunity, material evidence disclosure and a prompt review route.

(3) An urgent hold, warning or closure is permitted only where documented evidence shows an immediate material safety risk; it must be narrowly tailored, reasoned, promptly independently reviewed and subject to court remedy.

## 5. Fair enforcement and anti-vendetta rule

(1) No person may be targeted for a licence action, inspection, closure, penalty, warning, funding denial or public stigma because of lawful political belief, criticism, journalism, association, protected characteristic, market rivalry, whistleblowing or complaint.

(2) Credible evidence of wrongdoing shall nevertheless be investigated under the same neutral criteria, evidentiary threshold, case-allocation method, procedure and remedies applicable to all persons.

(3) Knowingly fabricating evidence, demanding an improper benefit, suppressing a material safety report or using official power for retaliation is serious official misconduct and shall be referred under applicable criminal, disciplinary and anti-corruption law.

## 6. Consumer, worker and vendor remedy

(1) A consumer, worker, vendor, animal keeper, journalist, whistleblower or public-interest organisation may file an accessible complaint without fee.

(2) The authority shall preserve relevant records, give an acknowledgement, issue a reasoned outcome within the prescribed period, permit independent review and retain court access.

(3) A knowingly false complaint intended to obtain an improper benefit or cause harm is subject to applicable law; a good-faith complaint, uncertainty or adverse outcome does not by itself establish falsity.

## 7. Public accountability

(1) The State shall publish quarterly, privacy-protective statistics on inspections, samples, laboratory turnaround, warnings, recalls, closures, penalties, appeals, reversals, complaints of official misconduct, retaliation findings, compensation and disparities.

(2) An independent annual audit shall test discrimination, selective enforcement, laboratory integrity, delays, conflicts, extortion risk, burden on small operators and effectiveness of support.

## 8. Rule-making, review and non-derogation

(1) Rules must be published in draft, accessible form and relevant languages; material rules require genuine consultation, reasons and legislative laying where applicable.

(2) No direction, contract, algorithm, funding condition or local by-law may achieve indirectly what this Act or the Constitution prohibits directly.

(3) This Act shall be reviewed at least every five years through a published independent report and public consultation.
