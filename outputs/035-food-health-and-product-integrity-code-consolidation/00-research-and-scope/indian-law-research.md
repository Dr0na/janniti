## 2. Executive finding and reliance limits

**Current law / verified source route.** The Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006 (FSS Act) is the central food-safety framework for food for human consumption and provides FSSAI’s science-based standard-setting and food-chain regulatory structure. The Consumer Protection Act, 2019 provides a consumer-protection architecture, including a central authority and consumer-dispute route. Legal Metrology, data-protection, environmental/plastic-waste and criminal-procedure law provide additional but separate controls.

**Legal interpretation.** A whole-of-food-chain package can be proposed as a Union-led framework only after competence is divided between a Central amendment/new law, State adoption/model law, local by-laws and subordinate regulations. Food/feed, public health, animal health, agriculture, local trade, waste, policing, taxes/levies, labour, data and criminal procedure do not all sit in one uncomplicated legislative field.

**Draft-text limitation.** The working text is a policy draft, not current law, legal advice, introduced legislation or an introduction-ready Bill. It contains provisions that require exact current-text comparison, fiscal design, State consultation and official rule/Gazette verification before formal introduction.

## 9. Rights, equality, federalism, and public-interest assessment

The proposed Bill has legitimate public-health, consumer-protection, animal-welfare, nutrition, environmental and accountability objectives. Its most significant constitutional risks are: excessive criminalisation; delegated unbounded standard-setting; privacy impacts of live streaming and delivery tracking; discriminatory enforcement against informal/small/traditional vendors; occupation/business restrictions; arbitrary closure/search/seizure; and central displacement of State/local functions. The working draft addresses these as policy safeguards, but a formal Bill must translate them into precise definitions, authorised decision-makers, reasons, appeal, court remedy, rulemaking limits, funding and commencement.
