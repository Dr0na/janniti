# Public Consultation Pack — Food, Feed and Delivery Integrity Package

## Purpose

Enable informed, accessible and non-captured public participation before any instrument is introduced. Consultation does not replace constitutional rights, expert evidence, legislative scrutiny or judicial review.

## Materials to publish

1. Plain-language summaries of each instrument, in English, Hindi and relevant State/UT languages.
2. A one-page rights-and-safeguards sheet: no arbitrary arrest, no political retaliation, fair sampling, privacy, appeal and court access.
3. A small-vendor guide explaining records, hygiene, expiry, recall, plastic transition, support and complaint rights.
4. A worker/consumer guide explaining kitchen-view privacy, reporting, refunds, retaliation protection and accessible remedies.
5. The complete draft package, source register, overlap audit, evidence limitations and fiscal-input template.
6. A response form that accepts online, paper, telephone-assisted, sign-language/video and community-facilitated submissions.

## Consultation safeguards

(1) Provide not less than sixty days for written responses, with reasonable extension where accessible translations or major evidence become available late.

(2) Publish all non-confidential submissions promptly, with consent/redaction options for personal data, whistleblowers and safety-sensitive information.

(3) Disclose all meetings, written representations, funding, lobbying, conflicts and technical advice received by the public authority.

(4) Do not treat volume of identical submissions as proof of merit. Analyse evidence, affected groups, disability/language access, small-operator impact and minority views.

(5) Publish a reasoned consultation report identifying every material proposal accepted, modified or rejected, the evidence relied on, dissenting views, uncertainty and the next review date.

## Required participation groups

Consumers; workers; small vendors; street-food and informal operators; farmers; animal keepers; feed businesses; restaurants; delivery workers; laboratories; public-health and veterinary experts; disability and language-access organisations; women’s, caste-equity, tribal and minority organisations; environmental groups; journalists; consumer bodies; State/UT and local authorities; and affected industry associations.
