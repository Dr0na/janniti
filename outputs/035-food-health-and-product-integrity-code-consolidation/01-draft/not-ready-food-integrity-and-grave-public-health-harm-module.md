# Not-Ready Food Integrity and Grave Public-Health Harm Module

## Core design

Food safety, medicine integrity and consumer-product safety remain distinct regulatory fields. This module creates one severe public-health accountability pathway for deliberate large-scale adulteration without treating every regulatory breach as murder.

### Scope and universal application

(1) In this Module, “food and feed” includes every substance, product, ingredient, additive, supplement, treat, drink, water, preparation or food-contact item intended, represented or reasonably likely to be consumed by a human or non-human animal, including pets, companion animals, livestock, poultry, birds, fish, aquatic animals, reptiles, amphibians, working animals, zoo animals and other animals. This Module applies to every such food and feed item placed, offered, prepared, handled, stored, processed, packaged, labelled, transported, imported, exported, distributed, displayed, advertised, sold, supplied, donated for consumption, delivered, tested, recalled, returned or disposed of in India, whether raw, cooked, processed, packaged, unpackaged, fresh, frozen, dry, liquid, ready-to-eat, made to order, physical, digital, online, offline, domestic, imported, institutional or supplied through an intermediary.

(2) It applies to every person or entity exercising actual or represented control at any point in that human-food or animal-feed chain, including a farmer, feed producer, hatchery, aquaculture operator or primary producer where the applicable duty concerns a product placed on the market; manufacturer; importer; exporter; brand owner; seller; shop; street vendor; hawker; restaurant; hotel; dhaba; caterer; canteen; cloud kitchen; home-based commercial food business; pet-food or livestock-feed business; veterinary or animal-care supplier; market; wholesaler; warehouse; cold store; transporter; delivery worker; delivery platform; marketplace; franchisor; supplier; laboratory; certifier; advertiser; influencer; public authority; public feeding, animal-care or institutional facility; contractor; waste/disposal operator; and any parent, affiliate, nominee or intermediary proved to control, direct, benefit from or knowingly facilitate the relevant conduct.

(3) No person or entity is excluded because it is small, informal, unregistered, rural, mobile, temporary, charitable, cooperative, online-only, cash-based, home-based, publicly operated, privately operated, platform-mediated or otherwise difficult to classify. Scale, species, risk, actual control, capacity and access to public infrastructure may inform proportionate compliance assistance, transition, measurement and sanction, but never the baseline duties of safety, honesty, traceability, non-discrimination, non-retaliation, evidence preservation and public accountability.

(4) This Module does not regulate food produced and consumed solely within a private household without being placed or offered in a market, institution, platform, public event or supply chain, except where another applicable law lawfully provides otherwise. The exception cannot be used to disguise commercial, repeated, platform-mediated, catered, donated or public supply.

(5) A person may not evade this Module by describing food or feed as a service, gift, sample, subscription, wellness product, animal supplement, pet treat, charitable aid, intermediary sale, peer-to-peer transaction, home preparation, marketplace listing, promotional item, trial, export-only item, contract manufacture or another label. Substance, actual control, benefit, public representation and risk determine scope.

### 1. Grave public-health harm offence

A person commits the offence only if the prosecution proves beyond reasonable doubt that the person knowingly or recklessly introduced, distributed, directed, concealed or failed to stop adulterated food; knew or consciously disregarded a substantial and unjustifiable risk of death or serious irreversible harm to a large or indeterminate group; and did so without lawful justification.

### 2. Consequences

(1) Where death results and the required mental element and causation are proved, prosecution shall proceed under the applicable homicide and public-health criminal law.

(2) Where the conduct creates a grave, widespread risk but death is not proved, it shall attract a severe custodial penalty, proportionate fine, disqualification, confiscation/recovery only by court order, victim compensation and mandatory recall/remediation.

(3) Negligent, technical or isolated non-compliance shall remain separately classified and proportionately sanctioned; it must not be escalated to the grave offence without the required proof.

### 3. Independent evidence and fair process

Sampling, laboratory testing, chain of custody, epidemiological evidence, expert evidence, disclosure, defence testing, notice, hearing, reasons, appeal and court adjudication are mandatory. No reverse criminal burden, automatic guilt, secret standard or retrospective offence is permitted.

### 4. Anti-capture architecture

Regulator intake, scientific testing, investigation, prosecution, adjudication, recall and audit must be separated. Appointments are plural and public; laboratory allocation is random or pre-set and conflict-screened; every recall, enforcement decision, exception and laboratory result is logged and independently auditable.

### 5. Public democratic backstop

Publish de-identified safety, recall, sampling, enforcement, delay and conflict data. Provide protected whistleblowing, consumer standing, legal aid, citizen petition for a public hearing/audit, multilingual accessible complaints and court preservation orders. Public participation cannot decide individual guilt or override due process, equality or courts.

### 6. Emergency and anti-evasion safeguards

No emergency, secrecy label, outsourcing, algorithm or funding condition may suspend recall, evidence preservation, independent testing, notice to affected people or court review. Every exception expires, is publicly recorded and is independently reviewable.

### 7. Ingredient prohibition and full disclosure

(1) No food may contain an artificial colour, artificial flavour, synthetic preservative or other ingredient prohibited by the applicable food-safety standard.

(2) The Code’s policy objective is that food sold for ordinary consumption contain no artificial colour, artificial flavour or synthetic preservative unless an independent scientific assessment, published in full, establishes a specified necessary use, a safe exposure level, no reasonably available safer alternative and a time-limited approval. A commercial claim of safety is not evidence.

(3) Every package, menu, online listing and delivery-platform listing shall disclose every ingredient, additive, processing aid, allergen, country of origin where prescribed, batch, manufacture date, laboratory certificate and recall status in an accessible form. Hidden, substituted or misleading ingredients are prohibited.

### 8. Evidence-based advertising and labelling

(1) A food, nutrition, health, safety, environmental, origin or performance claim may be made only if it is specific, independently verifiable, supported by reproducible evidence appropriate to the claim, and available in a public evidence register before publication.

(2) The evidence register shall disclose protocol, data, analysis plan, authorship, funder, sponsor, conflicts, adverse findings, limitations, independent replication status and all material contrary evidence.

(3) Paid, sponsored, manipulated, selectively reported, ghost-written or commercially influenced research shall never be represented as independent research. Advertising shall not conceal sponsorship, exaggerate findings, use unverifiable testimonials, target children with health claims, or exploit illness, fear or low literacy.

### 9. Independent evidence authority

(1) An Independent Food Evidence Register shall be operated separately from food licensing, inspection, prosecution and advertising enforcement. Its methods, appointment process, data rules and decisions shall be public and independently audited.

(2) Evidence assessment shall use pre-registered protocols, conflict screening, random or pre-set laboratory allocation, replication where proportionate, publication of adverse findings and a right of scientific response. No sponsor, regulator, advertiser or political office-holder may direct an assessment of a particular product or sponsor.

### 10. Research manipulation and commercial exclusion

(1) On credible evidence of research fabrication, falsification, concealment of material results, undisclosed sponsor control, manipulation of sampling/analysis, or deceptive use of research for commercial benefit, the competent authority shall immediately preserve records and may impose a narrowly tailored interim sales/advertising suspension for not more than thirty days, renewable once by a court after notice and an urgent hearing.

(2) A final finding requires an independent investigation, full disclosure of evidence, opportunity to test and answer it, a reasoned decision, public explanation and appeal to a court.

(3) Where a court finds deliberate research manipulation for commercial benefit that materially exposed people to a grave health risk or caused widespread deception, the court may prohibit the sponsor, responsible controlling persons and a subsidiary or affiliate proved to have knowingly participated from placing food products on the Indian market for up to twenty years. The order shall be individualised, proportionate, periodically reviewable for error, and shall not punish an innocent affiliate merely because of common ownership.

(4) The court may also order recall, corrective advertising, compensation, disgorgement of unlawful gain, publication of the full record, disqualification of responsible persons and referral under applicable criminal law.

### 11. Accountability and public disclosure

Publish, in searchable machine-readable form, every licence, ingredient approval, inspection, sample, laboratory result, conflict declaration, advertising evidence file, adverse finding, recall, enforcement referral, interim suspension, final order, appeal outcome, delay, exception and corrective action. Personal health data, protected whistleblower identity and material fair-trial information shall be minimised and redacted only with written reasons, expiry and independent review.

### 12. Advertising ethics and integrity code

(1) Every food advertisement shall be truthful, evidence-based, plainly understandable, socially responsible and respectful of dignity, equality, children and public health. It shall identify the advertiser, beneficial owner, sponsor, paid endorser and evidence-register entry.

(2) It shall not use false comparison, omission of material risk, fake review, fabricated scarcity, dark pattern, disguised advertorial, surrogate advertisement, undisclosed influencer promotion, targeting based on health vulnerability, deceptive child-directed design, manipulated image or video, fabricated expert endorsement, false origin claim, greenwashing, health-washing, fear appeal or intimidation.

(3) A platform, broadcaster, publisher, influencer, agency, affiliate, marketplace or delivery service that receives consideration or material benefit for an advertisement is a responsible publisher. It must label the advertisement, retain a public sponsor/evidence record, stop a plainly unlawful campaign on a reasoned notice, preserve records and provide an accessible complaint route. It is not liable merely for ordinary neutral transmission absent knowledge, control or material benefit.

(4) The same duties apply regardless of medium, language, cross-border location, algorithmic targeting, virtual character, affiliate link, discount code, data broker, packaging, point of sale, product placement, corporate social-responsibility campaign or campaign routed through a related entity.

### 13. Serious food adulteration and accountable enforcement

(1) Deliberate adulteration that causes death, serious injury, irreversible impairment, poisoning, widespread exposure to a toxic or prohibited substance, or a substantial and unjustifiable risk of such harm is a serious public-health crime.

(2) The offence requires proof beyond reasonable doubt of the prohibited conduct, responsible person’s knowledge or recklessness, the nature and scale of exposure, and causation or grave risk. It is not established merely by a technical non-compliance, trace contamination, lab error, good-faith recall, or a regulatory disagreement.

(3) On credible evidence, the authority shall preserve samples and records; assign independent conflict-screened laboratories and investigators; notify affected people and the court where grave risk exists; and refer the matter to the competent prosecution authority. No regulator, minister, platform, laboratory or sponsor may direct an individual charging decision.

(4) Final criminal liability, sentence, confiscation and market exclusion are determined only by a competent court after notice, disclosure, defence testing, expert challenge, reasoned judgment and appeal. Administrative recall, urgent warnings and temporary suspension must be necessary, proportionate, time-limited and independently reviewable.

### 14. Anti-loophole and integrity audit

(1) A person shall not evade this Module by changing product name, packaging, corporate form, advertiser, jurisdiction, ingredient supplier, platform, marketing technique, research contractor, laboratory, payment channel or contractual label.

(2) Every responsible actor shall maintain tamper-evident records of formulation, source, batch, testing, advertising creation, targeting, sponsorship, research funding, adverse-event signal and recall decision. An independent annual integrity audit shall test evasion, algorithmic targeting, ownership chains, conflicts, sampling integrity, laboratory performance and enforcement equality.

(3) The audit shall publish its methodology, aggregate findings, uncertainty, corrective actions and unresolved risks. It cannot guarantee that every future manipulation will be detected; its purpose is early detection, contestability and rapid repair.

### 15. Traceability, sampling and alerts

(1) Every packaged food shall carry an accessible QR or equivalent offline-readable traceability identifier stating responsible entity, ingredient source, batch, manufacture date, applicable laboratory certificate and current recall status. Lack of digital access shall not prevent access to the same information by telephone, label or public register.

(2) The authority shall conduct random, unannounced and independently auditable sampling of factories, warehouses, transport, restaurants, delivery kitchens, retail outlets and online marketplaces. Sampling allocation, chain of custody, laboratory allocation and results shall be public subject to lawful privacy and fair-trial limits.

(3) A credible grave risk requires a multilingual public “do not consume” alert through accessible web, SMS, radio, television and local channels, with product/batch identification, health advice, recall process and correction route.

### 16. Adverse-event surveillance and scientific review

(1) Hospitals, registered health professionals, laboratories, food businesses and consumers may report suspected food-related injury, poisoning or adverse event through a protected reporting system. The system shall use independent epidemiological analysis, publish de-identified signals and protect patient confidentiality.

(2) An independent annual scientific review shall reassess permitted additives, cumulative exposure, vulnerable-group impacts, adverse-event signals, safer alternatives and uncertainty. It shall give particular attention to children, pregnant people, older people and people with allergies, disability or chronic illness.

### 17. Responsible-person liability and disqualification

(1) A controlling officer, director, partner, manager or person exercising actual control is personally accountable where the court finds that person knowingly approved, directed, concealed or recklessly ignored a grave adulteration risk.

(2) Following a final court finding of deliberate adulteration causing death or grave widespread harm, the court may impose lifetime disqualification from controlling, managing, licensing or materially benefiting from a food business, after considering proportionality, individual responsibility and appeal. An innocent shareholder, employee or affiliate is not disqualified merely by association.

### 18. Consumer repair, whistleblowing and non-waiver

(1) An independently administered consumer repair fund, financed by risk-based industry levies and recoveries, shall provide urgent medical, nutrition, legal-aid and compensation support. Manufacturers, sponsors and trade bodies shall not control individual awards.

(2) A good-faith whistleblower may receive a proportionate reward, confidential reporting, identity protection, legal aid, employment protection and, where independently assessed as necessary, emergency relocation or safety support.

(3) No private settlement, confidentiality agreement, contractual term or arbitration clause may prevent a recall, public warning, evidence preservation, regulatory disclosure, protected report, criminal referral or a person’s access to a competent court.

### 19. Hotels, restaurants, vendors and delivery kitchens

(1) This Module applies to hotels, restaurants, caterers, canteens, street-food vendors, food stalls, temporary-event vendors, cloud kitchens, delivery kitchens, marketplaces and delivery platforms that prepare, store, transport, market or serve food.

(2) Each food business shall comply with published hygiene requirements covering potable water, handwashing, sanitation, pest control, waste handling, temperature control, cross-contamination prevention, allergen controls, staff illness exclusion, protective clothing, cleaning logs, equipment maintenance, safe storage, traceability and immediate reporting of a suspected hazard.

(3) A customer shall be able to view the principal food-preparation area during operating hours. A physical venue shall provide a safe, unobstructed public viewing window or equivalent direct view. A cloud/delivery kitchen shall provide a continuously available, current live visual feed or an equally effective independently verifiable viewing system on its ordering page and at a public register, subject only to narrowly tailored protection for private changing/toilet areas, security-sensitive credentials and confidential personal data.

(4) A venue shall display its current hygiene grade, most recent inspection date, material violations, recall/closure status, complaint route and traceability identifier at the entrance, menu, ordering page and delivery-platform listing.

(5) A food business, its controlling person, platform, franchisor or contractor shall not represent a recorded, edited, delayed, substituted, selectively framed, artificially generated or otherwise manipulated feed as a current live view of the principal food-preparation area. It shall not disable, obscure, interrupt or redirect the required view to conceal a hygiene breach. System-time, camera-identity, continuity and access logs shall be tamper-evident and retained for independent audit.

(6) On credible evidence of conduct under sub-section (5), the competent authority may order a narrowly tailored interim suspension of the affected ordering or food-service activity for not more than thirty days, with written reasons, preservation of evidence, an urgent independent review and access to a court. After an independent investigation, disclosure of the evidence, a meaningful opportunity to answer it, a reasoned decision and appeal, a competent court or independent statutory tribunal may suspend the food-business licence for ten years where it finds deliberate manipulation intended to conceal a material hygiene breach, contamination, unsafe practice or enforcement violation. A technical outage, promptly disclosed and independently verified, is not manipulation.

(7) A final order under sub-section (6) shall identify the responsible natural persons and entities, the manipulation, the concealed risk, the evidence, the reasons for the duration and the route of appeal. It may also require public correction, recall, audit, remediation and referral under applicable law. A person who did not control, direct, know of or recklessly disregard the manipulation shall not be penalised merely because of employment, shareholding or common ownership.

### 20. Hygiene inspection and enforcement

(1) Inspections shall use published, risk-based and randomised allocation, conflict screening, photographic/record evidence, chain of custody and a public reasoned report. Inspectors shall not solicit or accept an improper benefit and their visits, findings and follow-up shall be independently auditable.

(2) An imminent serious health risk permits a proportionate immediate stop-sale, closure or recall order, with written reasons, evidence preservation, notice, an urgent independent review and court access. A non-imminent breach requires notice, a reasonable correction period and review, unless deliberate concealment, repeated violation or grave risk justifies stronger action.

(3) Repeated or deliberate serious hygiene violations, falsified hygiene records, obstruction of inspection or concealment of contamination require referral for disciplinary, licensing and criminal action under applicable law, subject to independent investigation and fair process.

(4) No authority, hotel chain, platform, franchisor, landlord or local body may suppress an inspection, alter a grade, conceal a report or retaliate against a complainant, worker, inspector or whistleblower. Such conduct shall be logged, publicly reported and referred for lawful action.

### 21. Mandatory minimum hygiene standard and monitoring duty

(1) No food business may operate unless it continuously meets the minimum hygiene standard prescribed under this Module. The standard shall include safe water, sanitation, waste removal, pest control, safe temperatures, separation of raw and ready-to-eat food, allergen control, staff health/hygiene, cleaning, traceability, equipment safety and honest records. A rule may strengthen, but not weaken, these minimum duties.

(2) Every food business shall maintain daily tamper-evident hygiene logs, temperature records, cleaning records, supplier/batch records, staff illness exclusions and corrective-action records. Records shall be retained for the prescribed period, made available to an inspector and independently auditable.

(3) Monitoring shall combine risk-based scheduled inspection, random unannounced inspection, protected worker/consumer reporting, adverse-event signals, laboratory results, platform data and independently audited hygiene records. No food business may select its own inspector, laboratory or audit outcome.

### 22. Consequences for violation and enforcement failure

(1) A material hygiene breach requires written correction direction, reinspection and public status update. Repeated, deliberate, concealed or grave breaches require proportionate suspension, closure, recall, licence cancellation, compensation/recovery and referral for criminal action under applicable law.

(2) An inspector, licensing officer, laboratory officer, platform officer or public official who knowingly or recklessly ignores a grave hygiene risk, suppresses a complaint or sample, falsifies a record, accepts an improper benefit, alters an inspection outcome or obstructs lawful enforcement shall face independent investigation, disciplinary consequence and criminal referral under applicable law.

(3) No enforcement decision may be influenced by political office, ownership, wealth, party affiliation, criticism, media coverage, personal relationship or improper benefit. Every material enforcement decision, non-enforcement decision, exception and delay shall state reasons, evidence, responsible officer, review route and expiry where applicable.

(4) A food business, official, platform, contractor or related entity may not evade this Chapter by restructuring, subcontracting, franchising, relabelling, shifting jurisdiction, manipulating records or using an intermediary. Liability is determined by actual control, knowledge, benefit and conduct, with notice, evidence, hearing and appeal.

(5) These measures are designed to close foreseeable evasion routes; they do not displace constitutional remedies, independent courts, public reporting or periodic review needed to detect new forms of manipulation.

### 23. Live-view system integrity and retained evidence

(1) A required live-view system shall use authenticated time, camera identity, continuity records, tamper alerts and secure retention designed to permit independent verification that the view is current and relates to the stated preparation area. The technical standard shall be published, interoperable, privacy-protective and not controlled by a food business, dominant platform or political office-holder.

(2) The competent authority shall conduct random remote integrity checks and may conduct proportionate on-site verification. It shall retain the minimum footage and system metadata necessary for a complaint, inspection, outbreak investigation, appeal or audit; access shall be logged and restricted to protect workers, customers and security.

(3) No person may use the live-view requirement to surveil workers beyond food safety, identify customers, expose personal information or suppress lawful worker association. A worker, consumer or business may seek prompt independent correction of an inaccurate system-integrity finding.

### 24. Outbreak, suspected-harm and recall protocol

(1) A food business, hospital, laboratory, platform or public authority that has credible information of a suspected foodborne outbreak, contamination event or serious food-related injury shall promptly preserve samples and relevant records and report it through the protected reporting system.

(2) The competent authority shall promptly assess the evidence using independent epidemiological and laboratory expertise; where a credible material risk exists, it shall issue an accessible public warning, identify the product, venue, batch or exposure pathway to the extent safely known, give practical health advice, provide a recall route and update the public record as evidence changes.

(3) A person affected by a credible serious event shall have access to urgent medical information, referral, and the consumer-repair support in section 18. No person may be denied assistance because of income, migration status, language, disability, identity, residence, occupation or inability to produce a purchase record.

(4) A warning, recall, closure or disclosure shall be evidence-based, proportionate, time-limited, reasoned, independently reviewable and corrected promptly if materially inaccurate. It shall never be withheld or delayed for political, commercial, reputational or improper reasons.

### 25. Public enforcement and inspector-accountability dashboard

(1) The statutory regulator shall publish a searchable, downloadable and accessible dashboard showing, at minimum, inspections due and completed, inspection allocation method, reported conflicts, samples collected, laboratory turnaround, material findings, corrective directions, recalls, closures, appeals, reversals, enforcement delays, complaints, whistleblower-protection outcomes and substantiated disciplinary outcomes.

(2) Data shall be published by location, risk category and time period without exposing protected personal data, confidential whistleblower identity, legitimate trade secret or material fair-trial information. Any withholding shall state a lawful basis, named decision-maker, scope, expiry and independent review route.

(3) The dashboard shall publish equality and comparator indicators sufficient to identify apparent selective enforcement or unequal service. An independent auditor shall investigate statistically material anomalies, publish its methodology and recommendations, and allow affected persons to respond.

### 26. End-to-end supply-chain traceability

(1) A food business shall maintain traceability from ingredient, water, ice, additive, packaging and supplier through storage, preparation, transport, delivery and sale, including batch, source, temperature-control and responsible-entity records.

(2) Delivery platforms, warehouses, transporters, franchisors and marketplaces shall preserve the records they control and promptly provide them to a lawful inspection, recall, independent audit, affected person or court process, subject to data minimisation and protection of personal data.

(3) A customer without a smartphone or digital access shall receive equivalent traceability information through a label, telephone, SMS, venue notice or other accessible non-digital means. A system failure does not excuse preservation of paper or other durable backup records.

### 27. Food-worker safety, reporting and non-retaliation

(1) Every food business shall provide workers with paid sick leave sufficient to prevent food handling while the worker has a condition that presents a food-safety risk, without loss of job, wages, immigration status, licence or lawful benefit. The business shall arrange safe reporting, hygiene training and non-discriminatory reassignment where feasible.

(2) A worker, contractor or former worker may confidentially report a hygiene, contamination, retaliation, corruption or streaming-integrity concern. Retaliation includes dismissal, wage loss, transfer, threat, harassment, blacklist, adverse immigration action, loss of housing or compelled confidentiality.

(3) A retaliation complaint shall receive rapid independent interim protection, a reasoned decision and effective remedy, including reinstatement, compensation, penalty and referral where appropriate. Knowingly false reports made to cause harm remain subject to ordinary lawful process; a report made in good faith is protected even if not substantiated.

### 28. Accessible public complaint, petition and review route

(1) Every person may make a food-safety complaint without charge through local-language telephone, SMS, online, paper, in-person and disability-accessible routes. Anonymous reporting shall be accepted where it contains enough information to assess risk.

(2) The authority shall acknowledge a complaint, state its triage category and give a reasoned status update within published time limits. It shall provide a prompt independent review route for inaction, closure, retaliation or unreasonable delay; an affected person, public-interest organisation or community group has standing to seek that review.

(3) No complainant may be required to disclose party affiliation, political belief, caste, religion, identity, migration status or ability to pay as a condition of complaint, protection, inspection or remedy.

### 29. Anti-corruption, allocation and non-enforcement controls

(1) Inspector, laboratory, auditor and case allocation shall be random or determined by a published pre-set system, subject to conflict screening and recorded recusal. Rotation, cooling-off, improper-benefit declarations and independent asset/conflict review shall be used for roles prescribed as high risk.

(2) A closure, licence suspension, downgrade, non-enforcement decision, exception, delayed action or reopened venue shall receive random independent audit. The audit shall test evidence, comparable-case treatment, political or commercial contact, conflict, delay and compliance with reasons and review requirements.

(3) An oral, informal or undisclosed direction about a specific business, complaint, inspection, sample, laboratory result, recall, enforcement action or non-enforcement action is invalid. The recipient shall record and report it through a protected channel; compliance with an unlawful direction is not a defence.

### 30. Independent market sampling and review

(1) The competent authority shall conduct recurring random, unannounced, statistically defensible sampling of high-risk and ordinary food businesses, including physical venues, cloud kitchens, delivery services, transport, water, ice, packaging and ingredients. Sampling plans shall not target a person because of lawful political activity, criticism, media work, association or protected characteristic.

(2) A material adverse result requires prompt confirmatory testing by an independently allocated, conflict-screened laboratory; preservation and disclosure of the original sample; a reasonable opportunity for defence testing; and a reasoned public outcome. An urgent warning may issue before confirmation only where necessary to prevent a credible serious risk and subject to section 24.

(3) The sampling programme, laboratory performance, false-positive/false-negative indicators, turnaround time, geographic and business-category coverage, enforcement outcomes and corrective action shall be independently audited and published at least annually.

### 31. Review, resourcing and non-derogation

(1) No duty in sections 19 to 30 may be reduced by contract, waiver, platform term, franchise arrangement, informal instruction, secrecy label, funding condition, emergency label, algorithmic system or outsourcing arrangement.

(2) Before commencement, the appropriate government shall publish the staffing, laboratory capacity, legal-aid, health-support, multilingual-access, data-security and independent-audit plan, together with a transparent budget and implementation timetable. Lack of resources does not justify concealing a known serious risk.

(3) An independent public review shall occur two years after commencement and every three years thereafter. It shall assess health outcomes, equality, worker safety, false enforcement, delays, corruption, public accessibility, privacy and new forms of evasion; it shall publish proposed corrections and a reasoned response to public submissions.

### 32. Safeguards against misuse of hygiene and food-safety powers

(1) An inspection, sample, demand for records, live-view integrity check, warning, recall, closure, licence action, disclosure, audit or referral under this Module may occur only under published neutral criteria and on recorded objective grounds. It shall not be initiated, accelerated, delayed, intensified or withheld because of a person’s lawful political belief, party association, criticism, journalism, whistleblowing, union activity, caste, religion, gender, disability, language, economic status, identity or association.

(2) Every coercive or materially adverse action shall be necessary, the least restrictive reasonably effective measure, proportionate to the evidenced risk, limited in duration and scope, and supported by written reasons identifying the evidence, decision-maker, legal authority, review date and challenge route. No person shall be punished for refusing an unlawful demand or for seeking review.

(3) Except where an imminent serious health risk makes prior notice impracticable, an affected person shall receive notice, relevant non-sensitive evidence, a reasonable opportunity to respond and a reasoned decision. An urgent action without prior notice shall receive an independent review within forty-eight hours and an accessible court or tribunal hearing on an expedited basis.

(4) Inspectors and authorities shall collect only data necessary for a stated food-safety purpose. Access to footage, records, consumer data and worker data shall be role-limited, logged, encrypted where appropriate, independently auditable and deleted at the end of the published retention period unless needed for a lawful proceeding. It shall not be used for immigration enforcement, political surveillance, unrelated policing, commercial profiling or retaliation.

(5) No person who investigates an allegation may finally determine that person’s own alleged misconduct, approve that person’s own exception, or decide a complaint in which that person has a material conflict. An independent conflict-screened reviewer shall decide complaints of selective enforcement, corruption, retaliation, evidence suppression or misuse.

(6) A person materially harmed by an unlawful, discriminatory, retaliatory or negligent exercise of power has a prompt right to correction, reopening, restoration of licence or grade, removal or correction of a false public record, compensation for provable loss and a court or tribunal remedy. This does not prevent lawful action on genuinely independent evidence.

(7) Knowingly making a false material record, concealing exculpatory evidence, directing selective enforcement, retaliating against a complainant, accepting an improper benefit, or using data or a live-view system for a prohibited purpose shall result in independent investigation and proportionate disciplinary, civil or criminal referral under applicable law, with the same fair-process safeguards for the accused.

(8) A public annual misuse audit shall publish anonymised information on complaints, selective-enforcement indicators, urgent actions, reversals, compensation, data-access violations, official discipline and unresolved systemic risks. The audit shall be conducted by an entity institutionally separate from licensing, inspection, prosecution and political direction.

### 33. Independent certification and conflict disclosure

(1) A live-view system, food-safety laboratory, hygiene-audit system or high-risk digital system used for statutory compliance shall be certified against published, open, interoperable and privacy-protective standards by an assessor that is independent of the relevant food business, platform, regulator and political office-holder.

(2) An assessor shall disclose ownership, beneficial interest, funding, prior work, conflicts, methodology, material failures and disciplinary history. Certification, suspension and withdrawal decisions shall be reasoned, published and independently appealable.

(3) A certificate does not transfer legal responsibility from a food business or public authority, create an immunity, or prevent independent testing, audit or judicial review.

### 34. Evidence custody and integrity

(1) Samples, footage, sensor records, laboratory data, enforcement decisions, notices, directions, audit records and material communications shall receive a unique identifier, authenticated timestamp, custody record, alteration history and access log. The integrity system shall support independent verification and preserve the original evidence.

(2) A person shall not alter, destroy, substitute, backdate, selectively withhold or falsely attribute material evidence. A necessary correction shall preserve the original, identify the person and reason, and be independently auditable.

(3) A party affected by material evidence may obtain relevant non-sensitive records and seek preservation, independent testing, disclosure, correction or exclusion through the competent court or tribunal. Technical failure alone does not establish guilt; deliberate tampering or unexplained material custody failure shall be investigated.

### 35. Deadline escalation and transparent non-enforcement review

(1) The appropriate government shall prescribe published risk-based deadlines for triage, sampling, laboratory analysis, warning, recall assessment, inspection, reinspection, complaint decision and enforcement referral.

(2) Missing a serious-risk deadline automatically requires a written explanation, immediate preservation of the record, notice to the complainant or affected person where safely possible, and transfer to an independent conflict-screened reviewer. The reviewer shall decide whether interim protective action, correction of delay, disciplinary inquiry or public disclosure is necessary.

(3) The public dashboard shall display aggregated deadline compliance, delay reasons, transfer outcomes and corrective action. A delay caused by political, commercial, retaliatory or improper influence is a serious misconduct referral.

### 36. Beneficial ownership and control disclosure

(1) Every food business, franchisor, marketplace, delivery platform, laboratory, high-risk supplier and advertising sponsor shall disclose in a public register its legal identity, beneficial owners, controlling persons, material franchise or platform relationship, licence, responsible compliance officer and related entity relevant to food safety.

(2) The register shall use verification, correction, lawful privacy minimisation and a challenge route. It shall not publish a residential address, personal identifier or security-sensitive information unless strictly necessary and independently justified.

(3) Concealing or falsely reporting a material beneficial interest, controlling person or related entity to evade food-safety responsibility is a serious violation, subject to notice, independent investigation, reasons and appeal.

### 37. Final serious-violation register

(1) The regulator shall maintain a searchable, accessible register of final serious food-safety violations, final licence suspensions or cancellations, final court findings, final recalls and the responsible entity or person, with the date, provision, outcome, expiry or review date and appeal result.

(2) No allegation, interim action or unresolved proceeding shall be entered as a final violation. An entry shall be corrected promptly if varied or overturned and shall be removed or anonymised at the expiry of the legally prescribed publication period, unless a court orders otherwise.

(3) A listed person has notice, access to the record, correction and appeal rights. The register shall not become a collateral punishment or bar a person from lawful work beyond a final proportionate order.

### 38. Victim support and collective remedy

(1) A person reasonably affected by a credible serious food-safety event is entitled to accessible information, medical assessment, treatment referral, legal aid, independent testing where appropriate and compensation support without a requirement to first prove fault or produce a purchase receipt.

(2) The consumer-repair fund shall provide interim support without requiring a waiver of legal rights. A final recovery from a responsible person requires applicable lawful process.

(3) Affected persons, consumer organisations and public-interest bodies may bring or support a representative, collective or public-interest action before a competent forum, subject to safeguards against conflict, duplication, frivolous process and settlement that conceals a public risk.

### 39. Independent whistleblower protection and reward fund

(1) A statutory fund, governed independently of licensing, inspection, prosecution and individual food businesses, shall provide confidential reporting support, legal aid, interim income support, safety assistance and, where a protected disclosure materially assists a final recovery or prevention of grave harm, a proportionate reward determined by published criteria.

(2) Fund governance, awards, denials, conflicts, expenditure and outcomes shall be publicly reported in de-identified form and independently audited. No reward may be contingent on a preferred enforcement outcome, political affiliation or suppression of contrary evidence.

(3) A whistleblower retains the right to court remedy and may refuse a reward without losing protection.

### 40. Emergency safeguards

(1) An emergency food-safety order may issue only for a documented imminent serious health risk, shall identify the legal authority, evidence, scope, affected persons, reasons, least restrictive alternative considered, expiry and challenge route, and shall be publicly summarised without compromising an active investigation or protected personal data.

(2) An order without prior hearing expires within seventy-two hours unless an independent statutory reviewer confirms it on reasons after giving the affected person a meaningful urgent opportunity to respond. No order may be serially renewed to evade review; any extension beyond seven days requires a court order.

(3) An emergency may not suspend independent testing, evidence preservation, access to counsel or court, whistleblower protection, anti-discrimination duties, data minimisation or the prohibition on political or commercial direction.

### 41. Platform and marketplace algorithm accountability

(1) A delivery platform, marketplace or material food-advertising intermediary shall document and make available for independent audit the food-safety features of its ranking, recommendation, advertising, complaint triage, seller verification, alert, removal and reinstatement systems.

(2) The audit shall test whether the system suppresses safety complaints, rewards deceptive claims, hides adverse findings, treats comparable sellers unequally, discriminates, enables covert paid influence or impedes a lawful recall. It shall publish methodology, aggregate findings, limitations and corrective action without disclosing trade secrets beyond what is necessary for accountability.

(3) A platform shall not remove, downgrade, delay or amplify a food-safety report, seller or product because of political direction, improper benefit, lawful criticism or a protected characteristic. Material automated decisions require a reason, human review and accessible appeal.

### 42. National floor, State implementation and cooperative accountability

(1) This Module establishes a national minimum floor for food safety, evidence integrity, public warning, worker protection, transparency, review and anti-discrimination. A State, Union territory or local authority may provide stronger compatible protection but shall not authorise a lower level of protection.

(2) Each implementing government shall publish a capacity and coordination plan covering local inspection, laboratory access, outbreak communication, accessibility, State/local complaint routes, data interoperability, referral and judicial remedy. Federal coordination shall not give one Union, State or local actor control over inspection, testing, prosecution, adjudication, funding and audit.

(3) A public intergovernmental report shall identify compliance gaps, resource needs, cross-border outbreaks, interoperability failures and corrective action. It shall not be used to direct an individual enforcement case or weaken an affected person’s local remedy.

### 43. Protection from official harassment, false implication and extortion

(1) A police officer, food-safety official, local authority, inspector, laboratory officer, platform officer or person acting for any of them shall not demand money, goods, services, political support, publicity, employment, data, a payment through an intermediary, or any improper advantage from a food business, owner, worker, supplier or complainant. Nor shall any such person threaten, initiate, procure, delay or intensify an inspection, sample, seizure, closure, arrest referral, prosecution referral, licence action, public disclosure or other burden in order to obtain an improper advantage or punish lawful conduct.

(2) An inspection, search, seizure, sample collection, record demand, closure recommendation, police referral or other coercive step shall have a recorded legal basis, objective facts, named responsible officer, scope, time, evidence-preservation method and review route. It shall be conducted only by an authorised person, with an electronic or written receipt for every item or sample taken. A search, arrest, detention or other coercive criminal measure remains subject to the applicable criminal-law threshold, warrant or judicial-supervision requirement; this Module creates no shortcut around those safeguards.

(3) Except where immediate action is necessary to prevent a documented imminent serious health risk or evidence destruction, the affected person shall receive notice of the purpose and legal authority for the action, an opportunity to identify a representative or counsel, and a contemporaneous inspection record. During a visit, the person may make a non-obstructive recording and request an independent observer where practicable. The authority shall preserve its own contemporaneous audio-visual or written record and make the relevant non-sensitive record available promptly.

(4) An allegation against a food business shall not be publicly presented as proved before a final determination, except for a necessary, proportionate and reasoned public-health warning. The authority shall distinguish allegation, interim measure, laboratory result, final administrative finding and final judicial finding, and shall promptly correct an inaccurate public statement or record.

(5) Every jurisdiction shall maintain a protected, independent and accessible complaint route for official harassment, extortion, fabrication, retaliation, unauthorised disclosure or selective enforcement. A complaint alleging a grave risk of evidence fabrication, extortion or coercion shall be assigned outside the implicated chain of command, preserve available records, give a reasoned interim-protection decision within forty-eight hours and be subject to external review and court access.

(6) Knowingly fabricating or planting evidence, making a false material record, demanding or accepting an improper advantage, threatening false implication, retaliating for a protected complaint, or directing such conduct is serious misconduct and shall be referred for independent disciplinary and criminal process under applicable law. The accused official receives notice, a fair hearing, reasons and appeal; rank, political instruction or purported operational necessity is not a defence to unlawful conduct.

(7) A food business, owner, worker, supplier, consumer or public-interest organisation may seek urgent preservation of evidence, protection from retaliation, correction of a false record, return of unlawfully retained property, restoration of a licence or grade, compensation for provable loss and an independent court or tribunal remedy. A complaint of harassment does not immunise any person from a separate evidence-based investigation conducted under the same neutral rules.

(8) The annual misuse audit shall report, in anonymised and aggregated form, harassment/extortion complaints, response times, evidence-preservation compliance, interim protections, substantiated findings, discipline, prosecution referrals, remedies, repeat patterns and disparities. It shall publish corrective recommendations and a response from each responsible authority.

### 44. Severe penalties for deliberate or reckless grave public-health harm

(1) Subject to a final conviction and the sentencing safeguards in this section, a person convicted of the grave public-health offence in sections 1 and 13 may be sentenced to imprisonment for a term of not less than seven years and up to twenty years, a proportionate fine reflecting harm and unlawful gain, court-ordered compensation, recall/remediation, disqualification from responsible food-business control and confiscation or recovery authorised by law.

(2) Where the offence causes death and the requisite causation and mental element are proved, the person shall be prosecuted and sentenced under the applicable homicide and public-health criminal law. Nothing in this Module limits a court’s power to impose the most serious lawful sentence available for the proved offence.

(3) A food business or related entity convicted of deliberate or reckless grave public-health harm may receive a proportionate court-ordered fine, disgorgement, recall/remediation order, ten-to-twenty-year market exclusion, cancellation of the relevant licence and publication of the judgment. A penalty against an entity does not displace the individual liability of a person proved responsible, and no innocent person is punished merely because of ownership or employment.

(4) Technical, isolated or promptly self-reported non-compliance, a good-faith recall, trace contamination, laboratory uncertainty or a compliance dispute shall not attract this section unless the prosecution proves beyond reasonable doubt the required conduct, knowledge or recklessness and grave risk or harm. The court shall state reasons that distinguish conduct causing or consciously disregarding grave risk from lesser breaches.

### 45. Severe penalties for intentional misuse of public power

(1) An official or person acting for an authority who, with intent to obtain an improper advantage, punish lawful conduct, protect a favoured person or fabricate a case, knowingly—

  (a) plants, fabricates, destroys, substitutes or conceals material evidence;

  (b) makes or procures a false material record or false allegation;

  (c) demands or accepts an improper advantage by threat or exercise of food-safety, licensing, police or other public power;

  (d) initiates, delays, intensifies or suppresses an inspection, seizure, closure, licence action, public warning, prosecution referral or other enforcement action on a prohibited ground; or

  (e) directs, conspires, attempts or knowingly assists conduct in clauses (a) to (d),

commits a serious abuse-of-public-power offence.

(2) On final conviction, the serious abuse-of-public-power offence is punishable with imprisonment for a term of not less than five years and up to fourteen years, a proportionate fine, recovery of unlawful gain, compensation for provable harm, dismissal or removal through the applicable lawful process, and disqualification for up to fifteen years from holding a public office, enforcement role, regulatory role or contractual public function.

(3) Where the offence was intended to conceal or enable a grave food-safety risk, caused grave bodily harm or death, involved a pattern affecting multiple persons, or was committed by abuse of a position of trust, the court may impose imprisonment for a term of not less than ten years and up to twenty years, in addition to any more serious applicable offence.

(4) A finding under this section requires independent investigation outside the implicated chain of command, full disclosure of material evidence including exculpatory material, a fair hearing, reasons, appeal and a competent court determination. Political instruction, rank, emergency, workload, administrative convenience or a demand for loyalty is not a defence; an honest mistake, absence of the required intent, and lawful good-faith action remain material to liability.

### 46. Sentencing, equality and anti-abuse safeguards

(1) A court shall individualise every sentence using the proved harm, risk, culpability, role, concealment, benefit, prior final violations, cooperation, remediation, victim impact and capacity to pay. It shall not impose collective punishment, retrospective criminal liability, irrebuttable presumption, punishment for protected speech or association, or a penalty that is grossly disproportionate.

(2) No person may be punished twice for the same offence contrary to law. Parallel administrative, civil or criminal measures shall be clearly identified, independently justified and subject to applicable constitutional and statutory safeguards.

(3) Every final penalty, licence exclusion, confiscation, dismissal or disqualification requires a reasoned decision, publicly accessible summary subject to lawful redaction, appeal and effective court remedy. An interim protective action may not become a de facto final punishment through delay.

(4) The independent annual audit shall test whether penalties, referrals and non-enforcement are comparable across similarly situated persons and whether they reveal political, caste, religion, gender, language, disability, wealth, geography or other prohibited bias. It shall publish findings and require a time-bound corrective response.

### 47. Customer extortion through knowingly false food-safety allegation

(1) A person commits customer-extortion through false allegation only where the prosecution proves beyond reasonable doubt that the person knowingly made or threatened to make a materially false food-safety complaint, review, report, public statement or allegation and did so to obtain money, goods, services, employment, publicity, a waiver, preferential treatment or another improper advantage from a food business, owner, worker, supplier, platform or related person.

(2) On final conviction, the offence is punishable with imprisonment for a term of up to five years, a proportionate fine, restitution of any advantage obtained or loss caused, and any other lawful remedy ordered by a competent court.

(3) A complaint, review, report, warning, boycott, criticism or request for redress made honestly, on reasonable grounds or in good faith is not an offence merely because it is mistaken, cannot be substantiated, results in no enforcement action, is later withdrawn or causes reputational or commercial harm. No food business, official or platform may threaten this section to suppress a complaint, review, whistleblower disclosure, journalism, consumer advocacy or lawful criticism.

(4) A prosecution under this section requires an independent assessment that there is evidence of both knowing material falsity and an improper-advantage demand or threat. The original food-safety allegation shall remain capable of independent investigation on its own evidence. The accused is entitled to disclosure, a fair hearing, reasons, appeal and all applicable criminal-process safeguards.

(5) The annual misuse audit shall report anonymised aggregate information on complaints of customer extortion, referrals, dismissals, convictions, use of the provision by businesses or authorities, and any indication that it is being used to chill protected consumer speech or legitimate reporting.

### 48. Laboratory and certification fraud

(1) A laboratory, assessor, certifier, analyst, auditor, responsible officer or person acting for any of them shall not knowingly fabricate, suppress, substitute, backdate, selectively report, manipulate, falsely certify, commercially influence or conceal a material food-safety result, calibration result, conflict, adverse finding or chain-of-custody record.

(2) On final conviction for conduct that deliberately conceals or creates a grave public-health risk, the responsible person is punishable with imprisonment for a term of not less than five years and up to fourteen years, a proportionate fine, recovery of unlawful gain, compensation and disqualification from relevant work for up to fifteen years. Where the conduct causes or is intended to enable death or grave widespread harm, section 44 or any more serious applicable offence applies.

(3) A final finding requires an independent investigation, preservation of raw data and methods, disclosure of material exculpatory evidence, meaningful opportunity to respond, a reasoned decision and appeal. Good-faith scientific disagreement, reported error or validated correction is not fraud.

### 49. Calibration, proficiency and accreditation integrity

(1) Laboratories, sensors, thermometers, water-testing equipment, live-view integrity systems and other prescribed high-risk equipment shall be calibrated, maintained and tested against published standards at risk-based intervals. The calibration history, instrument identity, operator and corrective action shall be retained in the evidence-custody system.

(2) Accredited laboratories and assessors shall participate in blind external proficiency testing, surprise inspection and conflict-screened peer review. Material failure requires prompt disclosure, independent retesting of affected matters where necessary, corrective action and a public summary.

(3) A small, rural, street or informal food business shall have accessible low-cost or publicly supported testing and training routes. Lack of means may not excuse an imminent serious health risk, but implementation must not use accreditation or technology to exclude a business without practicable assistance and a fair transition.

### 50. Delivery-chain safety and accountable handover

(1) Food prepared for delivery shall use safe, tamper-evident packaging where proportionate; preserve required temperature controls; identify preparation and handover time; and maintain an accessible chain record from venue to customer. A delivery worker shall receive hygiene and safe-handling support without being treated as the sole responsible person for a defect outside the worker’s control.

(2) A restaurant, cloud kitchen, platform, logistics provider and person exercising actual control is responsible for the portion of the delivery chain it controls. Liability is determined by evidence of control, knowledge, benefit, conduct and preventability, not by a standard-form contract alone.

(3) A platform shall provide the customer an accessible route to report seal damage, temperature concern, wrong handover, suspected tampering or illness, preserve the relevant chain record and route credible serious reports for prompt independent assessment.

### 51. Counterfeit, unlicensed and concealed online food business controls

(1) A marketplace, delivery platform, payment intermediary or material advertising intermediary that enables sale of food shall take proportionate steps to verify the legal identity, relevant licence and traceable contact of a seller before listing, and shall retain the verified record and relevant payment trail for a lawful safety, recall, audit or court process.

(2) On credible evidence that a listing is counterfeit, unlicensed, impersonated, materially concealed or presents an imminent serious health risk, the intermediary shall preserve records and apply a necessary, proportionate, time-limited restriction or warning with written reasons, notice and independent review. It shall not remove a seller merely because of political direction, lawful criticism or a competitor’s unsupported assertion.

(3) A seller subject to restriction has access to the evidence consistent with safety and investigation needs, a prompt correction/appeal route, and reinstatement where the basis is not sustained. The intermediary shall publish aggregate verification, restriction, reversal and recall data.

### 52. Repeat-pattern and related-entity accountability

(1) A final repeat pattern exists only where a competent court or statutory tribunal finds two or more final serious violations within the prescribed period by the same responsible person or by related entities shown to have common actual control, coordinated conduct, shared benefit or deliberate evasion. Mere association, common ownership or franchise status is insufficient.

(2) A final repeat-pattern finding may justify enhanced but proportionate penalty, longer licence exclusion, independent compliance monitor, mandatory remediation and enhanced sampling. It requires notice, full evidence, an opportunity to contest related-entity responsibility, reasons, appeal and periodic review.

(3) A business that promptly discloses, recalls, remediates, compensates and cooperates may receive credit in a sanction decision, but not immunity for deliberate or reckless grave harm.

### 53. Waste, water, drainage and pest-control integrity

(1) Every food business shall safely manage waste, drainage, grease, wastewater, potable water, ice, cleaning chemicals, pests and vectors in accordance with published minimum standards designed to prevent contamination and protect workers, neighbours and the environment.

(2) Inspections shall address these risks through objective records, photographs where necessary, safe sampling and proportionate correction directions. A serious or repeated breach may trigger recall, closure or referral only under the safeguards in sections 20, 22, 32 and 43.

(3) The appropriate government shall provide accessible local guidance, training and practicable waste/water infrastructure options, particularly for street vendors, rural businesses and low-income areas. It shall not punish a business for public-infrastructure failure without providing a fair remedy and transition path, unless an imminent serious risk requires immediate protective action.

### 54. Public-health and complaint-data protection

(1) Health, complaint, purchase, location, worker, customer, footage and food-safety data may be collected, used, shared and retained only for a specified lawful food-safety purpose, using the minimum necessary data and the safeguards in section 32.

(2) Such data shall not be sold, used for behavioural advertising, credit scoring, employment screening, immigration enforcement, unrelated policing, political profiling, union suppression or discrimination. A lawful court-supervised disclosure remains subject to necessity, proportionality and recorded reasons.

(3) A data subject shall have accessible notice, correction, access, security-breach notification and independent complaint rights, subject to narrowly tailored limits necessary for an active investigation, safety response or another lawful purpose.

### 55. Independent screening of serious referrals

(1) A serious food-harm, laboratory-fraud, official-corruption or extortion referral shall be screened by an independent, conflict-screened prosecutorial or statutory review unit separate from the referring inspector, implicated authority, political office and affected commercial interest.

(2) The unit shall apply published evidence thresholds, give written reasons for charge, further investigation or closure, preserve exculpatory evidence and publish de-identified aggregate referral, delay, closure and outcome data. A victim, complainant or affected person has a prompt review route for an unexplained or materially delayed closure, without a right to dictate an individual prosecution.

(3) No Minister, political office-holder, police superior, dominant business, platform or donor may give undisclosed case-specific direction. Any attempted direction shall be recorded and reported through the protected channel.

### 56. Compensation security and equitable insurance access

(1) Prescribed higher-risk food businesses shall maintain proportionate compensation security, insurance or contribution to the independent consumer-repair fund sufficient to support urgent consumer harm, recall and remediation. The requirement shall be based on evidenced risk, scale and capacity, not political influence or arbitrary classification.

(2) The appropriate government shall provide a pooled, low-cost, publicly transparent or cooperative route for small, street, rural and low-income vendors, together with training and transition support. No small vendor may be excluded from lawful work solely because commercial insurance is unavailable or unaffordable where the vendor complies with the applicable risk-reduction and transition plan.

(3) An insurer, fund or intermediary shall not use the requirement to deny coverage or charge discriminatory terms on prohibited grounds. Denial, exclusion, premium and claims decisions shall be reasoned, recorded and independently reviewable.

### 57. Current-law integration, severability and transition

(1) Before any provision of this Module is enacted or commenced, the appropriate legislature and government shall publish an official current-law crosswalk identifying the exact constitutional, food-safety, criminal, consumer, labour, privacy, evidence, platform, State and local provisions affected; legislative competence; conflict; amendment/repeal; implementing authority; fiscal requirement; and judicial remedy.

(2) A provision shall not be interpreted to displace a stronger lawful right, safeguard, remedy or independent court jurisdiction. If any provision is held invalid, the remaining provisions continue to the extent they can operate consistently with the Constitution and applicable law.

(3) Commencement shall be phased, publicly notified and paired with rules, capacity, accessible compliance support, inspector training, independent audits, public guidance and a review date. A person shall not be penalised for a new technical duty before a reasonable published transition period, except where conduct creates an imminent serious health risk or is independently proved deliberate fraud or grave harm.

### 58. Expired-food sale, resale and date-label manipulation

(1) A person shall not knowingly or recklessly sell, offer, distribute, donate for consumption, resell, repack, relabel, re-date, alter, remove, obscure or cause another person to alter a mandatory expiry, use-by, safety, batch or traceability label for food, where the conduct represents expired or unsafe food as fit for consumption or conceals its actual age, safety status or identity.

(2) A food business, warehouse, platform, franchisor, distributor, marketplace, delivery service and person exercising actual control shall maintain auditable stock-rotation, batch, expiry, return, destruction and disposal records. A platform or marketplace that controls listing or fulfilment shall prevent listing of a product shown in its records to be expired, recalled, withdrawn or date-manipulated and shall preserve the evidence for a lawful inspection, recall, complaint or court process.

(3) On final conviction for deliberate or reckless conduct under sub-section (1), the responsible person is punishable with imprisonment for a term of not less than three years and up to ten years, a proportionate fine, restitution, recall, destruction or safe diversion of affected stock under independent supervision, and disqualification from responsible food-business control for up to ten years. A responsible entity may receive proportionate fine, recovery of gain, licence suspension or cancellation and market exclusion under a court or statutory tribunal order.

(4) If the conduct creates or consciously disregards a substantial and unjustifiable risk of death, serious injury or widespread exposure, or causes such harm, sections 13, 44 and any more serious applicable offence apply. Expiry-date manipulation to conceal a grave risk is an aggravating factor in sentence.

(5) A promptly disclosed and independently verifiable printing, scanning or inventory error, where the food is immediately quarantined and not knowingly offered as fit for consumption, is not an offence under sub-section (1); however, necessary correction, recall, audit and proportionate administrative action remain available. A “best before” date, where legally distinct from a safety-related expiry/use-by date, shall be clearly defined in the current-law crosswalk and may not be represented falsely or used to evade a safety duty.

(6) Every seizure, quarantine, destruction, diversion, recall, licence action or public warning under this section requires documented evidence, chain of custody, written reasons, necessary and proportionate scope, independent review, access to relevant non-sensitive evidence and court or tribunal remedy. No authority may use this section to extort, harass or selectively target a business.

### 59. Mandatory responsible destruction and non-repudiable disposal evidence

(1) Food that is expired, subject to a final recall or quarantine, or otherwise determined under applicable law to be unsafe for human consumption shall immediately be segregated, clearly marked, secured against access and entered in the batch-traceability register. It shall not be donated, resold, repacked, relabelled, diverted into another food product or removed from the custody record.

(2) The responsible food business and the person controlling the relevant warehouse, transport, platform fulfilment, marketplace inventory or disposal process shall ensure destruction through an authorised, environmentally responsible and independently auditable process within the prescribed risk-based period. Any non-destruction route is permissible only where a current-law crosswalk identifies a lawful, independently authorised non-human use that cannot re-enter the human food chain and preserves equivalent custody, safety and public-accountability safeguards.

(3) The disposal record shall identify the product and batch, quantity and weight at each transfer, expiry/recall basis, location, time, responsible persons, transport, receiving facility, destruction method, environmental compliance record and independently witnessed final destruction. Each record shall carry authenticated time, unique identifier, custody history, alteration history and cryptographic or equivalent verifiable signature of the responsible parties and independent witness.

(4) The authority shall use a public-verification system that enables a customer, auditor, court, affected person or authorised reviewer to verify the existence, integrity and final status of a disposal record without exposing personal data, security-sensitive facility details or protected commercial information. The system shall retain the original evidence, record every access or correction, and make any correction additive and attributable rather than overwriting the original.

(5) Destruction shall be independently witnessed or technically verified by a conflict-screened person or system; high-risk or large-volume destruction requires dual independent custody verification. A business, contractor, official, certifier or witness shall not falsely attest, substitute stock, manipulate weight, divert material, break custody or destroy disposal evidence.

(6) Knowingly falsifying, diverting, substituting, concealing or destroying a material disposal record or expired/unsafe food item to evade this section is punishable under sections 44, 45, 48 or 58 as applicable, and is an aggravating factor in sentence. A verified technical failure or good-faith error requires immediate preservation, disclosure, corrective action and independent review; it does not excuse deliberate concealment or unsafe diversion.

(7) The regulator shall publish aggregated disposal volumes, completion times, verification failures, audit findings, related enforcement action and environmental outcomes. An affected person has access to the relevant non-sensitive disposal evidence, a correction route and an independent court or tribunal remedy.

(8) The person or entity that manufactured, imported, sold, offered, supplied, stored, distributed, listed, fulfilled or exercised actual control over expired, recalled, quarantined or unsafe food shall bear the reasonable and documented cost of segregation, transport, storage, testing, recall, public warning, destruction, environmental compliance, verification and remediation to the extent of that person’s or entity’s proved responsibility and control. A contractual term may allocate cost between private parties but may not reduce the public-protection duty or defeat recovery from a responsible person.

(9) Where an authority incurs those costs to prevent an imminent serious risk, secure evidence or complete a lawful disposal, it may recover the actual, necessary and proportionate cost from the responsible person or entity through the applicable lawful process. It shall provide an itemised statement, underlying records, reasons, payment/review period and independent court or tribunal remedy. No recovery may be punitive, duplicative, politically selective or used as a substitute for a separately required criminal or administrative finding.

### 60. Cold-chain integrity and breach response

(1) A person controlling prescribed temperature-sensitive food shall maintain calibrated, tamper-evident temperature and time records for storage, transport, handover and delivery. A material safety-limit breach requires immediate quarantine, risk assessment, preservation of records and a documented decision on recall, safe disposal or release.

(2) Where the breach creates a credible serious risk, the responsible entity and competent authority shall issue an accessible customer alert and recall in accordance with section 24. An alert shall not disclose personal delivery or health data beyond what is necessary.

(3) A verified device failure or supply interruption requires prompt disclosure, alternative verification and proportionate correction; it does not excuse knowing concealment, fabricated logs or unsafe release.

### 61. Water, ice and food-contact material safety

(1) Food businesses shall use potable water, safe ice and food-contact materials that meet applicable standards. Higher-risk venues and areas with known vulnerability shall undergo recurring independent risk-based testing, with sample custody, laboratory allocation and results recorded under this Module.

(2) A material contamination result requires immediate source isolation, accessible public-health assessment, corrective action, retesting and, where necessary, stop-use, warning, recall or closure under sections 20 and 24. Public results shall identify risk and remedy without exposing protected personal data.

(3) The appropriate government shall publish local water/ice testing support and safe supply options. A business shall not be selectively penalised for a public water-system failure without fair notice, practical safe alternatives and a remedy, except where immediate protective action is necessary for an imminent serious risk.

### 62. Allergen safety and informed choice

(1) A food business shall accurately disclose prescribed allergens and material cross-contact risk on packaging, menus, ordering pages and through accessible non-digital means. It shall maintain separation, cleaning, staff-training and response controls proportionate to the food and risk.

(2) Knowingly concealing an allergen, deliberately making a false allergen-free claim, or recklessly ignoring a documented serious cross-contact risk is subject to sections 13 and 44 where the required grave-risk threshold is met, and to proportionate administrative and civil remedies otherwise.

(3) A consumer report of an allergic reaction shall be handled as a protected adverse-event report. The authority shall preserve relevant samples and records, support prompt medical advice and conduct an independent assessment without assuming fault before evidence is tested.

### 63. Enhanced protection for children, patients and public feeding

(1) Schools, childcare settings, hospitals, care homes, public nutrition schemes, shelters, prisons and other prescribed settings serving children, patients or dependent persons shall comply with enhanced food safety, nutrition, allergen, hygiene, traceability, water and inspection standards proportionate to the vulnerability of those served.

(2) The standards shall be evidence-based, non-discriminatory, accessible and publicly reported. They shall not restrict access to lawful nutrition, cultural food or religious dietary practice without a clear safety basis, least restrictive means, reasons and review.

(3) A credible serious risk in such a setting requires immediate evidence preservation, health support, independent investigation, family/affected-person communication where appropriate, and a corrected public account. No authority may conceal an event to protect institutional reputation.

### 64. Supplier restriction and rehabilitation safeguards

(1) A supplier, manufacturer, importer, laboratory or service provider may be restricted from public procurement, platform access or prescribed high-risk food supply only on a final serious finding or a necessary time-limited interim measure supported by credible evidence of imminent serious risk, reasons, notice and independent review.

(2) Any final restriction shall state its factual and legal basis, scope, duration, related-entity finding if any, rehabilitation conditions, review date and appeal route. It shall be proportionate, non-discriminatory and not based on political belief, criticism, identity, association or a mere allegation.

(3) A public register shall distinguish interim from final restrictions and promptly correct, vary or remove an entry after review. A restricted person has access to the relevant non-sensitive evidence and an independent court or tribunal remedy.

### 65. Import food integrity and cross-border recall coordination

(1) Imported food shall be subject to risk-based origin, batch, supplier, ingredient, safety-certification and transport verification, with random independently allocated testing proportionate to the documented risk. No importer may substitute origin, batch, certificate or laboratory result.

(2) On a credible serious imported-food risk, the competent authority shall preserve records, issue a necessary and proportionate hold or warning, notify relevant lawful domestic and cross-border safety channels, and coordinate an accessible recall. Any hold beyond the prescribed urgent period requires independent review and court access.

(3) Import controls shall be applied by published neutral criteria and may not be used for concealed protectionism, retaliation, discrimination or political favour. Results, delays, reversals and conflict disclosures shall be reported in aggregate.

### 66. Recall effectiveness and recovery verification

(1) A recall is not complete merely because it is announced. The responsible entity shall demonstrate through batch, inventory, platform, warehouse, retailer, delivery and disposal records the proportion of affected stock located, quarantined, returned, destroyed or otherwise lawfully resolved.

(2) The competent authority shall independently audit recall effectiveness, including customer notification reach, response times, residual-stock checks, platform delisting, small-vendor coverage, refunds/remedies and re-entry risk. It shall publish an accessible completion assessment and unresolved-risk notice.

(3) A person who knowingly reports a recall as complete while concealing material affected stock, consumer harm, failed notice or re-entry risk is subject to sections 44, 48, 58 and 59 as applicable.

### 67. Food-handler training and fair credentialing

(1) Persons performing prescribed food-handling roles shall receive practical, accessible hygiene, allergen, illness-reporting, contamination, traceability and complaint-rights training at risk-based intervals. Training shall be available in relevant local languages and accessible formats.

(2) Credentialing, where required, shall have a published competency standard, affordable or free route, reasonable accommodation for disability and literacy, correction/appeal process, and a transition period. It shall not be used to exclude workers or small vendors because of caste, gender, language, migration status, income, political belief or lack of digital access.

(3) An employer, platform or authority shall not require a worker to handle food contrary to health-safety advice, retaliate for reporting illness or safety concern, or shift its own compliance responsibility to a worker without actual control.

### 68. Independent consumer market testing

(1) A publicly funded, independently governed programme shall conduct recurring random and risk-based market testing of food in urban, peri-urban, rural and remote areas, including small vendors, platforms, warehouses and large businesses. Sampling selection shall be transparent in method and free from political or commercial direction.

(2) Results shall be independently confirmed before a final adverse finding, except that an urgent interim public-health warning may issue on credible evidence under section 24. The programme shall offer relevant non-sensitive evidence, defence testing and prompt correction for inaccurate results.

(3) The programme shall publish methods, geographic coverage, product categories, laboratory performance, findings, uncertainty, outcomes, equality indicators and corrective action. It shall not use testing as a pretext for harassment or selective enforcement.

### 69. Accessible public-warning and correction standard

(1) A material food-safety warning, recall or closure notice shall be timely, factual, plainly worded and available in relevant local languages and accessible formats. It shall state the product or venue, batch/traceability identifier where known, location or distribution scope, nature of risk, practical health advice, return/refund or recall steps, support route, source/date and correction/appeal route.

(2) A warning shall be no broader than necessary, distinguish allegation from verified finding and be reviewed as evidence develops. The issuer shall promptly correct, update or withdraw a materially inaccurate or outdated warning with equal practical prominence.

(3) No warning system may be used to shame, threaten, publicise unrelated allegations, suppress criticism or target a person on a prohibited ground. An affected person and the public have an accessible route to seek correction, clarification or independent review.

### 70. Contaminant and residue surveillance

(1) The independent market-testing programme shall conduct risk-based surveillance for pesticides, veterinary-drug and antibiotic residues, heavy metals, industrial contaminants, microplastics and other contaminants identified through published evidence and review. Sampling plans, thresholds, uncertainty and laboratory methods shall be transparent and independently audited.

(2) A material result requires confirmatory testing, source tracing, exposure assessment, risk communication and proportionate corrective action. An urgent warning may issue before confirmation only where credible evidence establishes a necessary public-health basis under section 24.

(3) The programme shall publish de-identified results, geographic and product-category coverage, uncertainty, corrective actions and unresolved risks. It shall identify gaps affecting vulnerable communities without using data to stigmatise a community, region, occupation or small-vendor group.

### 71. Novel-food, genetic-modification and irradiation transparency

(1) A food subject to a lawful pre-market requirement because it is genetically modified, genetically engineered, irradiated, produced through a novel process or otherwise prescribed as a novel food shall not be placed on the market without the applicable authorisation, safety assessment, traceability and disclosure.

(2) A label, menu, online listing and public register shall accurately state the relevant status where disclosure is required by law or necessary to prevent material consumer deception. The statement shall be factual, accessible and not imply danger or safety beyond the evidence.

(3) Rules under this section shall follow current primary law, published scientific evidence, independent conflict screening, public consultation, reasoned decisions, review and court remedy. They shall not be used to suppress lawful research, stigmatise food or communities, or create an unreviewable commercial barrier.

### 72. Nutrition integrity and high-risk ingredient transparency

(1) The appropriate authority shall establish evidence-based, periodically reviewed front-of-pack and menu disclosures for prescribed high levels of sugar, sodium, trans fat, saturated fat, caffeine or other ingredient linked by credible evidence to material public-health risk. The format shall be plain, accessible, non-discriminatory and independently tested for comprehension.

(2) A nutrition, health or wellness claim shall not contradict a required warning or conceal material serving size, ingredient, additive, sugar, sodium, fat or caffeine information. Child-directed marketing of prescribed high-risk food shall be subject to the least restrictive effective, evidence-based safeguards necessary to protect health and informed choice.

(3) Standards must publish their evidence, uncertainty, alternatives, equality impacts, affordability/access effects and review date. They shall not be set or changed by undisclosed industry direction, political preference or commercial sponsorship.

### 73. Food fraud, origin and quality-claim integrity

(1) A person shall not knowingly or recklessly dilute, substitute, counterfeit, falsely represent origin, production method, ingredient, grade, weight, organic status, halal status, quality certification, “natural” claim, geographic indication or other material food characteristic.

(2) A certification, religious or cultural claim shall be used respectfully and only under transparent, competent and conflict-screened verification where such verification is represented. No authority may favour or disadvantage a food business, community or consumer because of religion, culture, language or lawful dietary practice.

(3) Deliberate fraud that creates grave health risk, targets a protected dietary need, or conceals a prohibited ingredient is an aggravating factor under sections 13, 44 and 58 as applicable. Other fraud remains subject to proportionate civil, administrative or criminal process with proof, reasons and appeal.

### 74. Menu, portion and representation truthfulness

(1) A restaurant, vendor, caterer, platform or advertiser shall not materially misrepresent the food supplied through photographs, descriptions, portion, ingredient, allergen, source, preparation method, price, availability, serving size or nutrition claim. Reasonable illustrative variation shall be disclosed where relevant and shall not conceal a material difference.

(2) Prescribed menus and online listings shall provide accessible ingredient, allergen, additive and portion information, and nutrition information where proportionate to the business type, public-health risk and capacity. The rules shall provide simple compliant formats and no-cost public tools for small businesses.

(3) A consumer has access to correction, refund, complaint and proportionate remedy. A claim of deception requires materiality and evidence; trivial variation or good-faith error promptly corrected is not a grave offence.

### 75. Safe near-date donation and food-waste prevention

(1) Food that is lawfully safe for human consumption but is near a legally distinct “best before” date may be donated only through a registered or otherwise authorised, traceable channel that verifies safety, storage, transport, recipient information and non-commercial distribution. Expired, recalled, quarantined, unsafe or date-manipulated food is excluded and remains subject to section 59.

(2) A donor, recipient organisation, platform and transporter shall preserve batch, condition, storage and handover records and promptly report a suspected safety risk. A donation shall not be used to dispose of food that would be unlawful to sell or to transfer a risk to people experiencing poverty, disaster or displacement.

(3) The appropriate government shall support safe donation infrastructure and publish non-discriminatory guidance. A good-faith donor complying with this section is not liable merely because a recipient later declines the food; deliberate concealment, unsafe diversion or false record remains actionable.

### 76. Antimicrobial-resistance and veterinary-residue accountability

(1) The surveillance programme shall include risk-based monitoring of antibiotic and other veterinary-drug residues in prescribed food chains, with source tracing, laboratory integrity, public reporting and corrective action.

(2) A producer, manufacturer, importer, supplier or person controlling a relevant food chain shall not use, direct, conceal or market food produced through prohibited or undisclosed antimicrobial practices, or evade residue controls. A final finding requires evidence, independent testing, notice, reasons and appeal.

(3) The appropriate government shall coordinate public-health, veterinary, agricultural, environmental and food-safety evidence without allowing any one body or industry interest to control standard-setting, inspection, prosecution and adjudication.

### 77. Independent scientific standards and anti-capture process

(1) Any prescribed food-safety, nutrition, contaminant, additive, laboratory or novel-food standard shall be developed through a published independent scientific process with public methods, evidence register, conflict declarations, diverse relevant expertise, public comment, reasoned response and periodic review.

(2) A person with a material financial, political or organisational conflict shall not control the assessment, vote or final decision on the affected standard. Appointments, recusals, minutes, minority scientific views, data limitations and final reasons shall be public, subject only to narrowly tailored lawful confidentiality.

(3) The standard-setting body shall be institutionally separate from licensing, enforcement, prosecution and private commercial control. Its decision remains subject to judicial review; no standard may be secret, self-certified or insulated from evidence-based correction.

### 78. Court-ready food-safety evidence and fair-trial protocol

(1) A criminal or serious administrative case under this Module shall use documented sampling, custody, accredited or independently verified laboratory method, quality control, expert qualification, relevant raw data, uncertainty disclosure and preservation of potentially exculpatory evidence.

(2) The affected person has a reasonable opportunity to inspect relevant non-sensitive evidence, obtain independent testing where practicable, challenge method and expert evidence, call relevant evidence and receive a reasoned decision from a competent independent forum. A court retains its applicable evidentiary and constitutional powers.

(3) No algorithmic score, certification, laboratory result or official assertion alone is conclusive of guilt. Evidence obtained through material unlawful coercion, fabrication or concealed conflict is subject to exclusion or other effective remedy under applicable law.

### 79. Annual public food-integrity accountability report

(1) An independent public report shall be laid annually before Parliament and each relevant State legislature, and published in accessible formats. It shall cover foodborne harm, contaminants, inspections, recalls, expiry disposal, laboratory performance, official misconduct, consumer/worker complaints, enforcement disparities, platform accountability, implementation capacity, expenditure, unresolved risks and corrective recommendations.

(2) The Union, State and local authorities responsible for a material finding shall publish a reasoned response and implementation timetable within the prescribed period. Legislative debate, public questions and independent follow-up review shall be facilitated; the report shall not direct an individual prosecution or adjudication.

(3) The report shall include public submissions, minority or dissenting technical views, data limitations and a record of recommendations not accepted, with reasons. It shall not disclose protected whistleblower identity, medical data, personal data or material fair-trial information.

### 80. Complete prohibition of plastic in the food and feed chain

(1) From the final commencement date, no person may manufacture for use in the food or feed chain, import, export, sell, offer, supply, procure, possess for commercial use, store, transport, deliver, distribute, provide, install, maintain or use a plastic article, plastic material or plastic component at any stage of producing, handling, testing, processing, packaging, labelling, storing, preparing, cooking, serving, dispensing, sampling, transporting, delivering, retailing, feeding, recalling or disposing of food or feed.

(2) In this section, “plastic” includes a synthetic or semi-synthetic polymer and any item wholly or partly made with it, including plastic-lined, plastic-coated, plastic-laminated, plastic-composite, polymer-treated or polymer-bound articles. It includes packaging, containers, wraps, pouches, bottles, caps, seals, films, labels, sachets, trays, cups, bowls, plates, cutlery, straws, stirrers, gloves, aprons, utensils, scoops, liners, bags, crates, pallets, storage articles, transport articles, delivery articles, food/feed-contact machinery components and disposable operational articles used in the food or feed chain. It applies whether the item is described as single-use, reusable, recycled, recyclable, compostable, biodegradable, bio-based, food-grade, paper-based, plant-based or otherwise environmentally preferable.

(3) No description, certification, customer request, supplier assurance, contract, platform term, outsourcing arrangement, emergency label, scarcity claim, recycled-content claim, de minimis claim, export-only representation or other device permits a prohibited plastic use. An article that contains a plastic layer, coating, lining, adhesive, polymer treatment, component or intentionally added plastic material is prohibited even if another material forms its principal visible part.

(4) Every responsible actor shall use only a safe, independently verifiable, genuinely non-plastic alternative suitable for its actual food/feed, temperature, hygiene, allergen, chemical-migration, durability and reuse conditions. A substitute must not be treated as compliant merely because it is non-plastic: it must meet published safety and material standards and must not create a greater public-health, animal-health, worker-safety or environmental harm.

(5) The prohibition is a final prohibition, not a licensing exception. Before final commencement, the appropriate government shall publish open material standards, independent test methods, a time-bound transition schedule, accessible reusable/refill and non-plastic supply infrastructure, safe cleaning facilities, procurement reform, small-vendor support and responsible end-of-life capacity. The transition may only govern the date by which existing stock is withdrawn; it may not authorise new procurement, manufacture or import of prohibited plastic after the first commencement notice, and it may not create a permanent, secret, case-specific or commercially purchased exemption.

(6) A responsible actor shall maintain material-composition, supplier, batch, purchase, custody, cleaning and end-of-life records sufficient for independent verification. The authority shall use publicly specified, independently auditable, risk-based testing to detect concealed plastic, false certificates, unsafe substitutes and false environmental claims. Samples, methods, results and conflict declarations shall be published subject to necessary privacy and fair-trial safeguards.

(7) A final finding of deliberate concealment, false certification, repeated prohibited use, supply or distribution attracts proportionate licence action, recall/replacement, recovery of gain, responsible-party disposal cost, public correction and referral for applicable civil, administrative or criminal process. Serious consequences require proof of the applicable mental element and harm or grave risk; no person may be criminally punished for an unintentional, promptly disclosed and effectively corrected contamination or supplier fraud without personal knowledge, direction or recklessness.

(8) Enforcement is subject to sections 20, 32, 43 and 57: objective grounds, documented evidence, neutral selection, independent testing, notice, a prompt correction route, equality safeguards, anti-extortion safeguards and court or tribunal remedy. The public dashboard shall publish aggregated compliance, inspection, material-test, substitution, correction, enforcement and vendor-support data. No official may use this section to extort, selectively target, retaliate against or close a business without recorded lawful grounds and effective review.

(9) For food within the Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006, Parliament shall enact the amendment in Schedule A. That amendment repeals the Food Safety and Standards (Packaging) First Amendment Regulations, 2025, prevents revival of an earlier version of regulation 4(4)(e), and replaces the recycled-PET permission with an express statutory prohibition. No rule, regulation, guideline, direction, standard, licence condition or approval may authorise what Schedule A prohibits.

### 81. Waste-accountability levy and circular-food-service fund

(1) A prescribed food business, platform, caterer, manufacturer, importer, warehouse, marketplace or delivery-service operator that generates avoidable food-service waste shall pay a waste-accountability levy calculated from independently verifiable waste quantity, material type, avoidability, environmental/health risk and actual control over the waste stream. The levy is a public charge and shall be imposed only by clear law with published rate, classification, measurement, collection and review rules.

(2) The calculation shall exclude or credit food safely donated under section 75, verified reusable-container returns, lawful recycling, material recovered through an independently auditable circular system, and waste caused principally by documented public-infrastructure failure, disaster or an authority-directed recall. A credit does not apply where records are falsified or material is unlawfully diverted.

(3) Rates shall be progressive, risk- and capacity-sensitive, and designed to reduce avoidable waste rather than to punish lawful small-scale trade. Small, street, rural, cooperative and low-income vendors shall have access to simple measurement tools, free/low-cost collection and reuse infrastructure, training, correction support, instalment/payment-relief review and non-digital compliance routes. No exemption may permit unsafe disposal or deliberate waste generation.

(4) Levy revenue shall be held in a separately audited circular-food-service fund and used only for publicly disclosed safe collection, composting or other lawful treatment, reusable/refill systems, waste-worker safety, local infrastructure, independent measurement/audit, remediation and small-vendor transition support. The fund shall not be controlled by a levy payer, a single political office, licensing authority or waste contractor.

(5) Every levy assessment shall state the measured basis, data source, rate, credits, responsible entity, reasons, payment period and independent correction/appeal route. The authority shall not estimate liability arbitrarily, demand an undisclosed payment, condition an inspection/licence on an unlawful payment or use the levy to extort, target critics or favour competitors.

(6) A person who knowingly manipulates waste records, falsely claims a credit, diverts waste, or colludes to evade the levy is subject to proportionate recovery and applicable administrative, civil or criminal process after independent investigation, notice, reasons and appeal. An official or contractor who inflates, suppresses, selectively assesses or extorts through the levy is subject to sections 43 and 45.

(7) The fund and levy shall undergo annual independent financial, environmental, equality and anti-corruption audit. The audit shall publish revenue, expenditure, rates, credits, waste reduction, vendor impact, worker safety, regional disparities, unresolved risks and corrective action; Parliament and relevant State legislatures shall receive the report under section 79.

### 82. Expiry inventory alert and stock-control duty

(1) A prescribed food business, warehouse, platform fulfilment service, distributor and marketplace shall maintain an accessible digital or durable offline inventory system that gives timely batch-level alerts before a safety-related expiry/use-by date and records quarantine, return, donation under section 75, destruction under section 59 or other lawful disposition.

(2) The system shall be affordable, interoperable, auditable and available through simple non-digital routes for small and rural vendors. It shall not require collection of unnecessary customer or worker data, or make a vendor dependent on a proprietary platform.

(3) An alert does not itself establish liability. Knowingly ignoring, disabling, falsifying or using an alert system to conceal unsafe stock is evidence subject to sections 58 and 59 and ordinary fair-process safeguards.

### 83. Food-service environmental integrity scorecard

(1) Prescribed food businesses and platforms shall report standardised, independently auditable information on water use, waste generation, packaging/material use, energy, emissions where measurable, disposal integrity and improvement actions. The scorecard shall use published methods, uncertainty and context, and shall not be presented as a health or environmental claim beyond the evidence.

(2) The public register shall provide accessible aggregate and venue-level information proportionate to scale and risk. Small vendors shall receive simple reporting tools and support; a scorecard may not be used as an unreviewable licence condition or a substitute for food-safety evidence.

(3) Greenwashing, falsified scorecard data or concealment of material environmental harm is subject to independent correction, public clarification and applicable lawful enforcement after notice, evidence, reasons and appeal.

### 84. Delivery-worker safety and incentive integrity

(1) A platform, food business or logistics provider shall not set pay, rating, allocation, deadline, penalty, route or algorithmic incentive that foreseeably pressures a delivery worker to breach temperature, seal, hygiene, traffic-safety, rest or food-handling requirements.

(2) A worker may refuse an unsafe delivery instruction, report a temperature or tamper concern, quarantine a credible-risk order and seek assistance without loss of pay, rating, account, benefit or lawful status. The entity controlling the relevant incentive shall maintain auditable evidence of safety impact and corrective action.

(3) Material automated or managerial action against a worker for a safety-related refusal or report requires reasons, human review, an accessible appeal and protection from retaliation. Nothing in this section shifts a business’s food-safety responsibility onto a worker without actual control.

### 85. Fair allocation of recall and disposal cost in supply chains

(1) A dominant buyer, platform, franchisor, manufacturer, importer or chain shall not contractually transfer recall, destruction, levy, testing or remediation cost to a small supplier, vendor, worker or delivery service that did not cause, control, know of or recklessly disregard the relevant risk.

(2) Cost allocation shall follow actual control, culpability, benefit, contractual undertaking and capacity to prevent harm. Any indemnity, deduction, delayed payment or chargeback must state its evidence and calculation and be subject to an independent low-cost correction and appeal route.

(3) A contract term that waives a food-safety duty, suppresses reporting, prevents evidence preservation or imposes retaliatory cost is void to that extent. This section does not prevent a responsible person from bearing the cost required by section 59.

### 86. Community food-safety councils and public oversight

(1) Each prescribed local area shall support a community food-safety council with inclusive representation of consumers, workers, small vendors, disability advocates, public-health expertise, women, marginalised communities and local civil society, selected through transparent conflict-screened procedures.

(2) The council may review aggregate performance, accessibility, equality, infrastructure gaps, public warnings, local waste systems and implementation plans; receive protected systemic concerns; and publish recommendations. It has no power to direct an individual inspection, laboratory result, prosecution, licence decision or court outcome.

(3) Meetings, members, conflicts, agendas, minutes, submissions and reasoned official responses shall be public subject to narrowly tailored privacy/safety limits. Membership shall not be conditioned on political affiliation or support for any government, party, business or ideology.

### 87. Independent compliance audit and anti-entrapment safeguards

(1) The independent programme may conduct announced, random and mystery-customer compliance audits using published ethical protocols, conflict screening, proportionate evidence collection and oversight. An audit may observe, test or purchase ordinary products/services but shall not induce, pressure, deceive into or create an offence that would not otherwise have occurred.

(2) A finding based on a mystery audit requires preserved records, independent confirmation where material, notice, relevant non-sensitive evidence, response opportunity, reasons, review and appeal. It may not alone justify a grave criminal charge without independent corroborating evidence.

(3) Audit selection, outcomes, reversals, complaints and any allegation of targeting shall be independently reviewed and publicly reported in aggregate. Auditors shall not solicit improper advantage, disclose protected information or use the programme for commercial competition or political purpose.

### 88. Public-health emergency food-service reserve

(1) The appropriate government shall maintain a transparently procured, independently audited reserve of safe food, potable-water equipment, non-plastic reusable/verified-safe serviceware, hygiene supplies, mobile testing capacity and accessible communication tools for disaster, outbreak or displacement response.

(2) Procurement, stock rotation, storage, deployment, audit and disposal shall comply with the traceability, anti-corruption, expiry, evidence and public-disclosure safeguards of this Module. An emergency does not authorise unsafe, expired, plastic-prohibited or untraceable food-service supplies.

(3) Distribution shall use published need-based, non-discriminatory criteria with accessible complaints, independent observation and prompt public reporting. No political office, donor, contractor or police authority may condition essential food or supplies on political support, identity, publicity or waiver of rights.

### 89. Reusable-container return, sanitation and custody system

(1) A reusable food-contact container or cutlery system shall use verified safe materials, unique identification where proportionate, safe return points, recorded custody, cleaning/sterilisation standards, inspection, segregation of damaged items and independent verification sufficient to prevent cross-contamination or substitution.

(2) Deposit, return and refund terms shall be transparent, affordable, accessible and non-discriminatory. A person without a smartphone, bank account or digital identity must have a practicable alternative return/refund route.

(3) A platform, vendor, collection provider or contractor shall not falsely represent a container as sanitised, returned, reusable or plastic-free, or divert a returned container into an unsafe stream. Material failures require corrective action, evidence preservation and independent review.

### 90. Public decision, lobbying and revolving-door transparency

(1) A public authority, regulator, standard-setter, inspectorate, laboratory-accreditation body, procurement authority and member of a statutory advisory body shall publish meetings, written communications, gifts, hospitality, sponsorship, financial interests, employment negotiations and material lobbying concerning food rules, standards, enforcement policy, procurement, platform regulation, waste or exemptions.

(2) The record shall identify the participants, represented interests, subject, materials, outcome and conflict/recusal decision, subject only to narrowly tailored protection for personal security, lawful investigation or genuinely confidential information. An undisclosed case-specific direction is invalid under section 29.

(3) A prescribed cooling-off period, conflict-screened recusal, independent ethics review and effective sanction shall apply to revolving-door employment and material conflicts. Transparency shall not expose whistleblower identity, trade-secret detail unnecessary for public accountability, or personal data without a lawful basis.

### 91. Right to safe food and effective public remedy

(1) Every person has the right, without discrimination, to food that is reasonably safe, truthfully represented, traceable, accessible and protected by evidence-based public-health action, together with access to information, complaint, health support, correction, compensation and an effective independent remedy for breach.

(2) The Union, State and local authorities exercising functions under this Module have a duty to take reasonable, timely, transparent and proportionate steps within their lawful powers and available resources to prevent, investigate, disclose and remedy material food-safety risk. Resource constraints do not justify discrimination, deliberate inaction, concealment of a known serious risk, destruction of evidence or denial of court access.

(3) This section does not create an unchecked authority or displace existing constitutional, statutory, consumer, labour, environmental, criminal or judicial remedies. It shall be interpreted consistently with equality, dignity, due process, federalism, scientific evidence, public participation and judicial review.

### 92. Whole-of-food-chain application and non-fragmentation

(1) Every duty, safeguard, offence, remedy, record, transparency requirement and review mechanism in this Module applies across the whole food chain described in the Scope and universal application section, unless a provision expressly and lawfully limits its own application.

(2) A responsible person may not avoid a duty by separating ownership, contracting out a function, using multiple entities, transferring a batch, changing digital marketplace, moving between States, using a delivery intermediary, splitting an order, claiming an informal arrangement or assigning a nominal operator. Responsibility follows actual control, knowledge, benefit, conduct and capacity to prevent or correct the risk.

(3) The appropriate government shall implement the Module through coherent Union, State and local rules and support systems so that a human consumer, animal keeper, animal-care institution or feed purchaser receives equivalent baseline protection whether food or feed is bought from a restaurant, street vendor, supermarket, school, hospital, caterer, factory outlet, pet-food/feed supplier, animal-care facility, online marketplace, delivery service, public programme or any other channel. Implementation may be risk-, species- and capacity-sensitive but shall not create a lower safety or integrity standard for people with less income, rural residence, disability, migration status or limited digital access, or for animals supplied through informal or low-cost channels.

(4) A duty that by its nature concerns human consumption, such as a human nutrition warning or a customer dining view, applies to animal food or feed only to the extent a species-appropriate rule makes it relevant. This limitation does not reduce the core duties to prevent contamination, adulteration, unsafe storage, false labelling, unsafe packaging, expiry fraud, residue harm, traceability failure, evidence manipulation or public/animal-health risk in any food or feed chain.

### 93. Universal food and feed composition, safety and nutritional adequacy duty

(1) No person shall knowingly or recklessly manufacture, import, sell, supply, prepare, store, transport, advertise or represent food or feed for any human or non-human species where it contains a prohibited, contaminated, adulterated, toxic, unsafe, undeclared or harmful ingredient, contaminant, residue, additive, medication or substance; or where it materially fails the published species-appropriate safety or nutritional standard applicable to the representation made.

(2) A product represented as a complete food, complete feed, staple, balanced ration, therapeutic diet, infant/child food, medical nutrition, pet food, livestock feed, poultry feed, aquaculture feed, bird food or other health-supporting product shall meet the applicable published nutritional adequacy, ingredient, contaminant, species/life-stage and use standard. A product that is not nutritionally complete shall state that fact prominently and shall not be marketed or supplied as a substitute for a complete diet where that would create a material health risk.

(3) The standards shall be species-, life-stage-, health-condition- and use-appropriate; based on independent evidence; state uncertainty and testing method; include minimum nutritional value and maximum harmful thresholds where supported by evidence; and be reviewed periodically through the independent process in section 77. No industry, political office or commercial sponsor may secretly direct a standard or an exception.

(4) A material final violation requires recall, correction, safe replacement, consumer/animal-keeper warning, proportionate licence action, recovery of gain, compensation/remediation where harm is proved, and referral for applicable civil, administrative or criminal process. Deliberate or reckless material harm or grave risk attracts sections 13 and 44; a minor assay variation, promptly disclosed error, genuinely disputed science or clearly labelled non-complete product does not establish criminal liability without the required proof.

(5) Testing, sampling, nutritional analysis, veterinary/medical assessment, evidence preservation, notice, independent review, defence testing, reasons, appeal and court remedy are mandatory. No authority may treat a nutrition or ingredient deviation as a pretext for harassment, extortion, discrimination or selective enforcement.

### 94. Species-specific nutrition and contaminant standards

(1) The independent standards process shall publish risk-based nutrition, ingredient, contaminant, storage and labelling standards for human food and for pet food, livestock feed, poultry feed, aquaculture feed, bird food, reptile/amphibian feed and other prescribed animal food/feed, taking account of species, life stage, production purpose, health need and welfare.

(2) The process shall publish the evidence base, affected communities, affordability/access impact, environmental impact, uncertainty and transition support. It shall not require a single diet, cultural practice or production system absent a clear evidence-based safety or welfare need.

(3) A standard may be strengthened only through the published process and may not be weakened by private contract, informal instruction, political direction or a captured certification scheme.

### 95. Animal illness, mortality, poisoning and outbreak reporting

(1) An animal keeper, feed business, veterinary professional, laboratory, shelter, farm, hatchery, aquaculture operator, zoo, public authority or platform that has credible information of a suspected feed-linked illness, unusual mortality, poisoning, contamination or outbreak shall promptly preserve relevant samples/records and report through the protected system.

(2) The competent authorities shall coordinate veterinary, food-safety, environmental and public-health assessment, issue proportionate species-appropriate alerts, support veterinary response and trace the source. A report shall not be suppressed to protect a business, institution, donor, political office or local reputation.

(3) The system shall protect good-faith reporters, personal data and sensitive farm/location details where disclosure would create a genuine safety risk, while retaining public warning and independent review.

### 96. Zoonotic and cross-chain contamination barriers

(1) A responsible person shall maintain separation, traceability, sanitation and control measures sufficient to prevent contaminated animal feed, animal by-product, waste, medication, pathogen or residue from entering human food, and to prevent unsafe human-food waste or ingredient from entering animal feed.

(2) A credible cross-chain risk requires immediate preservation, source isolation, independent testing, risk assessment, necessary warning/recall and coordination under section 24. Any restriction shall be proportionate, reviewed and subject to remedy.

(3) The annual report shall identify cross-chain incidents, corrective action, data gaps and institutional coordination failures without disclosing protected personal or commercial information beyond what public safety requires.

### 97. Feed medication, hormone and growth-promoter integrity

(1) A person shall not use, direct, conceal, market or supply animal food or feed containing a prohibited, undeclared, unsafe or unlawfully administered antibiotic, antimicrobial, hormone, growth promoter, veterinary medicine or other pharmacologically active substance.

(2) Prescribed feed chains shall maintain medication, veterinary direction, withdrawal-period, batch, source and residue records. Independent risk-based testing and veterinary review shall assess compliance, animal welfare, antimicrobial resistance and human-food residue risk.

(3) A final finding requires evidence, independent testing, notice, reasons and appeal. Deliberate concealment, falsified records or grave risk is subject to sections 44, 48 and 93 as applicable.

### 98. Animal-food and feed recall, alert and support system

(1) A credible serious risk concerning animal food or feed requires a species-appropriate recall/alert to affected animal keepers, farms, shelters, veterinary facilities, feed stores, platforms and relevant public authorities, using accessible online, offline and local channels.

(2) The alert shall identify the product, species affected, batch/traceability information, risk, immediate animal-care advice, veterinary-support route, recall/return process and correction route. It shall distinguish an allegation, interim measure, laboratory result and final finding.

(3) Recall effectiveness, safe disposal, compensation/remediation and public reporting apply under sections 59 and 66, adapted to animal welfare and species-specific risk.

### 99. Animal welfare and adequate-food duty

(1) A commercial or institutional animal keeper, public authority, shelter, zoo, farm, hatchery, aquaculture facility, transport operator, animal-care facility or person supplying animals shall not knowingly or recklessly provide contaminated, spoiled, nutritionally inadequate, species-inappropriate or insufficient food/feed in a manner that causes or creates a substantial and unjustifiable risk of animal suffering, disease, injury or death.

(2) Compliance shall be assessed with species-appropriate veterinary, nutrition, welfare, environmental and practical conditions. A person shall not be penalised for an unavoidable emergency if that person promptly seeks assistance, preserves evidence and takes reasonable protective action; deliberate neglect, concealment or cruelty remains subject to applicable law.

(3) A protected complaint and independent remedy route shall be available to workers, caretakers, veterinarians, consumers and public-interest organisations. No complaint may be used to retaliate against lawful animal-welfare advocacy or evidence-based reporting.

### 100. Aquaculture and aquatic-feed safeguards

(1) An aquaculture operator, aquatic-feed manufacturer, supplier, laboratory, platform or person exercising control shall maintain traceability and risk controls for aquatic feed, water quality, medication, pathogens, residues, waste, storage and transport sufficient to protect aquatic animal welfare, environmental health and human-food safety.

(2) Independent risk-based monitoring shall address antimicrobial use, feed contamination, disease spread, water/feed interaction and residue transfer. Results, uncertainty and corrective action shall be reported in an accessible form.

(3) A serious risk requires source isolation, testing, necessary recall or movement restriction, veterinary/environmental coordination and independent review. Measures shall not be used to discriminate against small, coastal, inland, traditional or community producers.

### 101. Animal-feed ingredient and batch traceability

(1) Animal food and feed shall carry or be linked to accessible records identifying responsible entity, species/use, ingredients, additives, nutritional representation, source, batch, manufacture date, expiry/use-by date, storage condition, medication/residue information where prescribed, laboratory certificate and current recall status.

(2) A person controlling feed ingredients, processing, blending, storage, transport, sale or delivery shall preserve batch custody and composition records sufficient to trace a hazard backward to source and forward to affected animals/keepers. Records shall be available through non-digital as well as digital routes.

(3) False, incomplete or substituted feed traceability information is subject to sections 34, 48, 58, 59 and 93 as applicable.

### 102. Veterinary and animal-care protected reporting

(1) A veterinary professional, animal-care worker, laboratory worker, feed employee, farm worker, delivery worker or former worker may confidentially report suspected unsafe feed, medication misuse, animal illness, mortality, cruelty, residue, corruption, data manipulation or retaliation.

(2) The protections, interim support, non-retaliation remedies, legal aid and independent complaint process in sections 18, 27 and 39 apply equally to such a report. No licence, contract, employment, professional standing or lawful benefit may be threatened because of a good-faith report.

(3) A report shall be assessed on evidence and may not be used to target a reporter, political critic, community, producer or animal-welfare organisation. Knowingly false extortionate conduct remains subject to section 47 and ordinary lawful process.

### 103. Species-appropriate emergency feed and care reserve

(1) The public-health emergency reserve in section 88 shall include risk-based stocks and access plans for safe species-appropriate animal food/feed, potable water, veterinary support, non-plastic safe containers, mobile testing and animal-care communication for shelters, livestock emergencies, disasters, disease outbreaks and displacement.

(2) Distribution shall follow published animal-welfare, public-health and need-based criteria; include transparent inventory/expiry controls; and be independently audited. No political office, donor, contractor or authority may condition emergency animal food/feed or care on support, publicity, identity or waiver of rights.

(3) The reserve shall coordinate with local communities, animal-care organisations, veterinary expertise and affected keepers without allowing any private interest to control allocation, evidence, oversight and final review.

### 104. Online food-delivery identity, kitchen-view and price-integrity transparency

(1) Before a customer places an order, a delivery platform, marketplace, restaurant, cloud kitchen, caterer or other food-delivery intermediary shall prominently disclose on the relevant listing and checkout page:

  (a) the legal name, trade name, licence/registration identifier, verified beneficial owner and controlling person of the food business and of the platform where applicable;

  (b) the actual food-preparation business type—restaurant, cloud kitchen, home-based commercial kitchen, caterer, vendor, canteen, franchise, shared kitchen or other prescribed type—and the identity of any brand or intermediary under which it is presented;

  (c) the actual operating/preparation location and business address, service location/area and contact/complaint route, with the full verified operating address available to the competent authority and customer before purchase; a narrowly tailored protection for a home-based operator’s residential-security detail requires written reasons, alternative meaningful location disclosure, expiry and independent review;

  (d) a current direct link to the required live kitchen view or equally effective independently verifiable viewing system under section 19, together with its most recent integrity status and any material outage or limitation;

  (e) the current hygiene grade, inspection date, material violations, recall/closure status, traceability identifier, current menu, ingredients/allergen information, portion description and all prices; and

  (f) the restaurant’s or food business’s current offline/at-premises menu price for each identical item, together with the online item price and any lawful difference stated in plain language.

(2) The displayed online item price for an identical food item shall not exceed the current offline/at-premises price charged by the food business. A delivery platform, marketplace, restaurant, cloud kitchen, brand, affiliate, franchisor or intermediary shall not inflate, split, relabel or conceal an item price, reference price, portion, compulsory add-on, packaging charge or discount in order to evade this rule. A genuine change in item, quantity, ingredients, tax required by law or separately stated delivery/service must be identified before the order and may not be represented as the same offline item/price.

(3) Before the customer makes a binding order, the platform shall show a single plain-language total-cost statement containing: item price; offline comparator price; tax; delivery charge; packaging charge; platform service/convenience charge; tip; discount and the entity funding it; other compulsory/optional charge; final amount payable; and the amount/percentage of the order’s payment or value received by the platform and its affiliates. The statement shall show zero for any restaurant commission, customer-acquisition payment, discount reimbursement, referral payment or other order-linked deduction from the food business’s settlement, because such a deduction is prohibited by sub-section (3A).

(3A) A delivery platform, marketplace, affiliate, franchisor, advertising intermediary or other agency shall not charge, deduct, retain, recover or procure from the food business any commission, margin, cut, customer-acquisition charge, advertising/promotional payment, discount contribution, referral payment, listing-derived order charge or other order-linked consideration calculated from or tied to a customer’s food-item order. The platform’s permitted remuneration for an order, if any, shall be a separately stated customer-facing delivery or service charge disclosed before order placement, accurately reflected in the final total and settlement record, and never included in or used to inflate the food-item price. A voluntary, separately documented subscription or fixed non-order-linked technology service may not be structured or enforced to circumvent this prohibition.

(4) A restaurant or food business may fund a real discount from its own listed food-item price, and a platform may fund a real discount from its separately displayed delivery/service charge, only if it states the pre-discount lawful reference price, funder, conditions, duration and final customer amount. Neither may create a fictitious higher reference/offline price, hidden commission, hidden customer-acquisition charge, compulsory undisclosed add-on or false “free delivery” claim; a platform may not require, recover or offset a restaurant-funded discount through a prohibited deduction under sub-section (3A).

(5) The platform shall give the food business and customer an order-level settlement record showing the amounts collected, tax, each disclosed fee, platform/affiliate remuneration, delivery-worker payment where required by law, restaurant net settlement, discount contribution, refund, adjustment and any deduction. The record shall be preserved in tamper-evident form and be available to the affected customer, food business, independent auditor and competent court or tribunal, subject to data minimisation.

(6) A platform shall not demote, delist, delay payment to, threaten or otherwise retaliate against a food business, worker or customer for requesting a correction, refusing an undisclosed charge, reporting a misleading listing, challenging a settlement or making a protected disclosure. An automated ranking, pricing or listing decision that materially affects a food business or customer must have an accessible reason, human review and appeal.

(7) Knowingly misrepresenting identity, owner, location, kitchen type, live view, hygiene status, menu, item price, discount, charge, commission, settlement or platform remuneration; using an undisclosed mark-up; or concealing an order-linked payment is a material deception. It requires correction, refund/restitution, public clarification, independent audit, proportionate licence/platform action and referral for applicable civil, administrative or criminal process. Deliberate conduct that conceals a grave food-safety risk is subject to sections 13 and 44.

(8) The competent authority shall maintain an independently auditable public register and dashboard of platform compliance, price-parity findings, fee disclosures, revenue/settlement transparency, listing corrections, outages, complaints, retaliation findings, audits, reversals and enforcement outcomes. Enforcement shall comply with sections 32 and 43 and shall not be used to reveal unnecessary personal data, extort, suppress lawful business speech or selectively favour an incumbent platform or food business.

### 105. Delivery order integrity, consumer choice, worker fairness and dispute remedy

(1) A delivery platform shall provide the customer and food business a tamper-evident order record showing, in real time or as soon as safely practicable, order acceptance, preparation status, pickup, seal/temperature status where applicable, rider handover, delivery time, material delay, cancellation, refund and complaint outcome. The record shall preserve accountability without exposing unnecessary customer, worker or location data.

(2) A platform, food business or intermediary shall not use a dark pattern, including a preselected tip, hidden fee, false urgency/countdown, deceptive scarcity, forced subscription, obstructive cancellation, misleading consent, confusing refund path or interface designed to cause an unintended order, charge or data disclosure. A customer may cancel or correct an unintended order/charge through an accessible route before irreversible preparation, and a platform shall state any lawful post-preparation consequence plainly.

(3) A customer is entitled to a prompt, evidence-based and accessible refund, replacement or other proportionate remedy for a missing, unsafe, tampered, materially delayed, wrongly delivered or materially misrepresented order. A platform shall publish time limits, evidence standards, escalation route and outcome reasons; an automated refusal requires human review and independent challenge. A fraudulent claim remains subject to section 47, but uncertainty or a good-faith complaint does not defeat a safety remedy.

(3A) An undisputed refund shall be initiated no later than three business days after the platform receives the request or otherwise confirms the relevant failure, and shall be credited by the original payment route no later than seven business days after that event. For a credible food-safety, contamination, tampering, allergen or public-warning event, the platform shall issue an interim refund or replacement decision within twenty-four hours and may not delay the safety remedy while allocating responsibility between the platform, restaurant, worker or insurer.

(3B) A platform disputing a refund shall state the specific evidence and reasons within three business days, preserve the order-chain record and provide the human/independent review route. A refund ordered or accepted after the applicable deadline carries automatic simple interest from the day after that deadline until credit, at the published statutory rate, without preventing compensation or other remedy for additional proved loss. The rate and any exceptional extension must be clear, proportionate, publicly disclosed, independently reviewable and may not make delay commercially advantageous.

(4) A platform shall identify sponsored placement, paid promotion, affiliated listing and any material ranking influence. Reviews, ratings and testimonials shall be linked to verified orders or clearly labelled as otherwise verified; a platform shall not fabricate, buy, suppress, manipulate or selectively display reviews or ratings. A food business and customer shall have a correction, response and appeal route for a materially inaccurate review/rating action, without suppressing honest consumer opinion.

(5) A platform shall publish understandable, auditable criteria for food-business ranking, recommendation, promotion, demotion, suspension and delisting. It shall give a materially affected food business a reason, relevant non-sensitive evidence, human review and appeal. Political influence, improper benefit, lawful criticism, protected characteristic, refusal of an unlawful charge or a protected report shall never be a ranking or listing criterion.

(6) Before order placement and in the order record, a platform shall disclose the delivery worker’s minimum guaranteed payment for that order, applicable safety incentive, whether a customer tip is transferred in full to the worker, and any deduction affecting the worker’s payment. A platform shall not retain, offset or represent a tip as worker payment unless it is actually transferred as stated.

(7) A platform, marketplace, affiliate or dominant food-delivery intermediary shall not impose or enforce exclusivity, most-favoured-price, price-parity, non-discount, anti-steering, anti-multi-homing or equivalent term that prevents a food business from offering a lower price, better term or truthful information through another lawful channel, or from using another delivery service. A business may choose a genuinely voluntary arrangement only where it is separately documented, non-retaliatory, time-limited, independently terminable and does not produce the prohibited effect.

(8) A customer, food business and delivery worker may obtain their own order, menu, price, settlement, complaint, rating, safety and relevant account data in a structured, commonly used, machine-readable and accessible format, without unreasonable fee or delay. Portability shall protect other persons’ data, trade secrets and active safety investigations only to the minimum necessary extent and cannot be used to deny a correction or remedy.

(9) A delivery platform shall provide accessible app, web, telephone and, where practicable, assisted non-app ordering, complaint and support routes in relevant languages and formats. It shall not discriminate in food availability, delivery fee, delivery time, promotion, customer support, ranking or refund based on neighbourhood, income, caste, religion, gender, disability, language, migration status, political belief or other prohibited ground, except for objectively necessary and proportionate safety/logistics factors recorded and independently auditable.

(10) A customer, food business, worker or affected person may use a prompt, low-cost, independent dispute-resolution route for a delivery-related charge, settlement, rating, refund, delisting, safety report, data issue or retaliation. The forum shall be separate from the platform and food business, use trained conflict-screened decision-makers, provide reasons and accessible support, preserve interim safety/anti-retaliation protection, and never exclude access to a competent court or statutory remedy.

(11) A deliberate or repeated material violation of this section requires correction, refund/restitution, public clarification, independent audit, proportionate platform/food-business action and referral for applicable civil, administrative or criminal process. Enforcement and dispute resolution shall comply with sections 32, 43 and 46 and may not be used to suppress lawful consumer speech, worker organisation, business competition or public-interest reporting.

### 106. Delivery-platform financial integrity, resilience, privacy and concentration safeguards

(1) A delivery platform shall hold customer prepayments, restaurant settlements, delivery-worker earnings, tips, refunds and prescribed levy amounts in segregated, independently auditable arrangements that cannot be used for the platform’s ordinary debt, investment or creditor payment. The arrangement, custodian, balance, release rule and shortfall response shall be publicly disclosed in aggregate and independently reviewed.

(2) Restaurant settlement, worker earning, tip, refund and other order-related payment shall be made within the published statutory period. A platform shall not unilaterally set off, delay, withhold, reverse or deduct a payment except on recorded objective grounds, with notice, relevant evidence, human review, prompt appeal and the independent dispute route. Unjustified delay attracts interest, correction, restitution and applicable lawful consequence.

(3) A platform shall collect, retain, use and disclose customer, worker and food-business location data only to the minimum extent necessary for stated delivery, safety, recall, payment or legal purpose. Live location shall be visible only to the appropriate participant, for the minimum necessary period, through logged access, and shall not be sold, used for unrelated tracking, profiling, policing, political influence, retaliation or commercial steering.

(4) A platform shall maintain independently tested cybersecurity, outage, backup and continuity arrangements for orders, payments, settlements, tips, refunds, evidence custody, recall/closure alerts and food-safety reports. A material outage requires prompt public status information, safe alternative support routes, preservation of records, independent incident review and correction/compensation for harm caused by culpable failure.

(5) A platform shall not use a food business’s customer, order, menu, price, complaint, settlement or operational data to create, favour, price, rank or market a competing house brand, cloud kitchen, affiliate or preferred seller without the food business’s specific informed permission and a plainly disclosed conflict. It shall not steer a customer away from a restaurant using non-transparent comparative data or platform-controlled advantage.

(6) A person using telephone, assisted, non-app, low-bandwidth, local-language or disability-accessible ordering shall not be charged a higher food-item price, hidden fee or discriminatory service charge, or receive inferior complaint/refund access, because of that access route. Any objectively necessary delivery/logistics difference shall be plainly disclosed, evidence-based, proportionate and independently auditable.

(7) A platform shall disclose on every relevant listing and ranking the ownership or material financial relationship of the platform, its affiliates, controlling persons or material investors with any house brand, cloud kitchen, restaurant, advertiser, payment service, logistics provider, laboratory, certifier or promoted seller. A conflicted listing or ranking shall be clearly labelled and independently auditable.

(8) An independent annual platform-system audit shall test price parity, prohibited deductions, fee and settlement integrity, refund performance, worker pay/tips, delivery safety, ranking, reviews, discrimination, data use, conflicts, outages, recalls and retaliation. It shall publish method, findings, uncertainty, corrective action and unresolved risk, subject to necessary privacy and security limits.

(9) During a recall, closure, emergency or material food-safety warning, a platform shall promptly display the verified notice with equal practical prominence to relevant listings and orders, preserve evidence and prevent ordering of an affected item where necessary. It shall not charge for, demote, delay, suppress or sell placement around a public-health notice.

(10) A proposed merger, acquisition, control arrangement or conduct that materially concentrates control over restaurant access, delivery, payment, search/ranking, advertising, customer data, cloud kitchens or logistics shall undergo independent competition, consumer, worker, food-safety, privacy and democratic-impact review under applicable law. A review shall assess interoperability, data portability, non-discrimination, restaurant choice, worker rights, public warning access and effective remedies; it shall not be directed by an incumbent, party or political office.

(11) A material breach of this section requires restoration, payment/refund, interest, correction, public clarification, independent audit and proportionate civil, administrative or criminal referral as applicable. A platform, official or complainant retains all fair-process, evidence, review and court-remedy safeguards in sections 32, 43 and 46.

### 107. Delivery-price emergency protection, brand integrity, breach remedy and timely resolution

(1) A platform, food business, marketplace or intermediary shall not impose or increase a delivery, service, convenience, packaging or other compulsory order charge through surge pricing, dynamic pricing or equivalent mechanism during a declared disaster, emergency, curfew, public-health warning, recall, closure or other prescribed period of heightened food-access vulnerability. A necessary objectively evidenced cost change must be approved through a published, time-limited and independently reviewable rule and shall not be used to exploit a shortage or an affected community.

(2) A platform shall display, before order placement, the food business’s direct-order contact and lawful direct-order channel, the current direct/offline price for an identical item, and a simple comparison between that price and the platform’s final order cost. The display shall not use design, ranking, friction, false warning or contractual term to obscure or discourage a lawful direct order.

(3) A platform shall generate delivery-time estimates from documented, auditable criteria and shall not knowingly manipulate, selectively extend, falsely shorten or conceal an estimate to induce an order, avoid a refund or favour a listing. A material estimate change requires prompt notice, a cancellation/refund option and a preserved reason record.

(4) A platform shall not force a customer to accept a coupon, wallet credit, membership extension, gift balance or other non-cash substitute in place of a refund. A customer may freely choose an additional lawful remedy, but the refund required under section 105 shall be available through the original payment route or another accessible cash-equivalent route.

(5) A prescribed large delivery platform shall obtain and publish an annual independent order-integrity certificate assessing price parity, prohibited restaurant deductions, fee disclosure, settlement/tip custody, order-chain evidence, cybersecurity, outage resilience, refund timeliness, recall performance, ranking/review integrity, discrimination, data use and conflict controls. The assessor shall be conflict-screened, its method and limitations public, and its findings subject to correction and independent challenge.

(6) A platform, affiliate, house brand, cloud kitchen or person acting for any of them shall not copy, imitate, misappropriate or falsely associate a food business’s name, trade dress, menu, recipe representation, customer data, order history, photograph, review, location identity or goodwill to create, promote or rank a competing listing. A food business has a prompt takedown, correction, evidence-preservation, restitution and independent review route, without limiting lawful competition based on independently developed information.

(7) Following a material data breach, payment failure, unauthorised settlement/tip use or outage that creates a material risk or loss, the responsible platform shall promptly notify affected persons in accessible form; preserve evidence; provide practical protective support, correction and refund/restitution; and compensate provable loss caused by its culpable failure, subject to independent review and court remedy. It shall not conceal the event or condition support on waiver of rights.

(8) The independent dispute forum in section 105 shall issue a reasoned decision within thirty days of receiving a complete complaint, unless an independently justified extension is necessary for fairness or safety. Any extension shall be notified with reasons and review route. A platform or business that causes unreasonable delay is liable for the interest, correction, costs and other proportionate remedy ordered, without limiting court access.

(9) Deliberate or repeated material contravention of this section requires correction, restitution, public clarification, independent audit and referral for applicable civil, administrative or criminal process. Enforcement shall remain neutral, evidence-based, proportionate, appealable and subject to sections 32, 43 and 46.

### 108. Animal-product source-to-sale hygiene, welfare and traceability

(1) Meat, poultry, eggs, dairy, fish, shellfish, honey and other prescribed animal products shall be traceable from lawful source through rearing, collection, slaughter or harvest where applicable, processing, storage, transport, wholesale, retail, platform sale and final service. Records shall identify source, batch, responsible entity, animal-health/medication information where prescribed, hygiene controls, cold chain, laboratory result and recall status.

(2) A slaughter, harvest, collection, processing or animal-product facility shall use species-appropriate hygiene, humane handling, contamination prevention, waste control, veterinary oversight and inspection standards. No person may conceal an unsafe animal-product source, substitute species/product, falsify a welfare/hygiene record or process food from an animal known to present a prohibited safety risk.

(3) Inspection, sampling, closure, recall, warning and enforcement remain subject to objective grounds, independent testing, evidence custody, proportionality, notice/review and remedy under this Module. Cultural, religious, small-scale or traditional production may not be targeted discriminatorily; equal safety and welfare duties remain applicable.

### 109. Upstream agricultural, feed-input and water-source integrity

(1) A person who manufactures, imports, sells, supplies, applies or controls fodder, forage, seed, fertiliser, pesticide, herbicide, veterinary input, feed ingredient, irrigation water or other prescribed upstream input shall maintain source, composition, batch, safety, application, storage, withdrawal and disposal records sufficient to trace a material food/feed risk.

(2) Prohibited, counterfeit, adulterated, unsafe, undeclared or falsely represented upstream inputs shall not be supplied or used. Independent risk-based testing shall address residues, contaminants, water quality, soil/feed transfer and impacts on human food, animal feed, workers, animals and the environment.

(3) A credible material risk requires source isolation, preservation, independent testing, proportionate warning/recall and corrective action. Public infrastructure failure, weather event or lack of affordable compliant input requires support and a fair transition, but does not permit deliberate concealment or unsafe supply.

### 110. Species-authentication and substitution testing

(1) The independent testing programme shall conduct risk-based DNA, molecular, isotope, protein or other scientifically validated authentication testing for meat, fish, dairy, eggs, honey, feed and other prescribed products where species, origin, purity, substitution or dilution fraud presents a material safety, welfare, dietary, religious, cultural or consumer risk.

(2) A result requires accredited method, documented reference material, quality control, uncertainty disclosure, independent confirmation before a final adverse finding, relevant defence testing and a reasoned review. A test result shall not be misrepresented as conclusive beyond its validated scope.

(3) Knowingly substituting, diluting, falsely labelling or concealing a material animal-product/feed identity or origin is subject to sections 13, 44, 48, 73 and 93 as applicable, after proof and fair process.

### 111. Traditional, indigenous, local and community food protection

(1) Traditional, indigenous, local, artisanal, community, small-scale and culturally or religiously significant food/feed practices are entitled to equal respect, non-discriminatory safety support, accessible testing, translation, training, infrastructure and a meaningful role in setting practicable compliance pathways.

(2) A safety rule shall not ban, burden, stigmatise or force alteration of such a practice without clear lawful authority, independent evidence of a material risk, consultation with affected communities, least restrictive means, reasons, transition support, independent review and court remedy.

(3) This section does not permit unsafe, adulterated or falsely represented food/feed. A community, producer or consumer may not be targeted, exempted from basic safety, or denied a remedy because of culture, religion, caste, tribe, language, region, livelihood or political belief.

### 112. Food availability, anti-hoarding and emergency-diversion integrity

(1) A person with material control over prescribed essential food/feed stock shall not knowingly hoard, conceal, destroy, divert, create fictitious scarcity, manipulate inventory data, collude to restrict supply, or exploit a disaster, shortage, recall or emergency to impose an unconscionable food/feed price or deny equitable access.

(2) Any emergency stock-reporting, allocation, price or anti-hoarding measure shall have a clear legal basis, objective trigger, published duration, evidence, proportionate scope, independent review, equality audit and court remedy. It shall not be used to seize stock arbitrarily, retaliate against critics, favour connected entities or conceal official diversion.

(3) A material final violation requires restitution, release/recall or safe distribution order, recovery of unlawful gain, proportionate penalty and referral under applicable law. Essential food/feed distribution shall follow transparent need-based criteria without discrimination or political patronage.

### 113. Public nutrition and food/feed procurement integrity

(1) Public procurement for schools, hospitals, childcare settings, prisons, shelters, nutrition schemes, emergency reserves, animal-care institutions and other prescribed programmes shall purchase food/feed that is safe, nutritious, traceable, species-appropriate where relevant and compliant with this Module.

(2) Procurement specifications, tender criteria, bidder conflicts, beneficial ownership, quality tests, contract price, performance, delivery, recall, audit, payment and variation shall be publicly disclosed in an accessible open-contract record, subject only to narrowly tailored lawful confidentiality. No procurement authority may award, vary or withhold a contract through political direction, improper benefit, discrimination or retaliation.

(3) Small, local, cooperative, traditional and community suppliers shall have fair, accessible routes to compete, including lot design, transparent criteria, timely payment, low-cost documentation support and independent review. Support may not become favouritism or lower safety standards.

### 114. Consumer, keeper and worker food-safety education

(1) The appropriate government shall maintain an independent, accessible and evidence-based public education programme on allergens, ingredients, nutrition, expiry/use-by and best-before dates, safe storage, preparation, animal feed, recalls, safe donation, plastic-free serviceware, complaint rights, refund rights and how to verify a food/feed business or public warning.

(2) Materials shall be available in relevant languages and accessible formats through schools, health/veterinary services, community media, consumer groups, worker organisations, local bodies and non-digital channels. They shall state uncertainty, avoid commercial endorsement and be protected from political or industry capture.

(3) Education shall empower informed choice and reporting, not shift the legal safety duty from a business, platform, regulator or authority to a consumer, worker or animal keeper.

### 115. Independent national food/feed incident coordination protocol

(1) For a material multi-State, cross-border, zoonotic, mass-harm or otherwise prescribed food/feed incident, an independent incident coordination group shall be convened under published law. It shall coordinate evidence preservation, laboratory allocation, public warning, medical/veterinary support, traceability, recall, State/local communication and public reporting.

(2) The group may not investigate, prosecute, adjudicate, direct an individual court outcome, control all evidence or suspend the safeguards of this Module. Its membership, conflicts, decisions, data limitations, reasons, expiry and review route shall be public, subject to strictly necessary safety/privacy limits.

(3) Every extraordinary coordination measure expires within the published period unless independently renewed on recorded reasons. Affected persons, States, workers, businesses and public-interest bodies retain access to complaint, review and court remedy.

### 116. Export integrity and cross-border enforcement cooperation

(1) A person exporting food/feed from India shall maintain source, batch, destination, ingredient, test, certificate, cold-chain, labelling, recall and responsible-entity records sufficient to trace a material risk and to meet applicable lawful destination requirements without falsification or substitution.

(2) On a credible export-related food/feed risk or foreign recall notice, the competent authority shall preserve evidence, coordinate with lawful foreign and international safety channels, notify affected domestic and foreign recipients where appropriate, conduct proportionate source tracing and issue necessary warnings, holds or recalls subject to independent review and court access.

(3) Cross-border cooperation shall use published lawful channels, protect personal data and due process, and not be used for political retaliation, trade protectionism, surveillance or commercial favouritism. Import and export fraud, certificate fraud and cross-border recall evasion are subject to sections 48, 65, 73, 93 and other applicable law.

## Schedule A — Amendment and repeal of recycled-PET packaging permission

### 1. Insertion of new section 23A in the Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006

After section 23, insert:

**“23A. Prohibition of plastic in the food chain.**—(1) Notwithstanding anything inconsistent in this Act or in any rule, regulation, notification, order, guideline, standard, licence, approval or other instrument made under it, no food business operator or other person shall manufacture for food use, import, sell, offer, supply, procure, possess for commercial use, store, transport, distribute, deliver, provide or use a plastic article, plastic material or plastic component in the food chain.

(2) In this section,—

(a) “food chain” means every stage of the production, handling, processing, packaging, labelling, storage, preparation, cooking, serving, dispensing, sampling, transport, delivery, retail sale, recall and disposal of an article of food; and

(b) “plastic article, plastic material or plastic component” means a synthetic or semi-synthetic polymer and an article or component wholly or partly made with it, including recycled polyethylene terephthalate, and includes plastic-lined, plastic-coated, plastic-laminated, plastic-composite, polymer-treated and polymer-bound material.

(3) The prohibition in subsection (1) applies irrespective of an article’s claimed recyclable, recycled, reusable, compostable, biodegradable, bio-based, food-grade or other description.

(4) The Food Authority may make regulations only for genuinely non-plastic alternatives and for independently verifiable safety, testing, traceability, transition and end-of-life requirements consistent with subsection (1); it shall not prescribe, approve or create an exception for a plastic article, plastic material or plastic component.

(5) A rule, regulation, notification, order, guideline, standard, licence, approval or other instrument inconsistent with this section is void to the extent of that inconsistency.”

### 2. Repeal of the Food Safety and Standards (Packaging) First Amendment Regulations, 2025

The Food Safety and Standards (Packaging) First Amendment Regulations, 2025 are repealed.

### 3. Savings, non-revival and commencement

(1) The repeal in paragraph 2 shall not revive or continue in force an earlier version of clause (e) of sub-regulation (4) of regulation 4 of the Food Safety and Standards (Packaging) Regulations, 2018 to the extent that it permits a plastic article, plastic material or plastic component in the food chain.

(2) Every approval, guideline, direction, licence condition, standard or other instrument that permits recycled polyethylene terephthalate or another plastic article, plastic material or plastic component for a purpose prohibited by section 23A shall cease to have effect when section 23A comes into force.

(3) Section 23A and this Schedule shall come into force on the expiry of one hundred and eighty days from the date on which this Act receives the assent of the President. No new procurement, manufacture or import of a plastic article, plastic material or plastic component for use in the food chain is permitted after the date of assent.

(4) The Central Government and the Food Authority shall, within ninety days from the date of assent, publish the independently verifiable non-plastic material-safety standards, test methods, small-business support and enforcement safeguards necessary to give effect to section 23A. Failure to publish them does not authorise a plastic exception after commencement.”
