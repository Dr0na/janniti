# Food, Feed and Delivery Integrity Bill — Analysis and Drafting Record

## Bottom line

The consolidated working Bill is a broad policy draft covering human food, animal feed, food delivery, food integrity, hygiene, waste, accountability and remedies. It is **not ready for introduction**. The current official-source baseline shows that it cannot responsibly be filed as one freestanding enactment without a settled competence route and exact amendments to current Union, State, local and delegated law.

### Targeted BNS section 274 sentencing amendment — 16 August 2026

**User request (verbatim):** “Amend it and increase the sentence to minimum 2 years.”

**Refined task and route:** Draft a targeted Union amendment to BNS section 274, preserving its offence elements while changing its maximum six-month sentence to a statutory minimum of two years. This is **draft text**, not current law. The exact amendment is in `02-legal-review/bns-food-adulteration-minimum-sentence-amendment-schedule.md` and has been incorporated as Schedule B of the working Bill.

**Current-law baseline:** BNS section 274 currently provides imprisonment up to six months, a fine up to Rs. 5,000, or both. The controlling text is India Code, Act 45 of 2023, section 274, p. 94. A hash-pinned official PDF snapshot is retained in `14-legal-change-monitoring/snapshots/`; its acceptance status remains pending human review under `governance/SOURCE-ACCEPTANCE.md`.

**Draft choice:** The proposed sentence is imprisonment of either description for not less than two years and up to five years, plus fine. A matching BNSS First Schedule amendment preserves non-cognizable and bailable classification but assigns trial to the Chief Judicial Magistrate, whose statutory sentencing ceiling accommodates a five-year maximum. The provision deliberately leaves BNS section 275 untouched because the user requested amendment of the intentional adulteration offence, not the distinct knowing-sale offence. It retains the present act, noxiousness and mens rea elements, applies prospectively, and creates neither reverse burden nor strict liability.

**FSS Act interaction completed to the available primary-source level:** FSS Act section 59 has its own unsafe-food penalty ladder. The draft does not duplicate or increase that provision. It instead preserves the existing FSS Act sampling, analyst, referral-testing and prosecution channels and directs charging/sentencing to identify separate elements and avoid double punishment for the same offence. The exact current FSS Act and BNSS sources are snapshot-pinned in `14-legal-change-monitoring/snapshots/`.

**Authority and rights assessment:** Parliament may legislate criminal law under Concurrent List Entry 1, subject to Articles 14, 20(1), 21 and Article 254. A mandatory minimum sentence is a substantial liberty interference. The retained proof requirements, prospective commencement, independent prosecution and judicial trial remain necessary safeguards, but the minimum term warrants parliamentary proportionality evidence and formal legislative scrutiny before enactment.

**Remaining exact gate:** The current official BNS text is now pinpointed, but formal readiness is blocked by pending human source acceptance, official parliamentary filing-format verification, BNSS classification review, FSS Act interaction review and all-existing-matter gate conditions.

## Introduction-readiness blockers

1. Current official text has not been verified for every affected Act, regulation, rule, form, notification and State/UT/local implementation instrument.
2. Legislative competence has not been settled clause by clause, particularly for animal feed, local markets, waste levy, police/enforcement, public procurement and platform competition.
3. The Bill has no exact amendment/repeal schedule and no verified current cross-references.
4. Penalty ranges, refund-interest rate, levy-rate method, commencement dates and delegated rule limits require source-backed proportionality, fiscal and legislative-format review.
5. The revised complete food/feed-chain plastic prohibition is broader than the earlier food-contact proposal. It needs a final definition, transition date, safe non-plastic substitute standards and an exact Union/State/local legislative route; it cannot override the current recycled-PET permission by implication.
6. State/UT primary-source coverage, legislative history and judicial treatment remain incomplete. Official parliamentary procedure has now been located, but it does not determine this draft's sponsor route, House, financial incidence, Article 117 position or ancillary memoranda.
7. Required constitutional stress test, citation verification, power map, provenance ledger, impact-monitor plan and public-deliberation plan are in progress or not yet completed.
