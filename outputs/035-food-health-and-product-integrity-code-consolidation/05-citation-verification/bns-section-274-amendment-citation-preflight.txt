L51: possible legal claim without nearby citation: The amendment preserves every existing element of the offence: the prosecution must still prove adulteration that makes the article noxious and the required intent or knowledge. It does not create strict liability, reverse the burden of proof, retrospectively increase punishment, or displace a court’s ordinary fair-trial and sentencing duties. The matching BNSS amendment keeps the offence non-cognizable and bailable, and assigns trial to the Chief Judicial Magistrate because a Magistrate of the first class may not impose more than three years but a Chief Judicial Magistrate may impose up to seven years. A greater sentence may be available only where another applicable offence is proved on its own elements.
L53: possible legal claim without nearby citation: ## FSS Act interface and non-duplication rule
L55: possible legal claim without nearby citation: The FSS Act section 59 unsafe-food offence remains intact. Its outcome-based penalty ladder is not amended by this schedule. An investigation may examine all applicable law, but no person may be punished twice for the same offence. Charging and sentencing must identify the distinct statutory elements, act, mental state, harm, and remedy relied on; any overlap must be addressed by the competent court according to constitutional and criminal-law principles.
L57: possible legal claim without nearby citation: Food-safety inspection, sampling, analyst reports, referral testing and prosecution decisions remain governed by the FSS Act, including sections 41 to 47 and 59, and applicable BNSS procedure. The existing FSS Act provides four-part sampling, an accredited-laboratory request, referral testing on a material variance, analyst reporting, and a prosecution route. This Bill does not dilute those safeguards.
L61: possible legal claim without nearby citation: This is a proposed ordinary Union criminal-law amendment. Parliament’s criminal-law and criminal-procedure competence is principally engaged under Concurrent List Entry 1, subject to Articles 14, 20(1) and 21 and any applicable State-law interaction under Article 254. It should apply prospectively only, after publication and commencement.
L65: possible legal claim without nearby citation: The two-year statutory minimum meets the requested increase while retaining a five-year maximum and a required fine. Retaining the existing mens rea and noxiousness threshold is essential: a minimum sentence should not be imposed for an innocent error, unproven contamination, or a mere technical non-compliance. The separate Food Safety and Standards Act penalty framework and any offence involving injury or death require an independently verified, fact-specific charging assessment; this narrow amendment does not merge or override them.
