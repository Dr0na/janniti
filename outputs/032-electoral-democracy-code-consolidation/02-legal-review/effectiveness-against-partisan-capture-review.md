# Effectiveness Review — Partisan-Capture Risk and the Election Commission

## Bottom line

**The revised provisional Bill now directly addresses the principal structural concern, but is not ready for introduction.** Sections 16–18 replace the 2023 Act's search-and-selection framework in draft form and add Commission-wide complaint, transparency, staffing, funding and review safeguards.[^bill] Its effectiveness remains conditional on exact official-text redlining, current case-status verification, constitutional-competence analysis, operational evidence, public consultation and independent qualified legal review.

This review does **not** find as fact that the Election Commission is captured by a ruling party. That is a serious allegation requiring case-specific evidence. It identifies a structural vulnerability: the present statutory selection design can give the executive side two of three positions on the Selection Committee. The official [2024 Supreme Court order](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2024/146/146_2024_2_1501_51762_Judgement_22-Mar-2024.pdf) records that section 7(1) was challenged on this basis and that the Selection Committee then consisted of the Prime Minister, Home Minister and Leader of Opposition; the Court declined interim stay and expressly said its observations were tentative while the merits challenge was sub judice (paras. 2–4, 9–16). A current final-merits/docket check remains required before relying on the litigation status.

## What the Bill already does well[^bill]

| Safeguard | Effect on misuse risk | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Sections 8, 10 and 12 | Separates functions, prohibits undisclosed case-specific direction, requires conflict declarations, auditable allocation, records and remedies. | It regulates decisions under the Bill but does not independently supervise all Commission functions.[^bill] |
| Sections 11 and 13 | Restricts political/data targeting and the use of public resources for political reward or retaliation. | Needs a direct enforcement forum, budget and operational protocol.[^bill] |
| Section 12(1)–(1C) | Uses a plural process for the proposed Review Authority. | It does not reform selection of the Chief Election Commissioner or Election Commissioners themselves.[^bill] |
| Sections 14–15 | Requires reporting, review, transition and preservation of court remedies. | Periodic review cannot repair a captured appointment decision in time for an election.[^bill] |

## Recommended design standard

The appointment reform should not move control from the executive to a single court, party or regulator. A defensible option is a five-member Selection Committee comprising one executive representative, one opposition representative and three independently constituted members, with a rule that a recommendation needs a majority including at least two non-executive members. The statute should also require public eligibility criteria, prior disclosure of candidate material, conflict checks, recorded reasons and votes, a fixed non-renewable term, removal only through a fair independent process, and fast judicial process review. This is a **policy option**, not current law.

## Conclusion

The revised Bill now contains a direct draft response to appointment concentration and Commission-wide enforcement opacity.[^bill] It may credibly be taken forward for exact-redline, constitutional and public review, but cannot be described as an enacted or proven cure until those gates are completed.
