# Paper Ballot Voting — Public Access Guide

## What changes

After the transition date in the proposed Bill, votes would be cast and counted using paper ballots. The proposal does not allow a voter, official or party to see how another voter marked a ballot.[^bill]

[^bill]: Internal draft-text reference: `01-draft/not-ready-electoral-democracy-code-bill.md`, version reviewed on 2026-08-11. It is not an external legal authority.

## Your practical rights

- You can receive an accessible ballot and a tactile guide where needed.
- You can use assistance you choose where law permits, without that person disclosing your vote.[^bill]
- Candidates and their agents can observe ballot custody and counting under the same rules.
- You can submit a complaint online, by phone, post, in person or through an accessible local route.
- You can ask for reasons, seek review and use court/election-petition remedies.
- You cannot be targeted because of your party, opinion, criticism, journalism, protest, identity or a good-faith complaint.

## What the proposal does not do

It does not declare that EVM manipulation has been proved. It does not let any party control ballot printing, custody, counting or review. It does not remove judicial remedies or permit a voter’s ballot to be linked to their identity.

## Timeline in the proposal

| Time after assent | Step |
|---|---|
| 180 days | Paper-ballot rules and forms must be made and laid before Parliament. |
| 210–330 days | Local capacity plans, training and public dry runs. |
| 365 days | Paper ballots become the only permitted method for newly notified elections. |

## Help and feedback

The final public package must be available in English, Hindi and relevant State/UT languages, as screen-reader text, easy-read, large print, captioned and sign-language formats, low-data pages, phone support and printable forms. Public feedback must be answered with reasons and cannot be used to identify or penalise a voter.
