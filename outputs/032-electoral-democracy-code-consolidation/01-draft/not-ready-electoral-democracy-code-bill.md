# THE ELECTORAL DEMOCRACY, VOTER PROTECTION AND POLITICAL FINANCE (AMENDMENT) BILL, 2026

## Draft status

**Not ready for introduction.** This is a provisional statutory draft for review. It does not purport to amend, repeal or override any law until exact official cross-references, consultation and independent legal review have been completed.

### A

### BILL

to amend the electoral-law framework to protect equal voter participation, transparent political finance, democratic party governance, independent electoral administration and fair remedies; and for matters connected therewith.

BE it enacted by Parliament in the Seventy-seventh Year of the Republic of India as follows:—

### 1. Short title, extent and commencement

(1) This Act may be called the Electoral Democracy, Voter Protection and Political Finance (Amendment) Act, 2026.

(2) It extends to the whole of India.

(3) It shall come into force on such date or dates as the Central Government may, on the recommendation of the Election Commission of India, appoint after publishing an implementation plan, fiscal assessment and equality/accessibility assessment at least ninety days earlier.

(4) No provision affecting an electoral roll or a scheduled election may commence during a notified election period unless the Commission records that immediate commencement is required to prevent a specific, evidenced and otherwise irremediable risk to equal voting access or election integrity, gives affected persons an opportunity to be heard where practicable, and obtains prior approval from the High Court having jurisdiction.

### 2. Purposes and interpretation

(1) Every authority exercising a power under this Act shall protect free and fair elections, equal political participation, voter information, privacy, accessibility, political association, due process and judicial remedies.

(2) No action under this Act may be taken because of a person's lawful political opinion, party association, criticism, journalism, protest, whistleblowing, caste, religion, sex, gender identity, disability, language, region, income or other protected or analogous status.

### 3. Definitions

In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires,—

(a) “Commission” means the Election Commission of India;

(b) “electoral authority” means an officer or body legally authorised to prepare, revise, correct or administer an electoral roll or election process;

(c) “independent review authority” means a body established or designated by law that is structurally separate from the original decision-maker and has power to give binding reasons and relief;

(d) “reportable contribution” means a monetary or in-kind contribution for political purposes that meets a threshold prescribed by law after a published equality, privacy, retaliation-risk and fiscal assessment;

(e) “political-finance information” means information concerning contributions, expenditure, beneficial source and material in-kind support;

(f) “protected disclosure” means a disclosure restriction ordered on evidence of a substantial and particularised risk of violence, unlawful retaliation or serious privacy harm; and

(g) “writing” includes an accessible electronic or physical record.

(h) “materially adverse action” means deletion or adverse correction of an electoral-roll entry, refusal of inclusion, public accusation by an authority, investigation beyond preliminary assessment, coercive measure, prosecution referral, disqualification process, recognition or symbol consequence, withholding of a statutory service or remedy, or another action that materially impairs a person's electoral, political, civil or legal position.

### 4. Electoral-roll protection

(1) An electoral-registration authority shall not delete, correct adversely, or refuse to include an eligible person's entry except under the applicable Representation of the People Act and rules, after—

(a) individual notice in an accessible form and relevant language;

(b) a reasonable opportunity to inspect the material, respond and seek assistance;

(c) a written, reasoned decision; and

(d) notice of an accessible appeal and correction route.

(2) The Election Commission shall publish non-identifying, disaggregated data on additions, deletions, corrections, notices, decisions, appeals and outcomes, including disability, language and geographically relevant access measures where lawful.

(3) A person affected by an unlawful action is entitled to prompt correction, reasons, an independent appeal and such judicial relief as the competent court considers just.

### 5. Paper-ballot voting, count, audit and accessibility

(1) Every election to which this Act applies shall be conducted by paper ballot. No electronic voting machine or electronic device may be used to record, store, transmit, tabulate or determine a vote or vote count.

(2) The ballot paper shall state every candidate's name, symbol and ballot number in a legible, standardised and accessible form. The Commission shall provide tactile ballot guides, accessible ballot papers, trained assistance chosen by the voter where lawful, and any non-recording assistive device that enables a voter with a disability to mark a paper ballot without another person observing the selection or learning the vote.

(3) Paper ballots shall be issued, marked, sealed, transported, stored and counted under a published chain-of-custody protocol. Candidates and their authorised agents shall have equal opportunity to observe every material stage, obtain the prescribed account of ballots and votes, inspect seals and records, and make a written recount or scrutiny request.

(4) The returning officer shall reconcile ballot papers printed, issued, cancelled, tendered, spoiled, unused and counted before declaring a result. Every valid ballot shall be counted by hand in the presence of authorised observers, and the candidate-wise count, reconciliation statement, reasons for rejected ballots and recount decision shall be published promptly in an accessible form.

(5) A recount or scrutiny request shall receive a written reasoned decision before declaration where practicable and, in every case, an effective election-petition and judicial remedy. Ballot papers, seals, accounts, video records where lawfully made, and chain-of-custody records shall be preserved until the limitation period for an election challenge expires and until final disposal of a timely challenge.

(6) The Commission shall establish a published, evidence-based paper-ballot audit protocol after independent technical, accessibility and equality review and public consultation. The protocol shall include random public inspection, reconciliation, secure storage, preservation of ballot secrecy, prompt correction of clerical error and court-supervised verification on a properly supported challenge.

(7) No ballot design, count method, audit threshold or accessibility arrangement may be altered except by published reasons, an equality/accessibility assessment, prior public notice and a judicially reviewable procedure.

### 6. Political-finance transparency and protection against retaliation

(1) Political parties and candidates shall make timely, standardised disclosures of reportable contributions, expenditure, beneficial source and material in-kind support in the form, threshold and period prescribed by law.

(2) The disclosure regime shall include narrowly tailored protection where publication would create a substantiated risk of violence, unlawful retaliation or serious privacy harm; every protection decision shall be reasoned, time-limited, independently reviewable and reported in aggregate.

(3) The Election Commission shall maintain a searchable public register and audit compliance through an independent audit function that is structurally separate from investigation and adjudication.

### 7. Democratic party governance

(1) A registered political party shall maintain publicly available rules providing for periodic internal elections, membership participation, financial accountability, conflict-of-interest safeguards, accessible internal complaints and reasoned decisions.

(2) Non-compliance shall be addressed through proportionate, graduated measures beginning with notice, support for correction and an independent hearing.

(3) No recognition or symbol consequence may be imposed without an independent decision, published reasons, a proportionate assessment, and an effective judicial remedy.

### 8. Electoral integrity complaints and enforcement

(1) A complaint alleging electoral bribery, coercion, unlawful finance, deliberate voter exclusion or manipulation shall be assessed under published objective criteria and recorded reasons.

(2) Complaint intake, investigation, prosecution, adjudication and audit shall be institutionally separate.

(3) An investigation requires a documented factual threshold and shall be subject to time limits, evidence preservation, oversight and court supervision for coercive measures.

(4) The Commission and relevant prosecution authority shall publish non-identifying comparator data sufficient to identify selective or retaliatory enforcement.

(5) No materially adverse electoral, administrative or enforcement action may be initiated solely because a person made a complaint, exercised a political right, held or expressed a lawful view, associated with a party or organisation, practised journalism, protested peacefully, disclosed wrongdoing, or belongs to a protected or analogous group. A decision to act shall identify the applicable legal power, objective facts and evidence independently corroborating any allegation.

(6) A complaint, intelligence input or anonymous communication may prompt preservation of material or a preliminary assessment, but shall not alone justify a coercive measure, public allegation, disqualification, prosecution referral or other materially adverse action. A person structurally separate from the preliminary assessor shall record approval for any escalation, the factual threshold met and why a less restrictive response is inadequate.

(7) Each investigating or enforcing authority shall maintain a contemporaneous, tamper-evident case record containing intake, allocation, evidence received, material materially inconsistent with the allegation, directions, authorisations, disclosures and outcome. The affected person shall receive the record needed to challenge an adverse action, subject only to a court order that identifies a specific safety, witness-integrity or investigation risk, the information withheld, a time limit and a review date.

### 9. Anti-defection and inducement safeguards

(1) Parliament shall establish a fair, time-bound and independent process for deciding disqualification petitions and allegations of corrupt inducement, consistent with the Constitution and the Tenth Schedule.

(2) No member shall lose a seat, office or political right, and no person shall be criminally sanctioned, unless an independent decision-maker finds, on recorded material disclosed to the affected person subject only to court-approved confidentiality, that the applicable legal elements are proved to the required standard after notice, reasonable time to respond, a fair hearing and written reasons; every final decision remains subject to judicial remedy.

(3) This section does not affect any liability arising from bribery, coercion, violence, fabrication of evidence or other independently unlawful conduct. Lawful dissent, voting, criticism, political association and a complaint made honestly on reasonable grounds do not, without additional independently verifiable material, satisfy a statutory element of wrongdoing.

### 10. Independence, transparency and accountability of electoral administration

(1) The Election Commission shall publish appointment criteria, conflict disclosures, recusal decisions, written directions of general application, annual performance and equality reports, and audit responses, subject only to narrowly tailored lawful confidentiality.

(2) No Minister, political party, executive office or private person may give an undisclosed case-specific direction concerning rolls, complaints, investigation, enforcement, recognition, symbols, audit or results.

(3) A direction of general application shall be published promptly with its legal source, purpose, evidence relied upon, affected class, equality and accessibility assessment, duration and review route. A direction may not be retrospectively validated by publication.

(4) Every person exercising a material function under this Act shall file a current conflict-of-interest declaration, recuse from a matter where a reasonable observer would apprehend bias, and record any departure from ordinary allocation or procedure. The declaration, recusal and recorded departure shall be publicly reported in a form that protects personal safety and lawful confidentiality.

### 11. Data protection, transparency and anti-evasion

(1) Personal electoral data may be collected or used only for a specified statutory electoral purpose; the responsible authority shall publish the data categories, purpose, legal basis, access controls, retention period, deletion method, security measures, correction route and independent-audit result.

(2) A person shall not evade this Act through delegation, outsourcing, informal direction, data sharing, funding conditions, crisis labels or serial short-duration measures. Any departure from an ordinary safeguard shall identify the specific risk, evidence, named decision-maker, least restrictive alternative, commencement and expiry date not exceeding thirty days, public reasons, independent review and judicial remedy; it may be renewed once only on fresh recorded evidence.

(3) Electoral data shall not be used to infer, target, disadvantage or investigate a person because of lawful political belief, vote preference, association, expression, religion, caste, sex, gender identity, disability, language, ethnicity, region, income or another protected or analogous status. Machine-assisted processing that materially affects a person shall be independently audited for accuracy, discrimination and security, and shall not substitute for a reasoned human decision and effective challenge.

(4) A person who authorises, directs, procures or knowingly assists conduct prohibited by this section is subject to the same review, correction and lawful consequence as the person carrying it out. An authority shall not treat an external service provider, platform, intermediary or another public body as a means to do indirectly what it may not lawfully do itself.

### 12. Independent review, complaints, remedies and integrity safeguards

(1) There shall be an Independent Electoral Review Authority for matters under this Act. It shall be structurally separate from the original decision-maker, investigation, prosecution, audit and political direction, and shall consist of a Chairperson and four other Members appointed by the President on the recommendation of the Selection Committee in subsection (1A).

(1A) The Selection Committee shall consist of—

(a) a former Judge of the Supreme Court nominated by the Chief Justice of India, who shall be Chairperson;

(b) the Comptroller and Auditor-General of India;

(c) the Leader of Opposition in the House of the People or, where there is no recognised Leader of Opposition, the leader of the largest opposition group in that House;

(d) the Chief Election Commissioner; and

(e) the Chairperson of the National Human Rights Commission.

(1B) A vacancy shall be filled through a public call for nominations. The Selection Committee shall publish the eligibility criteria, shortlist, conflict screening, reasons for recommendation and voting record, except information whose disclosure would create a specific, recorded risk to personal safety and whose withholding is independently reviewable. No person who has held elected political office, been an office-bearer or employee of a political party, or been employed as a political adviser during the preceding five years is eligible for appointment.

(1C) A Member shall hold one non-renewable term of six years or until attaining the age of seventy years, whichever is earlier, and may be removed only for proved misbehaviour or incapacity after an inquiry conducted by a serving or former Judge of the Supreme Court nominated by the Chief Justice of India, with a reasoned public report and an opportunity to be heard. The Authority's approved budget, expenditure and any direction concerning its administration shall be published and externally audited; no executive direction may control its decision in an individual matter.

(2) The authority shall provide an accessible complaint process, assistance for persons unable to use digital systems, a hearing where material facts are disputed, a written reasoned decision, and a remedy within a time proportionate to the electoral consequence.

(3) No person exercising a function under this Act may investigate, prosecute, adjudicate and finally review the same matter.

(4) Nothing in this Act limits the constitutional jurisdiction of a court, an election petition, or another remedy available under law.

(5) A complaint or review shall be allocated by an auditable random or pre-set published method. Departure is permitted only for a recorded conflict, language or accessibility need, or an objectively evidenced immediate risk that cannot be addressed through the ordinary method; it expires when that risk ends and in any event after seven days unless a court records fresh facts supporting one further period of not more than seven days. The affected person shall receive reasons and may seek review of the departure.

(6) No officer or member may participate in a matter in which they have a financial, family, political, professional or other material interest that could reasonably raise apprehension of bias. A recusal decision, substitute allocation and any challenge shall be recorded and published in a form consistent with lawful privacy and safety safeguards.

(7) A person alleging retaliation, selective enforcement, conflict, unlawful data use, undisclosed direction or breach of procedure may seek prompt interim protection, preservation of evidence, suspension of the impugned action, correction, disclosure, restoration and any other lawful relief. An application for interim protection shall receive a reasoned decision within seven working days, unless a court records specific facts showing that a different period is required to protect a fair hearing or the affected person's rights.

(8) An authority may not dismiss or penalise a complaint merely because it concerns a politically sensitive person, body or issue. A knowingly fabricated document, deliberate false statement or obstruction may be addressed only under generally applicable law, on proof after fair process; an honest complaint made on reasonable grounds remains protected.

(9) The authority shall publish at least annually anonymised data on complaints, allocation, recusals, interim protection, outcomes, delays, enforcement referrals, equality indicators, corrective action and audit findings. An independent external audit shall test a representative sample for selective enforcement, conflicts, data misuse, inaccessible procedure and compliance with this section; the full audit and the authority's time-bound response shall be laid before Parliament.

### 13. Funding, implementation and accessibility

(1) The Central Government and the States shall provide adequate, transparent and audited resources to meet statutory duties under this Act.

(2) Implementation plans shall state staffing, training, language access, disability access, offline access, data security, rural and remote-area access, milestones and annual public expenditure.

(3) No person shall be denied a benefit, service, appeal or remedy solely because they lack internet access, identity technology, literacy, money, disability accommodation or fluency in a dominant language.

(4) No funds, facilities, advertising, information access, staff transfer, procurement decision or regulatory benefit under this Act may be used to reward political support, punish criticism or influence the result of an election. Allocation criteria, beneficiaries and reasons shall be published, audited and subject to the remedies in section 12.

### 14. Rules, review and report

(1) Rules may prescribe only technical and procedural matters consistent with this Act; they shall not create new grounds of liability, reduce rights, remove remedies, alter core disclosure duties or expand coercive powers.

(2) Draft rules shall be published for public comment, laid before Parliament and accompanied by an equality, privacy, accessibility and fiscal assessment.

(3) An independent review of this Act shall be laid before Parliament five years after commencement and every five years thereafter.

(4) The review under subsection (3) shall include public hearings and independent evidence on electoral equality, press and political freedom, privacy, accessibility, discrimination, retaliation complaints, enforcement comparators, data-security incidents, fiscal use and whether each exceptional power continues to serve a defined lawful purpose through the least rights-restrictive available means. It shall recommend continuation, amendment, suspension or repeal of any power not shown to meet those tests.

### 15. Savings, transition and relationship with existing law

(1) This Act shall be read with the Representation of the People Acts, applicable election rules and orders, and constitutional provisions.

(2) A transitional measure must preserve a pending claim, appeal, investigation or election proceeding unless a court orders otherwise after hearing affected persons.

(3) On identification of an inconsistency, the Constitution and the applicable provision of existing law prevail unless and until Parliament enacts an express amendment, repeal or savings provision.

### 16. Amendment of sections 6, 7 and 8 of the Chief Election Commissioner and other Election Commissioners (Appointment, Conditions of Service and Term of Office) Act, 2023

(1) For sections 6, 7 and 8 of the Chief Election Commissioner and other Election Commissioners (Appointment, Conditions of Service and Term of Office) Act, 2023, the following sections shall be substituted, namely:—

“6. Independent Search Panel.—(1) On the occurrence or anticipated occurrence of a vacancy in the office of the Chief Election Commissioner or an Election Commissioner, an Independent Search Panel shall prepare a list of not fewer than three eligible persons for each vacancy for consideration by the Selection Committee.

(2) The Panel shall consist of—

(a) a retired Judge of the Supreme Court nominated by the Chief Justice of India, who shall be the Chairperson;

(b) a person nominated by the Comptroller and Auditor-General of India;

(c) a person nominated by the Chairperson of the Union Public Service Commission; and

(d) a person nominated by the Chairperson of the National Human Rights Commission.

(3) The Panel shall issue a public call for nominations at least thirty days before shortlisting, publish objective eligibility and conflict criteria, invite public comments on the longlist, give every shortlisted person a fair opportunity to respond to material adverse information, and publish a reasoned shortlist with the individual assessment of every Panel member.

(4) A person is ineligible for inclusion in the shortlist if, during the preceding five years, the person has held elected political office, been an office-bearer or employee of a political party, or been employed as a political adviser.

(5) The Panel shall keep a complete, time-stamped record of nominations, disclosures, screening, comments, material considered, recusals, reasons and votes. A record may be withheld only under a court order identifying the information, a specific risk, the duration of withholding and a review date.

7. Selection Committee.—(1) The Chief Election Commissioner and other Election Commissioners shall be appointed by the President on the recommendation of a Selection Committee consisting of—

(a) the Prime Minister, who shall be the Chairperson;

(b) the Leader of Opposition in the House of the People or, where there is no recognised Leader of Opposition, the leader of the single largest opposition group in that House;

(c) a Judge of the Supreme Court nominated by the Chief Justice of India;

(d) the Comptroller and Auditor-General of India; and

(e) the Chairperson of the National Human Rights Commission.

(2) A recommendation requires at least three votes, including the votes of at least two members referred to in clauses (c), (d) and (e) of subsection (1). No recommendation may be made unless every seat on the Committee is filled and every member has received the Panel's record at least fourteen days before the meeting.

(3) The Committee shall publish its meeting notice, conflict declarations, the names and material qualifications of shortlisted persons, each member's recorded reasons and vote, and the final recommendation. Personal information may be redacted only under a court order satisfying the requirements in section 6(5).

(4) No person shall participate in the Panel or Committee where a reasonable observer, informed of all material facts, would apprehend bias. A recusal and the substitute process shall be recorded and published.

(5) A challenge to a failure to comply with this section may be brought by any voter, candidate, registered political party, journalist or civil-society organisation directly affected by the process, and shall be decided by the Supreme Court or a High Court, as applicable, on an expedited basis. The court shall review legality, procedure, equality, reasons and proportionality, without substituting its preference among eligible candidates.”.

“8. Procedure and integrity of appointment.—(1) The Selection Committee may regulate only procedural matters consistent with sections 6 and 7. It shall not consider a person who is not included in the final panel prepared under section 6, waive a statutory disclosure, eligibility, conflict, notice or voting requirement, or validate a recommendation made in breach of those sections.

(2) A vacancy in, absence from, or defect concerning the Selection Committee or Search Panel does not permit an appointment in breach of sections 6 and 7. The appointing authority for the affected seat shall nominate a substitute who meets the same independence and eligibility standard within fourteen days; the substituted process shall be disclosed and independently reviewable.

(3) Every direction or procedure made under this section shall be published before it is applied, with reasons, duration and review route. A direction may not operate retrospectively.”.

(2) Every appointment process commenced before this section comes into force shall be completed under the substituted sections unless the President has already made the appointment on a recommendation communicated before commencement.

### 17. Commission-wide integrity, transparency and operational independence

(1) The Commission shall maintain a publicly accessible, time-stamped register of every complaint alleging a material breach of electoral law or the Model Code of Conduct, including the date of receipt, category, anonymised respondent, action taken, closure reason, time taken, review route and final outcome. Publication may omit information only where disclosure would create a recorded and specific risk to personal safety, ballot secrecy, witness integrity or an ongoing investigation, and the omission is independently reviewable.

(2) The Commission shall acknowledge a complaint within two working days and issue a reasoned decision or a public progress record within seven working days. A further period, not exceeding seven working days, is permitted only on recorded facts showing why the complaint cannot fairly be decided within the ordinary period; each further period requires an order of the independent review authority.

(3) A decision not to investigate, not to take action, or to close a complaint is a materially adverse action for the purposes of sections 8 and 12 where it materially affects a complainant's equal electoral participation or the integrity of an election. The independent review authority may require reasons, disclosure, lawful reconsideration or interim protective action, but shall not direct the outcome of a vote, count, election petition or adjudication reserved by the Constitution or other law.

(4) The Commission shall publish every direction of general application concerning electoral rolls, election schedules, voting, counting, audit, political finance, party recognition, symbols, election-period transfers, use of public resources, or enforcement, with its legal source, evidence, equality and accessibility assessment, effective period and review route before it takes effect, except where a court authorises a shorter recorded period to prevent a specific and imminent risk to an election.

(5) The Commission shall submit its annual and election-period budget estimate directly to Parliament and publish the estimate, actual expenditure, material variation, staffing level and reasons for any variation. The Central Government shall lay the estimate before Parliament without alteration and separately state any amount not included in its demand for grants and the reasons for that exclusion.

(6) A designated electoral officer shall not be appointed, transferred, suspended, removed or materially disadvantaged during a notified election period except on published objective criteria, recorded reasons, notice to the officer and prior independent review; an immediate measure may operate for not more than seven days where recorded facts show a specific and imminent risk to election integrity, subject to a reasoned order of the independent review authority, and shall not be renewed.

(7) No person shall use a complaint, audit, transfer, funding decision, data system, direction, inspection or enforcement process under electoral law to obtain a political advantage, punish lawful criticism or association, or confer an electoral advantage on a party or candidate. This subsection applies equally to Ministers, public servants, the Commission, political parties, candidates and external service providers.

### 18. Review of Commission-wide integrity measures

(1) Any voter, candidate, registered political party, journalist, civil-society organisation or designated electoral officer may apply to the independent review authority for a review of an alleged breach of section 17.

(2) The authority shall decide an application under this section within seven working days and may grant interim preservation, disclosure, correction, suspension or other lawful relief. A final decision shall state the evidence, legal source, reasons, equality assessment, remedy and route to judicial review.

(3) The authority shall not review the merits of a vote, count, election petition or constitutional adjudication, except to the extent required to decide a procedural, equality, access, disclosure, conflict or anti-retaliation claim under this Act.

(4) Nothing in this section limits the constitutional jurisdiction of a court or a remedy available under an election law.

### 19. Amendment of the Representation of the People Act, 1951 and transition to paper ballots

(1) Section 61A of the Representation of the People Act, 1951 shall be omitted.

(1A) In section 169 of the Representation of the People Act, 1951, in sub-section (2), clauses (ee) and (gg) shall be omitted and, in clause (h), the words “voting machines,” shall be omitted.

(2) Within one hundred and eighty days after commencement of this Act, the Central Government shall, on the recommendation of the Commission and after public consultation, make and lay before Parliament the rules required for paper-ballot voting, accessible ballot design, ballot security, counting, recount, audit, record preservation, dispute resolution and transition from electronic voting machinery.

(3) Any provision of the Conduct of Elections Rules, 1961 or another delegated instrument that authorises electronic voting or electronic tabulation shall cease to have effect on the date specified in subsection (4), to the extent of that authorisation.

(4) Sections 5 and 19 shall come into force three hundred and sixty-five days after this Act receives assent. No election notification issued after that date may authorise electronic voting or electronic tabulation.

(5) Electronic voting machines may be retained after the date in subsection (4) only for secure storage, forensic examination ordered by a court, or disposal under a published, independently audited process. They shall not be used to conduct, record, tabulate or determine a vote.

## STATEMENT OF OBJECTS AND REASONS

This provisional Bill seeks to strengthen equal voter participation, paper-ballot voting with accessible and auditable counting, transparent political finance, democratic party governance, independent electoral administration and fair remedies through rights-respecting procedures, independent checks and effective remedies.

## FINANCIAL MEMORANDUM

The fiscal implications cannot be finalised until the evidence register is completed. Likely expenditure heads include paper-ballot printing, secure storage and transport, accessible ballot materials, additional trained polling and counting staff, audit systems, public disclosure infrastructure, independent review, legal aid, State/UT implementation support and periodic evaluation.

## MEMORANDUM REGARDING DELEGATED LEGISLATION

Rule-making is limited by clause 14. No rule may create a new ground of liability, reduce a right, remove a remedy or expand a coercive power. Exact delegation wording requires primary-source verification.
