# Electoral Democracy Code — Analysis and Drafting Record

## Status

The accompanying provisional Bill is a review draft, not an introduction-ready instrument. It retains voter protection, finance transparency, party democracy, independent electoral administration and anti-defection objectives while deliberately excluding automatic deprivation, retrospective punishment, complaint criminalisation and unreviewable enforcement.

## Anti-misuse safeguards added on 2026-08-11

The Bill now expressly closes the following exploitation paths: selective escalation from complaints; adverse action based on protected political activity or identity; off-record case direction; conflict and recusal concealment; non-auditable allocation; evidence suppression; retaliatory data use and automated discrimination; outsourced or contractor-led evasion; misuse of public funds or facilities; and delay in obtaining interim protection. Each safeguard is paired with an objective standard, a record, an independent check, a public accountability mechanism, an accessible challenge and a remedy.

These are draft-text safeguards, not statements of present law. Their final institutional form remains subject to the exact statutory/rule cross-reference, constitutional-competence and independent-review gates below.

## Appointment and Commission-wide integrity repair added on 2026-08-11

Following the effectiveness review, the Bill now directly substitutes sections 6 and 7 of the Chief Election Commissioner and other Election Commissioners (Appointment, Conditions of Service and Term of Office) Act, 2023 in section 16. The replacement design separates independent search from selection; requires public criteria, a longlist process, advance disclosure, conflict screening, recorded reasons and votes; and requires a selection majority that includes two independently constituted members. It does not give any one executive, party, court or regulator a controlling vote.

Sections 17 and 18 add a time-stamped public complaint register, reasoned and reviewable enforcement/non-enforcement, transparent general directions, budget and staff-transfer safeguards, neutral election-period controls, standing for voters and civil society, and limited independent review that cannot decide a vote, count, election petition or constitutional adjudication.

## Paper-ballot transition added on 2026-08-11

At the user's direction, the Bill now makes paper ballots the exclusive voting and counting method after a 365-day transition, omits section 61A of the Representation of the People Act, 1951 in draft form, and requires a paper-ballot rule package within 180 days. Clause 5 provides ballot secrecy, full manual counting, reconciliation, observer access, accessible marking, custody controls, recount, preservation and judicial remedy.

This is a **policy option and draft text**, not a finding that recent EVM manipulation has been established. The official Supreme Court decision in *Association for Democratic Reforms v Election Commission of India*, 2024 INSC 341, rejected the relief sought in that proceeding after reviewing the record; the decision records the Election Commission's evidence and submission concerning mismatch data, rather than an independent general finding that manipulation can never occur. The same decision treated a paper trail as important and directed limited post-result verification access. Any final Bill must fairly state that evidence and complete a current technical, operational, fiscal, disability-access and State/UT capacity assessment.

## Official-text retrieval and exact schedule update on 2026-08-11

Official India Code PDFs for the RPA 1951, Conduct of Elections Rules 1961 and CEC Act 2023 have been retrieved, hash-pinned and text-extracted. The schedule now corrects the draft to substitute sections 6–8 (not merely 6–7) of the CEC Act and to omit the EVM rulemaking hooks in section 169(2)(ee) and (gg) of the RPA 1951, as well as “voting machines” in section 169(2)(h). It identifies EVM-specific Rules and forms requiring repeal, retention or relocation.

The sources remain pending human acceptance. The exact schedule is therefore a not-ready filing artefact and must not be described as authenticated or introduction-ready.
