# THE NATIONAL UNIVERSAL FREE HEALTHCARE AND MEDICAL INTEGRITY BILL, 2026

### A

### BILL

to guarantee universal free healthcare, zero-cost medicines, surgeries, diagnostics, and essential medical equipment at all public healthcare facilities; to mandate maximum nominal price ceilings for private healthcare establishments; to enforce a minimum infrastructure radius density for ultra-modern public health facilities in urban and rural zones; to establish an AI-driven clinical audit system under the National AI Policy for monitoring prescriptions and operative procedures; to maintain a secure National Electronic Health Record System; to penalize pharmaceutical brand favoritism, doctor kickbacks, and irrational antimicrobial dispensing; to entrench the statutory right to healthcare; and for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto.

BE it enacted by Parliament in the Seventy-Seventh Year of the Republic of India as follows:—

### 1. Short title, extent and commencement

(1) This Act may be called the National Universal Free Healthcare and Medical Integrity Act, 2026.

(2) It extends to the whole of India.

(3) It shall come into force on such date as the Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, appoint, and different dates may be appointed for different provisions and different States or Union territories, provided that the whole of this Act shall be brought into force within two years from the date of its enactment.

### 2. Definitions

In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires,—

(a) "AI Clinical Audit System" means the automated, double-blind algorithmic prescription and operative procedure audit platform established under section 11 in alignment with the National AI Policy;

(b) "Antimicrobial Stewardship Protocol" means the statutory guidelines prescribed under section 16 for regulating the prescription, dispensation, and usage of antibiotic and antimicrobial drugs;

(c) "Brand Favoritism" means the practice of prescribing, recommending, or dispensing a drug, biological product, or medical device by its proprietary brand name rather than its International Nonproprietary Name (generic chemical name), or accepting any financial or non-financial inducement to promote a specific brand;

(d) "Competent Authority" means the National Universal Health Authority established under section 7;

(e) "Essential Life-Saving Medicines and Equipment" means all medicines, surgical consumables, diagnostics, biologicals, oxygen supplies, blood products, implants, and medical devices specified in the National List of Essential Healthcare Commodities under section 5;

(f) "Nominal Prescribed Price" means the maximum fee ceiling for healthcare services, procedures, consultations, and diagnostic tests in private healthcare establishments determined by the National Healthcare Tariff Authority under section 6;

(g) "National Electronic Health Record System" or "National EHR System" means the secure, unified, end-to-end encrypted digital repository of patient health records created and governed under section 13;

(h) "Public Healthcare Facility" means any hospital, primary health centre, community health centre, sub-centre, wellness centre, medical college hospital, sub-district hospital, district hospital, or specialized tertiary institute owned, controlled, or funded by the Central Government, a State Government, or a local authority;

(i) "Private Healthcare Establishment" means any clinical establishment, hospital, nursing home, day-care centre, diagnostic laboratory, or imaging centre owned or managed by a non-governmental entity or private practitioner;

(j) "Rural Zone" means any area not classified as an urban area under the relevant local government legislation; and

(k) "Urban Zone" means any area administered by a Municipal Corporation, Municipal Council, Town Panchayat, or Cantonment Board.

### 3. Right to Universal Free Healthcare in Public Facilities

(1) Every person residing in India shall have an enforceable statutory right to receive free healthcare services at all Public Healthcare Facilities without any fee, charge, co-payment, deposit, or registration expense.

(2) The universal free healthcare entitlement under sub-section (1) shall include—

  (a) outpatient consultations, clinical evaluations, emergency care, and preventive health services;

  (b) inpatient admissions, nursing care, intensive care unit (ICU) management, and surgical operations;

  (c) diagnostic tests, blood bank services, pathology, laboratory investigations, radiodiagnostics, and advanced imaging;

  (d) all over-the-counter medicines, prescription drugs, vaccines, biologicals, and essential life-saving medicines; and

  (e) medical consumables, surgical equipment, prosthetics, implants, oxygen support, and post-operative rehabilitation.

(3) No Public Healthcare Facility shall deny treatment, demand payment, or withhold any medicine, diagnostic service, or emergency care from any patient.

### 4. Regulation and Price-Capping of Private Healthcare Establishments

(1) Private Healthcare Establishments shall provide medical care, surgical procedures, diagnostic tests, and consultations at or below the Nominal Prescribed Prices published by the National Healthcare Tariff Authority.

(2) Every Private Healthcare Establishment shall display the schedule of Nominal Prescribed Prices prominently in English, Hindi, and the official language of the State at its entrance and digital platforms.

(3) No Private Healthcare Establishment shall demand any advance deposit as a precondition for emergency stabilization or life-saving care.

(4) Any Private Healthcare Establishment charging fees in excess of the Nominal Prescribed Prices shall be liable to refund three times the excess amount to the patient and pay a civil penalty under section 18.

### 5. Infrastructure Radius Density Mandates

(1) State Governments and Union Territory Administrations shall ensure that an ultra-modern, fully equipped Public Healthcare Facility is accessible within a maximum radius of—

  (a) fifteen kilometres in all Urban Zones; and

  (b) thirty kilometres in all Rural Zones.

(2) Every facility established pursuant to sub-section (1) shall maintain statutory standards regarding competent medical specialists, trained nursing staff, functional operation theatres, diagnostic laboratories, blood storage facilities, 24x7 emergency transport, and continuous power and water supply.

(3) The Central Government shall provide financial assistance from the National Universal Health Trust Fund to States failing to meet the density targets specified in sub-section (1).

### 6. National Healthcare Tariff Authority

(1) The Central Government shall, by notification, establish the National Healthcare Tariff Authority consisting of a Chairperson who is a retired judge of the Supreme Court or a High Court, and members representing health economics, clinical medicine, patient rights, and public auditing.

(2) The Authority shall annually determine and publish maximum price ceilings for all healthcare procedures, bed charges, diagnostic investigations, and medical packages in Private Healthcare Establishments based on a transparent cost-plus methodology.

### 7. National Universal Health Authority

(1) There shall be established a body to be called the National Universal Health Authority to implement the provisions of this Act.

(2) The Authority shall consist of—

  (a) an eminent public health expert as Chairperson;

  (b) Representatives of the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, National Health Authority, and State Health Departments; and

  (c) independent clinicians, bioethicists, and patient rights advocates.

### 8. Duties of Healthcare Facilities and Medical Practitioners

(1) Every medical practitioner shall prescribe medicines strictly by their International Nonproprietary Name (generic chemical name).

(2) Healthcare facilities and practitioners shall maintain digital clinical records and submit anonymized treatment logs to the AI Clinical Audit System in accordance with rules prescribed under this Act.

### 9. Prohibition of Brand Favoritism and Kickbacks

(1) No pharmaceutical manufacturer, medical device company, distributor, or healthcare enterprise shall offer, give, or promise any financial inducement, gift, travel sponsorship, commission, rebate, or brand incentive to any medical practitioner, hospital administrator, or purchasing authority.

(2) No medical practitioner or healthcare facility shall accept any inducement referred to in sub-section (1) or endorse any proprietary brand of drug or device in exchange for consideration.

(3) Any contravention of sub-section (1) or sub-section (2) shall be a cognizable offence punishable under section 17.

### 10. Antimicrobial Stewardship and Regulation of Prescription Sales

(1) No person shall sell, dispense, or distribute any antibiotic or antimicrobial drug listed in Schedule H, Schedule H1, or Schedule X without a valid prescription issued by a registered medical practitioner.

(2) Dispensing chemists and hospital pharmacies shall record every antimicrobial transaction in the real-time central digital tracking network.

(3) The unapproved or prophylactic use of broad-spectrum antimicrobials in livestock, agriculture, or aquaculture is prohibited.

### 11. AI Clinical Audit System under National AI Policy

(1) The Central Government shall, under the National AI Policy, establish an AI Clinical Audit System to monitor and evaluate clinical prescriptions, diagnostic orders, and operative procedure recommendations.

(2) The AI Clinical Audit System shall operate using double-blinded, privacy-preserving algorithms to flag—

  (a) irrational antibiotic prescribing and polypharmacy;

  (b) medically unwarranted surgical recommendations and invasive procedures; and

  (c) systematic brand-biased prescribing patterns.

(3) All flagged cases shall be referred automatically to the Independent Medical Review Board established under section 12 for expert clinical review before any administrative action is initiated.

### 12. Independent Medical Review Board

(1) The Central Government and State Governments shall establish Independent Medical Review Boards comprising senior clinicians, clinical pharmacologists, and medical ethicists.

(2) The Review Board shall evaluate flagged cases referred by the AI Clinical Audit System within thirty days, adhering to principles of natural justice and providing a hearing to the concerned practitioner.

(3) If the Review Board confirms a pattern of irrational prescribing, unnecessary surgery, or brand favoritism, it shall refer the matter to the National Medical Commission for disciplinary action and to the Competent Authority for prosecution.

### 13. National Electronic Health Record System and Data Security

(1) The Central Government shall establish the National Electronic Health Record (EHR) System to maintain the digital health history of all patients in India.

(2) The National EHR System shall incorporate state-of-the-art zero-trust architecture, end-to-end encryption, multi-factor authentication, and immutable audit logs compliant with the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023.

(3) Access to a patient's health record shall require explicit, revocable patient consent, except in emergency cases where consent cannot be obtained.

(4) No insurance company, employer, law enforcement agency, or third party shall access or utilize patient records without an explicit order issued by a competent court of law.

### 14. National Universal Health Trust Fund

(1) There shall be constituted a non-lapsable fund to be called the National Universal Health Trust Fund.

(2) The Fund shall be credited with—

  (a) allocations made by Parliament by law;

  (b) the proceeds of the National Universal Health Cess levied under section 15;

  (c) all penalties and fines recovered under this Act; and

  (d) grants, donations, and contributions from domestic sources.

(3) The Fund shall be administered by an autonomous Board of Trustees to fund facility expansion, diagnostic infrastructure, and medicine procurement.

### 15. Funding and National Health Cess

(1) The Central Government may, by law, levy a National Universal Health Cess on specified luxury taxes and corporate profits to finance the statutory entitlements under this Act.

(2) Not less than five per cent of the annual Central Budget allocation shall be dedicated to public healthcare infrastructure and free care provisioning under this Act.

### 16. Anti-Repeal and Non-Derogation Entrenchment

(1) The statutory right to free healthcare under section 3 is rooted in the fundamental right to life under Article 21 of the Constitution of India.

(2) No executive order, statutory rule, or subordinate legislation shall restrict, suspend, reduce, or dilute the scope of free healthcare entitlements or infrastructure density mandated by this Act.

(3) The draft Constitutional (One Hundred and Thirty-Ninth Amendment) Bill set forth in Schedule I to this Act shall be submitted to Parliament to insert Article 21B into the Constitution of India, providing constitutional entrenchment against legislative repeal.

### 17. Offences and Penalties for Kickbacks and Brand Favoritism

(1) Whoever contravenes the provisions of section 9 by offering, giving, accepting, or soliciting any kickback, gift, or brand-specific inducement shall be punishable with imprisonment for a term which shall not be less than one year but which may extend to five years, and with fine which shall not be less than ten lakh rupees.

(2) In addition to criminal prosecution, any medical practitioner convicted under sub-section (1) shall face cancellation of registration by the National Medical Commission for a period of not less than three years.

### 18. Offences and Penalties for Antimicrobial Overuse and Overcharging

(1) Whoever sells or dispenses prescription antimicrobials in violation of section 10 shall be punishable with a fine of not less than fifty thousand rupees for the first offence, and for subsequent offences, with imprisonment for a term which may extend to two years and fine up to five lakh rupees.

(2) Any Private Healthcare Establishment charging fees in excess of the Nominal Prescribed Prices in violation of section 4 shall be liable to a penalty of up to twenty lakh rupees per violation and suspension of license.

### 19. Compensation for Unnecessary Operative Procedures

(1) Where an Independent Medical Review Board confirms that a patient was subjected to an unnecessary surgical operation or invasive procedure, the patient shall be entitled to statutory compensation from the operating practitioner and facility of not less than ten lakh rupees.

(2) The compensation awarded under sub-section (1) shall be in addition to any criminal liability incurred under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023.

### 20. Protection of Action Taken in Good Faith

No suit, prosecution, or other legal proceeding shall lie against the Central Government, State Government, Authority, Board, or any officer acting under this Act for anything done or intended to be done in good faith under this Act or rules made thereunder.

### 21. Power of Central Government to Make Rules

(1) The Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, make rules to carry out the provisions of this Act.

(2) In particular, and without prejudice to the generality of the foregoing power, such rules may provide for—

  (a) the composition, terms, and procedures of the National Universal Health Authority and National Healthcare Tariff Authority;

  (b) technical parameters and data security standards for the National EHR System under section 13;

  (c) algorithmic governance standards for the AI Clinical Audit System under section 11; and

  (d) any other matter which is required to be, or may be, prescribed.

(3) Every rule made under this section shall be laid, as soon as may be after it is made, before each House of Parliament while it is in session.

### 22. Power of State Governments to Make Rules

(1) The State Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, make rules consistent with this Act and Central rules to carry out the implementation of facility density mandates and local administration under this Act.

(2) Every rule made by a State Government under this section shall be laid before the State Legislature.

### 23. Overriding Effect

The provisions of this Act shall have effect notwithstanding anything inconsistent therewith contained in any other law for the time being in force or any instrument having effect by virtue of any law other than the Constitution of India.

### 24. Periodic Review and Parliamentary Reporting

(1) The Central Government shall submit an annual implementation and health outcome report to Parliament detailing public facility density, AI clinical audit findings, EHR security audits, and financial disbursements.

(2) A Joint Parliamentary Committee shall review the operation of this Act every five years and lay its recommendations before Parliament.

### SCHEDULE I

#### COMPANION DRAFT CONSTITUTIONAL AMENDMENT BILL

THE CONSTITUTION (ONE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-NINTH AMENDMENT) BILL, 2026

A Bill further to amend the Constitution of India.

BE it enacted by Parliament in the Seventy-Seventh Year of the Republic of India as follows:—

1. Short Title.—This Act may be called the Constitution (One Hundred and Thirty-Ninth Amendment) Act, 2026.

2. Insertion of new Article 21B.—After Article 21A of the Constitution, the following Article shall be inserted, namely:—

"21B. Right to Free Healthcare and Essential Medicines.—Every citizen and resident shall have the fundamental right to free healthcare services, essential life-saving medicines, diagnostics, surgical care, and emergency treatment at all state-maintained healthcare facilities, and to access private healthcare services at fair and nominal state-prescribed tariffs as provided by law."

---

## STATEMENT OF OBJECTS AND REASONS

1. Out-of-pocket health expenditure in India remains disproportionately high, accounting for nearly half of all medical expenses and causing catastrophic financial distress for millions of families annually. Rural and remote populations suffer from acute shortages of modern healthcare facilities, diagnostic services, and medical specialists.

2. Furthermore, the healthcare sector is plagued by commercial distortions, including pharmaceutical brand favoritism, doctor kickbacks, irrational drug prescribing, unnecessary surgical procedures, and unregulated over-the-counter antimicrobial sales contributing to severe antimicrobial resistance.

3. The National Universal Free Healthcare and Medical Integrity Bill, 2026 seeks to operationalize the constitutional promise of Article 21 and Article 47 by guaranteeing universal free healthcare, medicines, diagnostics, and surgeries at all public facilities, while capping private healthcare tariffs.

4. The Bill mandates ultra-modern public healthcare infrastructure within a 15 km urban and 30 km rural radius, establishes an AI Clinical Audit System under the National AI Policy, enforces a secure National EHR System, criminalizes brand kickbacks, regulates antibiotic sales, and embeds constitutional entrenchment to ensure long-term sustainability.

---

## FINANCIAL MEMORANDUM

1. Clause 3 mandates universal free care, medicines, diagnostics, and surgical procedures across all Public Healthcare Facilities. Clause 5 mandates public healthcare facility density within a 15 km urban and 30 km rural radius. Clause 7 and Clause 6 establish the National Universal Health Authority and National Healthcare Tariff Authority. Clause 13 establishes the National Electronic Health Record System, and Clause 14 establishes the National Universal Health Trust Fund.

2. The non-recurring capital expenditure for expanding public health infrastructure, equipping ultra-modern diagnostic laboratories, and establishing the National EHR and AI Clinical Audit systems is estimated at approximately rupees seventy-five thousand crores over a five-year period.

3. The recurring annual expenditure for providing free medicines, diagnostics, surgical consumables, staffing, and administrative operations is estimated at approximately rupees one lakh twenty thousand crores annually, which shall be met out of the Consolidated Fund of India, Central Budget allocations under Clause 15, and the National Universal Health Cess.

---

## MEMORANDUM REGARDING DELEGATED LEGISLATION

1. Clause 21 empowers the Central Government to make rules regarding the composition and procedures of the National Universal Health Authority and National Healthcare Tariff Authority, technical security standards for the National EHR System, and algorithmic governance standards for the AI Clinical Audit System.

2. Clause 22 empowers State Governments to make rules regarding local facility density implementation and administrative operations.

3. The matters in respect of which rules may be made are administrative and technical details, and the delegation of legislative power is, therefore, of a normal character.
