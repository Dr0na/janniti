# Indian Law Research Pack — Clean Air, Water Bodies, Environmental Accountability and Single-Use Plastic Prohibition Bill

## 1. Scope and search record

**Matter and requested deliverable.** Matter 008 concerns a proposed Union Bill for India, as at **10 August 2026**, to end production, sale and use of specified single-use plastic products; prevent disposal or discharge of sewage, biological, religious, chemical and industrial waste into surface water, groundwater, recharge areas and connected drains; create accountable public duties and a public environmental-monitoring network to protect and restore water bodies and air quality; and use strong but constitutional penalties and safeguards.

**Working assumption.** The requested instrument is a Bill for Parliament, not an assertion that any proposed entitlement of non-human living beings is already an enforceable constitutional right. “Clean air and clean water for all living beings” is treated as a **draft-policy objective** and ecological interpretation principle, subject to the Constitution, existing statutes and judicial review.

**Search terms.** Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act 1974; section 24; section 25; section 26; Environment (Protection) Act 1986; Plastic Waste Management Rules 2016; single-use plastic; solid waste; bio-medical waste; hazardous waste; groundwater; water body; sewage; religious waste; Central and State pollution control board; Articles 102, 103, 105, 194 and 21; Prevention of Corruption Act 1988; Representation of the People Act 1951; Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita 2023; Articles 48A and 51A(g); Seventh Schedule water and environment.

**Repositories searched on 9–10 August 2026.** India Code Constitution collection; India Code Water Act collection; India Code Environment (Protection) Act collection; India Code National Green Tribunal Act collection; India Code Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, Prevention of Corruption Act and Representation of the People Act collections; Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC) Rules and Regulations collection and official Gazette PDFs; Supreme Court of India official judgment repository; Gazette of India route identified in `knowledge-base/sources.yaml`; and the State/UT directory registry and verification queue. Search-result material was used only to locate official records.

## 2. Executive finding and reliance limits

**Current-law finding.** The Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974 (Water Act) already prohibits particular pollution of streams and wells and establishes consent, inspection, emergency, court-restraint and appeal mechanisms. The Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 (EP Act) supplies central environmental powers and supports the Plastic Waste Management Rules, 2016, including a prohibition on identified—not all—single-use plastic items. The current framework also contains separate waste streams under delegated law. These are **current-law claims** supported below by official sources.

**Recommendation.** A new, carefully delimited Union Bill is the working policy option. It should establish a nationwide floor, a listed and reviewable single-use-plastic prohibition, a no-discharge/no-dumping rule for defined waste into protected water receptors, enforceable public restoration duties, and a cooperative State/UT implementation compact. It should not criminalise a government as an abstract entity or create automatic personal guilt for any official. Instead, it should provide public-law duties, court-enforceable compliance plans, independent audit, and personal criminal responsibility only for intentional or reckless unlawful authorisation, falsification, concealment, retaliation, or knowing failure after a binding compliance direction.

**Reliance limit.** This is preliminary primary-source research. It does not verify every State/UT enactment, local by-law, consent condition, notification, river-specific order, groundwater direction, current court proceeding, financial estimate, or Parliament Secretariat filing requirement. The working Bill is therefore expressly **not ready for introduction**.

**Marine and extra-territorial addition, 10 August 2026.** The Bill now makes its core rule express: an Indian-jurisdictional entity, including an Indian vessel and Indian-linked undertaking, must not dispose of or pollute any water body or marine environment anywhere—including the high seas and another State's waters. Article 245(2) permits extra-territorial operation of a parliamentary law, but it does not authorise an Indian agency to exercise coercive power in another State's territory or jurisdiction without an internationally lawful basis. The Bill therefore binds the Indian-linked actor and uses cooperation, flag/port/coastal-State processes and court jurisdiction over connected persons rather than asserting universal enforcement power over foreign actors.

**Existing-polluter addition, 10 August 2026.** The Bill now requires existing entities to cease polluting activities, disclose legacy pollution, finance containment and restoration, and pay proven remediation costs. It treats clean-up and prevention of continuing harm as restorative duties subject to causation, notice, hearing and review. It does not impose a new criminal penalty for a completed past act; Article 20(1) must be observed. Deliberate non-compliance with a final post-commencement remediation order is separately punishable.

## 7. Delegated law and implementation

The existing framework uses delegated rules, Board consents, notifications and directions. The Water Act official collection lists consent rules, Board guidelines and notifications, including recent consent-related material. A new Bill must not leave its essential policy—what is banned, what counts as a protected receptor, minimum public duties, core offences, proof thresholds, safeguards and review—to unbounded executive notification.

Any rules under the working Bill must be published in draft, accessible in relevant languages and formats, evidence-backed, laid before Parliament, subject to judicial review, and limited to technical standards, schedules, monitoring methods, transitional timetables and restoration protocols within express statutory boundaries.

## 9. Rights, equality, federalism, and public-interest assessment

- **Clean water and air.** Clean water and air are treated in the draft as public-health, ecological and statutory objectives. The Bill does not falsely declare a settled, separately enforceable constitutional entitlement for every non-human living being. Its interpretation clause recognises interdependence with ecosystems while preserving constitutionally protected human rights and remedies.
- **Air-pollution addition, 10 August 2026.** The Bill incorporates the Air Act's harm-based concept of air pollutant, prohibits intentional or reckless unlawful/harmful emissions, including open waste burning, and binds Indian-jurisdictional entities to that rule wherever they operate. Existing lawful emission and ambient-air standards remain the primary technical baseline; the Bill does not claim power to coerce foreign-only entities outside Indian jurisdiction.
- **Religious waste.** Regulation must be content-neutral: it targets physical pollution, not belief, worship, speech, assembly or identity. It requires accessible collection/segregation options, consultation with affected communities, neutral standards, and no selective targeting.
- **Livelihood, informal work and disability.** A total listed-product ban and strict no-discharge rule affect workers, waste pickers, small producers, municipalities, persons reliant on assistive or health-related products, and rural communities. A staged transition, public procurement, accessible alternatives, and independent equality review are necessary.
- **Due process.** “Pollution,” “industrial waste,” “groundwater body,” “dumping,” “religious waste” and “single-use plastic” must be defined. Criminal liability must demand proof of act/omission, causation and an intentional or reckless state of mind; sampling must use accredited labs and chain-of-custody safeguards; coercive searches and arrests must follow ordinary criminal procedure and judicial oversight.
- **Legacy remediation.** The Bill requires polluters to fund evidenced remediation and to stop continuing harmful activity, but reserves criminal liability for historical conduct to the law in force at the time. This preserves the constitutional prohibition on retrospective penal law while avoiding an amnesty for ongoing contamination or post-commencement defiance of a final order.
- **Federalism and local government.** States, UTs and local bodies must have funded, auditable duties and implementation discretion within a national minimum floor. A central regulator cannot unilaterally direct every local land-use or service decision.
- **Anti-vendetta and transparency.** Uniform inspection criteria, logged directions, conflict rules, random/auditable case allocation, public aggregate data, accessible complaints, independent review, and remedies for retaliatory conduct are required.
