# Working Draft — Clean Air, Water Bodies, Environmental Accountability and Single-Use Plastic Prohibition Bill, 2026

**Status:** This is proposed legislative text, not current law and **not ready for introduction or registry entry**. It includes proposed Central consequential amendments, special criminal procedure and a technical performance framework, but still requires a final competence opinion, State/UT law map, Gazette-by-Gazette subordinate-law consolidation, official parliamentary-form check, costed implementation plan, numerical local technical determinations, independent specialist review and the required quality gates.

## A BILL

to prohibit the manufacture, import, stocking, distribution, sale, supply and use of listed single-use plastic products; to prohibit the disposal, discharge, dumping, injection or release of sewage and specified waste into protected water receptors; to prevent and control air pollution; to require transparent, independently auditable environmental accountability, protection, restoration and stewardship of water bodies and air quality; to secure clean water and clean air as public and ecological goods essential to life; and for connected matters.

**BE it enacted by Parliament in the Seventy-seventh Year of the Republic of India as follows:—**

### 1. Short title, extent and commencement

(1) This Act may be called the Clean Air, Water Bodies, Environmental Accountability and Single-Use Plastic Prohibition Act, 2026.

(2) It extends to the whole of India and, to the extent permitted by the Constitution, international law and other law in force, applies in the maritime zones of India and to conduct outside India as provided in sub-section (2A).

(2A) This Act applies to an act or omission outside India where—

  (a) it is committed by an Indian citizen, a company or other body incorporated or registered under Indian law, an Indian ship, Indian aircraft, or a person ordinarily resident in India who directs, finances, arranges or knowingly benefits from the conduct;

  (b) it is committed by a person, ship, aircraft, installation or undertaking subject to India's jurisdiction or control under a law in force or international law; or

  (c) it causes, or creates a material and foreseeable risk of causing, pollution or ecological harm to a protected water receptor in India or a maritime zone of India.

(2B) Nothing in this Act authorises an Indian public authority to exercise enforcement power within the territory, territorial sea or exclusive jurisdiction of another State, or over a foreign ship or installation outside India's lawful jurisdiction, except through a lawful treaty, port-State, flag-State, coastal-State, mutual legal-assistance or other internationally lawful process.

(3) Sections 1 to 7, 13 to 24, and 28 to 33 come into force on the date of publication in the Official Gazette.

(4) Sections 8 to 12 and 25 to 27 come into force in phases on dates appointed by the Central Government by notification, subject to the minimum transition periods, publication, consultation and parliamentary-laying requirements in this Act.

### 2. Objects and interpretation

(1) The objects of this Act are—

  (a) to prevent pollution, degradation and conversion of water bodies and groundwater recharge systems into waste-disposal receptors;

  (aa) to prevent and control air pollution, including air pollution from the handling, burning, transport, processing and disposal of waste;

  (b) to eliminate listed single-use plastic products through a just, evidence-based and time-bound transition;

  (c) to protect the conditions of clean water and clean air on which human, animal, plant and ecological life depend;

  (d) to require every public authority with relevant functions to prevent, investigate, remedy and publicly account for water-body pollution within its jurisdiction; and

  (f) to maintain an accessible, independently auditable public environmental-monitoring network and information system for material air- and water-pollution risks; and

  (e) to secure equal, transparent, non-discriminatory and reviewable implementation.

(2) This section guides interpretation. It does not itself create a criminal offence, authorise a search, seizure, arrest or surveillance, displace a constitutional remedy, or convert a policy objective into an unqualified constitutional conclusion.

(3) This Act shall be interpreted consistently with the Constitution, including equality, liberty, due process, religious freedom, local self-government, federalism, judicial review and the least-restrictive effective means of achieving its environmental purpose.

### 2A. Statutory right to clean water and clean-air protection; civic and public duties

(1) Every citizen has a statutory right to timely, non-discriminatory access to safe and clean water and to effective protection against serious air and water pollution, enforceable through the duties, information rights and remedies in this Act.

(2) The appropriate Government and every public authority shall respect, protect and fulfil that right within its lawful functions by preventing pollution, securing safe essential access, investigating credible threats, restoring harm, publishing reasons and information, and providing an effective remedy for unlawful inaction or discrimination.

(3) No public authority or water-service provider shall deny, disconnect or materially restrict minimum essential access to safe water because of inability to pay, identity, place of residence, disability, caste, religion, gender, political belief, complaint, advocacy or another prohibited ground. A temporary restriction requires evidence of necessity, prior notice where safely practicable, a safe alternative supply, a prompt hearing and independent review.

(4) The appropriate Government shall define and periodically review a minimum core of safe water access on independently published public-health, hydrogeological, affordability and equality evidence. Budgetary difficulty does not excuse failure to provide an emergency minimum; progressive implementation beyond that minimum must have transparent targets, funding and review.

(5) Every person has a civic duty to avoid causing pollution, to use water and public resources responsibly, and to cooperate with lawful, proportionate pollution-prevention and restoration measures. A breach of this sub-section is not by itself an offence; criminal or administrative liability arises only where another provision expressly provides it and its elements are proved.

(6) This section creates a statutory right and duty. It does not represent that this Act by itself amends Part III of the Constitution or creates a new constitutional fundamental right. A proposed constitutional-amendment route is recorded separately in this matter and must comply with Article 368 and all applicable constitutional limits.

### 3. Definitions

In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires,—

  (a) “accredited laboratory” means a laboratory accredited for the relevant test by a body recognised by law and independent of the investigator in the particular matter;

  (aa) “air pollutant” means a solid, liquid or gaseous substance, including noise, present in the atmosphere in a concentration that may be or tend to be injurious to human beings, other living creatures, plants, property or the environment;

  (ab) “air pollution” means the presence in the atmosphere of an air pollutant;

  (ac) “climate pollutant” means methane, black carbon, hydrofluorocarbon, nitrous oxide, carbon dioxide or another substance prescribed by rule on the basis of independently published scientific evidence of material climate-forcing effect;

  (b) “biological waste” means waste containing, or reasonably suspected of containing, pathogens, human or animal tissue, blood, body fluids, microbiological material or other biological material capable of causing disease, but does not include material lawfully treated under an applicable statute and technical standard;

  (c) “competent authority” means the authority specified under section 14 for the relevant function;

  (d) “connected drain” means a drain, channel, pipe, storm-water system, culvert or other conduit that discharges, may discharge, or is reasonably likely to discharge into a protected water receptor;

  (da) “emission” means a direct or indirect release, escape, venting, burning, incineration, flaring, dispersal or introduction of an air pollutant into the atmosphere;

  (db) “environmental claim” means a representation, label, advertisement, certificate or other communication that a product, service, facility, waste stream or activity is biodegradable, compostable, recyclable, recycled-content, non-toxic, zero-pollution, climate-neutral, environmentally safe or has a substantially similar environmental attribute;

  (e) “groundwater body” means a distinct volume of water beneath the surface in an aquifer, including a recharge area, well, spring, seep, wetland connection or other hydrogeological feature identified under section 15;

  (f) “industrial waste” means a liquid, slurry, sludge, solid, gas, leachate, residue, by-product, effluent, chemical or material generated by an industrial, manufacturing, mining, processing, energy, construction, service or commercial activity, whether hazardous or non-hazardous;

  (g) “public authority” means the Central Government, a State Government, a Union Territory administration, a pollution-control board or committee, a local authority, a statutory authority, or any person exercising a statutory public function under this Act;

  (h) “protected water receptor” means—

    (i) a river, stream, lake, pond, tank, canal, wetland, estuary, coast, spring, reservoir, floodplain, watercourse, drain or other surface-water body;

    (ii) a groundwater body, well, recharge area, aquifer, subterranean channel or other underground water body; or

    (iii) the marine environment, including the sea, ocean, seabed, subsoil, coast, beach, tidal water, estuary, mangrove, coral reef, salt marsh, marine protected area and water column, whether within a maritime zone of India or affected by conduct within section 1(2A); or

    (iv) land, a drain, pit, bore, well, injection point or other place from which a substance is likely to enter a water body, groundwater body or marine environment;

  (ha) “maritime zone of India” means the territorial waters, contiguous zone, continental shelf, exclusive economic zone and other maritime zone of India as defined or declared under the Territorial Waters, Continental Shelf, Exclusive Economic Zone and Other Maritime Zones Act, 1976;

  (hb) “Indian-jurisdictional entity” means—

    (i) an Indian citizen;

    (ii) the Central Government, a State Government, a Union Territory administration, local authority or other public authority of India;

    (iii) a company, partnership, trust, society, body corporate, association, vessel owner, operator, charterer, manager or other undertaking incorporated, registered, licensed, controlled or ordinarily carrying on business in India;

    (iv) an Indian ship or Indian aircraft; and

    (v) a person, ship, aircraft, installation or undertaking otherwise subject to India's jurisdiction or control under a law in force or international law;

  (i) “religious waste” means an offering, decoration, food, ash, idol, cloth, flower, packaging, residue or other material generated in connection with religious or cultural observance;

  (j) “sewage” means domestic wastewater, blackwater, greywater, faecal sludge, septage or wastewater from a sanitation system;

  (k) “single-use plastic product” means a plastic product designed or ordinarily intended to be used once, or for a short duration, before disposal, and listed in Schedule I; and

  (l) “public servant” includes a person holding or performing a public office or statutory public function, whether elected, appointed, temporary, contractual or otherwise engaged by a public authority; and

  (m) “waste” includes sewage, biological waste, religious waste, chemical waste, industrial waste, solid waste, plastic waste, hazardous waste, e-waste, construction and demolition waste, ash, sludge, leachate, biomedical waste and any mixture of them.

### 4. National minimum floor and non-derogation

(1) The prohibitions and minimum duties in this Act are a national floor. A State, Union Territory or local authority may provide stronger compatible protection, restoration, participation or remedy.

(2) A permit, consent, licence, contract, grant, direction, funding condition, procurement term, private delegation or emergency label does not authorise an act prohibited by this Act.

(3) No person may do indirectly, through an intermediary, subsidiary, contractor, platform, label, dilution, transfer, successive temporary permission or cross-jurisdictional arrangement, what this Act prohibits directly.

(4) Nothing in this Act displaces a stricter requirement or remedy under another law, except to the extent of a direct inconsistency; a final Bill must include an exact, verified savings and consequential-amendment schedule.

(5) No trade, investment, procurement, concession, finance, insurance, settlement, contract or other arrangement may waive, freeze, narrow or deter the statutory rights, public control, safe-water access, pollution-prevention duties, transparency duties or remedies in this Act. A term inconsistent with this sub-section is void to that extent.

### 4A. Constitutional compliance, integrity of public power and anti-circumvention

(1) This Act shall be administered consistently with the Constitution, including equality before law, non-arbitrariness, due process, access to justice, federalism, transparent and accountable public power, and effective judicial review. Nothing in this Act purports to amend the Constitution or limit a constitutional remedy.

(2) A permit, exemption, classification, consent, procurement decision, subsidy, contract, direction, non-enforcement decision, data classification, corporate arrangement or other act is void and unenforceable to the extent that it is knowingly used to circumvent a prohibition, safeguard, duty, review mechanism or remedy under this Act.

(3) Every material decision under this Act that confers a benefit, permits an otherwise restricted activity, declines enforcement, changes a monitoring or restoration obligation, or concerns a connected entity shall state the legal source, facts, reasons, conflicts considered, beneficiary, duration, review date and appeal route. The decision and its supporting non-personal record shall be published within fourteen days, subject only to necessary and independently reviewable redaction.

(4) A person may challenge an act under sub-section (2) or a failure to meet sub-section (3) under section 19. A court or reviewing forum may suspend the act, preserve evidence, require disclosure, direct an independent conflict review, order corrective action and grant other lawful and proportionate relief.

(5) This section does not criminalise a good-faith, reasonably documented interpretation or technical error. Criminal responsibility requires proof of the elements of section 24A beyond reasonable doubt.

### 5. Prohibition on disposal into protected water receptors

(1) No person shall intentionally or recklessly discharge, dump, deposit, inject, bury, immerse, place, cause to enter, permit to enter, or arrange the disposal of waste in or into a protected water receptor.

(2) Without limiting sub-section (1), no person shall dispose of sewage, biological waste, religious waste, chemical waste, industrial waste, sludge, ash, leachate or plastic waste over, in, under, through, or so as to reach a protected water receptor.

(3) No industrial waste may be disposed of in, over, under, through or into a groundwater body, aquifer, recharge area, well, bore, subsurface injection point, river, lake, wetland, stream, canal, coastal water, floodplain or connected drain.

(3A) An Indian-jurisdictional entity shall not do any act prohibited by this section in any water body or marine environment, whether within India, in a maritime zone of India, on the high seas, or within the waters, groundwater, seabed, subsoil or marine environment of another State.

(3B) Without limiting sub-section (3A), an Indian-jurisdictional entity that owns, operates, charters, manages, supplies, directs, finances or controls a ship, aircraft, offshore installation, port operation, waste shipment, industrial undertaking or supply chain shall take all reasonable and effective measures to prevent a person acting on its behalf from committing a contravention under this section anywhere.

(4) A person does not contravene this section merely because waste is present at a place. The prosecution must prove beyond reasonable doubt that the person committed or knowingly authorised the relevant act or omission, that the material was waste within clause (m) of section 3, and that it entered or was likely to enter a protected water receptor.

(5) A valid emergency response may temporarily move material to prevent a greater imminent danger only where—

  (a) the response is necessary and is the least harmful practicable course;

  (b) the responsible officer records the factual basis, material, location, duration, containment and recovery plan contemporaneously or, where impossible, within six hours;

  (c) the material is recovered, treated and safely managed as soon as practicable; and

  (d) the action is notified to the independent reviewer under section 17 within twenty-four hours and is open to prompt judicial review.

### 6. Prohibition on pollution and obstruction

(1) No person shall intentionally or recklessly release, emit, spill, leak, allow to escape, burn, incinerate near, or otherwise introduce a pollutant or material into a protected water receptor or connected drain if it causes, or creates a material and foreseeable risk of, contamination, ecological harm, obstruction, toxic accumulation, reduced recharge, disease, or loss of safe public use.

(2) No person shall obstruct, fill, drain, encroach upon, divert, cover, destroy, contaminate or render substantially uninhabitable a protected water receptor, except under a law specifically authorising the work and only after independent environmental assessment, public notice, meaningful consultation, recorded reasons, a least-harm alternative assessment and access to judicial review.

(3) A religious or cultural practice receives equal protection under this Act. Regulation under this section shall be content-neutral and based solely on objectively evidenced pollution or ecological risk; it shall provide practical segregated collection, immersion alternatives where appropriate, public information and reasonable time for transition.

### 6A. Marine, ocean and transboundary protection

(1) No Indian-jurisdictional entity, and no person within section 1(2A), shall intentionally or recklessly cause, permit, finance, arrange, conceal or materially facilitate pollution of the marine environment from land-based sources, ships, aircraft, offshore installations, seabed activity, dumping, waste export, transboundary movement or any other source, whether the marine environment is within or outside Indian territory or maritime zones.

(1A) An Indian-jurisdictional entity shall ensure that its contract, charter, waste manifest, procurement term, corporate direction, supply-chain arrangement and operational procedure prohibits conduct under sub-section (1), requires contemporaneous waste records and safe-receipt evidence, and provides a protected route for workers and crew to report a proposed or actual contravention.

(2) The Central Government shall take reasonable, timely and effective measures, consistent with the Constitution and international law, to prevent, reduce, control, investigate and remedy marine pollution arising from activities under India's jurisdiction or control, including pollution that may spread beyond a maritime zone of India.

(3) Where credible evidence indicates that pollution originating outside India is causing, or creates a material and foreseeable risk of causing, serious harm to a protected water receptor in India or a maritime zone of India, the Central Government shall—

  (a) record and publish a reasoned assessment, subject to narrowly tailored safety, diplomatic and personal-data redactions;

  (b) notify and seek cooperation from the relevant State, flag State, coastal State, international organisation or treaty mechanism, as applicable;

  (c) offer and seek evidence-sharing, joint monitoring, containment and remediation through lawful channels; and

  (d) report material unresolved harm and action taken to Parliament at least annually.

(4) Nothing in this section permits a public authority to impose a penalty, seizure, closure or other coercive measure upon a foreign State, foreign public authority, foreign ship or person outside India's jurisdiction. It does not limit a court or other competent Indian authority from exercising lawful jurisdiction over an Indian-jurisdictional entity or another person within section 1(2A).

### 6B. Air-pollution prevention and transboundary clean-air protection

(1) No person shall intentionally or recklessly cause, permit or materially facilitate an emission that—

  (a) exceeds an applicable emission, ambient-air, fuel, vehicle, operational or other air-quality standard fixed by law;

  (b) causes, or creates a material and foreseeable risk of causing, serious injury to human health, other living creatures, plants, property or the environment; or

  (c) is made by open burning, incineration, flaring or destruction of waste except in a facility and manner expressly authorised by law, continuously monitored, independently audited and demonstrated not to cause the harm described in clause (b).

(2) An Indian-jurisdictional entity shall not do any act prohibited by sub-section (1) in India, in a maritime zone of India, on the high seas, in the airspace over them, or in the territory, airspace or maritime environment of another State.

(3) An Indian-jurisdictional entity that owns, operates, charters, manages, supplies, directs, finances or controls a ship, aircraft, offshore installation, industrial undertaking, waste shipment or supply chain shall take all reasonable and effective measures to prevent a person acting on its behalf from committing a contravention under this section anywhere.

(4) The Central Government and every relevant public authority shall maintain, within its lawful functions, a transparent air-quality and emissions monitoring system; publish standards, source and ambient monitoring, enforcement aggregates, exception records and health-risk notices; and take prompt, proportionate remedial action when a serious breach is credibly indicated.

(5) This section does not authorise a public authority to exercise coercive power against a foreign State or foreign-only entity outside India's lawful jurisdiction. It does not limit lawful action against an Indian-jurisdictional entity or another person within section 1(2A).

### 6C. Water as a public right and prohibition of commercial commodification

(1) Water, including surface water, groundwater, recharge water, rainwater captured from a public source and water supplied for essential domestic, public-health, sanitation, livelihood or ecological use, is a common life-sustaining resource held subject to the statutory rights and duties in this Act. It shall not be treated as an object of commercial speculation or profit-making sale.

(2) No person shall sell, trade, broker, speculate in, hoard for sale, securitise, acquire for resale, or create a private transferable entitlement to water or access to water as a commodity. No public authority shall privatise, alienate or grant an exclusive commercial right over a public water source or essential water service in a manner inconsistent with this Act.

(3) A public, community, cooperative or regulated service provider may recover only transparent, reasonable and independently audited costs of treatment, testing, storage, delivery, maintenance, safe containers and necessary service administration. Such recovery shall not include a price for water itself, an extractive profit, discriminatory charge, arbitrary disconnection, speculative return or a charge that denies the minimum essential access protected by section 2A.

(4) A person may supply water in an emergency medical, disaster-response, remote-access or other prescribed essential setting only under a publicly recorded, non-discriminatory and reviewable arrangement that secures safe quality, affordability, return or safe management of containers, and continuity of essential access. This subsection does not permit a commercial exception or private ownership of a public water source.

(5) Existing commercial water-extraction, bottling, trading or retail arrangements shall transition within twenty-four months to a public, community, cooperative or strictly cost-recovery service model approved under a published plan. The plan shall protect workers and small operators, prevent windfall compensation or concealed asset transfer, preserve water quality and access, and be subject to public consultation and judicial review.

(6) This section does not prohibit personal non-commercial sharing of lawfully obtained water, community water stewardship, or a lawful payment for the distinct cost of a safe service under sub-section (3).

### 6D. Public trust, equitable allocation and ecological flows

(1) The appropriate Government holds public water sources in trust for present and future generations. No title, licence, contract, concession, allocation or other arrangement under this Act creates private ownership of water, a perpetual entitlement to extract water, or a right to use water as collateral.

(2) In a declared drought, scarcity, contamination event or other material access emergency, water allocation shall follow this order of priority:—

  (a) minimum essential drinking water, sanitation and hygiene;

  (b) healthcare facilities, schools, disability-accessible services and emergency response;

  (c) ecological flows, aquifer recharge, wetlands and other functions necessary to sustain a protected water receptor;

  (d) subsistence livelihoods, including small farmers, fishers, pastoralists and forest-dependent communities; and

  (e) other uses, including non-essential commercial use.

(3) A restriction, allocation or rationing measure under sub-section (2) must be evidence-based, non-discriminatory, publicly notified, time-limited, regularly reviewed, subject to an accessible grievance route and use the least restrictive effective means. It shall not shift a disproportionate burden to a marginalised, tribal, rural, coastal or low-income community.

(4) Minimum essential household and community water access shall be provided without charge. A charge for use beyond that minimum may recover only the lawful cost described in section 6C(3), must be progressively structured to protect low-income users and may not include a profit margin.

### 6E. Large withdrawals, commercial diversion and water-stress protection

(1) Every withdrawal prescribed as large by rule, including withdrawal for industry, bottling, mining, construction, commercial agriculture or another commercial activity, shall be continuously metered, publicly reported and independently audited. The register under section 15B shall record the source, quantity, purpose, location, quality impact, recharge impact, recipient and compliance status.

(2) No person shall make or arrange a bulk export, inter-basin diversion, virtual-water extraction through a high-water-use activity, or comparable commercial transfer where it would impair the priorities in section 6D, ecological flows, recharge, essential access or the rights of an affected community. A proposed transfer may proceed only after an independently published assessment, meaningful consultation, a less-harm alternatives analysis, financial assurance, public reasons and judicial review.

(3) Where independent evidence identifies a groundwater body, water body or catchment as critically stressed, the competent authority shall phase out or reduce non-essential high-water-use commercial activity on a published, proportionate and time-bound plan. The plan shall protect workers and small operators, provide just-transition support, forbid discrimination and remain open to review under section 19.

(4) A person who knowingly falsifies a withdrawal meter, meter data, extraction record, allocation record or water-transfer document to conceal a contravention of this section commits an offence under section 24A or another applicable law.

### 7. Listed single-use plastic prohibition

(1) No person shall manufacture, import, stock, distribute, sell, supply, offer for sale, advertise for supply, or use a single-use plastic product listed in Schedule I.

(2) The prohibition in sub-section (1) applies to online, offline, wholesale, retail, institutional, promotional, public-procurement and supply-chain activity.

(3) Schedule I may be amended only by rules made after—

  (a) publication of the proposed item, evidence of avoidable environmental harm and reasonably available alternatives;

  (b) a public consultation of not less than ninety days, including accessible and language-appropriate participation for affected workers, waste pickers, small enterprises, persons with disabilities, healthcare providers, consumers and States and Union Territories;

  (c) an independently published assessment of health, safety, disability/accessibility, cost, informal-work, waste-system and equality impacts;

  (d) a transition period of not less than twelve months, unless an independently evidenced imminent and serious harm requires a shorter period; and

  (e) laying before Parliament and availability of judicial review.

(4) A narrowly defined exception for an emergency medical, public-health, disability-accessibility, safety-critical or disaster-response use may be granted only by rule, for a named product and purpose, with published reasons, an alternatives plan, independent audit, anti-diversion controls, annual review and a fixed expiry of not more than two years. It shall not become a general commercial exemption.

(5) A rule under sub-section (3) or (4) may clarify or narrow a product category only. It shall not remove the prohibition for a class of single-use plastic product, extend an exception beyond its fixed expiry, or permit disposal of plastic waste into a protected water receptor.

### 8. Public authority duty to protect and restore water bodies

(1) Every public authority has a binding statutory duty, within its lawful functions and control, to prevent pollution and degradation of every protected water receptor under its jurisdiction or functional responsibility and to secure the minimum essential safe-water access required by section 2A. Budgetary difficulty does not excuse failure to provide that minimum; it may be considered only in fixing a transparent, court-reviewable timetable for measures beyond the minimum.

(2) The duty includes—

  (a) mapping, uniquely identifying and publishing water bodies, groundwater bodies, recharge areas, outfalls, disposal sites and pollution-risk sources;

  (b) maintaining sewage, septage, waste collection, treatment, safe-disposal and spill-response capacity sufficient for the population and activities served;

  (c) monitoring water quality and relevant air impacts at published intervals using validated methods and accredited laboratories;

  (d) promptly investigating credible pollution complaints and publishing a reasoned outcome;

  (e) preventing unauthorised dumping and restoring contaminated or obstructed sites under an enforceable remediation plan;

  (f) providing accessible, non-discriminatory collection and treatment options for religious waste, household waste, sanitation waste and small-generator waste; and

  (g) publishing quarterly data, budgets, contracts, compliance plans, sampling results, enforcement aggregates and restoration progress, subject only to necessary and reviewable safety or personal-data redactions; and

  (h) where responsible for a maritime zone of India, maintaining marine-pollution prevention, port-reception, spill-response, coastal-ecosystem protection and transboundary-cooperation capability proportionate to the risk and function concerned; and

  (i) where responsible for air-quality functions, maintaining pollution-prevention, monitoring, emergency-response, public-warning and health-protection capacity proportionate to the risk and function concerned.

(3) This duty is enforceable through the compliance and remedy process in section 19. It does not make every public servant criminally liable for a systemic failure outside that person's legal authority, resources or ability to prevent; personal liability arises only under section 24.

### 8A. Binding duty to secure safe water and safe surroundings

(1) The appropriate Government shall ensure that every person can obtain minimum essential safe water without discrimination and that water points, collection systems, sanitation facilities, schools, healthcare facilities, community access points and their immediate surroundings are maintained in a clean, safe and accessible condition.

(2) The duty includes regular quality testing, publicly available results, safe sanitation and drainage, accessible design, prompt repair, emergency alternative supply, complaint routes and remedial action where contamination or unsafe conditions are credibly indicated.

(3) A public authority may contract for a technical or delivery function, but may not delegate or disclaim its ultimate duty under this section. Any contract shall comply with sections 4, 4A, 6C, 14A and 15A and shall be publicly disclosed.

### 8B. Universal sanitation, sewage control and safe wastewater reuse

(1) The appropriate Government shall provide or secure universal, accessible and safely managed sanitation, sewage, septage and wastewater collection, conveyance, treatment, monitoring and disposal systems sufficient for the population and activities served.

(2) Untreated sewage, septage or wastewater shall not be discharged into a protected water receptor, connected drain, open land, groundwater body, recharge area or another place from which it is likely to enter a protected water receptor.

(3) Where technically and health-safely feasible, a public authority and a prescribed large water user shall prioritise verified safe treated-wastewater reuse for non-drinking industrial, construction, landscaping, flushing and comparable purposes before a fresh-water withdrawal. A reuse requirement shall not compromise public health, worker safety, soil, groundwater, food safety, ecological flows or essential access.

(4) A public authority shall publish treatment capacity, inflow/outflow, quality results, failures, overflows, repair plans, reuse volumes and enforcement outcomes. A failure of capacity is not a permission to pollute; it triggers an emergency containment and remedial plan under sections 8A, 11A and 19.

### 8C. Rainwater, local recharge and water-sensitive public works

(1) A new building, campus, industrial site, road, public work or other prescribed development shall incorporate feasible rainwater capture, local recharge, permeable drainage, water conservation and pollution-prevention measures appropriate to its site and scale.

(2) A requirement under sub-section (1) shall be imposed only after site-specific hydrogeological evidence, accessibility and safety assessment, public notice where material, and a finding that it will not contaminate groundwater, increase flooding, displace a community, impair a wetland, forest, water body or customary water practice, or transfer disproportionate costs to low-income users.

(3) Public works must use a water-sensitive design that protects natural drainage, floodplains, springs, wetlands, catchments and recharge areas. A deviation requires published reasons, a less-harm alternatives assessment, restoration plan, financial assurance and review under section 19.

### 8D. Gender-responsive and dignity-centred water security

(1) The appropriate Government shall design and maintain water, sanitation and hygiene services so that women, girls, transgender and gender-diverse persons, children, older persons, persons with disabilities and caregivers have safe, nearby, accessible, affordable and dignified access without avoidable unpaid collection, waiting, harassment or safety burden.

(2) Schools, healthcare facilities, workplaces, shelters, public institutions and community access points shall provide safe, accessible sanitation and menstrual-hygiene facilities, adequate water, privacy, lighting, disposal arrangements and a complaint route.

(3) A water, sanitation, source-area, scarcity or service decision shall assess and publish foreseeable gender, care-work, safety, disability and livelihood impacts and measures to prevent disproportionate burden.

### 8E. Repair, maintenance and service-continuity duty

(1) Every public water utility, local authority and prescribed service provider shall maintain an asset register and publish at least quarterly its leakage, non-revenue water, downtime, service interruption, repair backlog, response time, maintenance expenditure, quality failure and corrective-action data.

(2) A provider shall give timely notice of a planned interruption, maintain accessible emergency supply for an unplanned material interruption, and complete repair within a published service standard unless objectively impossible. A persistent or serious failure triggers a corrective plan, independent audit and a remedy under sections 17A and 19.

(3) Maintenance data, funding and contractor arrangements shall be public and subject to sections 4A, 14A and 15A. A provider shall not use manufactured scarcity, avoidable leakage or deferred maintenance to justify higher charges, commodification or restriction of minimum essential access.

### 8F. Point-of-use safety and tanker supply control

(1) A public authority shall test water at representative points of use, including household distribution points, community sources, schools, healthcare facilities, public institutions and tanker-delivery points, in addition to source and treatment-plant testing. Results, failures, corrective action and safe alternative supply shall be published promptly in accessible form.

(2) No person may supply water by tanker or other mobile distribution service unless the source, quantity, quality, vehicle, delivery route, service charge, recipient category and lawful authority are recorded in a public, tamper-evident register. A supplier shall maintain safe containers, periodic independent quality testing and a complaint route.

(3) A tanker or mobile supplier shall not extract water unlawfully, conceal a source, falsify quantity or quality records, impose a profit-making water price, supply unsafe water, or deny essential access on a discriminatory ground. The appropriate Government shall suspend or cancel a non-compliant authorisation after notice, hearing and review, except for immediate temporary action necessary to prevent serious harm.

### 8G. Water, sanitation and waste-worker protection

(1) A public authority, utility, contractor and employer shall provide water, sanitation, wastewater and waste workers with safe equipment, training, protective gear, health monitoring, fair conditions, insurance, grievance protection and a right to refuse an objectively unsafe task without retaliation.

(2) No person shall require or permit manual handling of sewage, septage, hazardous waste, contaminated sludge or another dangerous material in a manner prohibited by applicable law or without the safety measures, supervision and emergency support required by law.

(3) Procurement, transition and restoration plans shall identify affected workers, provide retraining and social protection, and prohibit the transfer of hazardous or unpaid work to informal workers, waste pickers, women, children or marginalised communities.

### 9. National clean-water and plastic-transition plan

(1) Within twelve months after the commencement of this section, the Central Government shall publish a five-year National Clean-Water and Plastic-Transition Plan, made after consultation with States, Union Territories, local authorities, affected communities, workers, waste pickers, public-health and hydrogeology experts, disability organisations, small producers, farmers, fishers and civil-society organisations.

(2) The Plan shall state—

  (a) measurable national and jurisdiction-specific air-quality, pollution-prevention, collection, treatment, restoration and single-use-plastic phase-out targets;

  (b) funded minimum service standards and a transparent distribution formula, including a minimum essential safe-water standard and priority safeguards for children, schools, healthcare facilities, persons with disabilities, informal settlements, Scheduled Tribes, forest-dependent communities, fishers and other communities facing disproportionate access risk;

  (c) transition support, retraining, social protection and procurement for workers and small enterprises affected by Schedule I;

  (d) technical standards, data-quality controls, public dashboards and independent audit arrangements;

  (e) measures against pollution displacement to marginalised, rural, tribal, coastal or low-income communities; and

  (f) an annual report to Parliament and a public correction process.

(3) The Plan neither creates an offence nor authorises a coercive power not expressly granted by this Act or another law.

### 10. State, UT and local implementation plans

(1) Every State and Union Territory shall prepare, after local consultation, a five-year implementation plan consistent with section 9 within eighteen months after the commencement of this section.

(2) A plan must allocate named responsibilities, funding, treatment capacity, emergency response, water-body mapping, community access, grievance routes, public reporting and independent audit.

(3) The Central Government may issue written, generally applicable technical guidance after consultation; it shall not issue an undisclosed case-specific direction concerning an investigation, prosecution, adjudication, complaint, grant or contract.

(4) A State, Union Territory or local authority may provide stronger compatible protection, participation, access or remedy. The Central Government shall not impose an unfunded implementation requirement; any new mandatory national minimum standard must be accompanied by a published funding and administrative-capacity assessment and a fair support arrangement.

### 11. Water-body protection zones and restoration orders

(1) A competent authority may designate a water-body protection zone only after a published hydrogeological/ecological assessment, notice to affected persons, a hearing, recorded reasons, maps, a proportionality assessment and an appeal route.

(2) Designation must specify the protected receptor, boundary, risk, prohibited conduct, mitigation, duration, review date, compensation/relocation safeguards where legally required, and contact point for complaints.

(3) A court or competent adjudicatory body may issue a restoration order against a proven responsible person after notice and hearing. The order may require cessation, containment, clean-up, monitoring, restoration, community information, medical/public-health support where evidenced, and payment of proportionate remediation cost.

(4) No restoration order may be used to dispossess, displace or penalise a person who did not cause or knowingly facilitate the harm, or to bypass land, forest, tribal, labour, housing, compensation or due-process protections under another law.

### 11A. Existing polluting activities, cessation and legacy remediation

(1) An existing entity that, on the commencement of this section, carries on an activity that causes or is likely to cause a contravention of section 5, 6, 6A or 6B shall cease the polluting activity within the period specified in a compliance order under this section.

(2) Within ninety days after the commencement of this section, every existing entity reasonably identified by the competent authority as a significant actual or potential source of water, marine or air pollution shall submit a sworn pollution and waste inventory stating—

  (a) the source, quantity, composition, location, duration and pathway of each emission, discharge, waste stream, stockpile, disposal site and contaminated area;

  (b) every permit, consent, licence, contract, manifest, monitoring record, accident record, laboratory report and regulatory direction relevant to it;

  (c) an independently reviewed cessation, containment, treatment, removal and restoration proposal; and

  (d) the financial assurance, insurance, reserve or other lawful means available to implement that proposal.

(3) After notice, disclosure of material evidence and an opportunity to be heard, the competent authority shall issue a reasoned compliance order. The order shall require cessation of the polluting activity and may require containment, safe removal, treatment, restoration, monitoring, financial assurance, public disclosure, health-risk notice and payment of proven remediation costs.

(4) Where an objectively evidenced imminent and serious risk to life, health, a protected water receptor, the marine environment or air quality cannot safely await an ordinary hearing, the competent authority may order immediate temporary cessation or containment. It shall give written reasons, use the least restrictive effective measure, provide an urgent hearing within seven days and place the action before the reviewing forum under section 19.

(5) An existing entity may continue only an activity shown, by independent evidence and subject to public scrutiny, not to cause or be likely to cause a contravention of section 5, 6, 6A or 6B. Economic hardship, prior investment, a legacy permit, a contract, a licence or the absence of a convenient alternative does not authorise continued pollution.

(6) A compliance order shall set a restoration timetable and a measurable completion standard. It may not be extended except by a reasoned public decision, independent technical review, a hearing for affected communities and approval by the reviewing forum; no extension may permit continuing serious harm.

(7) A person who caused, knowingly permitted, directed, financed or materially facilitated contamination, pollution or disposal before commencement remains liable for remediation, restoration and cost recovery under this section where a competent authority or court finds, on evidence and after fair process, a material causal connection to existing or continuing harm.

(8) Sub-section (7) creates a restorative and preventive duty; it does not impose a criminal penalty for conduct occurring before this Act came into force. Criminal liability for past conduct may be pursued only under a law that applied at the time of the conduct, with all applicable defences, limitation rules and fair-trial rights.

(9) Failure to comply with a final compliance or restoration order under this section is a continuing contravention and may be enforced under this Act and other applicable law.

### 11B. Financial assurance, corporate continuity and restoration priority

(1) Before commencing or materially expanding an activity prescribed as high-risk by rule, an Indian-jurisdictional entity shall provide financial assurance adequate, on an independently assessed basis, to contain, remove, treat, restore and monitor a reasonably foreseeable pollution incident.

(2) Financial assurance may consist only of an insurance policy, escrow arrangement, performance bond, bank guarantee, parent-company guarantee, restoration trust or other instrument approved by the competent authority after conflict screening and public disclosure of its material terms.

(3) The competent authority shall reassess the adequacy of financial assurance annually and after any material change in risk, ownership, technology, waste stream or pollution event.

(4) A transfer of shares, assets, control, contract, vessel, installation or licence does not discharge any accrued restoration obligation. A successor, transferee or controlling person is liable to the extent provided by law where the transfer was made to evade, frustrate or materially impair remediation.

(5) An entity shall not distribute dividends, transfer material assets to a related party, pay extraordinary management compensation or undertake a comparable value-extraction transaction while a final remediation order remains unfunded, except with approval of the reviewing forum after notice to affected communities and creditors.

### 11C. Waste chain of custody, shipment and export controls

(1) Every commercial or institutional waste stream prescribed by rule shall have a tamper-evident, interoperable and publicly auditable chain-of-custody record from generation through transport, treatment, recovery and final safe disposition.

(2) An Indian-jurisdictional entity shall not export, arrange export of, transfer across a border, transship or cause another person to receive waste unless—

  (a) the movement and receiving facility are lawful under all applicable domestic and international law;

  (b) an independent, conflict-screened verification confirms that the receiving facility has safe treatment capacity and will not transfer disproportionate health or environmental harm to another community;

  (c) the entity retains a traceable manifest, receiving-facility certificate, treatment record and final-disposition record for the prescribed period; and

  (d) the entity accepts financial responsibility for return, containment and remediation if the movement or treatment fails or the records are false.

(3) No person may use an intermediary, free-trade zone, transshipment, relabelling, dilution, recycled-content claim, export contract or corporate restructuring to evade this section.

### 11D. Continuous monitoring, data integrity and public access

(1) A high-risk entity prescribed by rule shall install, operate and maintain continuous monitoring of relevant emissions, discharges, waste streams and pollution-control equipment, using calibrated devices and published quality-assurance methods.

(2) Monitoring data shall be transmitted to a secure, tamper-evident public system at intervals prescribed by rule; the system shall publish source data, methods, downtime, calibration, uncertainty, exceedances, corrective action and enforcement outcome, subject only to necessary and independently reviewable redaction.

(3) The competent authority shall conduct unannounced, independently allocated inspections and random split-sample testing. The entity may obtain independent retesting under section 12.

(4) A monitoring device failure, data gap or suspected tampering triggers immediate notification, precautionary containment and an independent inspection. It does not create a presumption of compliance.

(5) Subject to the capacity gates in section 29B, the Central Government shall coordinate with the competent authorities a phased national public environmental-monitoring network. The network shall use a published, evidence-based coverage plan that gives priority to materially affected communities, high-risk sources, pollution hotspots, underserved areas, water sources, vulnerable ecosystems and representative background locations; it shall not be limited to locations selected by an operator or political officeholder.

(6) The network shall publish validated, interoperable data on material ambient-air pollutants, relevant emissions, water-quality indicators, source and treatment performance, sampling methods, calibration, uncertainty, outages, corrective action and enforcement outcome. The published data shall be timely, machine-readable and accessible, subject only to necessary and independently reviewable redaction under this Act.

(7) The Central Government shall publish a costed implementation plan, including coverage, responsible bodies, laboratory and maintenance capacity, quality assurance, accessibility, privacy, public-warning arrangements, independent audit and correction dates, before each phase commences. The plan may not be used to delay an existing statutory pollution-control duty or an emergency protection measure.

### 11E. Community protection, health response and restoration participation

(1) Where credible evidence indicates material exposure to pollution, the appropriate Government shall promptly provide accessible public information, safe water or clean-air protection where needed, exposure assessment, health screening and referral, and an accessible route to obtain emergency support.

(2) A restoration plan for a materially affected community shall be prepared with meaningful, safe and non-retaliatory participation by affected residents, workers, waste pickers, fishers, farmers, Scheduled Tribes and other forest-dependent communities, persons with disabilities, children and caregivers, informal-settlement residents, schools, healthcare facilities and relevant local bodies.

(3) Relocation or restriction of access may occur only where necessary to avert an evidenced serious risk, under lawful authority, with notice, hearing, accessible assistance, non-discriminatory compensation and prompt independent review. It shall not be used to remove, silence or disadvantage an affected community.

### 11F. Climate-pollutant prevention

(1) No person shall intentionally or recklessly release a climate pollutant from waste handling, open burning, landfill, flaring, shipping, aviation, industrial activity or another prescribed source in a manner that contravenes an applicable standard or creates a material and foreseeable risk of serious environmental or health harm.

(2) Rules under this section shall be evidence-based, include source-specific measurement and reduction standards, avoid double counting, protect workers and low-income users from disproportionate cost, be laid before Parliament and remain subject to judicial review.

(3) This section supplements and does not displace any applicable climate, energy, transport, air-quality or international-law obligation.

### 11G. Groundwater, recharge and cumulative-impact protection

(1) The appropriate Government shall identify, map, monitor and protect groundwater bodies, recharge areas, springs, wetlands and connected ecological systems, using independently published hydrogeological methods and community knowledge where relevant.

(2) No public authority may authorise extraction, diversion, construction, discharge, disposal, land conversion or other activity that creates a material and foreseeable risk of serious depletion, contamination, loss of recharge, land subsidence or disproportionate loss of essential access, unless the authority publishes an evidence-based cumulative-impact assessment, less-harm alternatives, affected-community consultation, measurable safeguards, a restoration and financial-assurance plan, and a prompt review route.

(3) Rules may set aquifer-specific sustainable-use, recharge, monitoring and restoration standards only after publication of the evidence, equality and affordability assessment, and response to public submissions. They shall protect essential domestic needs and shall not transfer disproportionate water scarcity to marginalised, tribal, rural, coastal or low-income communities.

### 11H. Clean Water Restoration Fund

(1) The Central Government shall establish, by rules and subject to parliamentary appropriation, a Clean Water Restoration Fund for emergency safe-water access, restoration, independent monitoring, community health response, legal-access support and just transition measures under this Act.

(2) The Fund may receive court-ordered restoration amounts, disgorgement, public-loss recovery, administrative penalties and other lawful grants, but shall not treat polluter payments as permission to pollute or reduce a responsible person's full remediation duty.

(3) Fund allocation shall follow published, need-based and non-discriminatory criteria, prioritising communities facing serious pollution, essential-access failure or historical underinvestment. Its accounts, grants, beneficiaries, conflicts and outcomes shall be independently audited and published annually.

(4) No Minister, political officeholder or public authority may give an undisclosed case-specific direction concerning a Fund allocation. A decision is subject to sections 14A, 15A, 17 and 19.

### 11I. Source-area, catchment and environmental-justice protection

(1) A public authority shall identify and protect forests, wetlands, floodplains, springs, catchments, coastal buffers, mangroves, natural drainage and other source areas necessary for water quality, ecological flows, recharge, flood safety or essential access.

(2) No project, facility, land-use change, waste site, extraction activity or other activity may be authorised in or materially affecting a source area unless the authority establishes, on independently published evidence, that it is necessary for a legitimate public purpose, that no less harmful practicable alternative exists, that affected communities have meaningful participation, and that binding avoidance, mitigation, restoration, monitoring and financial-assurance measures are in place.

(3) A public authority shall not site, approve, tolerate or disproportionately concentrate a polluting industry, waste facility, contaminated site, extraction burden or loss of access in a marginalised, tribal, rural, coastal, low-income or politically under-represented community. A material decision shall include a published equality, cumulative-impact and displacement assessment and a review route under section 19.

### 11J. Water-quality standards and emerging contaminants

(1) The Central Government shall, after independent scientific review and public consultation, establish or update evidence-based water-quality, monitoring, treatment and public-notification standards for relevant contaminants, including heavy metals, pesticides, pharmaceuticals, microplastics, per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances, and other emerging contaminants where credible evidence indicates a material risk.

(2) A standard under sub-section (1) shall identify the evidence, uncertainty, method, detection limit, health/ecological purpose, affected groups, feasible control measures, implementation support, review date and public-data requirement. It shall not be weakened by a rule without the process in sections 28 and 29A.

(3) Where a credible risk exceeds an applicable standard or an independently published precautionary trigger, the competent authority shall promptly publish a risk notice, provide safe alternative water where necessary, investigate the source, preserve evidence and require proportionate containment, remediation and health-response measures.

### 11K. Drought, flood, contamination and heat emergency readiness

(1) The appropriate Government shall maintain a publicly available, accessible emergency plan for drought, flood, contamination, heat and other foreseeable water-access emergencies.

(2) The plan shall provide for early warning, public alerts, protected emergency stocks and delivery, priority access under section 6D, accessible communications, temporary sanitation, independent quality testing, continuity for hospitals and schools, evidence preservation, public reporting and a post-event review.

(3) An emergency response shall be time-limited, non-discriminatory, least restrictive, recorded and open to judicial review. It shall not be used to commercialise water, suppress information, evade pollution controls or bypass the rights and remedies in this Act.

### 11L. Source-community participation and customary water protection

(1) Before a material source-area, extraction, diversion, land-use, pollution-control, restoration or scarcity decision likely to affect a Scheduled Tribe, forest-dependent community, indigenous or other local community, the appropriate Government shall provide free, prior, accessible, culturally appropriate and informed consultation in the relevant language and form.

(2) The process shall disclose the proposal, evidence, alternatives, expected benefits and burdens, water and livelihood impacts, compensation/transition proposal, conflict information, decision-maker, reasons and remedy route; provide adequate time, independent technical support where necessary, and a written response to material objections.

(3) This section supplements, and does not reduce, any consent, forest, land, tribal, local-self-government, environmental, compensation or other protection required by another law. No decision may be used to extinguish a customary water practice or community access without lawful authority, necessity, proportionate safeguards and independent review.

### 11M. Extended producer responsibility and prevention-first duty

(1) A producer, importer, brand owner, manufacturer, formulator or commercial supplier whose product, chemical, packaging, pharmaceutical, pesticide, microplastic, waste stream or end-of-life system creates a material water-pollution risk shall finance and implement prevention, collection, safe treatment, take-back, monitoring, disclosure and restoration measures proportionate to the risk and lifecycle impact.

(2) Payment of a fee, compensation, restoration amount, insurance premium or penalty does not authorise a preventable discharge, pollution risk or failure to comply with an applicable standard. Prevention, substitution and source control take priority over offset, dilution, compensation or post-harm clean-up.

(3) An extended-producer-responsibility system shall use public registers, independently auditable data, conflict safeguards, equality assessment and public consultation. It shall not shift costs or hazardous handling burdens to waste pickers, workers, low-income communities or local bodies without lawful protection, consent and support.

### 11N. Industrial water efficiency, leakage and safe residual management

(1) A prescribed industrial or commercial water user shall implement independently verified water-efficiency, leakage-reduction, source-control, reuse and safe-residual-management measures before receiving or retaining a large withdrawal or cost-recovery approval.

(2) A closed-loop system, zero-liquid-discharge system or comparable technology may be required or claimed only where independently published lifecycle evidence shows that it is environmentally and health protective overall and does not create unsafe sludge, concentrated waste, excessive energy use, hidden discharge, worker harm or disproportionate community burden.

(3) All sludge, brine, concentrate, residual chemical, filter media and waste generated by treatment or reuse shall be tracked and safely managed under this Act and other applicable law; it shall not be diluted, relabelled, transferred or exported to evade a duty.

### 11O. Inter-State, downstream and transboundary water cooperation

(1) The Central Government, States, Union Territories and relevant public authorities shall share timely water-quality, flow, extraction, contamination and emergency information with affected downstream and neighbouring jurisdictions, subject only to necessary and reviewable redaction.

(2) Before a material action likely to cause a foreseeable significant downstream, inter-State or transboundary water impact, the responsible authority shall publish an assessment, consult affected public authorities and communities through lawful channels, consider less harmful alternatives and maintain emergency communication and monitoring arrangements.

(3) Nothing in this section authorises unilateral coercion outside India's lawful jurisdiction or displaces constitutional federal processes, tribunal jurisdiction, treaty obligations or another lawful dispute-resolution mechanism.

### 11P. High-risk sectors, desalination and antimicrobial-residue control

(1) A mine, thermal plant, data centre, chemical plant, pharmaceutical facility, port, desalination facility, large farm, livestock operation, laboratory, hospital, construction project or other prescribed high-risk activity shall obtain and maintain a publicly available water-impact and waste-management plan addressing withdrawal, source protection, water efficiency, reuse, discharge, residues, emergency response, worker safety, community health, restoration, financial assurance and closure.

(2) A desalination facility shall assess and publicly report its energy use, emissions, intake effects, brine and chemical management, marine impacts, affordability, alternatives and emergency plan. Brine, concentrate, chemical residue or waste shall not be discharged or managed in a manner that contravenes this Act.

(3) A healthcare facility, pharmaceutical facility, livestock operation, laboratory or other prescribed source shall prevent the release of antimicrobial residues, resistant organisms or other material presenting a credible antimicrobial-resistance risk into a protected water receptor. Rules shall set evidence-based monitoring, treatment, reporting and emergency requirements after public consultation and independent scientific review.

### 11Q. Legacy contaminated-site and infrastructure restoration

(1) Within three years after commencement, the appropriate Government shall publish a prioritised inventory of abandoned mines, legacy industrial sites, landfills, dams, contaminated public sites, derelict infrastructure and other significant actual or potential sources of water contamination or access harm.

(2) Each high-priority site shall have a publicly available risk assessment, responsible-person investigation, interim protection measure, restoration plan, funding source, measurable deadline, community-health response where evidence warrants, and review route under section 19.

(3) The absence, insolvency or disappearance of a responsible person does not extinguish the public authority's duty to protect people from an imminent serious risk. It may use the Clean Water Restoration Fund for interim protection and pursue lawful recovery from a proven responsible person, successor, insurer or financial-assurance instrument.

### 11R. Measurable service, access and restoration standards

(1) The Central Government shall publish a provisional national framework of measurable indicators for minimum essential safe-water access, affordability, water quality, point-of-use safety, continuity, downtime, leakage, sanitation, sewage treatment, wastewater reuse, ecological flows, recharge, contamination response, restoration and equality of access.

(2) An indicator shall state the measurement method, baseline, target, data source, publication frequency, uncertainty, responsible authority, review date, remedy route and the consequences of failure. State, Union Territory and local plans may adopt stronger compatible indicators.

(3) No technical value, threshold or commencement condition shall be treated as final unless it is supported by independently published scientific, public-health, hydrogeological, equality, accessibility and fiscal evidence and has completed the consultation process in section 28.

### 12. Sampling, evidence and information integrity

(1) A sample relied upon for an adverse action must be collected by an authorised person using a published protocol, unique identifier, contemporaneous location/time record, tamper-evident chain of custody and split-sample opportunity where reasonably practicable.

(2) The person affected must receive the testing method, laboratory report, chain-of-custody record and a reasonable opportunity to seek independent retesting, subject to necessary protection of an ongoing investigation.

(3) An accredited laboratory reporting on a sample for enforcement must disclose material conflicts, methods, uncertainty, detection limits and quality controls.

(4) Knowingly falsifying a sample, laboratory report, monitoring record, consent record, public dashboard, inspection record or restoration certificate is an offence under section 24.

### 13. Institutional separation and conflicts

(1) The functions of investigation, scientific sampling, administrative compliance assessment, prosecution, independent audit and adjudication must be institutionally and functionally separate.

(2) An officer shall disclose and recuse from a matter involving a financial, family, political, employment, contractual or other material conflict.

(3) A public authority shall maintain an auditable register of complaints, inspection selection, directions, samples, laboratory assignments, enforcement referrals, restoration orders, recusals and outcomes.

### 14. Competent authorities and coordination

(1) The Central Pollution Control Board, State Pollution Control Boards, Pollution Control Committees and local authorities shall perform only the functions assigned by this Act or other law, subject to their existing statutory constitution and safeguards.

(2) No new body is created by this Act with combined power to investigate, prosecute, adjudicate and finally review its own conduct.

(3) An intergovernmental Clean Water Coordination Council shall be constituted by rules as a consultative and reporting forum. It shall not issue binding case-specific directions, adjudicate disputes or displace a statutory authority or court.

### 14B. Local water councils

(1) A State, Union Territory or local authority may establish a local water council for a catchment, aquifer, water body or service area as an accessible advisory and participation body.

(2) Its membership shall be selected through published, conflict-screened and inclusive criteria and include affected residents, women, Scheduled Tribes and other forest-dependent communities where relevant, farmers, fishers, workers, waste pickers, persons with disabilities, public-health and hydrogeological expertise, and local-government representation. No political party, industry group, public authority or other aligned bloc may control its membership.

(3) A local water council may review public data, receive community evidence, publish recommendations, request reasons and trigger the complaint process under section 16. It shall not issue a binding permit, enforce an offence, direct an investigation or prosecution, override a statutory authority, displace an elected local body, or limit a court's jurisdiction.

(4) Meetings, agendas, minutes, member interests, recommendations and public-authority responses shall be published, subject only to necessary and reviewable redaction. Members shall receive no benefit from a matter in which they have a material conflict.

### 14C. Independent scientific panels and facilitated water disputes

(1) For a major standard, catchment decision, large allocation, source-area decision, significant pollution event or contested technical issue, the appropriate Government shall obtain advice from an independent scientific panel selected through published expertise, diversity and conflict-screening criteria. Its evidence, method, conflicts, dissenting views and advice shall be published.

(2) A person with a material financial, employment, political, litigation or other conflict may provide factual material but shall not be the sole or controlling adviser on the disputed issue.

(3) A dispute between communities, sectors, service providers or levels of government may be referred, with the parties' consent, to a time-bound, transparent and accessible facilitation or mediation process. That process shall not impose a binding outcome, waive rights, delay urgent protection, replace statutory or constitutional remedies, or exclude a court, tribunal or lawful federal process.

### 14D. Water-rights ombuds function

(1) The appropriate Government shall designate, through a published, conflict-screened and independently reviewable process, an existing statutory office or an ombuds function established by law to receive systemic complaints concerning water access, service failure, pollution response, retaliation, data integrity or recurring non-compliance under this Act.

(2) The ombuds function may require a reasoned response, publish systemic findings, refer evidence to the competent authority, auditor, prosecutor, court or tribunal, recommend corrective action, and report annually to the relevant legislature. It shall not investigate and prosecute the same matter, adjudicate a binding dispute, direct a court, or replace an existing remedy.

(3) Appointment criteria, term, budget, conflicts, complaints, findings, referrals and Government responses shall be public. No officeholder or aligned bloc may control appointment, funding, direction and removal without independent counterweights and judicial review.

### 14A. Integrity of public decisions and political-contact record

(1) A public servant, Minister, elected political officeholder, political appointee, member of a decision-making body or person acting on their behalf shall disclose and recuse from a matter under this Act where the person has a material financial, family, employment, contractual, political or other conflict, or has received a request, direction or representation from a person with a material interest in the outcome.

(2) A material representation concerning a permit, exception, consent, enforcement decision, contract, subsidy, monitoring obligation, restoration order or prosecution referral shall be made only through a recorded channel. An oral, informal, undisclosed or off-record instruction, assurance or request has no legal effect and shall not be acted upon.

(3) The public authority shall publish, in a searchable and tamper-evident register, every decision and representation covered by sub-section (2), including the date, participants, matter, legal authority, documents considered, disclosed interests, reasons, beneficiary, duration, review date and appeal route. Only necessary personal-data, safety, investigation-integrity or legally protected material may be redacted, with written reasons and independent review.

(4) A failure to disclose, recuse, record or publish as required by this section is subject to section 19. An intentional or knowing failure to do so for the purpose of concealing or facilitating circumvention is an offence under section 24A.

### 15. Water and groundwater register

(1) Within two years, each State and Union Territory shall publish and maintain an open, interoperable register of protected water receptors, subject to limited redaction of information whose publication would create a specific, evidenced safety risk.

(2) The register shall identify location, boundary, jurisdiction, known pollution sources, monitoring points, restoration status and the responsible public authority. For a groundwater body or recharge area, it shall also record, where technically available, aquifer connection, extraction pressure, recharge risk, cumulative-impact information and essential-access risk.

(3) A person may submit a correction request with evidence. A reasoned decision must be given within sixty days and may be challenged under section 19.

### 15A. Open environmental-decision and enforcement register

(1) Within twelve months after commencement, the appropriate Government shall maintain an open, interoperable and tamper-evident register of permits, consents, exceptions, compliance directions, non-enforcement decisions, inspection selections, samples, laboratory assignments, prosecutions, public contracts, subsidies, restoration orders, political-contact records, conflicts, recusals and final outcomes under this Act.

(2) The register shall identify the responsible authority, legal basis, relevant dates, affected location, beneficiary or recipient where lawful, published reasons, review route and status. It shall link to the register under section 15 where the decision concerns a protected water receptor.

(3) An entry may be withheld or redacted only to the minimum extent necessary to protect a named statutory interest. The authority shall publish a redaction note, review the redaction every ninety days, and provide a prompt challenge route under section 19.

### 15B. Public water-allocation and withdrawal inventory

(1) Within eighteen months after commencement, the appropriate Government shall publish and maintain a searchable, interoperable and tamper-evident inventory of every material water withdrawal, allocation, extraction approval, diversion, bulk transfer, water-service contract, commercial cost-recovery arrangement and water-related public asset under its jurisdiction.

(2) The inventory shall identify the source, catchment or aquifer, volume, purpose, recipient, legal basis, duration, charges, ecological-flow and essential-access assessment, meter data, compliance status, public benefit, conflicts, review date and remedy route.

(3) A public authority shall not withhold the identity of a material recipient or the terms of a material allocation or contract except to the minimum extent necessary for a named statutory interest, with written reasons, ninety-day review and challenge under section 19. Commercial confidentiality does not justify withholding information necessary to assess public water access, ecological impact, cost recovery, conflict or compliance.

### 15C. Public water-data infrastructure and privacy

(1) Data generated or collected by a public authority or publicly funded water service under this Act shall be maintained in interoperable public infrastructure and shall not be sold, licensed for commercial exploitation, used for behavioural profiling, or disclosed for a purpose incompatible with safe-water access, public-health, environmental protection, lawful service delivery, audit or accountability.

(2) Household-level water-use data shall be collected only where necessary, minimised, secured, retained for a defined period, accessible to the affected person, and published only in anonymised or aggregated form unless a lawful, necessary and proportionate exception applies.

(3) A person may seek correction, access, deletion where retention is no longer lawful, and an independent review of data practices under section 19. This section does not prevent lawful evidence preservation for a specific investigation, subject to judicial safeguards.

### 16. Public complaints, environmental guardians and whistleblowers

(1) Any person, community organisation, worker, waste picker, water user, local body, public servant, contractor, scientist, laboratory worker or other person with relevant information may make a complaint or protected disclosure about an alleged contravention, circumvention, corruption, retaliation or public-authority failure under this Act through online, offline, toll-free, language-accessible and disability-accessible routes.

(2) A complainant acting in good faith shall not be subjected to retaliation in employment, service delivery, licensing, contracting, benefits, intimidation, violence, vexatious inspection or prosecution because of the complaint, lawful advocacy, journalism, protest, association or political belief.

(3) An allegation knowingly made false with intent to cause harm may be addressed under generally applicable law; this subsection shall not be used to punish a good-faith report that is unsubstantiated.

(4) A protected disclosure route shall permit confidential reporting, preserve evidence, give a written receipt and risk assessment within seven days, and provide urgent interim protection where a credible risk of retaliation is shown. A decision to disclose the identity of a reporter requires written necessity and proportionality reasons and prompt independent review.

### 16A. Protection of environmental defenders and public participation

(1) A person shall not be subjected to violence, threat, intimidation, surveillance, harassment, strategic or vexatious litigation, discriminatory service action, abusive inspection, licence action, funding denial or other retaliation because the person lawfully seeks information, participates in a consultation, reports pollution, gives evidence, conducts scientific or journalistic work, assists an affected community, seeks a remedy or advocates for environmental protection.

(2) On a credible showing of risk under sub-section (1), the reviewing forum may grant urgent interim protection, evidence-preservation directions, an anti-retaliation order, costs, access to legal assistance or another lawful and proportionate remedy. The respondent retains notice, hearing and fair-process rights, except for a narrowly tailored temporary order necessary to prevent imminent harm.

(3) This section does not protect violence, threat, knowingly false evidence, unlawful discrimination or another unlawful act. It shall not be used to treat lawful criticism, dissent, reporting, research, journalism, association or peaceful advocacy as grounds for suspicion or adverse action.

### 16B. Accessible water-rights assistance and legal support

(1) The appropriate Government shall provide accessible local complaint centres, assistance in making a complaint or application, interpretation and disability support, and referral to legal-aid or public-interest representation for a person unable to afford effective access to a remedy under this Act.

(2) Assistance shall be available through online and offline routes and shall not depend on political affiliation, identity, literacy, residence status, ability to pay, party support or prior representation by a lawyer.

(3) An authority shall publish service standards, wait times, outcomes, funding and appeal routes for assistance under this section. The Clean Water Restoration Fund may support these functions in accordance with section 11H.

### 17. Independent audit and public reporting

(1) The Comptroller and Auditor-General, or an independent auditor selected through a published conflict-screened process under rules, shall conduct a biennial audit of national and State/UT implementation.

(2) The audit shall examine water and air quality, waste capacity, expenditure, contracts, pollution displacement, financial assurance, waste-chain integrity, monitoring-data integrity, enforcement equality, complaint outcomes, restoration, exceptions, worker transition, disability access, retaliation complaints, politically exposed decisions, political-contact records, conflicts, recusals, non-enforcement decisions and selective-enforcement indicators.

(3) Audit reports, underlying non-personal data and Government responses shall be laid before the relevant legislature and published within thirty days.

### 17A. Water-service performance audit and corrective action

(1) Every public water utility, local authority and other prescribed service provider shall undergo an annual independent performance audit on safe-water access, affordability, continuity, non-revenue water, leakage, sanitation, treatment, wastewater reuse, water quality, emergency readiness, accessibility, complaint outcomes, procurement, public participation, labour conditions and equality of service.

(2) The audit shall produce an accessible public scorecard, identify failures, require a time-bound corrective plan and state the funding, responsible authority and review date. A serious or repeated failure triggers a compliance direction and, where necessary, a remedy under section 19.

(3) An audit may not be used to privatise a public water source or evade the duties in sections 2A, 6C, 8 and 8A. Audit selection, terms and results shall be public and conflict-screened.

### 18. Inspection, search and emergency action

(1) An inspection under this Act requires written authority stating the legal basis, premises or location, purpose, scope and officer identity. Routine inspection selection must use published risk criteria and be auditable.

(2) Entry into a dwelling, search, seizure of property, access to private digital information, or use of force requires a warrant or another authority expressly provided by general criminal procedure law, except where an objectively evidenced and imminent threat of serious contamination or bodily harm makes delay unsafe.

(3) Emergency action shall be limited to containment and evidence preservation, recorded at the time, reported to a magistrate within twenty-four hours, and open to judicial review.

(4) Nothing in this Act authorises surveillance of political, religious, journalistic, associational or advocacy activity merely because of its viewpoint or identity.

### 19. Notice, hearing, appeals and judicial remedies

(1) Except in a documented emergency, an adverse compliance direction, closure, restoration order, recovery order or administrative penalty requires prior notice of the material facts, evidence, legal basis, proposed action, hearing date and accessible response route.

(2) A reasoned decision shall be served and published in redacted form within fourteen days after decision.

(3) An affected person, community, local authority, worker, complainant or public-interest organisation with a sufficient connection may seek review before the forum specified by applicable law, including the National Green Tribunal where jurisdiction exists, without excluding constitutional remedies.

(4) The reviewing forum may grant interim protection, set aside unlawful action, order reasons or records, direct non-discriminatory performance of a public duty, require a remediation plan, award costs, or make another lawful and proportionate remedy.

(5) Where a competent authority fails to decide a credible complaint, commence a required investigation, publish mandatory monitoring data or enforce a final order within the applicable period, an affected person, community organisation or public-interest organisation may seek a compliance direction directly from the reviewing forum without first obtaining executive approval.

(6) A credible complaint or protected disclosure under section 16 shall be acknowledged within seven days, assessed within thirty days, and decided with written reasons within ninety days, unless a reviewing forum permits a specified extension on public reasons. Failure to meet a deadline neither rejects the complaint nor prevents a remedy under sub-section (5).

### 20. Administrative penalties and remediation cost recovery

(1) For a contravention not prosecuted under section 23 or 24, an authorised adjudicating officer may impose a proportionate administrative penalty only after notice, hearing, reasons and appeal.

(2) In determining the amount, the officer shall consider the gravity and duration of harm, culpability, benefit obtained, ability to pay, cooperation, remediation, prior contraventions, impact on workers and third parties, and the need to avoid a penalty that is merely a cost of doing business.

(3) A public authority may recover reasonable, proven containment, testing and restoration costs from a responsible person after adjudication, but shall not recover costs from a person who neither caused nor knowingly facilitated the contravention.

(4) An administrative penalty may be imposed for a post-commencement failure to submit the inventory required by section 11A, to comply with a final compliance order, or to provide required financial assurance. No administrative penalty under this Act shall be imposed solely for conduct completed before its commencement.

(5) Where a public contract, subsidy, concession, licence, tax benefit, public asset or other public resource was obtained, retained or used through a contravention of section 4, 4A, 14A or 24A, the responsible person shall repay the proven public loss and unlawful gain, with interest, after notice, hearing and judicially reviewable adjudication. Recovery under this sub-section is restorative and does not replace a criminal fine, compensation or another lawful remedy.

### 21. Offence: prohibited water-receptor disposal

(1) A person who intentionally or recklessly contravenes section 5 commits an offence.

(2) On conviction, the person is punishable with imprisonment for a term of not less than two years and which may extend to seven years, and with a fine not less than ten lakh rupees; where the offender is a company or other commercial undertaking, the court shall also consider a fine proportionate to the economic benefit obtained and the restoration cost.

(3) For a first contravention causing no proven serious harm and promptly self-reported, contained and remediated before detection, the court may for recorded reasons impose a lesser term or a proportionate non-custodial sentence, but not dispense with restoration, public disclosure and independent audit.

### 21A. Offence: prohibited air pollution

(1) A person who intentionally or recklessly contravenes section 6B commits an offence.

(2) On conviction, the person is punishable with imprisonment for a term which may extend to five years and with a fine sufficient to remove the economic benefit, fund remediation and deter repetition.

(3) A first contravention causing no proven serious harm and promptly self-reported, contained and remediated before detection may be dealt with through a proportionate non-custodial sentence or administrative remedy, for recorded reasons, but not in a manner that permits continuing emission or conceals the event.

### 21B. Offence: deliberate non-compliance with legacy-remediation order

(1) A person who, without lawful excuse, knowingly fails to comply with a final cessation, containment, remediation, financial-assurance or restoration order under section 11A commits an offence.

(2) On conviction, the person is punishable with imprisonment for a term of not less than two years and which may extend to seven years, and with a fine sufficient to remove any economic benefit from the non-compliance, fund full remediation and deter repetition.

(3) This section applies only to a failure occurring after this Act comes into force and after the final order was served. It does not criminalise the historical pollution itself.

### 21C. Future-pollution rule and no safe harbour

(1) Every contravention of section 5, 6, 6A or 6B occurring after the relevant provision of this Act comes into force is immediately actionable and punishable under section 21, 21A or 23, as applicable.

(2) A warning, notice, pending application, transition arrangement, legacy permit, consent, licence, contract, funding condition, administrative delay or anticipated future treatment facility does not authorise a person to continue or commence a polluting activity after commencement.

(3) A competent authority may provide technical compliance assistance or a reasonable period to submit records, install monitoring or complete a non-polluting transition, but it shall not grant a period in which an emission, discharge, disposal or other conduct prohibited by section 5, 6, 6A or 6B may lawfully continue.

(4) Each day on which a person knowingly continues a prohibited activity after service of a compliance, cessation or restoration order is a separate continuing contravention for purposes of penalty, remediation and enforcement, subject to the rule against double punishment for the same offence.

### 21D. Offence: commercial commodification of water

(1) A person who knowingly contravenes section 6C after the applicable transition period commits an offence.

(2) On conviction, an individual is punishable with imprisonment for a term of not less than three years and which may extend to seven years, and with a fine sufficient to remove the economic benefit, restore the affected water source and deter repetition. A body corporate is punishable with a fine not less than five times the economic benefit or one crore rupees, whichever is greater, in addition to restoration, disgorgement and a proportionate order under section 25.

(3) A personal, community or emergency supply that complies with section 6C(3), (4) or (6) is not an offence. A person claiming such compliance bears the evidentiary burden for those facts, without displacing the prosecution's burden to prove the offence beyond reasonable doubt.

### 22. Offence: listed single-use plastic commercial activity

(1) A person who knowingly contravenes section 7 after the applicable transition period commits an offence.

(2) On conviction, the person is punishable with imprisonment which may extend to three years, or with fine, or with both; for a corporation or commercial undertaking, the fine shall be proportionate to turnover, benefit and repeated non-compliance, subject to a fair hearing.

(3) A first small-scale contravention without deception, after a documented good-faith effort to comply, should ordinarily be addressed by notice, accessible compliance support and a proportionate administrative response before prosecution, unless it forms part of repeated or organised evasion.

### 22A. Offence: false environmental claim and evasion label

(1) A person who knowingly or recklessly makes, causes to be made, uses or relies upon a materially false or misleading environmental claim in order to manufacture, sell, supply, export, dispose of, transport, finance or promote an activity or product covered by this Act commits an offence.

(2) On conviction, the person is punishable with imprisonment which may extend to three years, or with fine, or with both; a court may also order correction, withdrawal, recall, publication of the finding and compensation for proven remediation costs.

(3) A claim is not an offence merely because a good-faith scientific assessment is later revised, if the person disclosed the evidence, uncertainty, method and material conflict at the time of the claim.

### 22B. Offence: deliberate water-safety misinformation

(1) A person who knowingly makes, causes to be made, suppresses or materially falsifies a public statement, certificate, notice, test result, dashboard entry, service notice, allocation record or warning concerning water quality, contamination, availability, interruption, safety or emergency response, with the intention of deceiving a person, regulator or community or evading a duty under this Act, commits an offence.

(2) On conviction, the person is punishable with imprisonment which may extend to five years, or with fine, or with both; a court may also order prompt correction, public notice, restoration, compensation for proven harm and an independent audit.

(3) This section does not apply to good-faith reporting, scientific debate, journalism, criticism, an honestly stated uncertainty or a corrected error where the person promptly discloses the error and takes reasonable steps to prevent harm.

### 23. Aggravated offence

(1) A person commits an aggravated offence if the person knowingly or recklessly—

  (a) causes serious, widespread or long-term contamination of a protected water receptor or serious air pollution;

  (b) disposes of hazardous, biological, toxic, persistent or industrial waste in a groundwater body or recharge area;

  (c) causes a substantial risk of death, serious bodily harm, disease outbreak or irreversible ecological injury;

  (d) continues prohibited conduct after a binding judicial or adjudicatory stop order; or

  (e) directs, finances, organises or conceals conduct described in clauses (a) to (d); or

  (f) falsifies, disables, bypasses or tampers with continuous monitoring, a waste manifest, a treatment record, a financial-assurance instrument or a chain-of-custody record in order to conceal or facilitate pollution.

(2) On conviction, the person is punishable with imprisonment for a term of not less than five years and which may extend to ten years, and with a fine sufficient to remove the economic benefit, fund full restoration and deter repetition.

### 24. Official, company and laboratory misconduct

(1) A public official, company officer, laboratory officer, contractor or other person commits an offence if the person intentionally or recklessly—

  (a) authorises or directs conduct prohibited by section 5 or 6;

  (b) falsifies, suppresses, destroys or fabricates material evidence, a sample, report, consent, inspection record, public record or restoration certificate;

  (c) retaliates against a complainant, witness, worker, journalist or environmental guardian for a protected activity under section 16;

  (d) grants or obtains an exception, consent or benefit through corruption, material conflict or deception; or

  (e) knowingly fails to comply with a final court order requiring performance of a specific statutory duty under section 8.

(2) On conviction, the person is punishable with imprisonment which may extend to seven years, or with fine, or with both, without prejudice to removal, disciplinary action, restitution and other lawful remedy.

(3) A public authority or company is not criminally liable merely because an employee committed an offence. The prosecution must prove the legally required act, fault and attribution, and an accused officer retains all due-diligence and fair-trial protections available under law.

### 24A. Aggravated intentional or knowing circumvention

(1) A public servant, including a Minister, elected political officeholder or political appointee, company officer, contractor, laboratory officer, consultant, beneficial controller or other person commits an offence if the person intentionally or knowingly—

  (a) creates, procures, directs, approves, conceals or implements a sham permit, exemption, classification, consent, record, compliance certificate, procurement decision, subsidy, contract, corporate arrangement or non-enforcement decision that circumvents this Act;

  (b) causes a regulator, public authority, laboratory, contractor or another person to withhold, alter, misclassify, suppress or falsify material information, monitoring data, risk evidence, ownership information, waste-chain information, a conflict disclosure or an enforcement record;

  (c) grants, obtains, retains or directs a benefit, exception, consent, contract, licence, subsidy, enforcement preference or other advantage through corruption, deception, undisclosed material conflict, discriminatory favouritism or a device prohibited by section 4 or 4A; or

  (d) threatens, directs retaliation against, or knowingly uses an investigation, inspection, prosecution, licensing, contract, funding or public-service power to deter a good-faith report, challenge, audit, worker disclosure or judicial remedy concerning such circumvention.

(2) It is immaterial whether the person acts directly or through a political party, office staff, intermediary, associate, related entity, contractor, nominee, controlled undertaking or another person, or whether an improper benefit is ultimately obtained.

(3) For this section, “improper benefit” means a pecuniary or non-pecuniary advantage that the recipient is not lawfully entitled to receive, including avoidance of an environmental duty, penalty, restoration cost, monitoring requirement, procurement consequence or accountability process.

(4) On conviction under this section, an individual is punishable with rigorous imprisonment for a term of not less than seven years and which may extend to fourteen years, and with a fine not less than ten lakh rupees; where an improper benefit is proved, the fine shall not be less than twice its value. A body corporate is punishable with a fine not less than one crore rupees; where an improper benefit is proved, the fine shall not be less than five times its value, in addition to full restoration and disgorgement.

(5) In addition to the sentence under sub-section (4), the Court of Session may order confiscation of the proven improper benefit, compensation and restoration, debarment from public procurement, subsidy, concession, licence or high-risk activity for a fixed proportionate period not exceeding ten years, and independent compliance supervision. A public servant, Minister, elected political officeholder or political appointee remains subject to removal, disqualification or disciplinary proceedings only under the applicable constitutional or statutory procedure.

(6) An offence under this section is triable by a Court of Session. Investigation shall be conducted by an officer not below the rank of Superintendent of Police or an officer of equivalent rank, selected through a published, conflict-screened and auditable allocation process. No Minister, elected political officeholder, political appointee, public authority or person acting on their instruction may give an undisclosed case-specific direction to the investigator or prosecutor.

(7) No investigation, search, seizure, arrest, prosecution or trial under this section may proceed except under the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 and other applicable criminal-procedure law. A search, seizure or arrest requires prior judicial authorisation, except for immediate evidence preservation in a documented emergency; any such emergency action must be brought before the Court of Session within twenty-four hours. The accused must receive material exculpatory evidence, a reasoned charging decision, a fair trial and every appeal available under law.

(8) Where the facts disclose an offence under the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988, that Act applies in addition to this Act, including its applicable investigation, approval, sanction, special-court, attachment and appeal provisions. No investigator, prosecutor or public authority may use the label of one offence to evade a mandatory safeguard or duty under the other Act.

(9) A final conviction under this section shall be transmitted within seven days to the authority competent under the Constitution, the Representation of the People Act, 1951 or another applicable law to consider removal, disqualification or discipline. The transmission itself neither removes nor disqualifies a person; the competent authority shall act only through the procedure, standards, reasons, hearing and review required by that law.

(10) This section does not create liability solely for a good-faith speech, vote, advocacy or participation in a duly constituted legislative proceeding. It does not protect an independently proven act or arrangement described in sub-section (1) that is undertaken outside the lawful legislative process.

(11) Where the same act is charged under this section and another provision of this Act, no person shall be punished twice for the same offence; this does not prevent distinct civil, restorative, disciplinary or constitutional remedies authorised by law.

### 25. Corporate responsibility and beneficial control

(1) Where a body corporate commits an offence under this Act, the body corporate is liable, and a director, partner, manager or person exercising effective control is separately liable only if the prosecution proves consent, connivance, knowledge coupled with a duty and capacity to prevent, or neglect amounting to recklessness.

(2) A court may require disclosure of beneficial control, related transactions, waste manifests and financial benefit relevant to a proven offence, subject to privacy, confidentiality and fair-trial safeguards.

(3) On conviction for an aggravated offence under section 23 or 24A, deliberate non-compliance with a legacy-remediation order, or repeated material contravention, the court may for a proportionate period—

  (a) disqualify a responsible director, partner, manager or controlling person from managing a high-risk activity;

  (b) prohibit the entity and a controlled successor from public procurement, public subsidy, concession, licence renewal or government contract; and

  (c) require enhanced financial assurance, independent compliance supervision and periodic public reporting.

(4) An order under sub-section (3) requires notice, a hearing, written reasons, an individualised proportionality assessment, a fixed duration and an appeal route. It shall not punish an employee, worker, consumer or community for the wrongdoing of management.

### 26. Just transition and equitable implementation

(1) Before a Schedule I prohibition, the transition required by section 6C(5), or a phase-out under section 6E(3) takes effect for a class of manufacturers, workers, traders, water-service workers, small operators, farmers, fishers or waste pickers, the appropriate Government shall make accessible—

  (a) transition grants, retraining, social protection and procurement opportunities based on published non-discriminatory criteria;

  (b) alternatives that are safe, affordable, practical and accessible to persons with disabilities and low-income users; and

  (c) a time-bound grievance and appeal route for exclusion, delay or discrimination.

(2) No transition programme may be conditioned on political support, religious affiliation, caste, gender, location, identity, electoral activity, lawful advocacy or criticism.

### 27. Education, public information and religious-waste stewardship

(1) The appropriate Government shall fund public information and locally appropriate collection, segregation, composting, reuse and safe-treatment arrangements for religious and cultural waste, without privileging or penalising a belief or community.

(2) Educational material shall be science-based, accessible, multilingual where practicable, and clear that pollution prevention is a shared civic duty rather than a ground for harassment or vigilantism.

### 28. Rule-making power and limits

(1) The Central Government may make rules to carry out this Act only on the matters expressly identified in this Act, including Schedule I, technical sampling standards, data standards, audit methods, forms, emergency records, transition programmes, high-risk-activity criteria, financial-assurance methods, waste-chain records, shipment records, monitoring standards, climate-pollutant standards, registers, conflict screening, protected disclosures, auditable case allocation, cost-recovery controls and transition under section 6C, minimum essential safe-water standards under section 2A, large-withdrawal criteria, ecological-flow assessment, water-stress criteria, local-water-council procedures, safe wastewater reuse, rainwater/recharge design, source-area criteria, emerging-contaminant standards, emergency planning, public data protection, legal-support service standards, utility-performance audits, source-community participation, extended producer responsibility, industrial efficiency, residual management, scientific-panel process, inter-jurisdictional information sharing, point-of-use testing, tanker supply, worker safety, high-risk-sector plans, antimicrobial-resistance control, contaminated-site inventory, measurable indicators, ombuds procedures and capacity assurance.

(2) Before making rules, the Central Government shall publish a draft, explanatory memorandum, evidence register, equality/accessibility assessment and response to public comments, except for a temporary emergency rule necessary to prevent an imminent and serious harm.

(3) An emergency rule expires after ninety days unless replaced through the ordinary process. Every rule shall be laid before Parliament and is subject to judicial review.

(4) Rules shall not create a new offence, increase a maximum penalty, expand search/arrest powers, alter the core statutory definition of a protected water receptor, or grant a general exemption from section 5.

### 29. Periodic review and democratic correction

(1) An independent review shall be completed within five years after commencement and every five years thereafter.

(2) The review shall assess environmental outcomes, clean-water and clean-air access, climate-pollutant reduction, public health, waste capacity, fiscal cost, effects on workers and small enterprises, disability access, religious/community impacts, equality of enforcement, complaints, prosecutions, restoration, financial assurance, data integrity, corruption risk, political-contact records, recusal compliance, protected disclosures, public-loss recovery, selective enforcement, minimum essential access, affordability, ecological flows, groundwater stress, large withdrawals, water-allocation equity, local-water-council participation, sanitation coverage, wastewater reuse, rainwater/recharge, source-area protection, emerging contaminants, emergency readiness, water-data privacy, legal-support access, utility performance, gender and care-work impact, source-community participation, producer responsibility, industrial residual management, water-safety misinformation, downstream impact, point-of-use safety, tanker supply, worker protection, high-risk-sector compliance, legacy-site restoration, technical-indicator performance, ombuds findings, capacity assurance and State/UT implementation.

(3) The review report, underlying non-personal data and Government response shall be published, laid before Parliament and considered through a public consultation process before any material amendment is proposed.

### 29A. Democratic protection against repeal or material weakening

(1) A Bill, rule or other legislative proposal that repeals, suspends, narrows, delays, exempts from, or materially weakens a protection, remedy, public duty, transparency requirement, anti-circumvention safeguard or offence under this Act shall be accompanied by—

  (a) a published statement identifying every provision affected and the reason for the proposal;

  (b) an independent evidence, equality, health, environmental, fiscal, federalism and corruption-risk assessment;

  (c) a public consultation of not less than one hundred and twenty days, accessible in relevant languages and formats, followed by a published response to material submissions;

  (d) a statement of less restrictive alternatives, transition arrangements, protections for affected communities and workers, and measures preserving access to safe water, clean-air protection, restoration and remedies; and

  (e) an express savings schedule preserving pending claims, accrued liabilities, restoration obligations, public records, evidence, protected disclosures, enforcement proceedings and judicial remedies unless a later Act expressly and constitutionally provides otherwise.

(2) No rule or executive action may use an emergency, transition, administrative convenience, funding condition, contract, private delegation or other device to evade sub-section (1) or weaken this Act beyond the authority expressly conferred by Parliament.

(3) This section creates mandatory transparency and deliberation requirements for the administration and amendment of this Act. It does not purport to bind Parliament's constituent or legislative power, prevent a later Parliament from expressly legislating otherwise, or displace judicial review of a later enactment.

(4) No public authority may regress from the minimum essential access, pollution-prevention, information, participation, restoration or remedy protections in this Act except under an express law, for a compelling and independently evidenced public purpose, after considering less restrictive alternatives, with time limits, equal-or-stronger substitute safeguards and prompt judicial review. Budgetary difficulty or administrative convenience alone is not a compelling purpose.

### 29B. Capacity-gated commencement and implementation assurance

(1) Before appointing a commencement date for a service, transition, monitoring, standard, large-withdrawal, sanitation, data, emergency or restoration duty that requires operational capacity, the appropriate Government shall publish a capacity-assurance statement identifying the legal authority, responsible institutions, funding, workforce, infrastructure, public-access route, data system, emergency arrangements, independent verification and review date.

(2) The statement shall be independently audited and open to public comment for not less than sixty days. A material shortfall requires a corrective plan, interim safeguards and a revised date; it does not authorise pollution, denial of the minimum essential access under section 2A or suppression of a remedy.

(3) A person may challenge a deficient capacity-assurance statement under section 19. The reviewing forum may direct corrective planning, information disclosure, interim protection or another lawful and proportionate remedy.

### 30. Preservation of remedies and accountability

Nothing in this Act creates an immunity from civil, criminal, disciplinary, constitutional or other lawful accountability. A person acting under this Act remains subject to the Constitution, applicable procedure, recorded reasons, independent review and effective remedy.

### 31. Severability

If any provision of this Act or its application to a person or circumstance is held invalid, the remaining provisions and applications shall continue to the extent they can operate consistently with the Constitution.

### 32. Savings and consequential amendments

(1) Subject to this Act and Schedule II, this Act operates in addition to the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974, the Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981, the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986, the National Green Tribunal Act, 2010, the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988, the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023, and other applicable law.

(2) A consent, permit, authorisation, registration, rule, order, condition, proceeding, liability, record, contract or waste-management arrangement under another law continues only to the extent that it is consistent with this Act. It neither authorises an act prohibited by this Act nor removes a duty, remedy, standard or prosecution otherwise available under another law.

(3) Nothing in this Act—

  (a) repeals or narrows a pollution-control, environmental-protection, public-health, labour, local-government, forest, wildlife, coastal, maritime, criminal, anti-corruption, consumer, constitutional or civil remedy that affords equal or greater protection;

  (b) validates a past act that was unlawful when done;

  (c) creates retrospective criminal liability; or

  (d) changes a constitutional removal, privilege, disqualification, sanction, investigation, Special Judge, attachment, appeal or other procedure except where Schedule II expressly and lawfully provides.

(4) A pending proceeding or accrued civil, restorative, administrative or criminal liability under another law is not abated merely because the same facts may also engage this Act. A person shall not be punished twice for the same offence.

(5) Schedule III is the technical commencement and performance schedule for this Act. A value, method or date in that Schedule may be revised only by rule made in accordance with sections 28 and 29A, with the evidence and non-regression safeguards stated in those sections.

### 33. Schedule I — listed single-use plastic products

#### Part A — Prohibited products

For section 7, every single-use plastic product is prohibited. Without limiting that prohibition, the following products are included—

(a) bags, carry bags, shopping bags, produce bags, garment bags, courier pouches, delivery bags and sacks;

(b) food-service products, including plates, bowls, cups, glasses, lids, trays, cutlery, stirrers, straws, beverage carriers, take-away containers, sachets and pouches;

(c) food, drink, tobacco, confectionery, retail, e-commerce and consumer-goods packaging, including wrappers, labels, overwrap, shrink wrap, cling film, bubble wrap, void fill, packing film, laminated or multi-layer plastic packaging and single-use polystyrene or expanded-polystyrene packaging;

(d) balloons and balloon sticks; plastic flags; decorations; confetti; streamers; artificial flowers; plastic-coated religious offerings; and single-use plastic articles used for religious or cultural observance;

(e) ear buds with plastic sticks; candy sticks; ice-cream sticks; plastic razors; plastic toothbrushes; single-use toiletry containers; wipes containing plastic; and single-use personal-care articles;

(f) plastic cigarette filters, disposable vaping products and single-use plastic components supplied with tobacco or nicotine products;

(g) plastic banners, signs, labels, stickers, badges, wristbands, tickets and promotional articles intended for one-time or short-duration use;

(h) single-use fishing gear, agricultural mulch, seedling containers, nursery bags, film or sheeting, unless specifically authorised under Part C;

(i) any oxo-degradable, biodegradable, compostable, bio-based or recycled-plastic product that is otherwise a single-use plastic product; and

(j) any component, accessory, closure, cap, liner, coating or composite product supplied primarily for one-time use with a product listed in this Part.

#### Part B — Staged effect

(1) The prohibitions in Part A apply to the manufacture of a new product line from the commencement of section 7.

(2) The manufacture, import, stocking, distribution, wholesale supply, retail sale, e-commerce sale, free distribution and institutional procurement of products in Part A cease twenty-four months after the commencement of section 7.

(3) A person may use stock lawfully acquired before the date in sub-paragraph (2) for not more than thirty months after commencement, provided that the person maintains verifiable stock records and takes no action to evade this Schedule.

(4) The appropriate Government shall provide accessible collection and safe, non-water-receptor treatment/recovery routes for surrender, take-back or disposal of existing stock before the date in sub-paragraph (2). It shall not require incineration or export of waste in a manner that transfers disproportionate health or environmental harm to another community.

#### Part C — strictly limited temporary exceptions

(1) A product otherwise covered by Part A may be supplied only where all of the following conditions are met:

  (a) it is indispensable for an emergency medical intervention, a life-saving medicine or sterile medical device, a disability-accessibility aid, a legally required safety device, or immediate disaster response;

  (b) no reasonably available reusable, non-plastic or non-single-use alternative can safely achieve the required purpose;

  (c) supply is limited to the minimum necessary quantity and is traceable from producer or importer to user or authorised collection system;

  (d) the supplier funds take-back and safe collection, and no product or waste is disposed of in a protected water receptor;

  (e) the exception is recorded in a public register containing the product, purpose, quantity, evidence, end user category, take-back plan, start date and expiry date, with only necessary personal, health, commercial or security details redacted; and

  (f) the exception expires after two years unless a new rule, following section 7(4), independently reassesses the same product and purpose.

(2) An exception under this Part does not permit routine convenience packaging, general retail supply, advertising, promotion, stockpiling, export for disposal, substitution with a materially similar product, or extension by administrative inaction.

(3) The burden of establishing every condition in this Part rests on the person claiming the exception.

### Schedule II — criminal procedure, tribunal jurisdiction, Central consequential amendment and savings

#### Part A — special criminal procedure

1. For the purposes of section 4 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023, offences under this Act shall be investigated, inquired into, tried and otherwise dealt with under that Sanhita, subject to this Part and every applicable constitutional safeguard.

2. An offence under section 23 or 24A is cognizable and non-bailable, and is triable only by a Court of Session. No arrest, search or seizure for an offence under section 24A may occur except in accordance with section 24A(7).

3. An offence under section 21, 21A, 21B, 21D or 24 is non-cognizable and bailable, and is triable only by a Court of Session.

4. An offence under section 22, 22A or 22B is non-cognizable and bailable, and is triable by a Judicial Magistrate of the First Class.

5. No court shall take cognizance of an offence under this Act except on a complaint or police report by an officer authorised under section 14, an investigating officer authorised by law, or a person permitted by the Court after giving the authorised authority an opportunity of not more than thirty days to state its position. This paragraph does not prevent a constitutional court or the National Green Tribunal from exercising its lawful jurisdiction.

6. The classification in this Part does not displace a more protective procedural right, including bail, legal representation, disclosure, a fair trial, appeal or judicial review, available under the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 or another law.

#### Part B — amendment of the National Green Tribunal Act, 2010

1. In Schedule I to the National Green Tribunal Act, 2010, after serial number 7 and the entries relating thereto, insert:—

| Serial number | Act | Act number |
|---:|---|---:|
| 8. | The Clean Air, Water Bodies, Environmental Accountability and Single-Use Plastic Prohibition Act, 2026. | [to be assigned on enactment] |

2. The amendment in paragraph 1 brings disputes raising a substantial question relating to the environment under this Act within the jurisdiction otherwise conferred by sections 14, 15 and 16 of the National Green Tribunal Act, 2010. It does not expand that Tribunal's jurisdiction beyond those sections, exclude the jurisdiction of a constitutional court, or determine a criminal charge.

#### Part C — express Central-law savings and interaction

1. The Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974, the Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981 and the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 are not repealed or amended by this Act. Their Boards, consent and direction powers, standards, inspections, prosecutions, appeals and remedies remain available, subject to section 32.

2. The Plastic Waste Management Rules, 2016 and every rule made under the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 remain in force to the extent consistent with this Act. No listed product may be supplied under a subordinate-law exemption, registration or certificate that is inconsistent with section 7 or Schedule I.

3. Where facts disclose an offence under the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988, the applicable investigation, approval, sanction, Special Judge, attachment and appeal provisions of that Act continue to apply. This Act creates no substitute route to avoid them.

4. The Representation of the People Act, 1951 and the Constitution continue to govern every electoral or membership consequence. A conviction under this Act has only the consequence that those laws provide; this Act neither adds a disqualification nor permits an executive shortcut.

5. This Act does not amend a State, Union Territory or local enactment. A State, Union Territory or local authority may apply a stronger compatible rule under its lawful competence. Within eighteen months after commencement, each appropriate Government shall publish a jurisdiction-specific concordance identifying any local amendment, repeal, adoption measure, delegated instrument or administrative arrangement required for implementation. The concordance is subject to public consultation and judicial review and cannot authorise conduct prohibited by this Act.

#### Part D — transition, records and proceedings

1. Every consent, permit or authorisation existing on the commencement date must be entered in the public register under section 15 within ninety days. The entry must identify its legal source, holder, site, waste stream, duration, conditions, monitoring record, renewal route and consistency assessment under this Act.

2. Within one hundred and eighty days after commencement, the competent authority shall issue a written consistency determination for each listed consent, permit or authorisation. An inconsistent permission is suspended to the extent of the inconsistency after notice, reasons, an opportunity to be heard and access to review; an imminent serious risk may be addressed through an interim order under applicable law.

3. Existing contracts must be reviewed under section 4(5). A lawful payment for a public water service, sanitation service, testing, emergency delivery, treatment or restoration is not void merely because it recovers transparent, regulated and audited cost; an arrangement that unlawfully commercialises water or authorises a prohibited activity is void to that extent.

4. Public records, samples, manifests, monitoring data, financial-assurance instruments, pending complaints, proceedings, restoration obligations, protected disclosures and remedies are preserved. No person may destroy, conceal or transfer them to defeat this Act or another law.

### Schedule III — technical commencement, minimum standards and performance framework

#### Part A — controlling technical references and update discipline

1. Drinking water supplied for human consumption must meet the applicable requirements and sampling/test methods of **IS 10500:2012, Drinking Water — Specification (Second Revision), as reviewed by the Bureau of Indian Standards in 2023**, or a subsequently notified standard that provides equal or greater health protection. A departure is permissible only as an emergency measure under section 11K, for the shortest practicable period, with public notice, alternative supply, a corrective plan and independent review.

2. Alternative drinking-water service during a disruption must be planned and delivered consistently with **IS 19757:2026, Alternative Drinking Water Service Provision During a Crisis — Guidelines**, and the quantity norms then specified in the applicable Indian Standard. The Government must publish the exact operative standard version, its accessible summary, its local implementation plan and every material deviation.

3. A rule made under this Schedule must identify the source text, version, issuing body, parameter, method, uncertainty, detection limit, laboratory competency, chain of custody, baseline, target, responsible authority, budget, transition support, review date and remedy. Incorporation of a standard by reference does not permit secret or paywalled enforcement criteria: the Government must publish a free accessible statement of every binding requirement and method.

4. No standard may be weakened by dilution, averaging across unrelated locations, substitution of an untested proxy, exclusion of a disproportionately affected community, undisclosed downtime, retrospective reclassification, or a permit condition inconsistent with this Act.

#### Part B — minimum essential safe-water access

1. Before a date under section 1(4), each appropriate Government shall publish a service-area baseline for every habitation and institutional service point. It shall separately report source reliability, quality, continuity, collection time, physical accessibility, affordability, disability access, household connection, public point, tanker or alternative source, and affected population.

2. The baseline and implementation plan must expressly cover low-income and informal settlements, remote rural areas, Scheduled Tribe and forest-dependent communities, nomadic groups, older persons, persons with disabilities, schools, anganwadis, health facilities, shelters, places of detention and emergency locations.

3. The authority shall provide safe alternative supply immediately where a credible test or public-health assessment shows that the existing supply is unsafe. It shall publish the risk, source, result, corrective action, alternative-supply location, expected restoration date and appeal route, subject only to necessary and reviewable privacy protection.

4. A tariff, connection, identity, tenancy or documentation rule must not deny the statutory minimum under section 2A. A service-provider recovery plan must distinguish lawful audited service cost from profit from the water itself and must be available in an accessible form.

#### Part C — sampling, point-of-use supply and tanker controls

1. Each water service provider shall maintain a published risk-based sampling plan that includes source water, treatment outlet, distribution extremities, intermittently supplied areas, public standposts, schools, health facilities, tankers, temporary points of distribution and complaint-linked locations.

2. Sampling must use a uniquely numbered, tamper-evident record of collector, time, location, source, container, preservative, method, laboratory, transport conditions and every transfer of custody. A person affected by an adverse result may request split-sample retesting under section 12.

3. A tanker or mobile-supply operator must maintain a public digital and physical manifest identifying the approved source, treatment status, vehicle and container identifier, filling time, route, delivery point, quantity, cleaning/disinfection record and test certificate. A tanker may not be used for drinking water if it has carried incompatible material or cannot demonstrate safe cleaning and traceability.

4. An adverse result, loss of traceability, significant monitoring outage or credible tampering allegation requires prompt suspension or restriction proportionate to the risk, a public warning, alternative supply, independent retesting and a reasoned restoration decision.

#### Part D — wastewater, reuse, residuals and no-dilution control

1. An operator may reuse treated wastewater only for a specifically identified use after demonstrating, through independently published risk assessment and monitoring, that the use does not endanger people, workers, food safety, groundwater, a water body or ecological systems.

2. A treatment, reuse, desalination, filtration or recovery process must account for sludge, brine, concentrate, filter media, chemicals, microplastics and every other residual. A residual may not be diluted, relabelled, transferred, exported or stored so as to evade a treatment, disposal, monitoring or restoration duty.

3. A compliance claim based on dilution, mixing, seasonal flow, transfer to another receptor or off-site movement is invalid unless the authority demonstrates on published evidence that the receiving environment and affected community remain protected and the claim is consistent with section 5, section 6 and the applicable standard.

4. Every prescribed facility shall publish its design capacity, actual inflow, treatment performance, bypasses, downtime, residual stream, discharge/reuse destination, compliance record and corrective actions at least monthly.

#### Part E — ecological flows, groundwater and large withdrawals

1. Before authorising a prescribed large withdrawal, diversion or recharge-affecting activity, the authority must publish an aquifer or catchment baseline covering seasonal availability, recharge, current withdrawals, water quality, dependent ecosystems, downstream interests, essential domestic use, customary/community use, drought risk and uncertainty.

2. The authorisation must specify a metered limit, review date, ecological-flow or groundwater-protection condition, data publication duty, shortage curtailment sequence, restoration plan, financial assurance and appeal route. No authorisation may reduce the priority protections in section 6D.

3. Meter data, calibration records, exceptions, outage periods and enforcement outcomes must be published at least monthly in a usable non-proprietary format, with necessary redaction subject to independent review.

#### Part F — emerging contaminants, laboratories and quality assurance

1. The Central Government shall maintain a priority-watch list for pharmaceuticals, antimicrobial residues and resistant organisms, microplastics, PFAS, heavy metals, pesticides and other contaminants identified under section 11J. Each entry must state the reason for listing, known uncertainty, matrix, analytical method, reporting limit, sampling frequency, responsible laboratory and review date.

2. A laboratory result used for enforcement, public warning, suspension or restoration must be generated by a laboratory competent for the relevant method, with calibration, quality-control, proficiency testing where available, record retention and conflict disclosure. The accused and affected community must have a meaningful route to independent retesting consistent with evidence preservation and public safety.

3. The Central Government shall publish anonymised method-performance data, laboratory accreditation/competency scope, audit outcomes, invalidated results, corrective action and conflicts. It shall not publish personal-health information or information that creates a material security risk except as lawfully necessary and proportionate.

#### Part G — commencement gates, audit and correction

1. No operational duty in Parts B to F may commence for a service area until the capacity-assurance statement under section 29B identifies the named responsible body, baseline, technical reference, funding, workforce, procurement safeguards, laboratory and data capacity, accessible complaint route, emergency alternative supply, independent audit and correction date.

2. The first independent audit must occur within twelve months after operational commencement and annually thereafter. It must test a representative sample of locations, affected groups, service providers and records, publish uncertainty and limitations, and provide a corrective action plan with named owners and dates.

3. A missed target or failed capacity gate requires publication within fourteen days, an interim protection plan, a revised time-bound plan and a review route under section 19. It does not excuse ongoing pollution or denial of emergency minimum access.

## Statement of Objects and Reasons — working version

India’s current environmental and water-pollution framework contains significant protections and waste-management rules. The scale and persistence of pollution entering rivers, lakes, wetlands, groundwater and connected drains, together with the continuing leakage of short-lived plastic products, requires a coordinated statutory response that cannot be reduced to selective enforcement or unaccountable executive discretion.

The Bill creates a national minimum floor. It prohibits deliberate or reckless disposal of sewage and specified waste into protected water receptors, including groundwater bodies and recharge areas; addresses serious air pollution and climate-pollutant releases; prohibits profit-making water commodification while allowing transparent regulated recovery of necessary service costs; protects public water trust, ecological flows, essential-access priority, large-withdrawal transparency and stressed-aquifer transition; requires universal sanitation, safe wastewater reuse, water-sensitive development, source-area protection, emergency readiness, emerging-contaminant response, public data protection and accountable water services; protects gender-responsive access, community participation, customary water practices and downstream interests; requires prevention-first producer and industrial duties; regulates point-of-use and tanker safety, high-risk-sector water impacts, legacy contaminated sites, service performance and capacity-gated implementation; prohibits commercial activity involving listed single-use plastic products; requires transparent public protection, restoration, legacy-pollution remediation, mapping, monitoring, waste-management capacity, financial assurance, waste-chain traceability and public reporting; creates enforceable complaint and remedy routes; and establishes proportionate offences for serious wrongdoing, aggravated contamination, monitoring or record tampering, false environmental claims, deliberate water-safety misinformation, corruption and retaliation.

The Bill provides for scientific evidence, public consultation, federal cooperation, just transition, accessible alternatives, content-neutral management of religious waste, independent audit, due process, judicial review and periodic democratic correction. It establishes a statutory right to clean water and clean-air protection, a minimum essential access guarantee, affordability and non-disconnection safeguards, groundwater and recharge protection, a need-based audited restoration fund, environmental-defender protection, information/participation/remedy rights and a non-regression rule. It protects constitutional equality, accountable public power and judicial remedies through mandatory conflict disclosure and recusal, public decision and political-contact records, protected disclosures, auditable case allocation, recovery of public loss and unlawful gain, direct remedies for unjustified inaction, and periodic selective-enforcement review. It invalidates deliberate evasion and imposes serious, individually proven consequences on public servants, Ministers, elected political officeholders, political appointees and entities for intentional or knowing circumvention, whether or not an improper benefit is ultimately obtained. It does not criminalise mere presence near waste, ordinary religious practice, good-faith reporting, good-faith documented technical interpretation, systemic public failure without individual fault and legal responsibility, or a good-faith legislative speech or vote by itself.

## Memorandum Regarding Delegated Legislation — working version

Clauses 6C and 7 permit limited cost-recovery and product scheduling rules only after evidence, consultation, transition and parliamentary safeguards. Clauses 2A, 8B, 8C, 11B to 11K and 28 permit rules on the minimum essential access standard, sanitation and reuse, recharge design, high-risk criteria, financial assurance, waste-chain and shipment records, technical monitoring, groundwater safeguards, source-area protection, restoration-fund administration, climate-pollutant and emerging-contaminant standards, emergency planning, public-data safeguards, independent audit process, technical protocols, forms and transition implementation. The Bill expressly prevents delegated legislation from creating offences, raising maximum penalties, expanding coercive powers, rewriting core definitions or creating a general water-disposal or water-commodification exemption.
