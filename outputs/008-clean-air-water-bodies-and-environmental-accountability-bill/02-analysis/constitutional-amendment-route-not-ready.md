# Proposed Constitutional Amendment Route — Not Ready for Introduction

## Bottom line

The Clean Air, Water Bodies, Environmental Accountability and Single-Use Plastic Prohibition Bill can create a strong **statutory** right and statutory duties. It cannot by itself create a new fundamental right or entrench itself against a future Parliament. Those outcomes require a separate Constitution Amendment Bill under Article 368, subject to the Constitution's basic structure and judicial review.

## Working constitutional text — for specialist review

> **The Constitution (Amendment) Bill, 2026 — working text only**
>
> **1.** This Act may be called the Constitution (Amendment) Act, 2026.
>
> **2.** After article 21A of the Constitution, the following article shall be inserted, namely:—
>
> **“21B. Right to safe and clean water and a healthy environment.—**
>
> (1) Every person has the right to equitable, non-discriminatory and sufficient access to safe and clean water necessary for life, health and dignity, and to protection against serious environmental pollution affecting life, health and ecological systems.
>
> (2) The State shall take reasonable, evidence-based, non-discriminatory and progressively effective measures, consistent with the rights guaranteed by this Part, to respect, protect and fulfil the right under clause (1); prevent serious pollution; secure sanitation, safe wastewater management, source-area protection, climate and disaster resilience; protect gender-responsive and community access; provide timely information and participation; remedy unlawful harm; and secure effective judicial remedies.
>
> (3) Water is a common life-sustaining resource held in public trust for present and future generations and shall not be treated as an object of commercial speculation or profit-making sale. A law may permit only transparent, non-discriminatory and regulated recovery of necessary treatment, delivery and maintenance costs, while preserving minimum essential access, ecological flows, recharge and equitable allocation during scarcity.
>
> (4) Parliament may by law, and a State Legislature subject to this Constitution may by law, prescribe standards, duties, institutions, remedies and equitable transition measures for carrying this article into effect. Such law shall preserve due process, federalism, equality, access to justice and judicial review.”
>
> **3.** In the article so inserted, after clause (4), the following clauses shall be inserted, namely:—
>
> “(5) Every person has the right to timely environmental information, meaningful and accessible participation in material environmental decisions, and an effective remedy for unlawful pollution, denial of essential access, discrimination or public-authority inaction.
>
> (6) The State shall not regress from the minimum essential content of the rights under this article except by law for a compelling and independently evidenced public purpose, after considering less restrictive alternatives and securing equal or stronger substitute protections, subject to judicial review.
>
> (7) The Union and every State shall lay before the appropriate Legislature, at intervals not exceeding five years, a public report on access to safe water, sanitation, pollution, environmental health, inequality of impact, restoration, funding, implementation, emergency readiness and corrective action under this article.”

## Required constitutional procedure and limits

- Article 368 requires a Constitution Amendment Bill to pass each House by a majority of the total membership and at least two-thirds of members present and voting. Some amendments additionally require ratification by at least half of State Legislatures.
- This working text does not claim that a particular amendment route has already been verified as requiring State ratification, or that it is immune from basic-structure review.
- The final amendment proposal needs independent constitutional, federalism, environmental, public-health, equality, disability/accessibility, tribal/community and fiscal review; a public consultation; and current State/UT law mapping.

## Relationship to the main Bill

The main Bill supplies the detailed offences, safeguards, institutions, remedies, transition and restoration duties. The proposed amendment would provide the constitutional-right foundation; it should not be used to remove ordinary constitutional safeguards or to insulate environmental decisions from democratic and judicial review.
