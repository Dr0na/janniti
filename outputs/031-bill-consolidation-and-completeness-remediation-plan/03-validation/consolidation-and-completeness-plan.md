# Bill Consolidation and Completeness Remediation Plan

## Bottom line and assumptions

**Recommendation: do not delete substantive objectives merely to reduce the number of Bills.** Consolidate only shared machinery, offences, remedies, records, and institutions. Each retained measure must then pass a topic-completeness gate before further drafting or introduction.

This plan concerns repository drafts, assumed to be proposed Union-level measures unless a later legislative-competence review assigns a State, Concurrent List, or constitutional-amendment route. It is a **policy option and drafting plan**, not current law or an introduction-readiness finding.

## Operating rule: merge machinery, preserve outcomes

For every consolidation, use this allocation:

| Layer | What belongs there |
|---|---|
| Constitutional amendment | Only the precise constitutional change and mandatory transition; never ordinary administration, offences, or detailed schemes. |
| Framework statute / code | Common definitions, rights, institutional boundaries, data rules, appeals, enforcement coordination, funding architecture, review, and repeal/amendment schedule. |
| Sector module / amendment | The subject-specific duty, beneficiary entitlement, standard, offence, and remedy that cannot sensibly apply across sectors. |
| Rules | Technical forms, interoperable data specifications, thresholds supported by evidence, staffing detail, and accessible procedure—never core rights, offences, independent-body powers, or appeal rights. |

## Non-negotiable completeness gate

No Bill is ready for the introduction gate until its drafting record answers every item below with a provision, a reasoned decision not to legislate, or a cited existing-law cross-reference.

1. **Problem and scope:** defined harm, evidence base, affected people, and express exclusions.
2. **Authority:** constitutional entry, federal allocation, amendment need, existing-law amendments/repeals, and delegated-legislation limits.
3. **Rights and equality:** Articles and interests affected; necessity/proportionality; non-discrimination, accessibility, language access, anti-vendetta safeguards, privacy, and remedies.
4. **Institutional map:** one lead body per function; appointment, budget, records, conflicts, external audit, appeal, removal, and non-interference rules.
5. **Process:** objective triggers, notice, hearing, reasons, evidence standard, time limits, public information, and emergency limits.
6. **Enforcement and repair:** civil, administrative, disciplinary, and criminal boundaries; no double punishment; referral rules; compensation/restoration; legal aid and standing.
7. **Delivery:** fiscal memorandum, staffing, State/local roles, procurement, training, digital/offline access, phased commencement, and transition from existing schemes.
8. **Data and transparency:** minimum data, retention/deletion, security, public reporting, source protection, audit trail, and independent access to records.
9. **Accountability over time:** outcome metrics, disaggregated equality measures, independent evaluation, statutory review, correction trigger, sunset where exceptional, and repeal route.
10. **Cross-instrument coherence:** definitions, regulator, appeal forum, offences, recovery, court jurisdiction, and precedence expressly reconciled with every related Bill.

Failure of any item is a drafting blocker, not a matter to be deferred to rules.

## Sequence and safeguards

1. Freeze each existing introduction-ready instrument as a historical draft; do not silently overwrite it.
2. For each package, create a crosswalk: existing clause → retained/modified/repealed/new → destination instrument → reason.
3. Complete India research packs and legislative-competence memoranda before drafting consolidated text.
4. Draft the framework first, then each module; prohibit a new regulator, offence, special court, data system, or compensation scheme unless the crosswalk proves it is unique and necessary.
5. Run legislative lint, constitutional stress test, institutional-power map, citation verification, public deliberation, and democratic-impact monitoring for every resulting instrument.
6. Run the completeness gate twice: before public consultation and again before the introduction-readiness gate.
7. Publish a plain-language comparison explaining what was retained, merged, or not pursued and why.

## Priority order

1. Electoral democracy, police/criminal justice, and public assets: high risk of conflicting coercive powers or duplicate institutions.
2. Health/food/products and digital work/AI: high risk of split regulator, data, and consumer-remedy arrangements.
3. Judicial administration and environmental interfaces: resolve constitutional and federal boundaries before substantive redrafting.

## Residual risks

This plan cannot make a Bill complete by itself. Completeness depends on primary-source research, fiscal and administrative evidence, consultation with affected groups, federal competence analysis, and qualified review. A concise Bill is not incomplete if it deliberately incorporates a clearly identified existing regime; a lengthy Bill is incomplete if it leaves core power, remedy, funding, or review questions unresolved.
