# Indian Law Landscape Research Pack: Anti-Defection Laws, Tenth Schedule Reform, and Electoral Integrity

## 1. Scope and search record

- Matter ID: 002
- Topic and objective: Formulate a comprehensive Constitutional Amendment Bill ("The Constitution (One Hundred and Thirty-Sixth Amendment) Bill, 2026") and companion statutory framework amending the Tenth Schedule, Article 102(2), Article 191(2), Article 75(1B), Article 164(1B), and the Representation of the People Act, 1951. The law must eliminate all group defection and merger exemptions (repealing Paragraph 4 of the Tenth Schedule), institute mandatory immediate seat vacation and disqualification upon defection, mandate fresh electoral mandates for defecting members, bar defecting members from holding ministerial office or contesting elections for 6 years in cases of horse-trading/corruption, transfer defection adjudication from the Speaker to the Election Commission of India (ECI) with a 30-day binding timeline, and penalize political cartels/bribers financing post-election horse-trading.
- Jurisdiction(s): Union of India (All-India constitutional and statutory application across Parliament and State/UT Legislative Assemblies).
- As-of date: 2026-07-27
- Search terms and synonyms: Tenth Schedule, anti-defection law, 52nd Constitutional Amendment 1985, 91st Constitutional Amendment 2003, Paragraph 4 merger exemption, horse trading, resort politics, Kihoto Hollohan, Keisham Meghachandra Singh, Subhash Desai, Speaker adjudication delays, ECI adjudication, Representation of the People Act 1951.
- Requested legal question: How can India constitutionally eliminate all defection loopholes (including the 2/3rd merger exception), transfer adjudication authority away from partisan Speakers to the ECI, enforce immediate seat dissolution, and introduce severe deterrent disqualifications against political horse-trading?

## 2. Executive finding and reliance limits

The existing anti-defection framework under the Tenth Schedule to the Constitution of India (inserted in 1985 and amended in 2003) suffers from structural flaws:
1. **The 2/3rd Merger Loophole (Paragraph 4)**: While individual defections are penalized, wholesale defections executed by 2/3rd of a legislature party are exempted under Paragraph 4 under the guise of "merger". This has incentivized commercial horse-trading, organized defection cartels, and the engineered collapse of democratically elected state governments.
2. **Partisan Speaker Adjudication (Paragraph 6)**: Leaving defection disputes to the unconstrained discretion of the Speaker/Chairman has enabled partisan delays, selective non-adjudication, and protection of defectors during crucial trust votes (*Kihoto Hollohan*, *Keisham Meghachandra*, *Subhash Desai*).
3. **Absence of Severe Financial & Political Deterrence**: Current rules allow defecting members to be rewarded with ministerial berths or immediate re-nomination in by-elections funded by corrupt cartels.

A comprehensive Constitutional Amendment under Article 368 is legally required to amend the Tenth Schedule, repeal Paragraph 4, transfer adjudication to the ECI, and enforce a 6-year electoral/ministerial ban for corrupt defection.

## 5. State and Union Territory coverage

The Tenth Schedule applies uniformly to all State Legislative Assemblies and Legislative Councils (including UT Assemblies with legislatures like Delhi, Puducherry, Jammu & Kashmir). Reform of the Tenth Schedule under Article 368 requires ratification by at least 50% of State Assemblies.

## 6. Bills, amendments, ordinances, history, and Gazette

- Law Commission of India Report No. 170 (1999) recommended total deletion of split and merger exemptions under the Tenth Schedule and pre-election coalition binding rules.
- Law Commission of India Report No. 255 (2015) recommended transferring defection adjudication from the Speaker to the President/Governor acting on the binding advice of the Election Commission of India.

## 7. Delegated law and implementation

Subordinate rules (Members of Lok Sabha/Rajya Sabha (Disqualification on Ground of Defection) Rules 1985) currently prescribe procedure for petitions before the Speaker. Under the proposed Bill, procedural rulemaking is transferred to the Election Commission of India under Article 324.

## 9. Rights, equality, federalism, and public-interest assessment

- **Democratic Integrity**: Eliminating post-election defection protects popular sovereignty and voters' mandate from being corrupted by monetary gratification or political horse-trading.
- **Freedom of Conscience vs Party Discipline**: Members retain full freedom of conscience to vote against their party or leave it, but must face immediate seat vacation and seek re-election from voters.
- **Federal Stability**: Protects State Governments from engineered collapse caused by wholesale 2/3rd defections and partisan Speaker delays.
