# Draft Legislation and Policy Analysis: The Anti-Defection Reform and Electoral Integrity Bill, 2026

## 1. Bottom Line and Legal Expert Analysis

- **Jurisdiction**: Union of India (Parliament of India, operating under Article 368 of the Constitution of India, amending the Tenth Schedule, Article 102(2), Article 191(2), Article 75(1B), Article 164(1B), and the Representation of the People Act, 1951).
- **As-of Date**: 2026-07-27
- **Client / Deliverable**: Complete legislative formulation, constitutional amendment text, companion statutory provisions, power map, and implementation framework formulated in response to the user's request for a strong, loophole-free anti-defection law.
- **Core Legal Findings & Expert Reform Architecture**:
  1. **Complete Deletion of Group Defection / Merger Exemptions (Paragraph 4 Repeal)**: The 2/3rd merger exemption under Paragraph 4 of the Tenth Schedule has been systematically exploited to conduct wholesale horse-trading, "resort politics", and the engineered collapse of democratically elected state governments. This Bill completely repeals Paragraph 4. There is no exemption for 2/3rd or 100% defection. Any member defecting or joining another party automatically vacates their seat.
  2. **Mandatory Seat Vacation & Fresh Electoral Mandate**: Defection results in immediate, non-negotiable seat dissolution. Members wishing to change party allegiance must resign, return to the electorate, and seek a fresh mandate through a by-election.
  3. **Transfer of Adjudication from Speaker to Election Commission of India (ECI)**: Addressing the chronic judicial concern over partisan Speaker delays (*Keisham Meghachandra Singh* 2020 SC, *Subhash Desai* 2023 SC), adjudication authority is transferred from the Speaker/Chairman to the Election Commission of India, with a mandatory **30-day binding timeline** and **automatic interim suspension of voting rights** during pending proceedings.
  4. **Deterrent Sanctions against Horse-Trading & Corrupt Cartels**: Introduces a **6-year mandatory bar** on holding ministerial office or remunerative political posts, a **6-year electoral ban** and **5-10 years rigorous imprisonment** for corrupt defection involving financial gratification, and **7-14 years imprisonment** for commercial cartels or party leaders funding horse-trading.

## 2. Problem, Evidence, and Existing-Law Map

Existing Indian legal frameworks governing anti-defection suffer from structural defects:
- **Paragraph 4 (Merger Exception) Defection Loophole**: Enacted via the 52nd Amendment (1985) and retained in the 91st Amendment (2003), Paragraph 4 protects defections if 2/3rd of the legislature party agrees to a merger. In practice, political cartels bypass party voters by engineering 2/3rd defections in legislature parties without any genuine organizational merger (*Subhash Desai* 2023 SC).
- **Partisan Speaker Adjudication (Paragraph 6)**: The Speaker/Chairman is an elected member of the ruling party. Speakers frequently delay defection petitions for years while allowing defectors to vote in trust motions, invalidating democratic mandates (*Keisham Meghachandra Singh* 2020 SC).
- **Inadequate Deterrence**: Defecting members are routinely rewarded with immediate Cabinet berths or election funding by opposition cartels, subverting voter intent.

## 6. Draft Legal Text: Statutory Bill and Companion Constitutional Amendment Bill

### PART A: THE CONSTITUTION (ONE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIXTH AMENDMENT) BILL, 2026

**BILL NO. ______ OF 2026**

*A Bill further to amend the Constitution of India to eliminate all exemptions for defection under the Tenth Schedule, to transfer adjudication of defection petitions to the Election Commission of India, to enforce immediate seat vacation upon defection, and to bar defecting members from holding ministerial office or contesting elections for six years.*

BE it enacted by Parliament in the Seventy-Seventh Year of the Republic of India as follows:—

**1. Short title and commencement.**
(1) This Act may be called the Constitution (One Hundred and Thirty-Sixth Amendment) Act, 2026.
(2) It shall come into force on such date as the Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, appoint, being not later than thirty days from the date of Presidential Assent.

**2. Amendment of Tenth Schedule.**
In the Tenth Schedule to the Constitution,—
(a) **Repeal of Paragraph 4**: Paragraph 4 (Option on ground of merger) shall be omitted;
(b) **Amendment of Paragraph 2**: In Paragraph 2, after sub-paragraph (3), the following sub-paragraph shall be inserted, namely:—
    > "(4) Any member of a House who is disqualified under this Paragraph or who joins another political party after election shall immediately cease to be a member of the House, and their seat shall stand vacant by operation of law from the date of such defection or joining."
(c) **Substitution of Paragraph 6**: For Paragraph 6, the following Paragraph shall be substituted, namely:—
    > **"6. Decision on questions as to disqualification on ground of defection.**
    > (1) If any question arises as to whether a member of a House has become subject to disqualification under this Schedule, the question shall be referred for the decision of the Election Commission of India, and its decision shall be final.
    > (2) The Election Commission of India shall render its final decision on any reference made under sub-paragraph (1) within a period of **thirty days** from the date of receipt of the reference.
    > (3) Upon the filing of a reference under sub-paragraph (1), the voting rights of the member concerned in the House shall stand suspended until the final decision of the Election Commission of India."

**3. Amendment of Article 75 and Article 164.**
In Article 75 and Article 164 of the Constitution, for clause (1B), the following clause shall be substituted, namely:—
> "(1B) A member of a House belonging to any political party who is disqualified for being a member of the House under Paragraph 2 of the Tenth Schedule or who defects from such political party shall be disqualified to be appointed as a Minister or to hold any remunerative political or statutory post under the Union or State Governments for a period of **six years** from the date of their disqualification or seat vacation."

### PART B: THE REPRESENTATION OF THE PEOPLE AND ELECTORAL INTEGRITY (AMENDMENT) BILL, 2026

**BILL NO. ______ OF 2026**

*A Bill further to amend the Representation of the People Act, 1951, and the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988, to criminalize political horse-trading, impose electoral disqualification for corrupt defection, and regulate by-elections.*

BE it enacted by Parliament in the Seventy-Seventh Year of the Republic of India as follows:—

**1. Short title and commencement.**
(1) This Act may be called the Representation of the People and Electoral Integrity (Amendment) Act, 2026.
(2) It extends to the whole of India.

**2. Insertion of Section 8B in Act 43 of 1951.**
After Section 8A of the Representation of the People Act, 1951, the following Section shall be inserted, namely:—
> **"8B. Disqualification on ground of Corrupt Defection and Horse-Trading.**
> (1) Whosoever, being an elected member of Parliament or a State Legislature, defects from their political party or votes contrary to party direction in consideration of any financial gratification, monetary inducement, promise of office, or commercial benefit shall be guilty of the offence of Corrupt Defection.
> (2) Whosoever commits the offence of Corrupt Defection shall be punished with **rigorous imprisonment for a term which shall not be less than five years but which may extend to ten years**, and shall also be liable to fine.
> (3) A person convicted under sub-section (2) shall be disqualified from contesting any election to Parliament, State Legislatures, or local bodies for a period of **six years** from the date of conviction."

**3. Offence of Financing Horse-Trading.**
Whosoever offers, promises, pays, or facilitates any monetary gratification or political benefit to induce an elected member to defect shall be punished under Section 12 of the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988, with **rigorous imprisonment for a term which shall not be less than seven years but which may extend to fourteen years**, and shall suffer mandatory forfeiture of assets.

## 7. Clause-by-Clause Explanatory Notes

- **Part A Clause 2(a)**: Completely repeals Paragraph 4 of the Tenth Schedule. Eliminates the 2/3rd merger exception so that group defections carry the same immediate disqualification as individual defections.
- **Part A Clause 2(b)**: Amends Paragraph 2 to mandate immediate, automatic seat vacation upon defection, requiring defectors to seek a fresh mandate through a by-election.
- **Part A Clause 2(c)**: Substitutes Paragraph 6 to transfer defection adjudication from the Speaker to the Election Commission of India with a strict 30-day statutory timeline and automatic interim suspension of voting rights.
- **Part A Clause 3**: Amends Articles 75(1B) and 164(1B) to bar defecting members from holding any ministerial or remunerative public post for 6 years.
- **Part B Clause 2-3**: Amends the Representation of the People Act 1951 and Prevention of Corruption Act 1988 to introduce 5-10 years imprisonment and a 6-year electoral ban for corrupt defection/horse-trading, and 7-14 years imprisonment for bribers/cartels.

## 8. Equality, Freedom, Public Goods, and Fiscal Impact Assessment

- **Public Goods & Electoral Trust**: Restores public trust in elections by ensuring that votes cast for a party ticket cannot be bought or traded post-election.
- **Constitutional Rights Balance**: Protects the right of conscience by allowing members to leave their party, but enforces the democratic principle that they must return to voters for re-election.
- **Fiscal Impact**: Minor administrative cost for conducting by-elections, offset by massive savings from preventing state government destabilization and corrupt budget diversions.
