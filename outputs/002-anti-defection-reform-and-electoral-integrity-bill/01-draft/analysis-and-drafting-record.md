# Analysis and Drafting Record: Anti-Defection Reform and Electoral Integrity Bill, 2026

## 1. Filing Target and Legislative Authority

- **Target Legislature**: Parliament of India (Lok Sabha / Rajya Sabha).
- **Competence**: Article 368 of the Constitution of India (Constitutional Amendment requiring 2/3rd majority in Parliament + ratification by 50%+ State Assemblies under Article 368(2) Proviso), amending Tenth Schedule, Articles 102(2), 191(2), 75(1B), 164(1B), 361, and Representation of the People Act 1951.
- **Year**: 2026 (Seventy-Seventh Year of the Republic of India).

---

## 2. Problem, Evidence, and Existing-Law Map

Existing Indian legal frameworks governing anti-defection suffer from structural defects:
- **Paragraph 4 (Merger Exception) Defection Loophole**: Enacted via the 52nd Amendment (1985) and retained in the 91st Amendment (2003), Paragraph 4 protects defections if 2/3rd of the legislature party agrees to a merger. In practice, political cartels bypass party voters by engineering 2/3rd defections in legislature parties without any genuine organizational merger (*Subhash Desai* 2023 SC).
- **Discretionary Delay & Partisan Speaker Adjudication**: Unconstrained discretion over defection petitions enables partisan delays, selective non-adjudication, and protection of defectors during crucial trust votes (*Keisham Meghachandra Singh* 2020 SC).
- **Executive Immunity & Weaponization Risks**: Ruling ministers and party executives frequently orchestrate horse-trading, while penal laws targeting constitutional heads (Prime Minister / Chief Ministers) risk being weaponized by political adversaries filing bad-faith complaints.

---

## 3. Core Legal Reform Refinements & Anti-Misuse Safeguards

1. **Automatic Seat Dissolution by Operation of Law**: The seat of a defecting member stands automatically dissolved from the moment of defection. Neither Courts, nor Speakers, nor the ECI hold discretionary power to stay or delay dissolution.
2. **Prior Judicial Screening Gate for Prime Minister & Chief Ministers (Section 8C(3))**: No police report, FIR, or investigation under Section 8C against the Prime Minister or a Chief Minister may be registered except upon prior written certification by a **3-Judge Bench of the Supreme Court of India** confirming the existence of *prima facie* verifiable financial or documentary evidence.
3. **Preservation of Office Pending Conviction (Section 8C(4))**: Cabinet forfeiture and disqualification take effect only upon final conviction by a Special Court, preventing unproven allegations from forcing the premature collapse of an elected national government.
4. **Severe Penalty for Malicious Complaints (Section 8D)**: Filing a false, frivolous, or politically motivated complaint against the Prime Minister or Ministers carries **3 to 7 years rigorous imprisonment** and a mandatory fine.
