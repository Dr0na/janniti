## THE CONSTITUTION (ONE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIXTH AMENDMENT) BILL, 2026

### A

### BILL

further to amend the Constitution of India to eliminate all exemptions for defection under the Tenth Schedule, to mandate automatic seat dissolution by operation of law upon defection, to transfer administrative notification duties to the Election Commission of India, to bar defecting members from holding ministerial office, and to strip executive immunity for political horse-trading.

BE it enacted by Parliament in the Seventy-Seventh Year of the Republic of India as follows:—

**1. Short title and commencement.**
(1) This Act may be called the Constitution (One Hundred and Thirty-Sixth Amendment) Act, 2026.
(2) It shall come into force on such date as the Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, appoint, being not later than thirty days from the date of Presidential Assent.

**2. Amendment of Tenth Schedule.**
In the Tenth Schedule to the Constitution,—
(a) Paragraph 4 shall be omitted;

(b) For Paragraph 2, the following Paragraph shall be substituted, namely:—
  "2. Automatic Disqualification and Seat Dissolution on ground of Defection.—
  (1) Subject to the provisions of this Schedule, a member of a House belonging to any political party shall be disqualified for being a member of the House, and their seat shall stand automatically dissolved and vacant by operation of law from the precise date and moment when such member—
  (a) voluntarily gives up their membership of such political party; or

  (b) joins any other political party after election; or

  (c) votes or abstains from voting in such House contrary to any direction issued by the political party to which they belong.



  (2) No Court, Speaker, Chairman, President, Governor, or Authority shall possess any power or discretion to stay, delay, condone, or suspend the automatic dissolution of seat under sub-paragraph (1).";

(c) For Paragraph 6, the following Paragraph shall be substituted, namely:—
  "6. Administrative Notification by Election Commission of India.—
  (1) Upon receiving factual notification or evidence of an act specified in Paragraph 2, the Election Commission of India shall, within seven days, issue a formal notification recording the automatic dissolution of the seat and initiating by-election proceedings.

  (2) Upon the receipt of evidence under sub-paragraph (1), the voting rights and legislative privileges of the member concerned in the House shall stand suspended immediately."

**3. Amendment of Article 75 and Article 164.**
In Article 75 and Article 164 of the Constitution, for clause (1B), the following clause shall be substituted, namely:—
"(1B) A member of a House belonging to any political party whose seat stands dissolved under Paragraph 2 of the Tenth Schedule or who defects from such political party shall be disqualified to be appointed as a Minister or to hold any remunerative political or statutory post under the Union or State Governments for a period of six years from the date of such seat dissolution."

**4. Amendment of Article 361.**
In Article 361 of the Constitution, after clause (4), the following clause shall be inserted, namely:—
"(5) Nothing in this Article shall extend any immunity, privilege, or protection to any person, including the Prime Minister, the Home Minister, any Minister of the Union or State, or any Governor, in respect of any act involving the financing, inducement, direction, or facilitation of political defection or horse-trading under the Representation of the People Act, 1951."

## THE REPRESENTATION OF THE PEOPLE AND ELECTORAL INTEGRITY (AMENDMENT) BILL, 2026

### A

### BILL

further to amend the Representation of the People Act, 1951, and the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988, to criminalize political horse-trading by executive heads, ministers, and ruling party leaders, impose mandatory imprisonment and asset forfeiture, and enforce electoral disqualifications.

BE it enacted by Parliament in the Seventy-Seventh Year of the Republic of India as follows:—

**1. Short title and commencement.**
(1) This Act may be called the Representation of the People and Electoral Integrity (Amendment) Act, 2026.
(2) It extends to the whole of India.

**2. Insertion of Section 8B and Section 8C in Act 43 of 1951.**
After Section 8A of the Representation of the People Act, 1951, the following Sections shall be inserted, namely:—
"8B. Disqualification on ground of Corrupt Defection.—
(1) Whosoever, being an elected member of Parliament or a State Legislature, defects from their political party or votes contrary to party direction in consideration of any financial gratification, monetary inducement, promise of office, or commercial benefit shall be guilty of the offence of Corrupt Defection.

(2) Whosoever commits the offence of Corrupt Defection shall be punished with rigorous imprisonment for a term which shall not be less than five years but which may extend to ten years, and shall also be liable to fine.

(3) A person convicted under sub-section (2) shall be disqualified from contesting any election to Parliament, State Legislatures, or local bodies for a period of six years from the date of conviction."

"8C. Offence of Financing or Directing Horse-Trading by Executive Authorities and Party Leaders.—
(1) Whosoever offers, promises, pays, facilitates, directs, or authorizes any monetary gratification, ministerial post, or political benefit to induce an elected member to defect—explicitly including any Minister, the Home Minister, the Prime Minister, a Chief Minister, or ruling party office-bearer—shall be guilty of the offence of Executive Horse-Trading.

(2) Whosoever commits the offence of Executive Horse-Trading shall be punished with rigorous imprisonment for a term which shall not be less than seven years but which may extend to fourteen years, and shall suffer mandatory forfeiture of all personal assets.

(3) Any Minister, Home Minister, Prime Minister, or Chief Minister convicted under sub-section (2) shall immediately forfeit executive office and shall be permanently disqualified from holding any public or political office."

## STATEMENT OF OBJECTS AND REASONS

Political defection by elected representatives subverts the democratic mandate of voters, undermines stability in Parliament and State Assemblies, and promotes commercial horse-trading. The Tenth Schedule, enacted by the 52nd Amendment (1985) and amended by the 91st Amendment (2003), contained Paragraph 4, which protected defections executed by two-thirds of a legislature party under the guise of a merger.

In practice, this merger exception has been systematically exploited to conduct wholesale defections without genuine party mergers. Furthermore, discretionary delays under Paragraph 6 have enabled partisan protection of defectors during crucial trust votes (*Keisham Meghachandra Singh v. Speaker* 2020). Executive heads and ministers have frequently used state machinery and immunity to orchestrate defections.

This legislative package addresses these systemic defects by:—
1. **Automatic Seat Dissolution**: Mandating immediate, self-executing seat dissolution by operation of law from the moment of defection, removing any discretionary authority to stay dissolution.

2. **Repealing Paragraph 4**: Completely eliminating all merger and group defection exemptions.

3. **Executive Accountability without Immunity**: Amending Section 8C of the Representation of the People Act, 1951, and Article 361 of the Constitution to strip executive immunity for Prime Ministers, Home Ministers, Ministers, and ruling party leaders financing or directing horse-trading, imposing 7 to 14 years imprisonment, Cabinet forfeiture, asset confiscation, and a 10-year electoral ban.

The Bill seeks to achieve these objectives.

## FINANCIAL MEMORANDUM

Clause 2 of Part A provides for the 7-day administrative notification of by-elections by the Election Commission of India upon seat dissolution under the Tenth Schedule.

1. **Non-Recurring Expenditure**: An estimated initial capital outlay of Rupees Five Crores (₹50,000,000) will be required for establishing automated digital case notification portals for the Election Commission of India.

2. **Recurring Expenditure**: An estimated annual recurring expenditure of Rupees Two Crores (₹20,000,000) will be incurred for administrative processing and immediate Gazette notification of seat vacations.

The expenditure will be met from the Consolidated Fund of India.

---

## MEMORANDUM REGARDING DELEGATED LEGISLATION

Clause 2(c) of Part A empowers the Election Commission of India to make rules regulating the administrative verification and notification procedure for seat vacations under the Tenth Schedule.

The delegation of legislative power is of a routine procedural character to ensure 7-day administrative notification. All rules made by the Election Commission under this Act shall be published in the Official Gazette and laid before Parliament.
