## THE CONSTITUTION (ONE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIXTH AMENDMENT) BILL, 2026

### A

### BILL

further to amend the Constitution of India to eliminate all exemptions for defection under the Tenth Schedule, to transfer adjudication of defection petitions to the Election Commission of India, to enforce immediate seat vacation upon defection, and to bar defecting members from holding ministerial office or contesting elections for six years.

BE it enacted by Parliament in the Seventy-Seventh Year of the Republic of India as follows:—

**1. Short title and commencement.**
(1) This Act may be called the Constitution (One Hundred and Thirty-Sixth Amendment) Act, 2026.
(2) It shall come into force on such date as the Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, appoint, being not later than thirty days from the date of Presidential Assent.

**2. Amendment of Tenth Schedule.**
In the Tenth Schedule to the Constitution,—
(a) Paragraph 4 shall be omitted;

(b) In Paragraph 2, after sub-paragraph (3), the following sub-paragraph shall be inserted, namely:—
  "(4) Any member of a House who is disqualified under this Paragraph or who joins another political party after election shall immediately cease to be a member of the House, and their seat shall stand vacant by operation of law from the date of such defection or joining.";

(c) For Paragraph 6, the following Paragraph shall be substituted, namely:—
  "6. Decision on questions as to disqualification on ground of defection.—
  (1) If any question arises as to whether a member of a House has become subject to disqualification under this Schedule, the question shall be referred for the decision of the Election Commission of India, and its decision shall be final.

  (2) The Election Commission of India shall render its final decision on any reference made under sub-paragraph (1) within a period of thirty days from the date of receipt of the reference.

  (3) Upon the filing of a reference under sub-paragraph (1), the voting rights of the member concerned in the House shall stand suspended until the final decision of the Election Commission of India."

**3. Amendment of Article 75 and Article 164.**
In Article 75 and Article 164 of the Constitution, for clause (1B), the following clause shall be substituted, namely:—
"(1B) A member of a House belonging to any political party who is disqualified for being a member of the House under Paragraph 2 of the Tenth Schedule or who defects from such political party shall be disqualified to be appointed as a Minister or to hold any remunerative political or statutory post under the Union or State Governments for a period of six years from the date of their disqualification or seat vacation."

## THE REPRESENTATION OF THE PEOPLE AND ELECTORAL INTEGRITY (AMENDMENT) BILL, 2026

### A

### BILL

further to amend the Representation of the People Act, 1951, and the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988, to criminalize political horse-trading, impose electoral disqualification for corrupt defection, and regulate by-elections.

BE it enacted by Parliament in the Seventy-Seventh Year of the Republic of India as follows:—

**1. Short title and commencement.**
(1) This Act may be called the Representation of the People and Electoral Integrity (Amendment) Act, 2026.
(2) It extends to the whole of India.

**2. Insertion of Section 8B in Act 43 of 1951.**
After Section 8A of the Representation of the People Act, 1951, the following Section shall be inserted, namely:—
"8B. Disqualification on ground of Corrupt Defection and Horse-Trading.—
(1) Whosoever, being an elected member of Parliament or a State Legislature, defects from their political party or votes contrary to party direction in consideration of any financial gratification, monetary inducement, promise of office, or commercial benefit shall be guilty of the offence of Corrupt Defection.

(2) Whosoever commits the offence of Corrupt Defection shall be punished with rigorous imprisonment for a term which shall not be less than five years but which may extend to ten years, and shall also be liable to fine.

(3) A person convicted under sub-section (2) shall be disqualified from contesting any election to Parliament, State Legislatures, or local bodies for a period of six years from the date of conviction."

**3. Offence of Financing Horse-Trading.**
Whosoever offers, promises, pays, or facilitates any monetary gratification or political benefit to induce an elected member to defect shall be punished under Section 12 of the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988, with rigorous imprisonment for a term which shall not be less than two years but which may extend to seven years, and shall suffer mandatory forfeiture of assets.

## STATEMENT OF OBJECTS AND REASONS

Political defection by elected representatives subverts the democratic mandate of voters, undermines stability in Parliament and State Assemblies, and promotes commercial horse-trading. The Tenth Schedule, enacted by the 52nd Amendment (1985) and amended by the 91st Amendment (2003), contained Paragraph 4, which protected defections executed by two-thirds of a legislature party under the guise of a merger.

In practice, this merger exception has been systematically exploited to conduct wholesale defections without genuine party mergers. Furthermore, the unconstrained discretion of the Speaker under Paragraph 6 has led to partisan delays in resolving defection disputes. In *Keisham Meghachandra Singh v. Speaker* (2020), the Supreme Court recommended transferring defection adjudication to an independent statutory tribunal.

This legislative package addresses these defects by:—
1. **Repealing Paragraph 4**: Completely eliminating all merger and group defection exemptions, ensuring any defecting member automatically vacates their seat.

2. **Transferring Adjudication to the ECI**: Placing defection adjudication under the independent authority of the Election Commission of India with a strict 30-day decision deadline and automatic interim voting suspension during trial.

3. **Severe Deterrent Penalties**: Introducing a 6-year mandatory bar on holding ministerial posts, a 6-year electoral ban and 5 to 10 years imprisonment for corrupt defection, and criminal liability for cartels financing horse-trading.

The Bill seeks to achieve these objectives.

## FINANCIAL MEMORANDUM

Clause 2 of Part A provides for the adjudication of defection references by the Election Commission of India within thirty days, requiring dedicated ECI Adjudication Benches and digital case filing infrastructure.

1. **Non-Recurring Expenditure**: An estimated initial capital outlay of Rupees Five Crores (₹50,000,000) will be required for establishing dedicated digital hearing rooms and case management systems for ECI Defection Benches.

2. **Recurring Expenditure**: An estimated annual recurring expenditure of Rupees Two Crores (₹20,000,000) will be incurred for administrative staff, legal researchers, and publication of ECI orders.


The expenditure will be met from the Consolidated Fund of India.

---

## MEMORANDUM REGARDING DELEGATED LEGISLATION

Clause 2(c) of Part A empowers the Election Commission of India to make rules regulating the procedure for filing, processing, and hearing references concerning defection under the Tenth Schedule.

The delegation of legislative power is of a routine procedural character to ensure fast-track 30-day adjudication. All rules made by the Election Commission under this Act shall be published in the Official Gazette and laid before Parliament.
