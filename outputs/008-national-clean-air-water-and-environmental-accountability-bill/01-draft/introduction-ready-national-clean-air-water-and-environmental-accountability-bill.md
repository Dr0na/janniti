# THE NATIONAL CLEAN AIR, WATER, AND ENVIRONMENTAL ACCOUNTABILITY BILL, 2026

### A

### BILL

to guarantee every person in India the fundamental right to clean air and safe, non-toxic drinking water; to establish binding statutory air and water quality standards; to mandate a real-time public environmental monitoring sensor network across all districts; to impose strict absolute liability and criminal penalties for corporate ecocide and industrial toxic discharge; to create the National Environmental Protection Authority; and for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto.

BE it enacted by Parliament in the Seventy-Seventh Year of the Republic of India as follows:—

### 1. Short title, extent and commencement

(1) This Act may be called the National Clean Air, Water, and Environmental Accountability Act, 2026.

(2) It extends to the whole of India.

(3) It shall come into force on such date as the Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, appoint, provided that the monitoring sensor network mandated under section 5 shall be fully operational within eighteen months of enactment.

### 2. Definitions

In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires,—

(a) "Absolute Liability" means the legal standard establishing liability for environmental damage without any requirement to prove intent, negligence, or fault, and allowing no exceptions for act of God or third-party intervention;

(b) "Air Quality Target" means an annual average PM2.5 concentration not exceeding fifteen micrograms per cubic metre and an annual average PM10 concentration not exceeding forty micrograms per cubic metre;

(c) "Ecocide" means unlawful or wanton acts committed with knowledge that there is a substantial likelihood of severe and widespread or long-term damage to the environment being caused by those acts;

(d) "National Environmental Protection Authority" means the independent statutory authority established under section 6;

(e) "Real-Time Public Sensor Network" means the automated network of environmental sensors established under section 5 publishing unedited air and water quality data continuously to the public; and

(f) "Safe Drinking Water" means water complying with the chemical, microbiological, and radiological safety limits prescribed under section 4.

### 3. Right to Clean Air and Safe Water

(1) Every person in India has the statutory fundamental right to breathe clean air and to access safe, non-toxic drinking water.

(2) The State shall have a mandatory duty to achieve and maintain the Air Quality Target and Safe Drinking Water standards across all territorial jurisdictions.

### 4. Environmental Quality Standards

(1) The Central Government shall, on the recommendation of the National Environmental Protection Authority, prescribe statutory maximum permissible thresholds for air pollutants, heavy metals, industrial effluents, microplastics, and agricultural runoff.

(2) The standards prescribed under sub-section (1) shall be reviewed and tightened every three years to align with international health guidelines.

### 5. Mandatory Real-Time Public Sensor Network

(1) The National Environmental Protection Authority shall install, calibrate, and maintain an automated IoT environmental sensor station—

  (a) at every ten-square-kilometre grid in all urban zones; and

  (b) at every block headquarters in all rural zones.

(2) Every sensor station established under sub-section (1) shall stream continuous, unedited data regarding PM2.5, PM10, nitrogen dioxide, sulfur dioxide, heavy metal levels, and water toxicity to an open public dashboard.

### 6. National Environmental Protection Authority

(1) There shall be established an autonomous body to be called the National Environmental Protection Authority consisting of a Chairperson who is a retired judge of the Supreme Court or a High Court, and members representing environmental science, public health, and hydrology.

(2) The Authority shall have independent powers of inspection, search, seizure, plant closure, and civil penalty enforcement.

### 7. Absolute Corporate Liability for Environmental Damage

(1) Any commercial or industrial entity engaged in hazardous activity shall be subject to Absolute Liability for any environmental damage, air contamination, or water toxicity caused by its operations.

(2) The entity liable under sub-section (1) shall pay full restoration costs as determined by the National Environmental Protection Authority, in addition to statutory compensation to affected residents.

### 8. Offence of Ecocide and Penalties

(1) Whoever commits ecocide by intentionally or recklessly releasing toxic pollutants, untreated industrial effluents, or hazardous waste resulting in widespread environmental destruction shall be punishable with imprisonment for a term which shall not be less than seven years but which may extend to life imprisonment, and with fine up to one hundred crore rupees.

(2) Where an offence under sub-section (1) is committed by a company, every director and officer in charge of the business shall be deemed guilty of the offence.

### 9. Power to Make Rules

(1) The Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, make rules to carry out the provisions of this Act.

(2) Every rule made under this section shall be laid before each House of Parliament.

---

## STATEMENT OF OBJECTS AND REASONS

1. Severe air pollution and water contamination constitute an unprecedented public health crisis in India, causing over one million premature deaths annually and inflicting permanent harm on children.

2. Existing environmental statutes lack individual statutory entitlements, real-time public data transparency, and stringent deterrence against industrial polluters.

3. The National Clean Air, Water, and Environmental Accountability Bill, 2026 operationalizes Article 21 by guaranteeing clean air and safe water rights, mandating a real-time sensor network, establishing the National Environmental Protection Authority, and penalizing corporate ecocide with absolute liability.

---

## FINANCIAL MEMORANDUM

1. Clause 5 mandates the establishment of a real-time public sensor network across all urban grids and rural block headquarters. Clause 6 establishes the National Environmental Protection Authority.

2. Non-recurring capital expenditure for sensor deployment and authority infrastructure is estimated at rupees fifteen thousand crores. Recurring annual operational expenditure is estimated at rupees three thousand crores, funded through Parliamentary appropriations and clean environmental cesses.

---

## MEMORANDUM REGARDING DELEGATED LEGISLATION

1. Clause 9 empowers the Central Government to make rules regarding technical sensor calibration, data standards, and administrative procedures.

2. The delegation of legislative power is of a normal and administrative character.
