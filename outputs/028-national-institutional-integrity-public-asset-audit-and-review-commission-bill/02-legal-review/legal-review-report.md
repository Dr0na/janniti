# Expert Legal Review Report: National Institutional Integrity, Public Asset Audit, and Review Commission Bill, 2026

## 1. Review Conclusion

**Status**: COMPATIBLE WITH CONSTITUTIONAL CONDITIONS.

The proposed legislation successfully satisfies all core public-law and constitutional requirements of the Indian Constitution, provided that the statutory Commission operates strictly as an investigative and auditing body whose determinations are adjudicated by Division Benches of High Courts, and that criminal referrals proceed under pre-existing penal statutes without retrospective penal enhancement under Article 20(1).

## 5. Judicial and Enforcement Integrity

1. **Selection & Tenure**: Chairperson must be a former CJI or SC Judge, selected by a consensus committee including the Leader of Opposition and CJI, removable only for proved misbehaviour after SC Judge inquiry (Clause 4 & 5).
2. **Separation of Functions**: The Commission investigates and audits; it does not issue final quashing or penal orders. Final civil quashing and asset recovery rest exclusively with High Courts, and criminal trial rests with regular Special Judges.

---

## 6. Freedom, Equality, and Anti-Vendetta Assessment

- **Anti-Vendetta Safeguard**: The Bill avoids political vengeance or selective prosecution by establishing objective, evidence-based valuation criteria (Clause 7 & 8). Audits are triggered by threshold financial amounts (e.g., contracts exceeding ₹100 Crore) rather than party affiliation.
- **Equal Protection**: All affected leaseholders, contractors, and civil servants receive written notice, full access to audit documents, oral hearing, and judicial appeal.

All redlines identified during drafting have been embedded in `introduction-ready-national-institutional-integrity-public-asset-audit-and-review-commission-bill.md`:
- Clause 8: Transferred quashing authority from executive decree to High Courts.
- Clause 10: Express non-retrospectivity clause under Article 20(1).
- Clause 11: Direct statutory appeal to the Supreme Court of India.

## 8. Residual Risks

- **Litigation Delays**: Despite the 180-day statutory timeline for High Courts, complex commercial valuation disputes may experience interlocutory appeals before the Supreme Court.
- **Valuation Complexity**: Assessing historical market values of land and public enterprises as of 2014 requires highly qualified financial valuation panels.
