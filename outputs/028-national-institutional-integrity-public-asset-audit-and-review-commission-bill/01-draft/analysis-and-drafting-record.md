# Analytical and Drafting Record: National Institutional Integrity, Public Asset Audit, and Review Commission Bill, 2026

## 1. Bottom Line and Assumptions

### Bottom Line
The demand for blanket, retroactive cancellation of all executive decisions, public asset sales, land leases, and civil service appointments since 2014, combined with retrospective capital punishment or criminal penalization for all supporting personnel, is **flatly unconstitutional** under the basic structure of the Indian Constitution, Article 20(1) (prohibition of ex post facto criminal laws), Article 14 and Article 21 (due process and non-arbitrariness), Article 300A (deprivation of property only by authority of law), and Article 311 (procedural protection for civil servants).

However, public allegations of illegal undervaluation of public assets, fraudulent land concessions, irregular lateral entries bypassing constitutional recruitment processes, and institutional capture CAN be lawfully addressed. This legislative framework establishes the **National Institutional Integrity, Public Asset Audit, and Review Commission**, an independent, judicially headed statutory body. It equips the Commission with civil court powers, valuation panels, and statutory standing to petition High Courts for the quashing or revision of tainted transactions and recovery of public assets based on individualized evidence of fraud, corruption, or statutory illegality, while guaranteeing complete constitutional due process.

### Assumptions
1. **Jurisdiction & Competence**: Parliament has legislative competence under Schedule VII, List I, Entries 42 (Inter-State trade, public contracts & Union property), 70 (Union Public Services), and 95 (Jurisdiction of courts with respect to List I matters).
2. **As-of Date**: 2026-07-30.
3. **No Retrospective Criminalization**: All criminal referrals are strictly governed by laws in force at the time of the alleged act, preserving Article 20(1) guarantees.

## 2. Problem, Evidence, and Existing-Law Map

### Problem Statement
Over the past decade, major public asset transactions (including Central PSU disinvestments, strategic disinvestments, and long-term public land leases) and public service recruitments (such as lateral entry into senior civil service posts) have generated intense public concern regarding transparency, market valuation, and institutional integrity.

### Limitations of Existing Law
1. **Commissions of Inquiry Act, 1952**: Commissions created under this Act are purely recommendatory and lack direct authority to quash illegal leases, initiate civil asset recovery, or issue binding interim preservation orders.
2. **CAG Act, 1971**: The Comptroller and Auditor General provides post-facto audit reports to Parliament, but lacks enforcement machinery or legal standing to file recovery suits in High Courts.
3. **Article 311 & CCS (CCA) Rules, 1965**: Standard disciplinary procedures are designed for individual misconduct rather than auditing systemic, irregular recruitment schemes.

## 4. Options and Trade-offs

### Option 1: Executive Summary Nullification Act (User's Raw Demand)
- **Description**: A law declaring all transactions, leases, contracts, and appointments made between 2014 and 2026 void *ab initio*, with retroactive capital punishment for officials.
- **Trade-offs**: Strikes at the core of constitutional democracy; would be declared unconstitutional by the Supreme Court within hours for violating Article 20(1), Article 14, Article 21, Article 300A, and Article 311. Destroys economic predictability and rule of law.
- **Recommendation**: Rejected outright.

### Option 2: Executive Order / Ad-hoc Inquiry Commission
- **Description**: Appointing standard Commissions of Inquiry under the 1952 Act.
- **Trade-offs**: Reports are non-binding; no statutory asset recovery or quashing powers; prolonged litigation without resolution.
- **Recommendation**: Insufficient.

### Option 3: Statutory Institutional Integrity & Asset Audit Commission Bill, 2026 (Recommended)
- **Description**: Enacting a specialized Central Act establishing an independent Judicial Commission headed by a retired Chief Justice of India or Supreme Court Judge, assisted by valuation experts and special counsel, with powers to audit, quash tainted contracts via High Court petitions, recover public funds, and restore civil service recruitment integrity under full due process.
- **Trade-offs**: Requires formal statutory process, evidence collection, and judicial review, but provides permanent, legal, non-partisan, and constitutionally secure results.
- **Recommendation**: Recommended.

## 6. Explanatory Clause-by-Clause Notes

- **Clause 1**: Defines short title, extent (whole of India), and commencement (upon notification).
- **Clause 2**: Defines key terms including "public asset", "disinvestment", "long-term land lease", "irregular appointment", and "Commission".
- **Clause 3**: Establishes the National Institutional Integrity, Public Asset Audit, and Review Commission.
- **Clause 4**: Details composition: Chairperson (former CJI or SC Judge) and 4 Members (former CAG, financial valuation expert, former UPSC Member, senior advocate).
- **Clause 5**: Specifies selection committee comprising Prime Minister, Leader of Opposition, and Chief Justice of India to prevent political capture.
- **Clause 6**: Grants civil court powers under Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 (summoning, discovery, requisitioning public records).
- **Clause 7**: Mandates comprehensive audit of disinvestments, major land leases below market rates, and high-value procurement contracts executed since May 2014.
- **Clause 8**: Sets statutory criteria for identifying illegal or tainted transactions: proven bribery, fraud, procedural illegality, or intentional undervaluation causing exchequer loss.
- **Clause 9**: Empowers Commission to file recovery and quashing petitions before High Courts.
- **Clause 10**: Governs review of lateral civil service entry appointments in consultation with UPSC and in compliance with Article 311.
- **Clause 11**: Expressly bars retrospective criminal offences, reiterating Article 20(1) and directing criminal referrals to competent courts under established penal law.
- **Clause 12**: Establishes statutory appeal directly to the Supreme Court of India within 60 days.
- **Clause 13**: Annual reporting and public disclosure duties to Parliament.
- **Clause 14–16**: Delegated legislation, protection for action taken in good faith, and power to remove difficulties.
