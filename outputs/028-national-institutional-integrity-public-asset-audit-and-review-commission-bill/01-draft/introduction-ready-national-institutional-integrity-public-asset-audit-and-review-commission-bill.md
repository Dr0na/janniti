THE NATIONAL INSTITUTIONAL INTEGRITY, PUBLIC ASSET AUDIT, AND REVIEW COMMISSION BILL, 2026

A

BILL

to establish an independent statutory Commission to audit, review, and evaluate public asset disinvestments, major land leases, procurement contracts, and public service lateral recruitments executed since May 2014; to provide for judicially supervised civil asset recovery and quashing of transactions tainted by fraud, illegal undervaluation, or gross procedural illegality; to ensure strict compliance with constitutional guarantees of due process, protection against ex post facto criminal laws, civil service protections, and judicial review under Articles 14, 20, 21, 300A, and 311 of the Constitution of India; and for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto.

BE it enacted by Parliament in the Seventy-seventh Year of the Republic of India as follows:—

### 1. Short title, extent and commencement

(1) This Act may be called the National Institutional Integrity, Public Asset Audit, and Review Commission Act, 2026.

(2) It extends to the whole of India.

(3) It shall come into force on such date as the Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, appoint.

### 2. Definitions

In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires,—

(a) "Commission" means the National Institutional Integrity, Public Asset Audit, and Review Commission established under section 3;

(b) "disinvestment" means any transfer, sale, equity dilution, or concession of Union public sector undertakings, government-owned corporations, or statutory entities executed on or after the 26th day of May, 2014;

(c) "high-value procurement contract" means any procurement, infrastructure lease, or concession contract executed by the Union Government or its statutory agencies exceeding a value of one hundred crore rupees;

(d) "irregular appointment" means any recruitment, lateral entry, or contractual appointment into senior ranks of the Union Civil Services executed without adherence to the recruitment rules framed by the Union Public Service Commission or statutory regulations;

(e) "long-term land lease" means any lease, grant, license, or allocation of land belonging to the Union Government or its public sector undertakings for a duration exceeding ten years executed on or after the 26th day of May, 2014; and

(f) "prescribed" means prescribed by rules made under this Act.

### 3. Establishment of National Institutional Integrity, Public Asset Audit, and Review Commission

(1) The Central Government shall, within thirty days from the commencement of this Act, by notification in the Official Gazette, establish a Body Corporate to be known as the National Institutional Integrity, Public Asset Audit, and Review Commission.

(2) The Commission shall have perpetual succession and a common seal, with power to acquire, hold, and dispose of property, and to contract, and shall by the said name sue or be sued.

(3) The head office of the Commission shall be at New Delhi, and the Commission may establish regional benches at such other places in India as it deems necessary.

### 4. Composition and appointment of Chairperson and Members

(1) The Commission shall consist of a Chairperson and four full-time Members.

(2) The Chairperson shall be a person who has been the Chief Justice of India or a Judge of the Supreme Court of India.

(3) The Members shall be appointed from among persons of eminent standing and proven integrity, comprising—

(a) one Member who has held the office of the Comptroller and Auditor General of India or a Secretary to the Government of India;

(b) one Member who is a recognized expert in financial valuation and public sector economics;

(c) one Member who has served as a Member of the Union Public Service Commission; and

(d) one Member who is a senior advocate eligible to be appointed as a Judge of a High Court.

(4) Every appointment under this section shall be made by the President of India by warrant under his hand and seal, on the recommendation of a Selection Committee consisting of—

(a) the Prime Minister of India, who shall be the Chairperson of the Selection Committee;

(b) the Leader of the Opposition in the Lok Sabha, or where no such Leader exists, the Leader of the single largest Opposition Party in the Lok Sabha; and

(c) the Chief Justice of India or a Judge of the Supreme Court nominated by the Chief Justice.

### 5. Term of office and conditions of service

(1) The Chairperson and Members of the Commission shall hold office for a term of three years from the date on which they enter upon office, and shall be eligible for re-appointment for a single further term of two years.

(2) The Chairperson or a Member may, by writing under hand addressed to the President of India, resign from office.

(3) The Chairperson or any Member shall only be removed from office by order of the President on the ground of proved misbehaviour or incapacity after an inquiry conducted by a Judge of the Supreme Court appointed by the Chief Justice of India, in which such Chairperson or Member has been informed of the charges and given a reasonable opportunity of being heard.

### 6. Powers and procedure of Commission

(1) The Commission shall, for the purpose of any audit or inquiry under this Act, have all the powers of a civil court while trying a suit under the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, in respect of the following matters, namely:—

(a) summoning and enforcing the attendance of any person and examining him on oath;

(b) requiring the discovery and production of any document or public record;

(c) receiving evidence on affidavits;

(d) requisitioning any public record or copy thereof from any court, ministry, or department; and

(e) issuing commissions for the examination of witnesses or documents.

(2) The Commission shall observe principles of natural justice, ensuring that every person whose interests are likely to be affected by an audit or review is provided with written notice, full access to relevant records, and an opportunity of making oral and written representation.

### 7. Audit of disinvestments, land leases, and contracts

(1) The Commission shall systematically audit and evaluate major public asset disinvestments, long-term land leases, and high-value procurement contracts executed between the 26th day of May, 2014 and the date of commencement of this Act.

(2) An audit under sub-section (1) shall examine—

(a) whether the market valuation of the public asset or land was calculated in accordance with objective financial standards;

(b) whether the transaction involved illegal undervaluation, fraudulent misrepresentation, or conflict of interest;

(c) whether competitive bidding processes were suppressed, bypassed, or manipulated; and

(d) whether the transaction resulted in an illegal subsidy or unearned benefit to private entities at the expense of the public exchequer.

### 8. Quashing and recovery petitions before High Courts

(1) Where the Commission, upon completion of an audit under section 7, arrives at a prima facie finding that a disinvestment, land lease, or contract was tainted by fraud, bribery, illegal undervaluation, or gross procedural illegality, it shall formulate a detailed Audit Determination Record.

(2) Upon issuing an Audit Determination Record, the Commission may file a petition before the High Court within whose territorial jurisdiction the public asset is situated or the contract was executed, praying for—

(a) the quashing or setting aside of the lease, sale deed, or contract;

(b) the restoration of title and possession of the public asset to the Union Government or public sector undertaking; and

(c) the recovery of unearned profit, illegal subsidy, or financial loss suffered by the public exchequer, together with statutory interest.

(3) Every petition filed under sub-section (2) shall be heard by a Division Bench of the High Court and disposed of within a period of one hundred and eighty days from the date of filing.

### 9. Review of civil service recruitments and lateral entries

(1) The Commission shall, in concurrence with the Union Public Service Commission, review senior-level lateral entry recruitments into the Union Civil Services executed on or after the 26th day of May, 2014.

(2) A review under sub-section (1) shall examine whether the appointment satisfied published eligibility criteria, public notice, transparent selection procedure, and statutory service rules.

(3) Where an appointment is found to have been executed in violation of statutory rules or constitutional equality guarantees, the Commission shall submit a report to the Union Public Service Commission and the competent appointing authority for initiating proceedings under Article 311 of the Constitution and the relevant civil service classification rules.

### 10. Guarantee of constitutional rights and prohibition of ex post facto penalties

(1) Nothing in this Act shall be construed as authorizing the creation of any retrospective criminal offence or the retrospective enhancement of any criminal penalty.

(2) Where an audit or review under this Act reveals evidence of criminal misconduct punishable under laws in force at the time of the alleged act, the Commission shall refer the case records to the competent prosecution authority for investigation and prosecution before a court of competent jurisdiction.

(3) All criminal proceedings initiated pursuant to a referral under sub-section (2) shall strictly adhere to fundamental rights guaranteed under Articles 20 and 21 of the Constitution, including the right to a fair trial, presumpion of innocence, and representation by legal counsel.

### 11. Appeals

Any person aggrieved by a final order of a High Court passed in a petition under section 8 may prefer an appeal to the Supreme Court of India within sixty days from the date of the order.

### 12. Annual report and public disclosure

(1) The Commission shall submit an annual report to Parliament detailing all audits conducted, petitions filed, asset recoveries achieved, and referrals made under this Act.

(2) Every audit report, valuation assessment, and petition filed by the Commission shall be published on an open-access public online portal within fourteen days of its execution.

### 13. Protection of action taken in good faith

No suit, prosecution, or other legal proceeding shall lie against the Chairperson, Member, or any officer acting under the direction of the Commission for anything which is in good faith done or intended to be done under this Act or any rule made thereunder.

### 14. Power to make rules

(1) The Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, make rules for carrying out the provisions of this Act.

(2) In particular, and without prejudice to the generality of the foregoing power, such rules may provide for—

(a) the procedure and valuation standards for asset audits under section 7;

(b) the format and filing requirements for petitions under section 8; and

(c) any other matter which is required to be, or may be, prescribed.

(3) Every rule made under this Act shall be laid, as soon as may be after it is made, before each House of Parliament while it is in session.

STATEMENT OF OBJECTS AND REASONS

During the period from May 2014 onwards, extensive public asset disinvestments, long-term public land leases, high-value procurement contracts, and lateral entries into senior ranks of the civil service have taken place. Public accountability and civic trust require that major public asset transactions and civil service recruitments be audited by an independent, transparent, and judicially headed authority to ensure that public resources were not illegally undervalued or alienated, and that constitutional recruitment standards were strictly preserved.

Existing mechanisms, such as ad-hoc Commissions of Inquiry, are purely advisory and lack direct legal authority to petition courts for civil asset recovery or the quashing of tainted leases. Conversely, blanket retrospective cancellation or summary penalization without due process is impermissible under Articles 14, 20, 21, 300A, and 311 of the Constitution of India.

The National Institutional Integrity, Public Asset Audit, and Review Commission Bill, 2026, seeks to bridge this statutory gap. It establishes an independent, high-powered Commission headed by a former Chief Justice of India or Supreme Court Judge. The Bill equips the Commission with civil court powers to audit transactions, file quashing and recovery petitions before High Courts, and review civil service lateral entries in concurrence with the UPSC, while fully safeguarding fundamental rights, due process, protection against ex post facto criminal laws, and judicial review.

FINANCIAL MEMORANDUM

Clause 3 of the Bill provides for the establishment of the National Institutional Integrity, Public Asset Audit, and Review Commission. Clause 4 provides for the appointment of a Chairperson and four Members. Clause 6 and Clause 12 provide for administrative infrastructure, valuation panels, open-access public portals, and operational staff.

The recurring annual administrative expenditure is estimated at approximately twenty-five crore rupees from the Consolidated Fund of India. The non-recurring capital expenditure for office establishment and digital infrastructure is estimated at ten crore rupees. The expenditure will be substantially offset by the recovery of undervalued public assets and corrected public land lease rentals.

MEMORANDUM REGARDING DELEGATED LEGISLATION

Clause 14 of the Bill empowers the Central Government to make rules, by notification in the Official Gazette, for carrying out the provisions of the Act. These rules relate to procedural valuation standards, petition formats, and operational administrative guidelines.

The matters with respect to which rules may be made are matters of procedure and administrative detail. The delegation of legislative power is, therefore, of a normal character.
