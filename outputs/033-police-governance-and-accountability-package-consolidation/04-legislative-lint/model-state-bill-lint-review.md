# Legislative Lint Review — Model State Bill

## Status: clear with conditions

The mechanical linter reported eleven pattern matches. They are not unresolved authorisations of coercive power: most are triggered by protective terms such as “no power”, privacy rules, or a tightly bounded emergency exception. The contextual review below records the exact treatment. Formal readiness remains blocked by missing State primary-law verification.

| Rule ID | Severity | Location | Issue and exploitation path | Exact treatment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L015 | High pattern | 4(2), 20(2), 33, 42 | Aggregate reports or audits could re-identify people. | Clauses require minimisation, de-identification, access logs, stated purpose, re-identification assessment, independent privacy review and no direct individual-case disclosure. Local rules must specify thresholds and audit methods. |
| L010 | High pattern | 4(3), 44(2) | The linter treats “no power” and “may not create an offence” as a penalty phrase. | These clauses create no offence or punishment; they reserve functions and constrain rulemaking. No criminal penalty is implied. |
| L005/L008 | Medium/High pattern | 36(2) | An emergency roster exception could become routine. | Objective immediate-threat trigger; twelve-hour maximum; one independent extension only; reasons, compensatory rest, reporting and audit. No exception touches custody safeguards. State evidence must determine ordinary-hour standard. |
| L013 | Medium pattern | 43 | Contractor or technology could be used to evade the Act. | Clause 43 expressly applies the prohibition to contractors, algorithms, funding conditions and informal instructions. Procurement controls are added in clause 39. |
| L006 | High pattern | 44(1) | Delegated rules could enlarge executive power. | Subjects are exhaustively bounded; pre-publication, reasons, equality/privacy assessment, consultation response and legislative laying are required; clause 44(2) reserves core rights and remedies. |
