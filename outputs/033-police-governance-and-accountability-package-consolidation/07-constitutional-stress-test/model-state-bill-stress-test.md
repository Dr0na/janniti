# Constitutional Stress Test — Model State Police Governance and Custody Integrity Bill

## Conclusion: resilient with conditions

The model removes the most serious source-draft concentration: a complaints body cannot investigate, prosecute, discipline and decide the same matter. It is nevertheless **not ready for adoption** because its local competence, existing-law interactions, resource assumptions and remedies require State-specific verification.

| Actor / condition | Exploitation path | Right or principle | Rating before / after draft safeguard | Remaining failure and required repair |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Large State majority | Controls appointments, budget and informal directions. | Federal democracy, Articles 14 and 21. | Critical / High | Set exact plural selection, protected minimum operating budget, removal process and court access in the adopting State schedule. |
| Captured police hierarchy | Sends complaint back to implicated unit or deletes evidence. | Life, dignity, equality and remedy. | Critical / Medium | Verify external-investigator pool, custody-evidence technology and preservation-order procedure. |
| Executive emergency | Uses a public-order label to normalise fatigue or bypass scrutiny. | Liberty, equality and dignified work. | High / Medium | Clause 36’s trigger/12-hour limit needs local independent reviewer and transparent data standard. |
| Complaints Authority capture | Silently closes or delays politically sensitive complaints. | Equal protection and anti-vendetta rule. | High / Medium | Set acknowledgement and decision deadlines, public aggregate closure records and an accessible appeal/review forum. |
| Prosecutorial pressure | Selective charges or non-prosecution after police referral. | Fair trial, equality and political freedom. | High / Medium | Confirm State prosecution statute and create reasons/review mechanism that does not direct individual outcomes. |
| Technology contractor | Controls footage, access or deletion. | Privacy, evidence integrity and fair trial. | High / Medium | State procurement schedule must specify security, escrow/access, audit rights, incident notice and deletion verification. |
| Ordinary person with barriers | Cannot complain safely or understand the process. | Access to justice, equality and dignity. | High / Medium | Co-design district offline access, legal aid, disability/language services, confidential reporting and anti-retaliation protection. |
| Hostile majority targets dissidents or journalists | Uses arrest, transfer or complaint process as punishment. | Articles 14, 19 and 21; free public debate. | Critical / High | Define comparator-audit metrics, judicial preservation/remedy route and whistleblower/source safeguards after local law research. |

## Single points of failure

1. **Appointments:** generic pluralism is insufficient until each selecting member, quorum, vote and replacement procedure is specified.
2. **Funding:** annual appropriations can still starve complaints, evidence preservation or legal aid.
3. **Information:** police-controlled operational data can be incomplete or manipulated unless independently auditable.
4. **External investigation:** “external” is not independent without fixed allocation and conflict rules.
5. **Court access:** remedies fail if urgent preservation, legal aid and language access are not operational.

## Minimum pre-adoption safeguards

- State-specific competence memorandum and conflict/repeal schedule.
- Appointment, tenure, removal, funding and review clauses with no single-bloc control.
- Defined thresholds and deadlines for complaint acknowledgement, preservation, referral and closure.
- Court-access, legal-aid and witness-protection protocol.
- Evidence-retention/privacy impact assessment and independently audited procurement.
- Sunset review at five years and a published legislative response to every independent review.

## Residual risk

Even a well-designed law cannot prevent covert pressure, deliberate falsification, prejudice or resource withdrawal. Courts, an informed public, free media, civil society, protected disclosures and periodic democratic review remain essential safeguards.
