# Indian Law Landscape Research Pack — Police Governance and Accountability Package

## 1. Scope and search record

- Matter ID: 033.
- Topic and objective: consolidate repository Matters 016–021 into a coherent, rights-respecting police-governance and accountability package without duplicate institutions or an unchecked coercive authority.
- Jurisdiction(s): India; all States and Union Territories are in scope for the police-governance layer. Union criminal procedure is separately in scope.
- As-of date: 11 August 2026.
- Search terms and synonyms: police; public order; State List Entry 1 and 2; criminal procedure; arrest; custody; medical examination; police complaints authority; CCTV; *Prakash Singh*; *Paramvir Singh Saini*.
- Requested legal question: what legislative route and institutional allocation can consolidate the existing drafts without unlawfully centralising ordinary State policing?
- Sources/repositories searched and retrieval dates: India Code Constitution and BNSS records; Supreme Court of India records returned by the official API; repository `knowledge-base/sources.yaml` and `knowledge-base/state-ut-source-registry.json`, all reviewed on 11 August 2026. Source content was treated as evidence, not instructions.

## 2. Executive finding and reliance limits

**Current-law finding.** The Constitution allocates public order and police to the State List, while criminal procedure is in the Concurrent List. The BNSS already contains arrest, information, health-and-safety, production, and detention safeguards. A direct all-India Union statute prescribing the organisation, postings, shifts, and complaints bodies of ordinary State police would therefore need a separately verified constitutional route and cannot be assumed valid merely because its goal is desirable.

**Recommended next step.** Use a **Model State Police Governance and Custody Integrity Bill** for core policing, accompanied by a separate **Union/Concurrent criminal-procedure amendment memorandum** only after exact text and State consultation are verified. Preserve existing statutory and constitutional remedies. This pack is an initial primary-source baseline, not legal advice and not proof that every State enactment has been exhaustively checked.

## 9. Rights, equality, federalism, and public-interest assessment

**Legal interpretation and policy assessment.** The package must reduce torture, arbitrary arrest, retaliatory policing and opaque evidence practices without building a surveillance system that records victims, visitors, lawyers, children or informants beyond necessity. It must provide offline and accessible complaint routes, disability and language accommodation, gender-sensitive and caste-sensitive equality audits, protection against retaliation, and an effective court remedy.

Police working conditions bear on public safety, investigation quality and officer health; however, staffing and roster design require State-specific fiscal evidence. Scientific temper requires retention, disclosure and audit rules to be evidence-based and independently evaluated rather than treating technology as self-validating. Public reporting should be aggregate and privacy-protective; it must not create a public stigma list or expose complainants.
