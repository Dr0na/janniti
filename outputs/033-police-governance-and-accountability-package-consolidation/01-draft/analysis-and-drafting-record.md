# Analysis and Drafting Record — Model State Police Governance and Custody Integrity Bill

## Status and assumption

**Draft text, not current law and not ready for introduction.** The proposed instrument is a model for enactment by an individual State legislature after verification of that State’s police legislation, service rules, complaints arrangements, fiscal capacity and constitutional route. It does not purport to amend the BNSS or apply by parliamentary command to ordinary State policing.

## Problem and objective

The source drafts seek to prevent political control of policing, custodial abuse, evidence tampering, opaque complaints, selective prosecution and unsafe working conditions. Their principal defect is overlap: several create institutions that could intake, investigate, punish, direct or audit the same case. The model preserves the objectives while separating functions and retaining court supervision.

## Authority and design decision

Police and public order are State List subjects; criminal procedure is Concurrent List. The Bill is therefore drafted as a State model. It preserves BNSS and constitutional safeguards rather than asserting a State power to contradict Central criminal procedure. Any proposal to amend arrest, evidentiary, prosecution or compensation law must be separately redlined against accepted current official texts and tested for Article 254 consequences.

## Options considered

| Option | Benefit | Defect | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| One Union police statute | Uniformity. | Unresolved competence and federalism failure. | Rejected for ordinary State policing. |
| Retain six separate State Bills | Preserves every source-draft label. | Duplicated bodies and conflicting processes. | Rejected. |
| One model State framework with modules | Common rights/process baseline, State adaptation and separated functions. | Requires State-specific schedules and consultation. | Recommended. |
| Implementation-only measures | Faster operational change. | Fragile against reversal and lacks remedies. | Insufficient alone. |

## Design rules used

- No case-specific executive, ministerial, commission or board direction.
- A complaint authority receives, preserves, refers and reviews; it does not prosecute, discipline or adjudicate.
- Investigation, prosecution, disciplinary action and court adjudication are distinct functions.
- Custody evidence is preserved proportionately, with protected confidential zones and access logs; an outage is evidence for an independent fact-finder, not automatic guilt or innocence.
- An exceptional operational direction must be written, necessary, time-limited, logged and reviewable. It cannot suspend core custody safeguards.
- Service wellbeing measures require annual staffing and fatigue planning rather than an unfunded uniform roster.

## Completeness and introduction blockers

1. Identify the adopting State and retrieve its current police Act, service law, complaints regime, rules, Gazettes and case law.
2. Verify accepted current BNSS/evidence-law sources before creating any consequential amendment schedule.
3. Set locally evidenced budgets, staffing ratios, procurement controls, retention periods and language/disability-access arrangements.
4. Complete survivor, personnel, medical, forensic, disability, legal-aid, journalist and civil-society consultation.
5. Obtain independent State constitutional and legislative-drafting review.

## Clause notes

- Clauses 4–10: governance and service insulation; the Commission is not a super-police body.
- Clauses 11–19: custody integrity and evidence preservation; no reversal of the criminal burden.
- Clauses 20–28: complaints/referral architecture; referrals use pre-set conflict rules.
- Clauses 29–34: prosecution and anti-vendetta safeguards; these do not alter court powers or prosecutorial statutory duties.
- Clauses 35–39: working conditions and public reporting, subject to State appropriation.
- Clauses 40–46: review, remedies, anti-evasion and adaptation safeguards.

## Residual risk

No legal framework can make political pressure, discrimination, falsification or institutional capture impossible. The practical safeguards are public records, independent courts, legal aid, whistleblower protection, independent audit, free media, informed public participation and enforceable remedies.
