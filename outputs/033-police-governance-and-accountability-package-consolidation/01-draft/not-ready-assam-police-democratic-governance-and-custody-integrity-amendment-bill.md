# THE ASSAM POLICE (DEMOCRATIC GOVERNANCE AND CUSTODY INTEGRITY) AMENDMENT BILL, 2026

> **Draft status:** Complete standalone draft text for consultation. It is not introduced or certified for the Assam Legislative Assembly. Filing-format, Gazette/later-history, the remaining Service/Prosecution Rule crosswalk, consultation and qualified Assam review remain formal-readiness gates.

## A BILL

to amend the Assam Police Act, 2007 to secure impartial, rights-respecting and accountable policing; protect persons in custody; prevent selective or retaliatory policing; strengthen independent public complaints and service integrity; preserve the separation of complaint intake, investigation, prosecution, discipline and adjudication; and provide for matters connected therewith.

BE it enacted by the Assam Legislative Assembly in the Seventy-seventh Year of the Republic of India as follows:—

### 1. Short title and commencement

(1) This Act may be called the Assam Police (Democratic Governance and Custody Integrity) Amendment Act, 2026.

(2) It shall come into force on a date notified by the State Government after publication of the implementation plan under section 14.

### 2. Insertion of sections 44A to 44D

After section 44 of the Assam Police Act, 2007, the following sections shall be inserted:—

**“44A. Limits on State Security Commission.**—The State Security Commission shall publish non-case-specific policing priorities, monitor aggregate performance and lay an annual report before the Legislative Assembly; but it shall not direct, supervise or influence an identifiable investigation, arrest, prosecution, disciplinary matter or court proceeding.

**44B. Appointments, conflicts and removal.**—Members of the State Security Commission shall be selected through a published process with stated eligibility criteria, conflict screening, recorded reasons and representation that prevents one political bloc from controlling appointments. Terms shall be staggered. A member may be removed only for stated incapacity, proved misconduct or material conflict after notice, hearing, recorded reasons and an effective independent review.

**44C. Written-direction and anti-evasion rule.**—Every executive or police-policy direction shall state its legal source, public purpose and duration and shall be published within seven days, subject only to a time-limited confidentiality order recording a concrete safety or evidence risk. A case-specific direction is void. No person shall achieve indirectly, through a contractor, funding condition, algorithm, task force, secrecy label, informal instruction or serial temporary measure, what cannot lawfully be done directly.

**44D. Public information.**—Reports under this Chapter shall use minimised and de-identified information. A disclosure capable of identifying a person requires a written legal basis, necessity and risk assessment, independent review and the least intrusive publication method.

**44E. Protection against institutional capture.**—(1) The State Government shall not withhold, condition, re-route or delay funds, staff, premises, records or technology of the Commission or Accountability Commission in order to influence an identifiable complaint, investigation, prosecution, disciplinary matter, audit or report.

(2) Each such body shall submit its independently prepared annual budget requirement directly to the Legislative Assembly and publish the amount requested, amount provided and any material operational shortfall.

(3) A vacancy shall not be used to disable a body. A temporary appointment must use the same independence and conflict safeguards, last no more than ninety days, and be publicly reasoned.

(4) No emergency, executive order, administrative instruction, secrecy classification or outsourcing arrangement may suspend sections 44A to 44D, 70 to 87L or a court-preservation order.”

**44F. Democratic public backstop.**—(1) The people are the source of public authority under the Constitution. Every body under this Act shall facilitate informed, equal and safe public participation, access to information and judicial remedy.

(2) A petition signed by not fewer than one per cent of the registered electors of Assam, including the minimum geographical distribution prescribed by rules to prevent domination by one locality, may require the Legislative Assembly to hold a public hearing on a published systemic non-compliance report under this Act within ninety days.

(3) The hearing shall be accessible, multilingual, publicly recorded and open to affected persons, police personnel, civil-society organisations, experts and the responsible public bodies. The responsible Minister shall publish a reasoned response and corrective timetable within thirty days.

(4) A petition or hearing does not decide an individual case, remove a person, direct an investigation or displace court jurisdiction. It shall not be used to abridge equality, dignity, minority rights, due process, free expression, peaceful dissent or any constitutional remedy.”

### 3. Amendment of sections 45 and 46

In sections 45 and 46 of the principal Act, after the provisions relating to the Police Establishment Board and transfer or posting, the following shall be inserted:—

“Every material posting, transfer, appraisal or service recommendation shall apply published objective criteria and record reasons. The officer affected shall receive reasons and an accessible review route. Aggregate transfer and posting data shall be audited annually for direct and indirect discrimination. No person shall receive a transfer, posting, appraisal, disciplinary measure or public stigma because of lawful political belief, criticism, journalism, protest, whistleblowing, association or protected status; this does not prevent action based on independently recorded objective evidence under the same rules applied to everyone else.”

### 4. Insertion of section 63A

After section 63 of the principal Act, the following section shall be inserted:—

**“63A. Investigation integrity.**—The Director General of Police shall publish a phased plan for functional separation of investigation from routine law-and-order deployment, based on workload, staffing, forensic capacity, training and equality impact. For an allegation against police personnel or a politically sensitive matter, the police shall use a pre-set conflict-screened allocation rule and record any reassignment. An investigator may be diverted only for an immediate recorded threat to life, physical safety or essential disaster response, for not more than seventy-two hours unless independently reviewed.”

### 5. Substitution of section 70

For section 70 of the principal Act, the following shall be substituted:—

**“70. State Police Accountability Commission.**—(1) The State Police Accountability Commission shall receive, acknowledge, preserve, refer, monitor and report on public complaints against police personnel.

(2) The Commission shall not investigate an offence, prosecute, decide criminal guilt, impose disciplinary punishment or hear an appeal from its own decision.

(3) The Commission shall provide filing without charge in person, by post, telephone, online or through an authorised representative, with disability accommodation, language assistance, confidentiality and protection against retaliation.

(4) The Commission shall acknowledge a complaint within seven days, give written reasons for a closure, deferral or referral, and state the review route in an accessible form.”

### 6. Substitution of section 71

For section 71 of the principal Act, the following shall be substituted:—

**“71. Composition and independence.**—(1) The Commission shall have an independent Chairperson and four members selected by a publicly notified panel including government, opposition and independent constitutional or judicial representation. No serving police officer, serving prosecutor, minister, legislator or political-office holder may be Chairperson or a member.

(2) At least two members shall have substantial experience in human rights, law, disability access, public health, social work, forensic practice or community accountability, and at least two members shall be women.

(3) Appointment, conflict disclosure, budget request, annual accounts and removal reasons shall be published. Removal is permissible only for stated incapacity, proved misconduct or material conflict after notice, hearing, recorded reasons and independent review.”

### 7. Amendment of section 78

In section 78 of the principal Act, after the provision concerning serious misconduct, the following shall be inserted:—

“On receiving an allegation of death, disappearance, torture, sexual violence, serious injury in custody, evidence tampering, retaliatory policing, or conflict in the ordinary investigating unit, the Commission shall immediately issue a preservation notice and refer the matter to an investigator chosen under the pre-set allocation rule. The Commission shall notify the competent prosecution and disciplinary authorities as required by law, but shall not direct their individual outcome.”

### 8. Substitution of sections 84 to 87

For sections 84 to 87 of the principal Act, the following shall be substituted:—

**“84. District Police Accountability Panels.**—(1) The State Government shall establish an accessible District Police Accountability Panel for each police range or other published area.

(2) A Panel shall have a non-serving independent Chairperson and four members selected through the process in section 71. A serving police officer, a serving member of a legislature, an office-holder of a political party, or an officer in the police chain concerned shall not be a member.

(3) The Panel shall perform the functions in section 70 within its area and shall refer serious misconduct under section 78. It shall not investigate, prosecute, impose discipline or adjudicate guilt.

**85. Integrity of referral and inquiry.**—(1) An investigator assigned following a Panel or Commission referral shall record material steps, conflicts, evidence access and reasons for reassignment.

(2) An affected person may seek an urgent preservation, protection or review order from a competent court where evidence is at risk, referral is obstructed, or the allocation rule is breached.

**86. Anti-retaliation and support.**—No person may be threatened, detained, transferred, disadvantaged or publicly stigmatised for making, assisting, reporting on or lawfully criticising a complaint. The State shall provide information, legal-aid referral, medical and psychosocial referral, witness-protection assessment and reasonable participation support without conditioning assistance on a particular outcome.

**87. Public accountability.**—The Commission shall publish quarterly de-identified data on complaints, preservation, referrals, timeliness, outcomes and systemic recommendations, and an annual equality and access report. It shall protect personal data, fair trial and safety.”

### 9. Insertion of Chapter VIIIA

After Chapter VIII of the principal Act, the following Chapter shall be inserted:—

## “CHAPTER VIIIA — CUSTODY INTEGRITY AND EVIDENCE

**87A. Non-derogable duties.**—Nothing in this Act authorises torture, cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment, arbitrary detention, discrimination, retaliation, denial of counsel or avoidance of a duty under the Constitution or applicable criminal procedure.

**87B. Custody record and care.**—On arrest or entry into custody, the responsible officer shall record the legal basis, time, place, identity of officers, visible condition and communications required by applicable law. The person shall receive accessible information about grounds, legal assistance, communication, medical care and complaint routes.

**87C. Medical confidentiality.**—Medical assessment shall be conducted by a practitioner not subject to the investigating officer’s case direction. Health information may be disclosed only under a specified law or court order, to the minimum extent stated, with a disclosure log and notice unless a court records a concrete safety or evidence risk.

**87D. Custody evidence.**—The Director General shall implement a necessity-based custody-evidence plan. It shall exclude toilets, changing areas and confidential lawyer-client and clinical consultations. Evidence shall be held by a designated custodian separate from the unit under complaint where practicable, with access, export, retention and deletion logs.

**87E. Preservation and failure.**—A serious incident or credible complaint requires immediate preservation. Equipment failure shall be logged and remedied. Failure creates neither automatic guilt nor innocence; only a competent court may draw an inference under the applicable law of evidence from proved facts.

**87F. Audit.**—An independent annual audit shall test evidence coverage, outages, access, deletion, procurement conflicts, privacy, equality and complaint outcomes, and publish a de-identified report.”

### 10. Insertion of section 115A

After section 115 of the principal Act, the following section shall be inserted:—

**“115A. Limits on rules.**—Rules may provide only for forms, access methods, retention periods, audit methodology, training, procurement safeguards, staffing plans and implementation timetables. A draft rule shall be published with reasons, equality and privacy assessment, consultation summary and response, and laid before the Legislative Assembly. Rules shall not create an offence, remove a remedy, authorise case-specific direction, weaken this Act’s custody duties or alter the separation of functions.”

### 11. Insertion of Chapter VIIIB — accountability consequences and due process

After Chapter VIIIA as inserted by this Act, the following Chapter shall be inserted:—

## “CHAPTER VIIIB — ACCOUNTABILITY CONSEQUENCES AND DUE PROCESS

**87G. Duty to act on substantiated misconduct.**—Where an independent investigation, disciplinary inquiry or court establishes police misconduct, the competent authority shall record and publish, subject to lawful privacy and fair-trial limits, the corrective action, disciplinary consequence, referral and implementation date. A finding shall not be ignored or reduced because of the rank, political association, office or public profile of any person.

**87H. Interim protective measures.**—Where credible evidence shows a material risk to life, liberty, evidence, complainants or witnesses, the competent authority may impose a time-limited non-punitive protective measure, including reassignment from the relevant function, preservation of records, supervised access or no-contact direction. The person affected must receive written reasons, a hearing within seven days, periodic review and an effective review route.

**87I. Disciplinary consequences.**—(1) Serious misconduct established through a fair disciplinary process shall attract a proportionate consequence, including censure, loss of command, transfer from sensitive duties, suspension, reduction in rank, compulsory retirement or dismissal, as justified by gravity, harm, intent, repetition, abuse of position, obstruction and mitigation.

(2) Torture, custodial death or disappearance, sexual violence, fabrication or destruction of material evidence, knowingly retaliatory policing, or deliberate defiance of a court-preservation order shall require referral for criminal investigation and disciplinary proceedings without delay. No administrative process may prevent or substitute for lawful criminal investigation or prosecution.

(3) No person may be disciplined except on disclosed allegations, a meaningful opportunity to respond, an impartial decision-maker, a reasoned decision and an accessible appeal or judicial review. A criminal acquittal does not automatically determine a disciplinary question; a disciplinary decision does not determine criminal guilt.

**87J. Criminal accountability.**—Where facts reasonably disclose an offence under applicable criminal law, the designated independent investigator shall refer the matter with its evidence record to the competent prosecution authority. The prosecution authority shall record reasons for a material charging or non-charging decision, subject to witness safety, investigation integrity and fair trial, and shall be free from case-specific executive direction.

**87K. Reparation and recovery.**—(1) A court or other competent authority may award compensation, medical and psychosocial support, legal-aid costs, restoration of service or other lawful relief to a person harmed by misconduct.

(2) Recovery from an officer may occur only after a reasoned final finding of wilful or grossly negligent misconduct, a hearing on ability to pay and proportionality, and an appeal or review opportunity. Recovery shall not diminish urgent relief owed to the harmed person.

**87L. Equality and anti-vendetta audit.**—The annual audit shall examine whether complaint, investigation, arrest, prosecution, transfer and discipline patterns show direct or indirect discrimination or retaliation for political belief, criticism, journalism, protest, whistleblowing, association, caste, religion, sex, disability, gender identity, language or other protected status. A material unexplained disparity requires a published corrective plan and independent follow-up audit.

**87M. Non-compliance and public challenge.**—(1) A refusal, delay, non-publication, record-withholding or other non-compliance under this Act shall be recorded with reasons and notified to the affected person.

(2) The Commission, a Panel, the auditor, an affected person or a public-interest organisation acting in good faith may seek a direction from a competent court to enforce this Act. No officer has immunity for a knowing or reckless contravention.

(3) An annual report shall identify every material non-compliance, responsible office, corrective deadline and completion status. The report shall be laid before the Legislative Assembly and remain publicly accessible.”

**87N. People’s accountability review.**—(1) The Commission shall convene an annual public accountability review in every police range and a State review every two years. It shall publish the agenda, evidence base, uncertainty, equality data, submissions and a reasoned response to material recommendations.

(2) Review procedures shall provide confidential channels and anti-retaliation protection, language and disability access, and outreach to rural, marginalised and low-literacy communities. A public body shall not use participation data to identify, monitor or disadvantage a participant.

(3) The review may recommend legislative, budgetary, administrative and audit action. Its recommendations must receive a reasoned public response, but it shall not control individual criminal, disciplinary or judicial outcomes.”

**87O. Citizen-triggered audit.**—Where published data reaches a threshold prescribed by Act-level schedule for custody death or serious injury, repeated evidence loss, substantiated retaliation, unexplained equality disparity or material complaint delay, an independent audit shall begin automatically. A petition meeting the threshold in section 44F may request such an audit. The auditor shall publish its methodology, report and corrective recommendations, subject to privacy and fair-trial safeguards.

**87P. Protected disclosure and free public reporting.**—A person who in good faith reports, assists, publishes or preserves evidence of misconduct, systemic failure or corruption is protected against retaliation. The identity of a confidential source shall not be compelled or disclosed except under a specific court order that finds disclosure strictly necessary, proportionate and unavailable by less intrusive means. A knowing retaliatory act or unlawful disclosure shall trigger disciplinary action and referral for investigation under applicable criminal law.

**87Q. Open accountability data.**—The Commission shall publish accessible, machine-readable and de-identified data on arrests, custody deaths and injuries, complaints, preservation notices, evidence outages, transfers, disciplinary outcomes, prosecution referrals, delays, audit findings and budget shortfalls. The data must include methodology, correction process and independent annual verification; it shall never identify protected persons or prejudice a fair trial.

**87R. Access to justice.**—The State shall establish an independently administered public-interest legal-aid and enforcement fund for persons without adequate means to obtain advice, representation, preservation orders or remedies under this Act. Allocation shall use published neutral criteria, conflict rules, reasons and appeal; neither government nor police may direct individual funding decisions.

**87S. Randomised case allocation.**—Police-misconduct investigators and prosecutors shall be assigned by auditable random or pre-set allocation, subject to conflict screening. Any departure requires written reasons, independent approval, public aggregate reporting and court review.

**87T. Exceptional technology and emergency powers.**—Any exceptional custody-surveillance, data-retention or emergency measure authorised under this Act expires after five years unless reenacted by the Legislative Assembly after a published independent evaluation, public consultation and recorded vote. No reenactment may weaken sections 87A to 87S or constitutional remedies.

**87U. Legislative accountability.**—The annual report under section 87 shall be debated by the Legislative Assembly within one hundred and twenty days of laying. The debate, votes, responsible offices, deadlines and compliance status shall be published in an accessible form.

**87V. Independent medical and forensic access.**—Every serious custodial incident requires prompt access to independent medical and, where relevant, forensic expertise selected without direction from the implicated police unit. Reports shall be preserved, provided to the person or representative subject to lawful limits, and available to a competent court.

**87W. Tamper-evident evidence systems.**—Custody-evidence systems shall use access-controlled, tamper-evident event logs, independent technical audit, incident notification and verifiable deletion. A contractor shall not control access, alteration, retention or disclosure decisions in an individual case.

**87X. Knowing obstruction.**—A public officer who knowingly suppresses a complaint, destroys or alters material evidence, retaliates against a protected person, makes a prohibited case-specific direction, or knowingly defies a court order shall be referred for disciplinary and criminal action under applicable law. Liability is determined only through the ordinary fair process, with notice, evidence, hearing, reasons and review.”

**87Y. Independent appointments.**—(1) The Director General of Police, Chairperson and members of the Commission, head of the independent forensic service and Director of Prosecution shall be selected from published shortlists by a plural selection panel that includes government, opposition and independent constitutional or judicial representation. The panel shall publish eligibility criteria, conflicts, reasons and a response to material public objections.

(2) No one political bloc may control selection, removal, funding, direction and audit of the same office. A vacancy shall be filled by the same process and shall not justify a lower independence standard.

**87Z. Budget, disclosure and cooling-off.**—(1) The Commission, Panels, independent forensic service and legal-aid fund shall receive a separately published minimum annual operating allocation. A material mid-year reduction requires a written public reason, Legislative Assembly notice and independent audit.

(2) Senior police officers, prosecutors, forensic heads and Accountability Commission members shall publish annual asset and conflict declarations and a post-employment disclosure. For two years after leaving office they shall not accept a paid role with a political party, a contractor serving their former body, or a private security business materially connected with their former official functions.

**87AA. Citizen oversight panels.**—(1) The Commission shall convene advisory citizen oversight panels selected by transparent lottery from a voluntary, representative and privacy-protected pool. Panels shall review aggregate audit findings, public data and systemic recommendations only.

(2) A panel has no power to decide an individual case, direct an investigation, appoint or remove an official, disclose protected information or override a court, legislature or constitutional right.

**87AB. Emergency register.**—Every exceptional order under this Act shall be entered in a public register stating its legal source, objective evidence, decision-maker, geographical and personal scope, reviewer, duration, expiry and renewal. Confidential details may be withheld only by a time-limited court order, with a public justification summary.

**87AC. Automatic judicial and independent notice.**—The State shall notify a competent court, the Commission and the designated human-rights authority without delay of every custodial death, disappearance, credible torture allegation, sexual violence allegation or material evidence-tampering event. Notice does not determine liability or displace an independent investigation.

**87AD. Equality and human-rights audit.**—An independent annual audit shall assess the Act’s operation and police outcomes for caste, tribe, religion, sex, gender identity, disability, age, language, economic status and other relevant inequality impacts, using safe disaggregation, community participation and a published correction plan.

**87AE. Independent access to custody locations.**—Legislative committees, statutory human-rights bodies and accredited independent monitors may inspect police custody locations with reasonable notice or, where a serious risk is recorded, without notice. Access may be limited only by a written, necessary and proportionate safety measure capable of prompt court review; it shall not prevent private communication with a detained person or confidential access to records required by law.”

### 12. Workforce plan

Within six months, the Director General shall publish an annual workforce plan stating authorised strength, vacancies, duty hours, fatigue indicators, rest, leave, training, equipment, housing access and confidential occupational and mental-health support. An emergency departure from ordinary duty-hours must be based on an immediate recorded threat, expires after twelve hours per officer and may be extended once for twelve hours by a senior officer outside the immediate command; it requires compensatory rest and audit.

### 13. Procurement and data protection

Technology or service procurement under this Act shall use open criteria, conflict disclosure, security testing, audit access, data-location and deletion duties, and a prohibition on vendor case direction. Personal data shall be collected, used, shared and retained only under specified law, for a recorded purpose, using the least intrusive reasonably available means.

### 14. Judicial remedies and savings

(1) A person affected by unlawful refusal, delay, retaliation or evidence risk may seek preservation, disclosure, protection, compensation or other lawful relief before a competent court.

(2) This Act supplements and does not reduce constitutional protections, BNSS safeguards, judicial remedies, fair-trial rights or stronger lawful protection.

### 15. Implementation and review

(1) Within six months, the State shall publish a costed implementation plan addressing staff, facilities, technology, procurement, legal aid, disability and language access, training and data protection.

(2) An independent review shall be completed three years after commencement and every five years thereafter, assessing rights, equality, public confidence, costs, workforce wellbeing, evidence integrity, complaints, remedies and capture risk. The review and the State response shall be laid before the Legislative Assembly.

### 16. Consequential amendment of Assam Police Manual

The Assam Police Manual, including rules 73 and 75 as amended in 2023, shall be read subject to sections 45 and 46 of the principal Act as amended by this Act. Operational transfer competence remains unaffected, but each decision must satisfy the objective criteria, reasons, anti-retaliation and review duties inserted by this Act.

---

## STATEMENT OF OBJECTS AND REASONS

The Bill reforms existing Assam police-governance and accountability institutions instead of creating parallel bodies. It protects independent complaint handling, impartial service administration, custody integrity, evidence preservation and accessible remedy, while keeping investigation, prosecution, discipline and adjudication separate. It adds mandatory action on substantiated misconduct, proportionate severe service consequences, independent criminal referral, victim reparation, recovery after fair process and equality/anti-vendetta audit.

## FINANCIAL MEMORANDUM

Implementation requires expenditure for independent complaints access, evidence preservation, audit, training, legal-aid referral, accessibility and public reporting. A costed plan is required by clause 14 before commencement.

## MEMORANDUM REGARDING DELEGATED LEGISLATION

Clause 10 narrowly permits rules on technical implementation only. It requires publication, assessment, consultation and legislative laying, and forbids rules that weaken core rights, remedies or institutional separation.
