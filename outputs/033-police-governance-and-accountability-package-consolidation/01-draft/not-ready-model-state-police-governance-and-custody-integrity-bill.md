# MODEL STATE POLICE GOVERNANCE AND CUSTODY INTEGRITY BILL

> **Status:** Draft model text for State adaptation. It is not current law, legal advice, or ready for legislative introduction. The adopting State, local statutory cross-references, commencement date, fiscal memorandum and consequential amendments remain unresolved.

## CHAPTER I — PRELIMINARY

### 1. Short title, application and commencement

(1) This Act may be called the State Police Governance and Custody Integrity Act, [year].

(2) It applies to the State police service and every person exercising a policing function under State law, subject to the Constitution and applicable Central law.

(3) It commences on a date notified after publication of the implementation plan under section 38.

### 2. Objects

The objects of this Act are to secure impartial, rights-respecting and accountable policing; prevent torture, discriminatory and retaliatory policing; protect the integrity of custody evidence; provide accessible complaints and remedies; and promote safe, effective working conditions without creating an unchecked institution.

### 3. Interpretation

In this Act, unless context otherwise requires—

(a) “case-specific direction” means a direction about the opening, course, evidentiary assessment, arrest, charge, prosecution, discontinuance, discipline or outcome of an identifiable matter;

(b) “serious custodial incident” means a death, disappearance, allegation of torture or sexual violence, serious injury, or credible allegation of retaliatory or discriminatory policing in custody;

(c) “custody evidence” means a record made for lawful accountability of an arrest, transport, detention, interview, search or medical transfer;

(d) “retaliatory policing” means a benefit, burden, investigation, arrest, transfer, licence action or stigma imposed because of lawful political belief, criticism, journalism, protest, whistleblowing, association or protected status; and

(e) “Authority” means the Independent Police Complaints Authority established under section 20.

## CHAPTER II — GOVERNANCE AND SERVICE INTEGRITY

### 4. State Police Governance Commission

(1) The State shall establish a State Police Governance Commission.

(2) The Commission shall publish non-case-specific policing priorities, review aggregate performance, monitor compliance with this Act and lay an annual report before the Legislature. A report shall use aggregate, minimised and de-identified information; any proposed disclosure capable of identifying a person requires a written legal basis, necessity finding, risk assessment and independent review.

(3) The Commission has no power to investigate an offence, direct an identifiable investigation or prosecution, decide discipline, or interfere with a pending court proceeding.

### 5. Composition and appointment

(1) The Commission shall have a chair and members appointed through a published process with recorded criteria, conflict screening and reasons.

(2) The selection panel shall include government, opposition and independent constitutional or judicial representation in proportions that prevent one political bloc from controlling appointments.

(3) Terms shall be staggered. Removal is only for stated incapacity, misconduct or conflict after notice, hearing, published reasons and judicial review.

### 6. Written-direction rule

(1) An executive or police-policy direction must be in writing, identify its lawful source and public purpose, and be published within seven days unless a court permits a shorter, specified confidentiality period.

(2) A case-specific direction is invalid. Informal, delegated, outsourced or funding-based conduct that achieves the same result is also invalid.

### 7. Police Establishment Board

(1) The State shall constitute a Police Establishment Board for postings, transfers and service recommendations as prescribed by State law.

(2) The Board shall apply published objective criteria; record reasons for material departures; publish aggregate transfer data; and provide a prompt internal review and court remedy.

(3) No transfer, posting, appraisal or disciplinary measure may be retaliatory policing.

### 8. Functional separation plan

(1) Within one year, the police chief shall publish a phased plan to separate investigation functions from routine law-and-order deployment to the extent staffing, case volume and local conditions permit.

(2) The plan shall include workload data, training, forensic access, equality impact, public consultation and annual independent evaluation.

### 9. Exceptional deployment

(1) An investigator may be diverted from an assigned investigation only for a documented immediate threat that cannot reasonably be met otherwise.

(2) The decision expires after seventy-two hours unless independently reviewed. It does not suspend any custody or evidence-preservation duty.

### 10. Public information and audit

The Commission shall publish anonymised quarterly data on arrests, custody, complaints, transfers, deployment exceptions, outcomes and disparities, subject to privacy, fair-trial and safety safeguards.

## CHAPTER III — CUSTODY INTEGRITY AND EVIDENCE

### 11. Non-derogable custody duties

Nothing in this Act authorises torture, cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment, arbitrary detention, discrimination, retaliation, denial of legal counsel, or avoidance of duties under the Constitution or applicable criminal procedure.

### 12. Immediate safeguards

(1) On arrest or entry into police custody, the responsible officer shall record the legal basis, time, place, identity of officers, visible condition and communication required by applicable law.

(2) The person shall receive accessible information about the grounds, available legal assistance, communication rights, medical assistance and complaint route in a language and format they can understand.

### 13. Medical care and independent documentation

(1) A request for medical care or an indication of injury shall be acted on without delay.

(2) Medical assessment shall be conducted by a medical practitioner who is not subject to the investigating officer’s case direction. Health information is confidential and may be disclosed only under a specified law or court order, to the minimum extent stated in the order or law, with a disclosure log and notice to the person unless a court records why notice would create a concrete safety or evidence risk.

### 14. Custody evidence plan

(1) The police chief shall implement a custody-evidence plan that identifies locations and events requiring recording or other preservation, based on necessity, safety, dignity, privacy and accessibility.

(2) The plan shall exclude toilets, changing areas and confidential lawyer-client and clinical consultations. It shall not record a person’s intimate bodily functions or confidential legal or clinical communications.

### 15. Evidence custodian and access log

(1) Custody evidence shall be held by a designated evidence custodian separate from the unit under complaint where practicable.

(2) Every access, export, alteration request, retention extension and deletion shall be logged and auditable.

### 16. Preservation notice

(1) On a serious custodial incident or a credible complaint, relevant evidence shall be preserved immediately and a preservation notice given to the Authority and the assigned investigator.

(2) A person affected may seek an urgent preservation order from a court.

### 17. Retention and deletion

(1) The State shall prescribe a minimum retention period after a published privacy and operational assessment.

(2) Evidence subject to a preservation notice, court order, complaint, investigation or proceeding shall not be deleted until lawful final disposition and any review period have ended.

### 18. Equipment failure

(1) Equipment failure shall be logged promptly, notified to the evidence custodian and remedied within a prescribed period.

(2) Failure does not create automatic criminal guilt, innocence or an irrebuttable evidentiary presumption. Only a competent court may draw an inference, and only under the applicable law of evidence from proved facts including the cause, duration, reporting and remediation of the failure.

### 19. Independent audit

An independent auditor shall annually test the custody-evidence system for coverage, outages, access, deletion, procurement conflicts, privacy, equality and complaint outcomes, and publish an aggregate report.

## CHAPTER IV — COMPLAINTS, REFERRAL AND REMEDY

### 20. Independent Police Complaints Authority

(1) The State shall establish an accessible independent Authority at State level and provide district-level filing access.

(2) The Authority shall receive, acknowledge, preserve, refer, monitor and report on complaints. It shall use only the minimum personal information needed for those functions, maintain access logs, and publish only de-identified aggregate information. It has no power to prosecute, decide criminal guilt, impose service punishment or hear an appeal from its own decision.

### 21. Independence and accessibility

(1) Appointments, terms, conflicts, removal, budget request and annual reporting of the Authority shall be published.

(2) A complainant may file in person, by post, telephone, online or through an authorised representative without charge. Disability accommodation, language assistance, confidentiality and anti-retaliation protection shall be available.

### 22. Mandatory referral

(1) A serious custodial incident shall be referred without delay to an investigator selected under a pre-published allocation rule and screened for conflict.

(2) The same rule applies where the complaint credibly alleges torture, sexual violence, disappearance, evidence tampering, retaliatory policing or a conflict in the ordinary investigating unit.

### 23. Complaint assessment

(1) The Authority shall give a written acknowledgement and preserve available evidence.

(2) A decision to close, defer or refer a complaint must give reasons, state the review route and be communicated in an accessible form.

### 24. No retaliatory action

No person may be threatened, transferred, detained, disadvantaged or publicly stigmatised for making, assisting, reporting on or lawfully criticising a complaint, unless independently established evidence supports lawful action under the same standards applied to everyone else.

### 25. Referral integrity

(1) The assigned investigator shall record material steps, conflicts and reasons for any reassignment.

(2) The Authority may seek a court direction where a referral is obstructed, evidence is at risk or the allocation rule is breached.

### 26. Disciplinary process

(1) A disciplinary authority may act on independently obtained evidence but shall provide notice, disclosure subject to lawful limits, a hearing, reasons and appeal.

(2) Discipline does not prevent criminal investigation or prosecution, and a criminal proceeding does not automatically prevent proportionate interim protective measures.

### 27. Victim and complainant support

The State shall provide information, legal-aid referral, medical and psychosocial referral, witness-protection assessment and reasonable participation support without conditioning assistance on a particular outcome.

### 28. Public reporting

The Authority shall publish quarterly anonymised information about receipts, referrals, timeliness, outcomes, systemic recommendations and equality indicators. It shall protect personal data, fair trial and safety.

## CHAPTER V — FAIR INVESTIGATION AND PROSECUTION

### 29. Objective opening criteria

An investigation, coercive step, transfer for alleged misconduct or prosecution recommendation shall be based on recorded objective grounds, relevant evidence and lawful authority, not protected expression, association, identity or political affiliation.

### 30. Case allocation and conflict

The State shall use pre-set or randomisable case-allocation rules, documented conflict screening and recorded reassignment reasons for politically sensitive or police-misconduct matters.

### 31. Independent prosecution function

(1) Prosecution decisions shall be made by the competent prosecution function independently of the investigating police chain, subject to applicable law and court supervision.

(2) A case-specific executive direction to prosecute, not prosecute or frame a charge is invalid.

### 32. Reasons and review

(1) A material prosecution decision shall be recorded with reasons sufficient for lawful review, subject to protection of witnesses, investigations and fair trial.

(2) A person alleging selective or retaliatory enforcement may seek internal review and constitutional or other judicial remedies available in law.

### 33. Comparator audit

An independent annual audit shall test aggregate patterns of arrests, investigations, charges, diversions, closures and prosecution outcomes for disparities and indicators of selective enforcement. The auditor shall use minimised, de-identified data, a documented methodology, access controls and a public methodology statement; it shall not disclose protected personal data or direct individual cases.

### 34. Savings for courts and criminal procedure

Nothing in this Chapter alters a court’s jurisdiction, the burden of proof, the rights of an accused, the prosecution’s statutory duties, or an existing constitutional remedy.

## CHAPTER VI — WORKFORCE WELLBEING AND DELIVERY

### 35. Workforce plan

The police chief shall publish an annual workforce plan with authorised strength, vacancies, duty hours, fatigue indicators, rest, leave, training, equipment, housing access and mental-health support. Any disaggregation shall use the smallest reliable aggregate and may be published only where a written equality purpose, re-identification assessment and independent privacy review show that publication will not identify an individual.

### 36. Fatigue and rest safeguards

(1) The State shall prescribe maximum ordinary duty hours and minimum rest after a published evidence assessment, workforce consultation, equality assessment and legislative scrutiny of draft rules.

(2) An emergency exception may be authorised only for an immediate, recorded threat to life, physical safety or essential disaster response that cannot reasonably be met through ordinary deployment. It expires after twelve hours for each officer; one further twelve-hour extension requires written reasons by a senior officer independent of the immediate command. It requires compensatory rest, prompt aggregate reporting and independent audit, and shall not be used as a routine staffing substitute.

### 37. Confidential support

Police personnel shall have confidential access to independent mental-health and occupational-health support. Use of that support shall not by itself prejudice posting, appraisal or discipline.

### 38. Implementation plan and appropriation

(1) Within six months, the State shall publish a costed implementation plan, including phased staffing, accessibility, procurement, training and data-protection measures.

(2) Implementation is subject to legislative appropriation; a funding shortfall does not excuse non-derogable custody duties or evidence preservation once a serious incident is identified.

### 39. Procurement integrity

Technology and service procurement under this Act shall use open criteria, conflict disclosure, security testing, audit access, data-location and deletion duties, and a prohibition on vendor case direction.

## CHAPTER VII — ACCOUNTABILITY, REVIEW AND MISCELLANEOUS

### 40. Judicial remedy

Any person aggrieved by a materially unlawful act, refusal, delay, retaliation or evidence risk under this Act may seek the remedies available before a competent court, including preservation, disclosure, protection, compensation or other lawful relief.

### 41. Reasons, records and access

Every material decision under this Act shall be recorded. An affected person shall receive reasons and access to records unless a specified statute or court order permits a restriction. A restriction must identify the concrete harm, the least restrictive redaction, an expiry no later than thirty days unless renewed by a court, and the independent review route.

### 42. Data minimisation and security

Personal data shall be collected, used, shared and retained only where a specified law identifies the purpose and the record shows that no less intrusive reasonably available measure can achieve it. Sharing requires a written recipient, purpose, data fields, retention date and access control. The State shall conduct periodic independent security and rights audits.

### 43. Anti-evasion

No person may do indirectly, through a contractor, algorithm, funding condition, task force, secrecy label, informal instruction or serial temporary measure, what this Act prohibits directly.

### 44. Rules

(1) The State Government may make rules only for forms, access methods, retention periods, audit methodology, staffing plans, training, procurement safeguards and implementation timetables consistent with this Act. Before making a rule it shall publish a draft, reasons, equality and privacy assessment, consultation summary and response, and lay the final rule before the Legislature.

(2) Rules may not create an offence, remove a remedy, authorise case-specific direction, weaken non-derogable custody duties, or alter the functional separation in this Act.

### 45. Periodic review

(1) An independent review shall be completed three years after commencement and every five years thereafter.

(2) The review shall assess rights, equality, public confidence, costs, workforce wellbeing, investigation quality, evidence integrity, complaints, remedies and capture risks, and be laid before the Legislature with a public response.

### 46. Severability and saving

(1) If a provision is held invalid, the remaining provisions continue to the extent possible.

(2) This Act supplements and does not reduce constitutional protections, criminal-procedure safeguards, judicial remedies or any stronger lawful protection.
