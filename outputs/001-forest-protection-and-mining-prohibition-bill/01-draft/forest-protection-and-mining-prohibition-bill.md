# Comprehensive Draft Legislation and Constitutional Entrenchment Analysis: The Forest Protection, Anti-Mining, Public Official Deterrence, and Ecological Restoration Act, 2026

## 1. Bottom Line and Legal Expert Analysis

- **Jurisdiction**: Union of India (Parliament of India, operating under Entry 17A and 17B of List III - Concurrent List, read with Articles 21, 48A, and 368 of the Constitution of India).
- **As-of Date**: 2026-07-27
- **Client / Deliverable**: Legal formulation, constitutional analysis, statutory entrenchment mechanism, and companion Constitutional Amendment Bill ("The Constitution (One Hundred and Thirty-Fifth Amendment) Bill, 2026") guaranteeing that the Forest Protection and Anti-Mining Act cannot be easily repealed or diluted by a transient majority.

### Constitutional Analysis of Legislative Entrenchment in India:
1. **The Problem of Statutory Repeal**: Under standard parliamentary procedures (Articles 100 & 107), an ordinary statutory law can be amended or repealed by a simple majority of members present and voting.
2. **The Mechanism for Entrenchment**: To create a legally binding, non-dilutable statutory protection:
   - **Ordinary Law Mechanism (Clause 13 of the Bill)**: Establishes a statutory non-amendment rule linking any repeal or dilution of the Act to the Constitutional Amendment procedure under Article 368.
   - **Constitutional Amendment Mechanism (Companion Bill under Article 368)**: Inserts **Article 48B** into the Constitution of India. This elevates forest preservation and mining prohibition to a Constitutional Ecological Core, mandating that any Bill seeking to alter, dilute, or repeal the statutory protections requires:
     - **Supermajority**: An affirmative vote of **not less than two-thirds (2/3rd) of the total sanctioned strength** of each House of Parliament.
     - **Anti-Suspension & Anti-Circumvention Rule**: The total sanctioned strength is calculated strictly against the statutory maximum capacity of the House (543 for Lok Sabha, 245 for Rajya Sabha) **without deducting suspended, absent, or vacant seats**. Mass suspension of opposition MPs by the ruling executive cannot lower the voting threshold required for repeal.
     - **Federal Ratification**: Ratification by resolution passed by **not less than two-thirds (2/3rd) of the State Legislative Assemblies** across India prior to Presidential Assent.
     - **Basic Structure & Judicial Review**: Declares environmental integrity under Article 21 as part of the Basic Structure of the Constitution (*MK Ranjitsinh* 2024 SC, *Godavarman* 1997 SC), subjecting any attempt to repeal to automatic review by a 5-Judge Constitution Bench of the Supreme Court of India.

## 2. Problem, Evidence, and Existing-Law Map

Existing Indian legal frameworks governing forests and mining suffer from structural defects:
- **Van (Sanrakshan Evam Samvardhan) Adhiniyam 1980 (FCA 1980)**: Broad executive discretion permits forest diversion for mining upon payment of compensatory fees. Recent 2023 amendments diluted protections for unrecorded/deemed forests.
- **Mines and Minerals (Development and Regulation) Act 1957 (MMDR Act)**: Focuses primarily on mineral exploitation and revenue generation rather than permanent ecological preservation. Illegal mining penalties (Section 21) are frequently treated by commercial entities as a routine cost of doing business.
- **Lack of Institutional Accountability**: Public servants, forest officers, police officers, and politicians who collude with mining cartels or land grabbers rarely face mandatory imprisonment, asset forfeiture, or political disqualification.
- **Deforestation and Climate Vulnerability**: India's forest and tree cover stands at approximately 24.62% (ISFR 2021/2023). Unchecked mining and encroachment degrade critical watersheds, destroy wildlife corridors, and increase human-wildlife conflict.

## 6. Draft Legal Text: Statutory Bill and Companion Constitutional Amendment Bill

### PART A: THE FOREST PROTECTION, ANTI-MINING, PUBLIC OFFICIAL DETERRENCE, AND ECOLOGICAL RESTORATION BILL, 2026

**BILL NO. ______ OF 2026**

BE it enacted by Parliament in the Seventy-Seventh Year of the Republic of India as follows:—

#### CHAPTER I: PRELIMINARY
**1. Short title, extent, and commencement.**
(1) This Act may be called the Forest Protection, Anti-Mining, Public Official Deterrence, and Ecological Restoration Act, 2026.
(2) It extends to the whole of India.
(3) It shall come into force on such date as the Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, appoint, being not later than thirty days from the date of Presidential Assent.

**2. Definitions.**
In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires,—
(a) **"Deemed Forest"** means any land matching the dictionary meaning of "forest" regardless of ownership, classification, or registration, including contiguous wooded areas, high-altitude catchments, riverine forests, and unclassed state forests, as recognized in *T.N. Godavarman Thirumulpad v. Union of India* (1997);
(b) **"Ecologically Sensitive Area (ESA)"** means any zone declared under Section 3 of the Environment (Protection) Act 1986, including Eco-Sensitive Zones around National Parks, Wildlife Sanctuaries, Tiger Reserves, biosphere reserves, and critical wildlife corridors;
(c) **"Forest Land"** includes Reserved Forests, Protected Forests, Village Forests, Deemed Forests, ESAs, and lands declared as forests under any Central or State law;
(d) **"Illegal Mining"** means any operation involving the reconnaissance, prospecting, exploration, quarrying, surface or sub-surface mining, or extraction of minerals, soil, or stone conducted within or underneath Forest Land;
(e) **"Public Servant"** shall have the meaning assigned to it under Section 21 of the Indian Penal Code, 1860 / Section 2(28) of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, and includes Ministers, Members of Parliament, Members of State Assemblies, officers of police, forest, mining, revenue, and administrative services;
(f) **"Recognized Forest Rights"** means rights recognized and vested under the Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act, 2006 (FRA 2006).

**3. Non-Derogation and Overriding Effect.**
(1) The provisions of this Act shall have effect notwithstanding anything inconsistent contained in the Mines and Minerals (Development and Regulation) Act 1957, the Van (Sanrakshan Evam Samvardhan) Adhiniyam 1980, the Environment (Protection) Act 1986, or any other Central or State law for the time being in force.
(2) **Protection of Indigenous Rights**: Nothing in this Act shall abridge, restrict, alter, or extinguish any right, title, or interest recognized, vested, or protected under the Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act, 2006, or under the Provisions of the Panchayats (Extension to the Scheduled Areas) Act, 1996 (PESA).

#### CHAPTER II: ABSOLUTE MINING BAN AND ANTI-ENCROACHMENT
**4. Absolute Ban on Mining in Forest Lands.**
(1) Notwithstanding any permit, lease, concession, clearance, or licence granted under any law prior to or after the commencement of this Act, no person, corporation, state enterprise, or authority shall conduct any mining or mineral extraction activity on, within, or underneath any Forest Land or Ecologically Sensitive Area.
(2) All existing mining leases, clearances, and concessions operating within or underneath Forest Land or ESAs stand terminated by operation of law on the expiry of ninety (90) days from the commencement of this Act.
(3) Every holder of a terminated lease shall, within one hundred and eighty (180) days, complete full ecological land reclamation at their sole expense under the supervision of the National Forest Audit Authority.

**5. Prohibition on Encroachment and Mandatory Eviction.**
(1) No person or commercial entity shall encroach upon, clear, construct upon, or divert Forest Land for non-forest commercial, industrial, or residential use.
(2) The State Forest Protection Force shall initiate mandatory eviction proceedings against commercial or industrial encroachers within thirty (30) days of detection.
(3) **Tribal Exemption**: Exercise of customary dwelling, traditional agriculture, or community forest resource use by Scheduled Tribes or traditional forest dwellers under FRA 2006 shall not be treated as encroachment under this Act.

#### CHAPTER III: PENALTIES AND PUBLIC OFFICIAL DETERRENCE
**6. Offences by Private Violators.**
Whosoever violates Section 4 or Section 5 shall be punished with rigorous imprisonment for a term which shall not be less than five (5) years but which may extend to ten (10) years, and shall also be liable to a mandatory fine equal to two hundred percent (200%) of the estimated commercial value of the minerals extracted or ecological damage caused, whichever is higher.

**7. Heightened Offences and Deterrent Penalties for Public Servants.**
(1) Any Public Servant, Law Enforcement Officer, Forest Official, Revenue Officer, or Executive Official who—
    (a) willfully abets, permits, assists, or conspires in illegal mining or encroachment on Forest Land;
    (b) fails to act upon or report illegal mining or commercial encroachment within their statutory jurisdiction after receiving written notice or satellite alert; or
    (c) issues fraudulent permits, backdated clearances, or false survey reports covering Forest Land;
shall be guilty of an offence under this Act and shall be punished with **rigorous imprisonment for a term which shall not be less than seven (7) years but which may extend to fourteen (14) years**, and shall also be liable to fine.
(2) Any Public Servant convicted under sub-section (1) shall suffer mandatory forfeiture of all personal properties acquired during their service tenure and shall be permanently disqualified from holding any public office or employment under the Union or State Governments.

**8. Special Offence for Political Executives and Ministers.**
Any Minister, Member of Parliament, Member of Legislative Assembly, or elected executive who issues directions, exercises political pressure, takes monetary or political gratification, or shields any person or entity engaged in illegal mining or forest encroachment shall be punished with **rigorous imprisonment for a term which shall not be less than ten (10) years but which may extend to fourteen (14) years**, and shall be disqualified from contesting elections to Parliament, State Assemblies, or local bodies for a period of ten (10) years from the date of release from prison, under Section 8 of the Representation of the People Act, 1951.

#### CHAPTER IV: ECOLOGICAL RESTORATION MANDATE AND AUDIT
**9. Statutory Trajectory to 50% Forest Cover.**
(1) The Union of India and all State Governments shall execute an Integrated National Forest Restoration Plan to expand India's biodiverse forest and tree cover to at least **fifty percent (50%)** of the total geographical area of the country by the year **2046**.
(2) **Binding 5-Year Interim Milestones**:
    (a) **Milestone I (2031)**: Achieving complete net-zero forest loss and expanding total biodiverse cover to **30%**;
    (b) **Milestone II (2036)**: Expanding biodiverse forest cover to **38%**;
    (c) **Milestone III (2041)**: Expanding biodiverse forest cover to **44%**;
    (d) **Milestone IV (2046)**: Achieving and maintaining at least **50%** biodiverse forest and canopy cover.

**10. Establishment of National Forest Audit Authority (NFAA).**
(1) The Central Government shall, by notification, establish an independent statutory body to be called the National Forest Audit Authority (NFAA), comprising eminent ecological scientists, retired Supreme Court judges, ISRO remote sensing experts, and tribal rights representatives.
(2) The NFAA shall conduct annual satellite-based GIS forest cover audits, verify biodiversity quality (preventing monoculture plantations from being misreported as natural forests), and publish public annual scorecards for every State and District.

**11. Sanctions for Failure to Meet Interim Milestones.**
Where any State or District fails to achieve its statutory 5-year interim restoration milestone due to gross administrative negligence, misallocation or diversion of restoration funds, or fraudulent data reporting:
(a) The NFAA shall recommend direct departmental and criminal investigation against the Principal Chief Conservator of Forests (PCCF) and the concerned Departmental Secretary;
(b) The Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) shall conduct a forensic financial audit of CAMPA funds in the defaulting jurisdiction;
(c) The Central Government shall withhold non-ecological discretionary grants to the defaulting State until a corrective restoration plan is approved and implemented.

#### CHAPTER V: SPECIAL COURTS AND PROCEDURE
**12. Designated Special Environmental Courts.**
(1) The State Government, in consultation with the Chief Justice of the High Court, shall designate at least one Court of Session in each district as a Special Environmental Court for the speedy trial of offences under this Act.
(2) Special Courts shall conclude trials within six (6) months from the filing of the charge sheet.
(3) Offences under Section 7 and Section 8 shall be non-bailable and cognizable.

#### CHAPTER VI: PARLIAMENTARY ENTRENCHMENT AND AMENDMENT SAFEGUARDS
**13. Parliamentary Supermajority and State Ratification Requirement for Repeal or Dilution.**
(1) Notwithstanding anything contained in Article 107 of the Constitution or any other law for the time being in force, no Bill seeking to repeal, suspend, weaken, or dilute any provision of this Act shall be passed by either House of Parliament unless it satisfies the conditions set forth in sub-sections (2), (3), and (4).
(2) **Supermajority Requirement**: Any such Bill must be passed in each House of Parliament by a majority of **not less than two-thirds (2/3rd) of the TOTAL SANCTIONED STRENGTH** of that House.
(3) **Anti-Suspension Safeguard**: For the purposes of sub-section (2), the "total sanctioned strength" shall mean the total statutory maximum capacity of seats in the House as specified under Articles 81 and 83 of the Constitution, without deducting any members who may be suspended, absent, disqualified, or whose seats are vacant at the time of voting.
(4) **Federal State Ratification**: No amendment or repeal passed under sub-section (2) shall take effect or be presented for Presidential Assent unless it has been ratified by resolutions passed by **not less than two-thirds (2/3rd) of the Legislative Assemblies of the States** across the Republic of India.
(5) **Judicial Constitutional Audit**: Any Bill seeking to amend or repeal this Act passed under this Section shall automatically be referred to a 5-Judge Constitution Bench of the Supreme Court of India to verify compliance with Article 21 and the Basic Structure Doctrine prior to Presidential Assent.

### PART B: COMPANION CONSTITUTIONAL AMENDMENT BILL

**BILL NO. ______ OF 2026**

**THE CONSTITUTION (ONE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-FIFTH AMENDMENT) BILL, 2026**

*A Bill further to amend the Constitution of India to entrench forest protection, prohibit mining in forest lands, and mandate special supermajority and state ratification procedures for any legislative repeal or dilution of environmental protection laws.*

BE it enacted by Parliament in the Seventy-Seventh Year of the Republic of India as follows:—

**1. Short title.**
This Act may be called the Constitution (One Hundred and Thirty-Fifth Amendment) Act, 2026.

**2. Insertion of new Article 48B.**
After Article 48A of the Constitution, the following Article shall be inserted, namely:—

> **"48B. Protection of Forest Core and Supermajority Entrenchment for Repeal of Forest Laws.**
> (1) The State shall preserve all existing forest lands, deemed forests, ecologically sensitive zones, and natural wild ecosystems, and shall enforce an absolute ban on commercial mining and extraction within forest areas.
> (2) No law enacted by Parliament providing for an absolute ban on forest mining, severe deterrence against public official collusion, or a mandatory ecological restoration trajectory to expand forest cover to fifty percent (50%) shall be repealed, suspended, or diluted in its material protection, except by a Bill passed in each House of Parliament by a majority of not less than **two-thirds of the total sanctioned membership of that House** (calculated without deducting suspended, absent, or vacant seats), and ratified by resolutions passed by **not less than two-thirds of the Legislative Assemblies of the States**.
> (3) Any act or omission by any public authority that indirectly dilutes or evades the protection guaranteed under clause (1) or (2) shall be void ab initio."

**3. Amendment of Article 368.**
In Article 368 of the Constitution, in clause (2), after the proviso, the following proviso shall be inserted, namely:—

> "Provided further that if any amendment seeks to alter, repeal, or weaken the statutory or constitutional protections governing forest preservation, anti-mining bans, or ecological restoration under Article 48B, the amendment shall require ratification by the Legislative Assemblies of not less than two-thirds of the States."

## 7. Clause-by-Clause Explanatory Notes

- **Clause 1-3**: Sets out short title, commencement, definitions, and explicit non-derogation of tribal forest rights under FRA 2006. Reinstates the broad *Godavarman* definition of deemed forest.
- **Clause 4-5**: Establishes absolute mining ban in all forest types and ESAs; terminates existing leases within 90 days; mandates mine site reclamation; prohibits commercial land grabbing while exempting FRA 2006 title holders.
- **Clause 6-8**: Establishes severe deterrent penalties. Heightened minimum 7-14 years imprisonment for public servants and 10-14 years for political executives who collude or permit violations, coupled with asset forfeiture and political disqualification.
- **Clause 9-11**: Establishes scientifically sound 20-year national trajectory (50% target by 2046) with 5-year audited interim milestones, independent ISRO/FSI auditing via NFAA, and severe institutional penalties for misfeasance or false reporting.
- **Clause 12**: Creates fast-track Special Courts ensuring trial completion within 6 months.
- **Clause 13 & Companion Constitutional Amendment Bill (Art. 48B)**: Inserts the Parliamentary Supermajority & State Ratification Entrenchment Clause. Requires 2/3rd of total parliamentary seats (ignoring suspensions) + 2/3rd State Legislative Assemblies ratification + Supreme Court Constitution Bench audit before any repeal or dilution can take effect.

- **Anti-Circumvention & Political Safeguards**: Prevents ruling parties from engineering temporary majorities via mass suspension of opposition MPs to force through forest land relaxations.
- **Tribal Equality & Due Process**: Guarantees that indigenous STs and traditional forest dwellers exercising FRA 2006 rights are fully protected from criminalization, ensuring anti-encroachment powers focus strictly on commercial and political land grabbers.
