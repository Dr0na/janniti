## 2. Problem, Evidence, and Existing-Law Map

Existing Indian legal frameworks governing forests and mining suffer from structural defects:
- **Van (Sanrakshan Evam Samvardhan) Adhiniyam 1980 (FCA 1980)**: Broad executive discretion permits forest diversion for mining upon payment of compensatory fees. Recent 2023 amendments diluted protections for unrecorded/deemed forests.
- **Mines and Minerals (Development and Regulation) Act 1957 (MMDR Act)**: Focuses primarily on mineral exploitation and revenue generation rather than permanent ecological preservation. Illegal mining penalties (Section 21) are frequently treated by commercial entities as a routine cost of doing business.
- **Lack of Institutional Accountability**: Public servants, forest officers, police officers, and politicians who collude with mining cartels or land grabbers rarely face mandatory imprisonment, asset forfeiture, or political disqualification.
- **Deforestation and Climate Vulnerability**: India's forest and tree cover stands at approximately 24.62% (ISFR 2021/2023). Unchecked mining and encroachment degrade critical watersheds, destroy wildlife corridors, and increase human-wildlife conflict.

## 3. Legal and Constitutional Analysis

### A. Statutory Entrenchment & Article 368 Supermajority
Under standard parliamentary procedure (Articles 100 & 107 of the Constitution of India), an ordinary Act of Parliament can theoretically be amended or repealed by a simple majority.
To legally enforce a non-repealable ecological core:
- **Clause 13 of the Bill** establishes a statutory entrenchment rule linking any repeal or dilution to Article 368.
- **The Constitution (One Hundred and Thirty-Fifth Amendment) Bill, 2026** inserts **Article 48B** into the Constitution of India, requiring:
  1. **2/3rd Supermajority of Total Sanctioned Capacity**: Any repeal or dilution requires an affirmative vote of not less than two-thirds (2/3rd) of the total statutory capacity of each House of Parliament (543 for Lok Sabha, 245 for Rajya Sabha).
  2. **Anti-Suspension Safeguard**: The voting denominator is fixed to the full sanctioned strength, preventing ruling parties from lowering the quorum by suspending opposition MPs.
  3. **Federal State Ratification**: Ratification by not less than two-thirds (2/3rd) of State Legislative Assemblies.
  4. **Basic Structure Review**: Mandatory referral to a 5-Judge Constitution Bench of the Supreme Court of India.

### B. Indigenous Rights Safeguard (FRA 2006 Non-Derogation)
Clause 3(2) and Clause 5(3) explicitly preserve all rights recognized under the Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act, 2006 (FRA 2006) and PESA 1996 (*Samatha*, *Niyamgiri*). Customary dwelling and community forest resource collection are strictly exempted from anti-encroachment offences.
