# Introduction-Ready Legislative Package — Parliament of India

## THE FOREST PROTECTION, ANTI-MINING, PUBLIC OFFICIAL DETERRENCE, AND ECOLOGICAL RESTORATION BILL, 2026

### A

### BILL

to provide for an absolute prohibition on all mining and mineral extraction in forest lands, deemed forests, and ecologically sensitive areas across India; to prohibit illegal encroachments while protecting the recognized customary rights of Scheduled Tribes and traditional forest dwellers; to establish mandatory deterrent penalties and heightened criminal liability for public servants, law enforcement officers, and political executives who collude in or fail to prevent forest destruction; to establish a statutory trajectory with enforceable 5-year interim targets to expand India's biodiverse forest and canopy cover to at least fifty percent (50%); to establish the National Forest Audit Authority; to institute parliamentary supermajority and State ratification requirements for any repeal or dilution; and for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto.

BE it enacted by Parliament in the Seventy-Seventh Year of the Republic of India as follows:—

### CHAPTER I: PRELIMINARY

**1. Short title, extent, and commencement.**
(1) This Act may be called the Forest Protection, Anti-Mining, Public Official Deterrence, and Ecological Restoration Act, 2026.
(2) It extends to the whole of India.
(3) It shall come into force on such date as the Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, appoint, being not later than thirty days from the date of Presidential Assent.

**2. Definitions.**
In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires,—
(a) **"Deemed Forest"** means any land matching the dictionary meaning of "forest" regardless of ownership, classification, or registration, including contiguous wooded areas, high-altitude catchments, riverine forests, and unclassed state forests;

(b) **"Ecologically Sensitive Area (ESA)"** means any zone declared under Section 3 of the Environment (Protection) Act 1986, including Eco-Sensitive Zones around National Parks, Wildlife Sanctuaries, Tiger Reserves, biosphere reserves, and critical wildlife corridors;

(c) **"Forest Land"** includes Reserved Forests, Protected Forests, Village Forests, Deemed Forests, ESAs, and lands declared as forests under any Central or State law;

(d) **"Illegal Mining"** means any operation involving the reconnaissance, prospecting, exploration, quarrying, surface or sub-surface mining, or extraction of minerals, soil, or stone conducted within or underneath Forest Land;

(e) **"Public Servant"** shall have the meaning assigned to it under Section 2(28) of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, and includes Ministers, Members of Parliament, Members of State Assemblies, officers of police, forest, mining, revenue, and administrative services;

(f) **"Recognized Forest Rights"** means rights recognized and vested under the Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act, 2006.

**3. Non-Derogation and Overriding Effect.**
(1) The provisions of this Act shall have effect notwithstanding anything inconsistent contained in the Mines and Minerals (Development and Regulation) Act 1957, the Van (Sanrakshan Evam Samvardhan) Adhiniyam 1980, the Environment (Protection) Act 1986, or any other Central or State law for the time being in force.
(2) Nothing in this Act shall abridge, restrict, alter, or extinguish any right, title, or interest recognized, vested, or protected under the Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act, 2006, or under the Provisions of the Panchayats (Extension to the Scheduled Areas) Act, 1996.

---

### CHAPTER II: ABSOLUTE MINING BAN AND ANTI-ENCROACHMENT

**4. Absolute Ban on Mining in Forest Lands.**
(1) Notwithstanding any permit, lease, concession, clearance, or licence granted under any law prior to or after the commencement of this Act, no person, corporation, state enterprise, or authority shall conduct any mining or mineral extraction activity on, within, or underneath any Forest Land or Ecologically Sensitive Area.
(2) All existing mining leases, clearances, and concessions operating within or underneath Forest Land or ESAs stand terminated by operation of law on the expiry of ninety days from the commencement of this Act.
(3) Every holder of a terminated lease shall, within one hundred and eighty days, complete full ecological land reclamation at their sole expense under the supervision of the National Forest Audit Authority.

**5. Prohibition on Encroachment and Mandatory Eviction.**
(1) No person or commercial entity shall encroach upon, clear, construct upon, or divert Forest Land for non-forest commercial, industrial, or residential use.
(2) The State Forest Protection Force shall initiate mandatory eviction proceedings against commercial or industrial encroachers within thirty days of detection.
(3) Exercise of customary dwelling, traditional agriculture, or community forest resource use by Scheduled Tribes or traditional forest dwellers under the Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act, 2006 shall not be treated as encroachment under this Act.

---

### CHAPTER III: PENALTIES AND PUBLIC OFFICIAL DETERRENCE

**6. Offences by Private Violators.**
Whosoever violates Section 4 or Section 5 shall be punished with rigorous imprisonment for a term which shall not be less than five years but which may extend to ten years, and shall also be liable to a mandatory fine equal to two hundred percent of the estimated commercial value of the minerals extracted or ecological damage caused, whichever is higher.

**7. Heightened Offences and Deterrent Penalties for Public Servants.**
(1) Any Public Servant, Law Enforcement Officer, Forest Official, Revenue Officer, or Executive Official who—
  (a) willfully abets, permits, assists, or conspires in illegal mining or encroachment on Forest Land;

  (b) fails to act upon or report illegal mining or commercial encroachment within their statutory jurisdiction after receiving written notice or satellite alert; or

  (c) issues fraudulent permits, backdated clearances, or false survey reports covering Forest Land;
shall be guilty of an offence under this Act and shall be punished with rigorous imprisonment for a term which shall not be less than seven years but which may extend to fourteen years, and shall also be liable to fine.
(2) Any Public Servant convicted under sub-section (1) shall suffer mandatory forfeiture of all personal properties acquired during their service tenure and shall be permanently disqualified from holding any public office or employment under the Union or State Governments.

**8. Special Offence for Political Executives and Ministers.**
Any Minister, Member of Parliament, Member of Legislative Assembly, or elected executive who issues directions, exercises political pressure, takes monetary or political gratification, or shields any person or entity engaged in illegal mining or forest encroachment shall be punished with rigorous imprisonment for a term which shall not be less than ten years but which may extend to fourteen years, and shall be disqualified from contesting elections to Parliament, State Assemblies, or local bodies for a period of ten years from the date of release from prison, under Section 8 of the Representation of the People Act, 1951.

---

### CHAPTER IV: ECOLOGICAL RESTORATION MANDATE AND AUDIT

**9. Statutory Trajectory to 50% Forest Cover.**
(1) The Union of India and all State Governments shall execute an Integrated National Forest Restoration Plan to expand India's biodiverse forest and tree cover to at least fifty percent of the total geographical area of the country by the year 2046.
(2) The statutory restoration milestones shall be as follows:—
  (a) Milestone I (2031): Achieving complete net-zero forest loss and expanding total biodiverse cover to thirty percent;

  (b) Milestone II (2036): Expanding biodiverse forest cover to thirty-eight percent;

  (c) Milestone III (2041): Expanding biodiverse forest cover to forty-four percent;

  (d) Milestone IV (2046): Achieving and maintaining at least fifty percent biodiverse forest and canopy cover.

**10. Establishment of National Forest Audit Authority.**
(1) The Central Government shall, by notification, establish an independent statutory body to be called the National Forest Audit Authority, comprising eminent ecological scientists, retired Supreme Court judges, ISRO remote sensing experts, and tribal rights representatives.
(2) The National Forest Audit Authority shall conduct annual satellite-based GIS forest cover audits, verify biodiversity quality, and publish public annual scorecards for every State and District.

**11. Sanctions for Failure to Meet Interim Milestones.**
Where any State or District fails to achieve its statutory five-year interim restoration milestone due to gross administrative negligence, misallocation or diversion of restoration funds, or fraudulent data reporting:—
(a) The National Forest Audit Authority shall recommend direct departmental and criminal investigation against the Principal Chief Conservator of Forests and the concerned Departmental Secretary;

(b) The Comptroller and Auditor General shall conduct a forensic financial audit of compensatory afforestation funds in the defaulting jurisdiction;

(c) The Central Government shall withhold non-ecological discretionary grants to the defaulting State until a corrective restoration plan is approved and implemented.

---

### CHAPTER V: SPECIAL COURTS AND PROCEDURE

**12. Designated Special Environmental Courts.**
(1) The State Government, in consultation with the Chief Justice of the High Court, shall designate at least one Court of Session in each district as a Special Environmental Court for the speedy trial of offences under this Act.
(2) Special Courts shall conclude trials within six months from the filing of the charge sheet.
(3) Offences under Section 7 and Section 8 shall be non-bailable and cognizable.

---

### CHAPTER VI: PARLIAMENTARY ENTRENCHMENT AND AMENDMENT SAFEGUARDS

**13. Parliamentary Supermajority and State Ratification Requirement for Repeal or Dilution.**
(1) Notwithstanding anything contained in Article 107 of the Constitution or any other law for the time being in force, no Bill seeking to repeal, suspend, weaken, or dilute any provision of this Act shall be passed by either House of Parliament unless it satisfies the conditions set forth in sub-sections (2), (3), and (4).
(2) Any such Bill must be passed in each House of Parliament by a majority of not less than two-thirds of the total sanctioned strength of that House.
(3) For the purposes of sub-section (2), the "total sanctioned strength" shall mean the total statutory maximum capacity of seats in the House as specified under Articles 81 and 83 of the Constitution, without deducting any members who may be suspended, absent, disqualified, or whose seats are vacant at the time of voting.
(4) No amendment or repeal passed under sub-section (2) shall take effect or be presented for Presidential Assent unless it has been ratified by resolutions passed by not less than two-thirds of the Legislative Assemblies of the States across the Republic of India.
(5) Any Bill seeking to amend or repeal this Act passed under this Section shall automatically be referred to a 5-Judge Constitution Bench of the Supreme Court of India to verify compliance with Article 21 and the Basic Structure Doctrine prior to Presidential Assent.

## THE CONSTITUTION (ONE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-FIFTH AMENDMENT) BILL, 2026

### A

### BILL

further to amend the Constitution of India to entrench forest protection, prohibit mining in forest lands, and mandate special supermajority and state ratification procedures for any legislative repeal or dilution of environmental protection laws.

BE it enacted by Parliament in the Seventy-Seventh Year of the Republic of India as follows:—

**1. Short title.**
This Act may be called the Constitution (One Hundred and Thirty-Fifth Amendment) Act, 2026.

**2. Insertion of new Article 48B.**
After Article 48A of the Constitution, the following Article shall be inserted, namely:—

> **"48B. Protection of Forest Core and Supermajority Entrenchment for Repeal of Forest Laws.**
> (1) The State shall preserve all existing forest lands, deemed forests, ecologically sensitive zones, and natural wild ecosystems, and shall enforce an absolute ban on commercial mining and extraction within forest areas.
> (2) No law enacted by Parliament providing for an absolute ban on forest mining, severe deterrence against public official collusion, or a mandatory ecological restoration trajectory to expand forest cover to fifty percent shall be repealed, suspended, or diluted in its material protection, except by a Bill passed in each House of Parliament by a majority of not less than two-thirds of the total sanctioned membership of that House (calculated without deducting suspended, absent, or vacant seats), and ratified by resolutions passed by not less than two-thirds of the Legislative Assemblies of the States.
> (3) Any act or omission by any public authority that indirectly dilutes or evades the protection guaranteed under clause (1) or (2) shall be void ab initio."

**3. Amendment of Article 368.**
In Article 368 of the Constitution, in clause (2), after the proviso, the following proviso shall be inserted, namely:—

> "Provided further that if any amendment seeks to alter, repeal, or weaken the statutory or constitutional protections governing forest preservation, anti-mining bans, or ecological restoration under Article 48B, the amendment shall require ratification by the Legislative Assemblies of not less than two-thirds of the States."

## STATEMENT OF OBJECTS AND REASONS

India's forest cover, biodiversity, and critical watersheds are facing unprecedented degradation due to commercial mining, illegal land encroachments, and regulatory loopholes in existing forest diversion frameworks. While the Constitution under Article 48A and Article 51A(g) places a duty on the State and citizens to protect forests and wildlife, existing penal frameworks lack statutory deterrent minimum sentences for public servants and political executives who collude with mining cartels or permit illegal forest clearance.

Furthermore, temporary legislative majorities have previously diluted environmental protections. In *MK Ranjitsinh v. Union of India* (2024), the Supreme Court affirmed that the right to be free from adverse climate impacts forms an integral part of the Right to Life under Article 21.

This legislative package addresses these systemic vulnerabilities through three core measures:—

1. **Absolute Mining Ban**: It places an absolute ban on all mining and mineral extraction in designated and deemed forests and Ecologically Sensitive Areas, while safeguarding indigenous rights under the Forest Rights Act, 2006.

2. **Heightened Public Servant Liability**: It introduces mandatory minimum sentences of 7 to 14 years rigorous imprisonment for public servants and 10 to 14 years for political executives who collude in or permit illegal mining or forest encroachments, alongside asset forfeiture and political disqualification.

3. **Phased Restoration & Entrenchment**: It establishes a binding 20-year national trajectory to achieve 50% biodiverse forest cover by 2046 with 5-year interim targets audited by an independent National Forest Audit Authority, and entrenches the law against easy repeal by requiring a 2/3rd supermajority of total parliamentary seats (calculated without deducting suspended members) and 2/3rd State Legislative Assembly ratification.

The Bill seeks to achieve these objectives.

---

## FINANCIAL MEMORANDUM

Clause 10 of the Bill provides for the establishment of the National Forest Audit Authority (NFAA) to conduct annual satellite-based GIS audits and biodiversity quality assessments. Clause 12 provides for the designation of Special Environmental Courts in each district for fast-track trials.

1. **Non-Recurring Expenditure**: An estimated initial capital outlay of Rupees Fifty Crores (₹50,000,000,000) will be required for setting up the National Forest Audit Authority's satellite monitoring infrastructure, remote sensing software integration with ISRO, and establishing digital evidence hardware for Special Environmental Courts.

2. **Recurring Expenditure**: An estimated annual recurring expenditure of Rupees Twenty Five Crores (₹25,000,000,000) will be incurred for operational expenses, staff salaries, field verification teams, GIS data processing, and Special Court administration.

The expenditure will be met from the Consolidated Fund of India and supported by allocations from the National Compensatory Afforestation Fund (CAMPA).

---

## MEMORANDUM REGARDING DELEGATED LEGISLATION

Clause 10(1) of the Bill empowers the Central Government to issue notifications establishing the National Forest Audit Authority and making rules regarding the qualifications of its members, terms of office, annual auditing protocols, and reporting procedures.

The delegation of legislative power is of a routine and administrative character, designed to operationalize independent satellite auditing and scientific evaluation. All rules made under this Act shall be laid before each House of Parliament for a total period of thirty days while it is in session, in accordance with standard parliamentary laying procedures.
