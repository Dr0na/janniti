# Indian Law Landscape Research Pack: Forest Conservation, Mining Prohibition, Public Servant Deterrence, and Reforestation Mandate

## 1. Scope and search record

- Matter ID: 001
- Topic and objective: Formulate a comprehensive Indian Union statutory framework ("Forest Preservation, Mining Prohibition, and Mandatory Ecological Restoration Bill, 2026") prohibiting mining in critical and protected forests, outlawing forest land encroachment, establishing strict deterrent liability (with heightened criminal and administrative penalties for public servants, law enforcement, and political executives who abet or permit violations), and instituting a legally enforceable, ecologically realistic 50% forest cover restoration framework with binding interim targets, institutional accountability, and tribal/forest-dweller rights protection under the Forest Rights Act 2006 and Fifth/Sixth Schedules.
- Jurisdiction(s): Union of India (All-India application under Concurrent List Entry 17A and 17B, Schedule VII).
- As-of date: 2026-07-27
- Search terms and synonyms: Forest Conservation Act 1980, Van (Sanrakshan Evam Samvardhan) Adhiniyam 1980, Mines and Minerals (Development and Regulation) Act 1957, Wildlife Protection Act 1972, Forest Rights Act 2006, Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers Act 2006, Environment (Protection) Act 1986, Godavarman, Samatha, Niyamgiri, public servant liability, anti-encroachment, CAMPA, forest cover 50 percent, tree cover targets.
- Requested legal question: How can India legally formulate a constitutional, non-arbitrary, highly deterrent statutory ban on forest mining and encroachments, with heightened penalties for complicit public servants and executives, alongside an ecologically sound, legally binding 50% forest cover mandate?
- Sources/repositories searched and retrieval dates: India Code (indiacode.nic.in), Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (moef.gov.in), Supreme Court of India judgments (sci.gov.in), Constitution of India official text (legislative.gov.in).

## 2. Executive finding and reliance limits

Existing laws in India (such as the Van (Sanrakshan Evam Samvardhan) Adhiniyam 1980, Mines and Minerals (Development and Regulation) Act 1957, Wildlife Protection Act 1972, and Environment (Protection) Act 1986) provide fragmented regulatory mechanisms for forest diversion and mining permits. However, they contain regulatory loopholes, grant broad executive discretion for forest clearances in eco-sensitive zones, lack statutory mandatory minimum criminal sentences for complicit public servants, and lack a legally binding national target to expand biodiverse forest cover to 50%. A new comprehensive parliamentary enactment under List III Entry 17A/17B is necessary to consolidate mining prohibitions, institute mandatory severe deterrence for public servants/executives, establish fast-track environmental courts, protect indigenous forest rights (FRA 2006), and create a phased, scientifically realistic 50% forest cover mandate with mandatory 5-year audited milestones.

## 5. State and Union Territory coverage

All Indian States and Union Territories containing forest areas (including Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh, Karnataka, Goa, Rajasthan, Assam) fall within the scope of Central forest legislation under List III Entry 17A. State Forest Acts (e.g., Karnataka Forest Act 1963, Odisha Forest Act 1972) supplement Central legislation but must yield to stronger Central prohibitions under Article 254(1) of the Constitution.

## 6. Bills, amendments, ordinances, history, and Gazette

Recent amendments (Forest Conservation Amendment Act 2023) narrowed the scope of forest land under Section 1A of FCA to officially recorded forests, causing significant legal debate and Supreme Court interim orders (*TN Godavarman* interim directions 2024 directing adherence to the broad *Godavarman* 1996 dictionary definition of forest). The proposed draft statutory framework reinstates the expansive definition of forest land.

## 7. Delegated law and implementation

Existing CAMPA (Compensatory Afforestation Fund Act 2016) mechanisms handle compensatory afforestation funds, but suffer from administrative delays, inappropriate species selection, and inadequate satellite monitoring. The proposed Bill institutes mandatory ISRO/FSI remote sensing audits every 12 months.

## 9. Rights, equality, federalism, and public-interest assessment

- Tribal Rights Safeguard: The law must explicitly exempt traditional forest dwellers and STs exercising recognized FRA 2006 rights from "encroachment" offences, ensuring anti-encroachment powers target commercial, industrial, and illegal land grabbers.
- Due Process: Criminal liability for public servants must require clear proof of mens rea, willful neglect, or corruption, with fast-track trial by Special Designated Green Courts.
- Scientific Reforestation Target: Expanding forest cover from ~24.6% to 50% within 5 years is ecologically impossible without causing severe socio-economic disruption or false data reporting. The legal framework establishes a 20-year binding national trajectory (50% target by 2045) with enforceable 5-year interim targets (e.g., 30% by 2030, 38% by 2035, 45% by 2040, 50% by 2045).
