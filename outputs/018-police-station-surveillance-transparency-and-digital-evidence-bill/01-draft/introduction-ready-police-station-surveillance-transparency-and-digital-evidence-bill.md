# THE POLICE STATION SURVEILLANCE, TRANSPARENCY, AND DIGITAL EVIDENCE BILL, 2026

### A

### BILL

to mandate twenty-four-hour audio-video closed-circuit television surveillance across all police stations; to require mandatory body-worn cameras for police officers during arrests, interrogations, and searches; to establish an independent digital audit authority; to prescribe evidentiary consequences for un-explicated surveillance outages; and for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto.

BE it enacted by Parliament in the Seventy-Seventh Year of the Republic of India as follows:—

### 1. Short title, extent and commencement

(1) This Act may be called the Police Station Surveillance, Transparency, and Digital Evidence Act, 2026.

(2) It extends to the whole of India.

(3) It shall come into force on such date as the Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, appoint.

### 2. Definitions

In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires,—

(a) "Body-Worn Camera" means a portable audio-video recording device worn on a police officer's uniform capable of continuous recording;

(b) "CCTV Network" means the closed-circuit television surveillance system installed in police stations under section 3; and

(c) "Digital Audit Authority" means the State Oversight Committee established under section 5.

### 3. Mandatory Closed-Circuit Television Coverage in Police Stations

(1) Every police station, lockup, interrogation room, reception area, corridor, and compound shall be equipped with night-vision, high-definition closed-circuit television cameras with continuous audio-video recording capability.

(2) All CCTV footage recorded pursuant to sub-section (1) shall be stored in tamper-proof servers for a minimum retention period of eighteen months.

### 4. Mandatory Officer Body-Worn Cameras

(1) Every police officer conducting an arrest, search, seizure, or raid shall wear and activate an operational Body-Worn Camera.

(2) The audio-video recording generated by the Body-Worn Camera shall form an essential part of the case diary and trial record under the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023.

### 5. Evidentiary Presumption and Outage Consequences

(1) Where an arrest, search, or interrogation takes place during an un-explicated outage of the CCTV Network or Body-Worn Camera, the court shall draw an adverse inference against the prosecution.

(2) Any confession or statement alleged to have been made during an un-explicated surveillance outage shall be inadmissible in evidence.

### 6. Power to Make Rules

(1) The Central Government may make rules to carry out the provisions of this Act.

(2) Every rule made under this section shall be laid before Parliament.

---

## STATEMENT OF OBJECTS AND REASONS

1. Unrecorded interrogations and dark spots in police stations enable custodial violence and unconstitutional detentions (*Paramvir Singh Saini* judgment).

2. The Police Station Surveillance, Transparency, and Digital Evidence Bill, 2026 mandates 24x7 CCTV coverage, body-worn cameras, 18-month data retention, and renders statements made during un-explicated CCTV outages inadmissible.

---

## FINANCIAL MEMORANDUM

1. Clause 3 and Clause 4 mandate CCTV and body-worn camera infrastructure.

2. Central capital assistance is estimated at rupees three thousand crores over three years.
