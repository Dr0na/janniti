# Draft Legislation and Policy Analysis: The Election Commission Integrity, Voter Protection, and Electoral Accountability Bill, 2026

## 1. Bottom Line and Legal Expert Analysis

- **Jurisdiction**: Union of India (Parliament of India, operating under Article 368 of the Constitution of India, amending Article 324, Article 325, Article 326, Article 368, the Representation of the People Act 1950, the Representation of the People Act 1951, and repealing the Chief Election Commissioner and other Election Commissioners Act 2023).
- **As-of Date**: 2026-07-27
- **Client / Deliverable**: Complete legislative formulation, constitutional amendment text, companion statutory provisions, power map, and implementation framework formulated in response to the user's request for an independent, accountable Election Commission of India (ECI), strict voter roll protection, 100% paper audit voting, retrospective accountability, and supermajority entrenchment.
- **Core Legal Findings & Refined Reform Architecture**:
  1. **Restoration of Independent Selection Panel (Art 324(2) Amendment)**: Repeals the executive-dominated selection panel in the 2023 Act (which replaced the CJI with a Cabinet Minister). Mandates a 3-member Selection Committee comprising the Prime Minister, Leader of Opposition in Lok Sabha, and Chief Justice of India, requiring 2/3rd concurrence and public transparency.
  2. **Equal Constitutional Security for Election Commissioners (Art 324(5) Amendment)**: Amends Article 324(5) so that Election Commissioners enjoy the exact same removal protection as the Chief Election Commissioner (removal only by parliamentary impeachment under Art 124(4)).
  3. **Voter Roll Integrity & Criminalization of Arbitrary Deletions**: Prohibits voter deletion without 30-day registered written notice and personal hearing. Intentionally deleting or excluding valid voters carries **7 to 14 years rigorous imprisonment** and asset forfeiture for complicit election officers.
  4. **Voting System Transparency & 100% Paper Audit Mandate**: Enforces physical paper ballot availability and **100% VVPAT paper slip counting**. In any discrepancy between EVM counts and VVPAT paper slips, the physical paper slip count is legally binding.
  5. **Retrospective Criminal Accountability & Special Electoral Courts**: Enforces forensic investigation and criminal prosecution for past electoral fraud, voter deletion conspiracies, or machine tampering under PCA 1988 and RPA 1951.
  6. **Supermajority Parliamentary & State Ratification Safeguard**: Entrenches election laws under Article 368, requiring a 2/3rd supermajority of total sanctioned seats of Parliament (calculated without deducting suspended MPs) + 2/3rd State Legislative Assembly ratification.

## 2. Problem, Evidence, and Existing-Law Map

Existing Indian legal frameworks governing election administration face critical vulnerabilities:
- **Executive Selection Panel Dominance (2023 Act)**: The 2023 Act replaced the CJI with a Cabinet Minister on the Selection Committee, giving the ruling government a 2:1 majority (*Anoop Baranwal* 2023 SC).
- **Intra-Commission Vulnerability**: Article 324(5) protects only the CEC from executive removal, leaving ECs vulnerable to executive pressure.
- **Mass Voter Roll Deletions**: RPA 1950 lacks mandatory prior notice or judicial appeal before deleting voters, enabling systemic exclusion.
- **Lack of 100% Physical Audit**: Current rules permit sample counting of VVPAT slips (5 polling stations per segment), leaving 95%+ of polling stations without physical paper audit (*ADR v. ECI* 2024 SC).

## 6. Draft Legal Text: Statutory Bill and Companion Constitutional Amendment Bill

### PART A: THE CONSTITUTION (ONE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SEVENTH AMENDMENT) BILL, 2026

**BILL NO. ______ OF 2026**

*A Bill further to amend the Constitution of India to entrench the independent selection committee for the Election Commission of India, to grant equal constitutional protection to Election Commissioners, to guarantee voter roll integrity, and to mandate supermajority parliamentary and state ratification safeguards for election law amendments.*

BE it enacted by Parliament in the Seventy-Seventh Year of the Republic of India as follows:—

**1. Short title and commencement.**
(1) This Act may be called the Constitution (One Hundred and Thirty-Seventh Amendment) Act, 2026.
(2) It shall come into force on such date as the Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, appoint, being not later than thirty days from the date of Presidential Assent.

**2. Amendment of Article 324.**
In Article 324 of the Constitution,—
(a) For clause (2), the following clause shall be substituted, namely:—
    > "(2) The Election Commission shall consist of the Chief Election Commissioner and two Election Commissioners, who shall be appointed by the President by warrant under his hand and seal, upon the recommendation of a Selection Committee consisting of—
    >   (a) the Prime Minister, who shall be the Chairperson;
    >   (b) the Leader of Opposition in the House of the People, or where no such Leader has been recognized, the Leader of the single largest opposition party in the House of the People; and
    >   (c) the Chief Justice of India."
(b) In clause (5), for the first proviso, the following proviso shall be substituted, namely:—
    > "Provided that the Chief Election Commissioner and the Election Commissioners shall not be removed from office except in like manner and on the like grounds as a Judge of the Supreme Court, and the conditions of service of the Chief Election Commissioner and the Election Commissioners shall not be varied to their disadvantage after their appointment."

**3. Insertion of new Article 326A.**
After Article 326 of the Constitution, the following Article shall be inserted, namely:—

> **"326A. Integrity of Electoral Rolls and Voting Procedure.**
> (1) Every citizen entitled to be registered as a voter under Article 326 shall have a non-derogable right to remain registered on the electoral roll, and no name shall be deleted without prior personal written notice of not less than thirty days and a fair hearing.
> (2) All elections to Parliament and State Legislatures shall provide for physical paper verification, including a paper ballot option and mandatory one hundred percent counting of VVPAT paper slips, which shall legally override electronic tallies in any case of discrepancy."

**4. Amendment of Article 368.**
In Article 368 of the Constitution, in clause (2), after the third proviso, the following proviso shall be inserted, namely:—

> "Provided further that if any amendment seeks to alter, repeal, or weaken the provisions governing the composition, appointment, removal, voter roll integrity, or paper audit counting of the Election Commission of India under Article 324 or Article 326A, the Bill must be passed in each House of Parliament by a majority of not less than **two-thirds of the total sanctioned strength of that House** (calculated without deducting suspended, absent, or vacant seats), and ratified by resolutions passed by not less than **two-thirds of the Legislative Assemblies of the States**."

### PART B: THE ELECTION COMMISSION INTEGRITY, VOTER PROTECTION, AND ELECTORAL ACCOUNTABILITY BILL, 2026

**BILL NO. ______ OF 2026**

*A Bill to provide for the statutory operationalization of the independent Selection Committee for the Election Commission of India; to repeal the Chief Election Commissioner and other Election Commissioners Act 2023; to establish strict voter roll protection protocols; to mandate 100% VVPAT paper slip counting and paper ballot voting; to enforce severe criminal liability and retrospective prosecution for election rigging and voter exclusion; and for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto.*

BE it enacted by Parliament in the Seventy-Seventh Year of the Republic of India as follows:—

#### CHAPTER I: PRELIMINARY
**1. Short title, extent, and commencement.**
(1) This Act may be called the Election Commission Integrity, Voter Protection, and Electoral Accountability Act, 2026.
(2) It extends to the whole of India.
(3) It shall come into force on the date of Presidential Assent.

**2. Repeal of Act 49 of 2023.**
The Chief Election Commissioner and other Election Commissioners (Appointment, Conditions of Service and Term of Office) Act, 2023, is hereby repealed.

---

#### CHAPTER II: VOTER ROLL INTEGRITY AND ANTI-EXCLUSION PROTOCOL
**3. Absolute Ban on Arbitrary Voter Deletion.**
(1) No Electoral Registration Officer (ERO) or election official shall delete or remove the name of any registered voter from an electoral roll except after issuing a personal written notice by registered post giving not less than thirty days to show cause.
(2) Every proposed deletion shall be published on a public online GIS portal and displayed at the local Panchayat or Municipal office for public verification.
(3) Any voter aggrieved by a proposed deletion may file an appeal before the District Judge, which shall be decided within fifteen days.

**4. Penalty for Willful Deletion or Exclusion of Voters.**
Whosoever, being an Electoral Registration Officer, election official, or public servant, intentionally or negligently deletes, suppresses, or excludes valid voters from an electoral roll, or conspires to alter electoral rolls for political advantage, shall be punished with **rigorous imprisonment for a term which shall not be less than seven years but which may extend to fourteen years**, and shall suffer mandatory forfeiture of personal assets and permanent dismissal from service.

---

#### CHAPTER III: VOTING TRANSPARENCY AND 100% PAPER AUDIT
**5. Mandatory 100% VVPAT Paper Slip Count.**
(1) In all elections conducted using Electronic Voting Machines (EVMs), counting of votes shall mandatorily include the physical counting of **one hundred percent (100%) of the VVPAT paper slips** across all polling stations in every constituency.
(2) Where any discrepancy arises between the electronic count displayed on an EVM control unit and the physical count of VVPAT paper slips, the physical count of VVPAT paper slips shall prevail and be recorded as the true count.
(3) Every polling station shall provide physical paper ballots for voters who choose to cast their vote by paper ballot.

---

#### CHAPTER IV: PENALTIES, RETROSPECTIVE ACCOUNTABILITY, AND SPECIAL COURTS
**6. Offences by Election Commissioners and High Officials.**
Any Chief Election Commissioner, Election Commissioner, or senior election official who colludes with any political party, manipulates voting machines, falsifies election results, or willfully permits election rigging shall be punished with **rigorous imprisonment for a term which shall not be less than seven years but which may extend to fourteen years**, mandatory asset forfeiture, loss of pension, and a permanent bar on holding any public office.

**7. Retrospective Prosecution for Past Electoral Fraud.**
Nothing in this Act shall restrict or preclude the investigation and criminal prosecution of any past act of electoral fraud, voter list manipulation, or corruption committed prior to the commencement of this Act under the Prevention of Corruption Act 1988, the Representation of the People Act 1951, or the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita 2023.

**8. Designated Special Electoral Courts.**
(1) The High Court of each State shall designate Special Electoral Courts in every district to try offences under this Act.
(2) Special Electoral Courts shall conclude trials within six months from the filing of the charge sheet.

## 7. Clause-by-Clause Explanatory Notes

- **Part A Clause 2**: Amends Article 324(2) to entrench the independent Selection Committee (PM, LOP, CJI) and amends Article 324(5) to grant Election Commissioners equal removal protection with the CEC.
- **Part A Clause 3**: Inserts Article 326A establishing a constitutional right against arbitrary voter deletion and mandating paper verification.
- **Part A Clause 4**: Amends Article 368 to require a 2/3rd supermajority of total parliamentary seats (ignoring MP suspensions) + 2/3rd State Assembly ratification for election laws.
- **Part B Clause 2-4**: Repeals the 2023 Act; prohibits arbitrary voter roll deletions without 30-day notice; imposes 7-14 years imprisonment for complicit election officers.
- **Part B Clause 5-8**: Mandates 100% VVPAT paper slip count and paper ballot option; imposes 7-14 years imprisonment for CEC/ECs colluding in rigging; preserves retrospective criminal prosecution under PCA 1988; establishes fast-track Special Electoral Courts.
