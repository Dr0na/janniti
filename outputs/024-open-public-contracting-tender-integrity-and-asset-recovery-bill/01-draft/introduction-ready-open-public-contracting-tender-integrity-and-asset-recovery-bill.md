# THE OPEN PUBLIC CONTRACTING, TENDER INTEGRITY, AND ASSET RECOVERY BILL, 2026

### A

### BILL

to enforce absolute transparency and non-arbitrariness in public procurement under Article 14; to mandate the Open Contracting Data Standard across all government tenders; to institute a mandatory ten-year defect liability for contractors; to establish criminal asset forfeiture for substandard public infrastructure; to create the Independent Public Procurement Ombudsman; and for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto.

BE it enacted by Parliament in the Seventy-Seventh Year of the Republic of India as follows:—

### 1. Short title, extent and commencement

(1) This Act may be called the Open Public Contracting, Tender Integrity, and Asset Recovery Act, 2026.

(2) It extends to the whole of India.

(3) It shall come into force on such date as the Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, appoint.

### 2. Definitions

In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires,—

(a) "Open Contracting Portal" means the centralized digital procurement portal established under section 3;

(b) "Procurement Ombudsman" means the Independent Public Procurement Ombudsman established under section 5; and

(c) "Substandard Infrastructure" means any public road, bridge, building, dam, or utility that suffers structural failure, collapse, or severe degradation within its statutory defect liability period.

### 3. Mandatory Open Contracting Data Standard Disclosure

(1) Every Ministry, Department, public sector undertaking, and local body shall publish all public procurement notices, tender documents, bid evaluation scores, awarded contracts, and payment milestone logs on the open digital portal.

(2) All data published under sub-section (1) shall comply with the international Open Contracting Data Standard and shall be accessible to the public in open machine-readable formats without cost or registration.

### 4. Mandatory Defect Liability and Criminal Liability for Substandard Works

(1) Every public infrastructure contract shall include a statutory mandatory defect liability period of ten years from the date of completion.

(2) Where Substandard Infrastructure suffers collapse or severe structural failure, the contractor, project engineers, and approving authorities shall be guilty of an offence punishable with imprisonment for a term which shall not be less than five years but which may extend to life imprisonment, and fine.

(3) Upon conviction under sub-section (2), the court shall order mandatory summary forfeiture of all assets and bank accounts of the contractor and guilty officials to recover public losses.

### 5. Independent Public Procurement Ombudsman

(1) There shall be established an autonomous body to be called the Independent Public Procurement Ombudsman headed by a retired Judge of the Supreme Court.

(2) The Ombudsman shall have independent powers of investigation, tender cancellation, blacklisting, and prosecution in respect of procurement corruption, cartelization, and bid-rigging.

### 6. Power to Make Rules

(1) The Central Government may make rules to carry out the provisions of this Act.

(2) Every rule made under this section shall be laid before Parliament.

---

## STATEMENT OF OBJECTS AND REASONS

1. Opacity and corruption in public procurement result in immense financial loss and dangerous substandard public infrastructure (*Ramana Dayaram Shetty* judgment).

2. The Open Public Contracting, Tender Integrity, and Asset Recovery Bill, 2026 mandates open data tender disclosure, 10-year contractor defect liability, criminal asset forfeiture for infrastructure collapse, and creates the Procurement Ombudsman.

---

## FINANCIAL MEMORANDUM

1. Clause 3 and Clause 5 establish the Open Contracting Portal and Procurement Ombudsman.

2. Recurring annual operational expenditure is estimated at rupees three hundred crores.
