THE CONSTITUTION (ONE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-NINTH AMENDMENT) BILL, 2026

A

BILL

further to amend the Constitution of India to expand the Fundamental Right to Education under Article 21A from early childhood to tertiary and vocational levels, to mandate universal free, scientific, and experiential education, to protect student mental health and constitutional ethics, and to entrench the Right to Education against unilateral statutory or executive repeal.

BE it enacted by Parliament in the Seventy-Seventh Year of the Republic of India as follows:—

### 1. Short title and commencement

1. This Act may be called the Constitution (One Hundred and Thirty-Ninth Amendment) Act, 2026.

2. It shall come into force on such date as the Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, appoint, being not later than thirty days from the date of Presidential Assent.

### 2. Substitution of Article 21A

For Article 21A of the Constitution, the following Article shall be substituted, namely:—

"21A. Right to Universal Free, Scientific, and Research-Oriented Education.—

(1) The State shall provide free, compulsory, high-quality, and non-discriminatory education to all children and youth from the age of three years to twenty-two years in such manner as the State may, by law, determine.

(2) The right under clause (1) shall encompass universal pre-primary, primary, secondary, senior secondary, higher, technical, professional, and vocational education without tuition fees, admission fees, examination fees, or mandatory cost for learning materials, digital devices, uniforms, and daily nutritional meals."

### 3. Insertion of new Articles 21B and 21C

After Article 21A of the Constitution, the following Articles shall be inserted, namely:—

"21B. Content, Quality, and Curricular Standards of Free Education.—

(1) The education provided under Article 21A shall strictly adhere to mandatory national minimum educational, physical, technological, sanitation, and structural safety facility standards in every school across the territory of India.

(2) The curriculum shall be rooted in scientific temper, empirical inquiry, secular rationalism, humanism, equality, fraternity, and constitutional ethics as mandated under clause (h) of Article 51A, excluding all pseudoscientific assertions, unverified dogmas, and superstitious beliefs.

(3) The State shall ensure absolute academic parity across all streams, including Arts, Performing Arts, Science, Commerce, Vocational Skills, and Sports, and shall prioritize early childhood exposure from age three onwards in sports, music, vocational crafts, and foreign languages.

(4) Every school shall provide mandatory daily secular Vipassana mindfulness practice to foster student mental clarity, emotional resilience, and psychological focus.

(5) Education under Article 21A shall integrate practical field-based ecological conservation, humane treatment of animals under clause (g) of Article 51A, practical traffic safety, civic sanitation, and a balanced fifty-fifty ratio between theoretical instruction and practical experiential learning.

(6) The State shall progressively allocate not less than three per cent of Gross Domestic Product towards national scientific, technological, and academic research and development."

"21C. Constitutional Entrenchment and Anti-Dilution Safeguard.—

(1) No law, ordinance, rule, regulation, or executive notification seeking to repeal, restrict, diminish, or dilute the scope of free education entitlements, scientific temper principles, minimum facility standards, or research allocation targets under Article 21A or Article 21B shall be valid or have legal effect.

(2) Notwithstanding anything contained in this Constitution, any Bill seeking to amend or repeal any provision of Article 21A, Article 21B, or this Article shall be passed in each House of Parliament by a majority of the total membership of that House and by a majority of not less than two-thirds of the members of that House present and voting, and shall be ratified by the Legislatures of not less than one-half of the States before being presented to the President for assent."

## STATEMENT OF OBJECTS AND REASONS

Article 21A was inserted into the Constitution of India by the Constitution (Eighty-Sixth Amendment) Act, 2002 to guarantee free and compulsory education for children between the ages of six and fourteen years. Over the past two decades, this limited statutory coverage has left early childhood education (ages 3–6) and secondary, tertiary, technical, and vocational education (ages 14–22) vulnerable to severe commercialization, out-of-pocket expenditure, and geographic inequality.

Furthermore, statutory provisions guaranteeing free education or scientific learning under ordinary Acts of Parliament remain susceptible to dilution, under-funding, or repeal by future administrations.

To permanently constitutionalize universal free education as an indelible fundamental right under Part III of the Constitution, operationalize the Fundamental Duties of scientific temper (Article 51A(h)) and environmental stewardship (Article 51A(g)), and entrench these rights against political repeal, this Bill seeks to:

1. Substitute Article 21A to extend the Fundamental Right to Universal Free Education to cover all children and youth from age three to twenty-two years across pre-primary, primary, secondary, higher, technical, and vocational levels.

2. Insert Article 21B establishing constitutional guarantees for mandatory minimum school facility standards, empirical scientific temper, stream parity, early childhood skills and foreign language education, daily secular Vipassana mindfulness, practical ecological conservation, 50:50 experiential learning, and a statutory 3 per cent GDP target for national R&D expenditure.

3. Insert Article 21C establishing a constitutional entrenchment safeguard requiring a two-thirds special majority of both Houses of Parliament and formal ratification by not less than one-half of the State Legislatures for any future amendment or dilution of the Right to Education.

The Bill seeks to achieve these transformative constitutional objectives.

## FINANCIAL MEMORANDUM

1. Clause 2 and Clause 3 provide for extending the Fundamental Right to Free Education from age three to twenty-two and enforcing national minimum facility benchmarks. The estimated recurring annual expenditure from the Consolidated Fund of India and State funds is ninety-five thousand crore rupees, phased over five financial years.

2. Clause 3 inserts Article 21B mandating a 3 per cent GDP public expenditure target for national research and development and establishing student research fellowships, with a progressive annual recurring research grant allocation of twenty-five thousand crore rupees.

---

## MEMORANDUM REGARDING DELEGATED LEGISLATION

This Bill is a Constitutional Amendment Bill and does not directly delegate rulemaking power. Implementing procedural rules and administrative regulations shall be framed under ordinary central legislation enacted pursuant to Article 21A and Concurrent List Entry 25.
