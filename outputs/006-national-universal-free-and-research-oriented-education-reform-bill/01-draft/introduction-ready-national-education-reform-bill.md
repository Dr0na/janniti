THE RIGHT TO FREE, UNIVERSAL, SCIENTIFIC AND RESEARCH-ORIENTED EDUCATION BILL, 2026

A

BILL

to expand the fundamental right to education under Article 21A of the Constitution of India; to guarantee universal, equitable, and free education from early childhood care and pre-primary education through senior secondary, higher, vocational, and technical education; to enforce mandatory national minimum educational, physical, digital, and safety facility standards across all schools; to establish absolute parity across Arts, Performing Arts, Science, Commerce, Vocational, and Sports streams; to prioritize early childhood learning in sports, music, vocational skills, and foreign languages; to mandate daily secular Vipassana mindfulness practice for mental health and focus; to embed a national curriculum rooted in scientific temper, empirical inquiry, constitutional ethics, equality, fraternity, anti-discrimination, and civic responsibility; to integrate practical ecological stewardship and compassion for living creatures; to enforce a fifty-fifty ratio between theoretical instruction and practical experiential learning; to establish a statutory framework for national research and development with mandatory budget targets; to prohibit the commercialization of education and collection of capitation fees; to penalize corruption, financial misappropriation, criminal negligence, and safety breaches under Special Anti-Corruption Courts; and for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto.

BE it enacted by Parliament in the Seventy-Seventh Year of the Republic of India as follows:—

### 1. Short title, extent and commencement

1. This Act may be called the Right to Free, Universal, Scientific and Research-Oriented Education Act, 2026.

2. It extends to the whole of India.

3. It shall come into force on such date as the Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, appoint:

Provided that different dates may be appointed for different provisions of this Act, and any reference in any such provision to the commencement of this Act shall be construed as a reference to the coming into force of that provision.

### 2. Definitions

In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires,—

(a) "Capitation Fee" means any donation, contribution, payment, or consideration, in cash or in kind, collected directly or indirectly by an educational institution over and above the fee notified by the State Fee Regulation Commission under section 21;

(b) "Criminal Negligence" means gross, reckless, or intentional failure by an educational management, official, or contractor to maintain mandatory structural, fire, health, transport, or sanitation safety standards resulting in risk to student life or physical harm;

(c) "Early Childhood Education" means structured care, early learning, and pre-primary education provided to children between the ages of three and six years;

(d) "Educational Institution" means any public, private, aided, unaided, central, state, or autonomous school, college, polytechnic, institute of technology, central university, state university, or deemed university imparting education in India;

(e) "Experiential Learning" means hands-on, laboratory-based, field-oriented, workshop, or project-based learning where theoretical concepts are directly applied through practice;

(f) "Foreign Language Education" means instruction in international languages including French, German, Spanish, Japanese, Mandarin, or Russian;

(g) "Higher Education" means undergraduate degree, postgraduate degree, technical diploma, professional, or research education imparted to students between the ages of eighteen and twenty-two years or beyond;

(h) "Minimum Infrastructure Benchmarks" means the mandatory national physical, academic, technological, and safety standards specified in Schedule I;

(i) "National Scientific Research Foundation" means the statutory research funding body established under section 18;

(j) "Prescribed" means prescribed by rules made under this Act;

(k) "Research and Development Expenditure" means monetary outlay allocated specifically for basic, applied, scientific, technological, medical, environmental, or social science research and innovation;

(l) "Scientific Temper" means an empirical, evidence-based, rational, and inquiring mindset as mandated under clause (h) of Article 51A of the Constitution;

(m) "Universal Free Education" means the provision of education without tuition fees, admission fees, examination fees, or mandatory cost for textbooks, learning materials, digital access, and nutritional meals; and

(n) "Vipassana Mindfulness" means a universal, secular, non-sectarian mental training practice focusing on awareness of breath and bodily sensations to cultivate mental clarity, focus, emotional resilience, and stress reduction.

### 3. Statutory purpose and constitutional principles

1. It is hereby declared that this Act is for securing the fundamental right to education under Article 21A, giving effect to the Fundamental Duties under clauses (g) and (h) of Article 51A, and upholding the constitutional principles of equality, fraternity, anti-discrimination, secularism, and rule of law under Articles 14, 15, and 21 of the Constitution of India.

2. The state shall ensure that public education is delivered as an essential non-profit public service, free from commercial exploitation, dogma, or discriminatory exclusion.

### 4. Right to universal free education from age three to twenty-two

1. Every child and youth in India shall have the right to universal free education in a neighborhood public or state-assisted educational institution from the age of three years until the completion of an undergraduate degree, higher technical diploma, or professional vocational qualification up to the age of twenty-two years.

2. No educational institution shall charge any tuition fee, admission fee, capitation fee, development fee, examination fee, or hidden charge from any student entitled to free education under sub-section (1).

3. The Central Government and State Governments shall provide, free of cost, to every student enrolled under sub-section (1)—

  (a) age-appropriate textbooks, workbooks, and digital learning devices;

  (b) two sets of school or institute uniforms per academic year;

  (c) balanced, high-protein daily nutritional meals up to senior secondary level; and

  (d) free public transportation or transport subsidy between residence and educational institution.

### 5. Neighborhood access and mandatory minimum facility standards

1. The Central Government and State Governments shall, within five years of the commencement of this Act, expand public educational infrastructure to ensure that—

  (a) a composite pre-primary and primary public school is accessible within a walking distance of one kilometer from every habitation;

  (b) a secondary and senior secondary public school is accessible within a distance of three kilometers from every habitation; and

  (c) a public university campus, technical institute, or vocational college is accessible within every revenue district.

2. Every educational institution, whether public or private, shall strictly maintain the mandatory national physical, academic, digital, and safety infrastructure standards specified in Schedule I.

3. Failure by any educational institution to maintain the benchmarks specified in Schedule I within two years of the commencement of this Act shall result in immediate administrative penalty, suspension of management, or revocation of recognition.

### 6. Non-discriminatory admission and equal opportunity guarantee

1. No student shall be denied admission to any educational institution on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth, disability, economic status, or language.

2. All private aided and unaided educational institutions shall reserve not less than thirty per cent of their entry-level seats for students belonging to economically weaker sections and disadvantaged groups, whose education shall be fully reimbursed by the state at prescribed public cost rates.

### 7. Absolute parity across all academic streams

1. All academic streams, including Arts, Performing Arts, Fine Arts, Science, Commerce, Vocational and Skill-based education, and Sports, shall enjoy equal dignity, academic credit weighting, and public funding.

2. No educational institution or board shall classify non-STEM subjects or vocational trades as inferior or secondary, and all secondary and higher education institutions shall offer interdisciplinary credit transfer across streams.

### 8. Early prioritization of sports, music, vocational skills, and foreign languages

1. Every educational institution shall integrate physical education, sports, music, performing arts, vocational skills, and foreign languages into its core curriculum beginning from early childhood education at age three.

2. The curriculum from age three onwards shall mandate—

  (a) daily structured physical education, gymnastics, athletics, and traditional or modern sports training;

  (b) daily vocal or instrumental music, rhythm, dance, and theater instruction;

  (c) hands-on foundational vocational crafts, woodwork, pottery, electronics, and digital design modules; and

  (d) optional foreign language education offering French, German, Spanish, Japanese, Mandarin, or Russian alongside regional and national Indian languages.

### 9. Compulsory daily secular Vipassana mindfulness practice

1. Every school, whether public or private, shall schedule a mandatory fifteen-minute daily session of secular Vipassana mindfulness practice at the beginning of the academic day for all students.

2. Vipassana mindfulness practice under sub-section (1) shall be conducted in a strictly universal, secular, non-dogmatic, and scientific manner focusing exclusively on natural breath awareness and bodily sensation observation.

3. No religious rituals, prayers, sectarian chants, or dogmatic doctrines shall be combined with or introduced into the daily Vipassana mindfulness practice.

### 10. National Curriculum Framework for Scientific Temper and Secular Rationalism

1. The Central Government shall, through the National Council of Educational Research and Training, notify a National Curriculum Framework rooted in scientific temper, empirical inquiry, humanism, and secular rationalism under clause (h) of Article 51A of the Constitution.

2. The National Curriculum Framework shall ensure that—

  (a) all science, humanities, and social science subjects are taught using evidence-based, peer-reviewed scientific methodology;

  (b) pseudoscientific assertions, unverified dogmas, and superstitious beliefs are strictly excluded from textbooks and classroom instruction;

  (c) critical thinking, logical reasoning, scientific method, and independent inquiry are cultivated across all grade levels; and

  (d) constitutional values, equality, fraternity, anti-discrimination, secularism, gender justice, and respect for diversity are integrated across all subjects.

### 11. Practical civic responsibility, traffic safety, and cleanliness education

1. Every school and college curriculum shall incorporate mandatory practical modules on civic duty, constitutional rights, rule of law, traffic safety, and public cleanliness (Swachhata).

2. Every student shall complete mandatory practical hours in—

  (a) traffic safety rules, pedestrian safety, and responsible road behavior through practical traffic management drills conducted in coordination with local traffic authorities;

  (b) civic sanitation, waste segregation, recycling, and neighborhood cleanliness drives organized by the educational institution; and

  (c) community service, legal literacy, and constitutional awareness projects.

### 12. Practical ecological conservation and environmental stewardship

1. Environmental education under this Act shall be delivered primarily through practical, field-based ecological conservation projects under clause (g) of Article 51A of the Constitution.

2. Every educational institution shall dedicate not less than two hours per week to mandatory practical ecological activities, including—

  (a) planting, nurturing, and monitoring native trees and urban forests;

  (b) organic gardening, composting, and rainwater harvesting on institution premises;

  (c) local waterbody rejuvenation and plastic pollution cleanup drives; and

  (d) energy conservation and carbon footprint audits conducted by students.

### 13. Practical compassion for animals and nature

1. Every school curriculum shall include practical instruction and experiential activities designed to foster empathy, ethical treatment, and compassion for animals and nature.

2. Practical animal welfare education shall include—

  (a) humane treatment of domestic and street animals, rescue protocols, and anti-cruelty education;

  (b) field visits to animal shelters, wildlife rescue centers, and nature reserves; and

  (c) prohibition of dissecting or harming live animals for educational demonstration, replacing such exercises with digital 3D virtual simulation models.

### 14. Fifty-fifty ratio between theoretical instruction and practical experiential learning

1. Every educational institution shall structure its academic timetable to achieve a balanced ratio of fifty per cent theoretical instruction and fifty per cent practical experiential learning.

2. Practical experiential learning under sub-section (1) shall comprise—

  (a) hands-on laboratory experimentation and scientific research projects;

  (b) industrial workshops, coding labs, robotics, and design thinking modules;

  (c) vocational trade training, agricultural field practice, and artisanal crafts; and

  (d) sports, physical education, arts, theater, and personality development activities.

### 15. District Skill Development and Entrepreneurship Incubators

1. Every district secondary school cluster and higher education campus shall establish a District Skill Development and Entrepreneurship Incubator.

2. The Incubator established under sub-section (1) shall provide—

  (a) certified vocational skill training aligned with regional employment needs;

  (b) seed funding, mentorship, and legal assistance for student-led startup ventures;

  (c) industry apprenticeship placements and micro-entrepreneurship workshops; and

  (d) financial literacy, digital business management, and patent filing guidance.

### 16. Statutory national R&D expenditure target and funding allocation

1. The Central Government and State Governments shall progressively increase public expenditure on Research and Development to achieve a national target of not less than three per cent of Gross Domestic Product within ten years from the commencement of this Act.

2. Not less than fifty per cent of the national R&D budget allocated under sub-section (1) shall be directed to basic scientific research, medical innovation, agricultural sustainability, clean energy technologies, and higher education university research laboratories.

### 17. National Scientific Research Foundation

1. The Central Government shall establish an independent statutory body to be known as the National Scientific Research Foundation to plan, fund, coordinate, and evaluate scientific and academic research across India.

2. The Foundation shall administer a competitive research grant scheme for faculty, independent researchers, and university research centers based strictly on peer-reviewed scientific merit and national development priorities.

### 18. Undergraduate and Postgraduate Research Fellowships

1. Every student enrolled in an undergraduate science, technology, engineering, medical, humanities, or social science degree program shall complete a mandatory research project in their final academic year.

2. The National Scientific Research Foundation shall award monthly stipend-backed Undergraduate and Postgraduate Research Fellowships to not less than five hundred thousand high-performing research students annually to encourage full-time research careers.

### 19. Total ban on commercialization and capitation fees

1. No educational institution, management, trust, society, or individual shall demand, collect, or accept any capitation fee, donation, or unaccounted financial consideration for admitting any student or granting any degree or diploma.

2. Education in all institutions shall be operated strictly on a non-profit basis. Any surplus income generated by an educational institution shall be reinvested exclusively into institutional infrastructure, research laboratories, library resources, or student welfare.

### 20. Establishment of State Education Fee Regulation Commissions

1. Every State Government shall establish an independent statutory State Education Fee Regulation Commission headed by a retired High Court Judge.

2. The Commission shall determine and notify the maximum fee structure chargeable by private self-financed educational institutions for non-subsidized seats, calculating fees strictly on a cost-recovery, non-profit basis after auditing institutional accounts.

### 21. National Education Quality and Integrity Inspectorate

1. The Central Government shall establish an autonomous National Education Quality and Integrity Inspectorate to conduct unannounced academic, infrastructural, and financial audits of educational institutions across India.

2. The Inspectorate shall publish annual district-wise education performance scorecards evaluating learning outcomes, scientific temper implementation, practical learning compliance, infrastructure standards, R&D outputs, and anti-corruption standards.

### 22. Offence of financial corruption and misappropriation of education funds

1. Whosoever criminally misappropriates, siphons, or diverts any public funds, government grants, mid-day meal funds, or research grants allocated for educational purposes shall be punishable with rigorous imprisonment for a term which shall not be less than seven years but which may extend to fourteen years, and fine which shall be equal to three hundred per cent of the misappropriated amount.

2. All property, equity, and financial assets acquired through the proceeds of financial corruption under sub-section (1) shall be subject to immediate statutory attachment and civil forfeiture to the public education fund.

### 23. Offence of criminal negligence and safety breaches in educational institutions

1. Where the management, official, contractor, or administrator of any educational institution acts with gross or criminal negligence regarding structural building safety, fire safety, sanitation hygiene, mid-day meal food safety, or student transport safety, resulting in student injury, illness, or loss of life, such person shall be punishable with rigorous imprisonment for a term which shall not be less than three years but which may extend to seven years, and fine.

2. In the event of fatal injury resulting from criminal negligence under sub-section (1), the offending educational institution shall be subject to immediate administrative takeover by the State Government.

### 24. Offence of academic misconduct, examination fraud, and paper leaks

1. Whosoever engages in examination paper leaks, organized cheating, falsification of academic records, or issuance of fraudulent degrees or certificates shall be punishable with rigorous imprisonment for a term which shall not be less than five years but which may extend to ten years, and fine.

2. Any member of an accreditation or inspection panel who submits false, misleading, or corrupt inspection reports regarding school infrastructure or academic standards shall be punishable under sub-section (1).

### 25. Special Anti-Corruption Courts and fast-track trials

1. The State Government, in consultation with the Chief Justice of the High Court, shall designate Special Courts of Sessions for the exclusive trial of offences under sections 19, 22, 23, and 24 of this Act.

2. A Special Court shall conduct proceedings under this Act on a day-to-day basis and shall complete the trial within twelve months from the date of taking cognizance of the offence.

### 26. Elimination of prior executive sanction for prosecution

1. Notwithstanding anything contained in section 197 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 or section 17A of the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988, no prior executive sanction or permission shall be required for initiating investigation, search, seizure, attachment, or prosecution against any educational official, manager, or contractor accused of an offence under this Act.

### 27. Whistleblower protection and financial rewards

1. Any person who provides verified information or evidence leading to the detection, investigation, or recovery of misappropriated education funds, capitation fee extortion, paper leaks, or criminal safety breaches shall be granted statutory protection of identity, physical security, and employment immunity.

2. A whistleblower whose information leads to successful recovery of funds or conviction under sub-section (1) shall be entitled to a statutory financial reward equal to ten per cent of the recovered fine or penalty, capped at two crore rupees.

### 28. Permanent disqualification from educational management and public office

1. Any person convicted of an offence under section 19, 22, 23, or 24 shall, from the date of conviction, stand permanently disqualified from—

  (a) managing, running, or establishing any educational institution;

  (b) holding any public office or appointment in any university or statutory authority; and

  (c) serving on any educational board, council, or inspection committee.

### 29. Statutory entrenchment safeguard against political dilution

1. Notwithstanding anything contained in any other law for the time being in force, no bill seeking to repeal, amend, or dilute the provisions of sections 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10, 16, 19, 22, 23, or this section shall be passed in either House of Parliament unless it is passed by a majority of the total membership of that House and by a majority of not less than two-thirds of the members of that House present and voting.

2. Before any bill referred to in sub-section (1) is introduced in Parliament, the Central Government shall obtain a formal resolution of concurrence passed by the Inter-State Council.

### 30. Power to make rules

1. The Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, make rules for carrying out the provisions of this Act.

2. In particular, and without prejudice to the generality of the foregoing power, such rules may provide for all or any of the following matters, namely:—

  (a) the national infrastructure benchmarks and neighborhood school distance standards under section 5 and Schedule I;

  (b) the guidelines for early childhood sports, music, vocational skills, and foreign languages under section 8;

  (c) the operational framework for daily secular Vipassana mindfulness practice under section 9;

  (d) the guidelines for National Curriculum Framework for Scientific Temper under section 10;

  (e) the practical ecological, animal welfare, traffic safety, and cleanliness education modules under sections 11, 12, and 13;

  (f) the guidelines for 50:50 theory-practical learning timetables under section 14;

  (g) the research fellowship grant disbursement criteria under sections 17 and 18; and

  (h) any other matter which is required to be, or may be, prescribed.

3. Every rule made under this Act shall be laid, as soon as may be after it is made, before each House of Parliament, while it is in session, for a total period of thirty days which may be comprised in one session or in two or more successive sessions, and if, before the expiry of the session immediately following the session or the successive sessions aforesaid, both Houses agree in making any modification in the rule or both Houses agree that the rule should not be made, the rule shall thereafter have effect only in such modified form or be of no effect, as the case may be; so, however, that any such modification or annulment shall be without prejudice to the validity of anything previously done under that rule.

### SCHEDULE I

#### MANDATORY NATIONAL MINIMUM INFRASTRUCTURE AND FACILITY STANDARDS FOR EDUCATIONAL INSTITUTIONS

1. Physical and Structural Safety Standards:

  (a) Earthquake-resistant, fire-safe, and structurally audited permanent building infrastructure certified by a licensed structural engineer once every three years;

  (b) Functional fire safety alarms, fire extinguishers, emergency exit stairwells, and annual mandatory fire evacuation drills; and

  (c) Barrier-free ramps, tactile paths, braille signage, and accessible toilet facilities for students with disabilities across all floors.

2. Sanitation and Health Standards:

  (a) Separate, clean, fully functional flush sanitation facilities for male and female students with continuous running water supply at a minimum ratio of one toilet per twenty-five students;

  (b) Safe, tested drinking water filtration points available on every floor; and

  (c) Dedicated first-aid health room staffed by a trained nurse or health coordinator, equipped with emergency medical kits and automated external defibrillators.

3. Academic and Research Facilities:

  (a) Fully equipped science laboratories for physics, chemistry, biology, and environmental science with individual student experiment kits;

  (b) High-speed broadband digital computer laboratory with a student-to-computer ratio of not more than two to one;

  (c) Interactive digital smart boards and audio-visual projection equipment in every classroom; and

  (d) Well-stocked library containing physical books, scientific journals, reference encyclopedias, and digital open-access research portal access.

4. Sports, Musical, and Cultural Infrastructure:

  (a) Dedicated outdoor sports ground or indoor athletic complex equipped for track sports, football, cricket, basketball, volleyball, or traditional Indian games;

  (b) Dedicated music studio equipped with vocal and instrumental equipment (Indian and Western instruments); and

  (c) Dedicated performing arts and theater auditorium or multi-purpose hall.

5. Vocational and Skill Facilities:

  (a) District Skill Development Workshop equipped for carpentry, electronics, mechanics, textile design, coding, and digital fabrication; and

  (b) Environmental green space, organic school garden, and rainwater harvesting installation for practical ecological learning.

## STATEMENT OF OBJECTS AND REASONS

India's long-term economic prosperity, national security, democratic strength, and global scientific standing depend fundamentally on building a world-class, highly skilled, research-oriented, and constitutionally conscious society. However, the existing educational framework remains constrained by statutory coverage limits, commercialization, out-of-pocket financial barriers, theoretical rote-memorization, under-funded R&D, infrastructure disparities, and a deficit in practical civic and environmental education.

While Article 21A of the Constitution guarantees free and compulsory education for children between the ages of six and fourteen years, pre-primary education (ages 3–6) and secondary, tertiary, technical, and vocational education (ages 14–22) remain un-guaranteed and heavily commercialized. Furthermore, national R&D expenditure remains under-allocated at approximately 0.65 per cent of Gross Domestic Product, limiting the nation's capacity for scientific breakthrough and industrial innovation.

To fulfill the mandate of Article 21A, operationalize the Fundamental Duties of scientific temper (Article 51A(h)) and environmental stewardship (Article 51A(g)), establish absolute stream parity across Arts, Science, Commerce, Vocational Skills, and Sports, embed daily secular Vipassana mindfulness practice for student mental health, enforce strict minimum facility standards across all schools, and position India as a global research and skills superpower within ten to fifteen years, this Bill provides for:

1. Guaranteeing universal free education from pre-primary (age 3) through undergraduate degree, technical diploma, or professional vocational training (age 22) in all public and state-assisted institutions.

2. Enforcing mandatory national minimum physical, academic, digital, and safety infrastructure standards across every school under Schedule I.

3. Establishing absolute academic parity across Arts, Performing Arts, Science, Commerce, Vocational Skills, and Sports, and prioritizing early childhood education (ages 3+) in sports, music, vocational crafts, and foreign languages.

4. Mandating daily secular Vipassana mindfulness practice (15 minutes) for mental focus, emotional resilience, and stress reduction.

5. Formulating a National Curriculum Framework focused on empirical scientific temper, secular rationalism, critical inquiry, constitutional ethics, fraternity, anti-discrimination, traffic safety rules, civic cleanliness, and law-abiding citizenship.

6. Mandating field-based practical ecological conservation and compassionate animal welfare education.

7. Enforcing a balanced fifty-fifty ratio between theoretical instruction and practical experiential learning, supported by District Skill Development and Entrepreneurship Incubators.

8. Enacting a statutory target to increase national research and development spending to not less than three per cent of Gross Domestic Product within ten years, establishing the National Scientific Research Foundation, and awarding stipend-backed Undergraduate Research Fellowships.

9. Outlawing the commercialization of education, banning capitation fees, establishing State Education Fee Regulation Commissions, and imposing severe criminal penalties (7 to 14 years imprisonment for fund misappropriation; up to 7 years for criminal safety negligence; up to 10 years for capitation fee extortion and paper leaks) under Special Anti-Corruption Courts without prior executive sanction requirements.

The Bill seeks to achieve these transformative objectives while respecting the fundamental rights guaranteed under Articles 14, 15, 21, 25, 28, 29, and 30 of the Constitution.

## FINANCIAL MEMORANDUM

1. Clause 4, Clause 5, and Schedule I provide for expanding public educational infrastructure, maintaining minimum facility standards, universal free tuition, free learning materials, digital devices, uniforms, and daily nutritional meals for students aged three to twenty-two. The additional annual recurring expenditure from the Consolidated Fund of India and State funds is estimated at ninety-five thousand crore rupees, phased over five financial years.

2. Clause 16 and Clause 17 provide for increasing national R&D expenditure to three per cent of GDP and establishing the National Scientific Research Foundation. The initial non-recurring allocation for establishing the Foundation and digital research networks is estimated at one thousand crore rupees, with a progressive annual recurring research grant allocation of twenty-five thousand crore rupees.

3. Clause 18 provides for awarding five hundred thousand stipend-backed Undergraduate and Postgraduate Research Fellowships annually, incurring an estimated recurring annual expenditure of three thousand crore rupees.

4. Clause 21 and Clause 25 provide for establishing the National Education Quality and Integrity Inspectorate and Special Anti-Corruption Courts, incurring an estimated recurring annual operational expenditure of three hundred crore rupees.

## MEMORANDUM REGARDING DELEGATED LEGISLATION

Clause 30 of the Bill empowers the Central Government to make rules for carrying out the provisions of the Act. The matters with respect to which rules may be made pertain to procedural and administrative details regarding:

1. Infrastructure benchmarks and neighborhood school distance standards under Clause 5 and Schedule I;

2. Guidelines for early childhood sports, music, vocational skills, and foreign languages under Clause 8;

3. Operational framework for daily secular Vipassana mindfulness practice under Clause 9;

4. Guidelines for the National Curriculum Framework for Scientific Temper under Clause 10;

5. Practical ecological, animal welfare, traffic safety, and cleanliness education modules under Clauses 11, 12, and 13;

6. Operational guidelines for fifty-fifty theory-practical learning timetables under Clause 14; and

7. Research fellowship grant disbursement criteria under Clauses 17 and 18.

The delegated powers are administrative in nature and strictly bounded by statutory criteria. All rules made under the Act are subject to mandatory parliamentary scrutiny and laying requirements under sub-clause (3) of Clause 30. The delegation of legislative power is, therefore, of a normal and routine character.
