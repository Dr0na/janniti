THE RIGHT TO FREE, UNIVERSAL, SCIENTIFIC AND RESEARCH-ORIENTED EDUCATION BILL, 2026

A

BILL

to expand the fundamental right to education under Article 21A of the Constitution of India; to guarantee universal, equitable, and free education from early childhood care and pre-primary education through senior secondary, higher, vocational, and technical education; to mandate a national curriculum rooted in scientific temper, empirical inquiry, constitutional values, equality, fraternity, anti-discrimination, and civic responsibility; to integrate practical ecological stewardship and compassion for living creatures; to enforce a fifty-fifty ratio between theoretical instruction and practical experiential learning; to establish a statutory framework for national research and development with mandatory budget targets; to prohibit the commercialization of education and collection of capitation fees; and for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto.

BE it enacted by Parliament in the Seventy-Seventh Year of the Republic of India as follows:—

### 1. Short title, extent and commencement

1. This Act may be called the Right to Free, Universal, Scientific and Research-Oriented Education Act, 2026.

2. It extends to the whole of India.

3. It shall come into force on such date as the Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, appoint:

Provided that different dates may be appointed for different provisions of this Act, and any reference in any such provision to the commencement of this Act shall be construed as a reference to the coming into force of that provision.

### 2. Definitions

In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires,—

(a) "Capitation Fee" means any donation, contribution, payment, or consideration, in cash or in kind, collected directly or indirectly by an educational institution over and above the fee notified by the State Fee Regulation Commission under section 25;

(b) "Early Childhood Education" means structured care, early learning, and pre-primary education provided to children between the ages of three and six years;

(c) "Educational Institution" means any public, private, aided, unaided, central, state, or autonomous school, college, polytechnic, institute of technology, central university, state university, or deemed university imparting education in India;

(d) "Experiential Learning" means hands-on, laboratory-based, field-oriented, workshop, or project-based learning where theoretical concepts are directly applied through practice;

(e) "Higher Education" means undergraduate degree, postgraduate degree, technical diploma, professional, or research education imparted to students between the ages of eighteen and twenty-two years or beyond;

(f) "National Scientific Research Foundation" means the statutory research funding body established under section 20;

(g) "Prescribed" means prescribed by rules made under this Act;

(h) "Research and Development Expenditure" means monetary outlay allocated specifically for basic, applied, scientific, technological, medical, environmental, or social science research and innovation;

(i) "Scientific Temper" means an empirical, evidence-based, rational, and inquiring mindset as mandated under clause (h) of Article 51A of the Constitution; and

(j) "Universal Free Education" means the provision of education without tuition fees, admission fees, examination fees, or mandatory cost for textbooks, learning materials, digital access, and nutritional meals.

### 3. Statutory purpose and constitutional principles

1. It is hereby declared that this Act is for securing the fundamental right to education under Article 21A, giving effect to the Fundamental Duties under clauses (g) and (h) of Article 51A, and upholding the constitutional principles of equality, fraternity, anti-discrimination, secularism, and rule of law under Articles 14, 15, and 21 of the Constitution of India.

2. The state shall ensure that public education is delivered as an essential non-profit public service, free from commercial exploitation, dogma, or discriminatory exclusion.

### 4. Right to universal free education from age three to twenty-two

1. Every child and youth in India shall have the right to universal free education in a neighborhood public or state-assisted educational institution from the age of three years until the completion of an undergraduate degree, higher technical diploma, or professional vocational qualification up to the age of twenty-two years.

2. No educational institution shall charge any tuition fee, admission fee, capitation fee, development fee, examination fee, or hidden charge from any student entitled to free education under sub-section (1).

3. The Central Government and State Governments shall provide, free of cost, to every student enrolled under sub-section (1)—

  (a) age-appropriate textbooks, workbooks, and digital learning devices;

  (b) two sets of school or institute uniforms per academic year;

  (c) balanced, high-protein daily nutritional meals up to senior secondary level; and

  (d) free public transportation or transport subsidy between residence and educational institution.

### 5. Neighborhood access and infrastructure expansion mandate

1. The Central Government and State Governments shall, within five years of the commencement of this Act, expand public educational infrastructure to ensure that—

  (a) a composite pre-primary and primary public school is accessible within a walking distance of one kilometer from every habitation;

  (b) a secondary and senior secondary public school is accessible within a distance of three kilometers from every habitation; and

  (c) a public university campus, technical institute, or vocational college is accessible within every revenue district.

2. All educational institutions shall conform to mandatory national infrastructure benchmarks, including clean drinking water, separate functional sanitation facilities for male and female students, science laboratories, computer labs, libraries, sports grounds, and barrier-free disability access.

### 6. Non-discriminatory admission and equal opportunity guarantee

1. No student shall be denied admission to any educational institution on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth, disability, economic status, or language.

2. All private aided and unaided educational institutions shall reserve not less than thirty per cent of their entry-level seats for students belonging to economically weaker sections and disadvantaged groups, whose education shall be fully reimbursed by the state at prescribed public cost rates.

### 7. National Curriculum Framework for Scientific Temper and Secular Rationalism

1. The Central Government shall, through the National Council of Educational Research and Training, notify a National Curriculum Framework rooted in scientific temper, empirical inquiry, humanism, and secular rationalism under clause (h) of Article 51A of the Constitution.

2. The National Curriculum Framework shall ensure that—

  (a) all science, humanities, and social science subjects are taught using evidence-based, peer-reviewed scientific methodology;

  (b) pseudoscientific assertions, unverified dogmas, and superstitious beliefs are strictly excluded from textbooks and classroom instruction;

  (c) critical thinking, logical reasoning, scientific method, and independent inquiry are cultivated across all grade levels; and

  (d) constitutional values, equality, fraternity, anti-discrimination, secularism, gender justice, and respect for diversity are integrated across all subjects.

### 8. Practical civic responsibility, traffic safety, and cleanliness education

1. Every school and college curriculum shall incorporate mandatory practical modules on civic duty, constitutional rights, rule of law, traffic safety, and public cleanliness (Swachhata).

2. Every student shall complete mandatory practical hours in—

  (a) traffic safety rules, pedestrian safety, and responsible road behavior through practical traffic management drills conducted in coordination with local traffic authorities;

  (b) civic sanitation, waste segregation, recycling, and neighborhood cleanliness drives organized by the educational institution; and

  (c) community service, legal literacy, and constitutional awareness projects.

### 9. Practical ecological conservation and environmental stewardship

1. Environmental education under this Act shall be delivered primarily through practical, field-based ecological conservation projects under clause (g) of Article 51A of the Constitution.

2. Every educational institution shall dedicate not less than two hours per week to mandatory practical ecological activities, including—

  (a) planting, nurturing, and monitoring native trees and urban forests;

  (b) organic gardening, composting, and rainwater harvesting on institution premises;

  (c) local waterbody rejuvenation and plastic pollution cleanup drives; and

  (d) energy conservation and carbon footprint audits conducted by students.

### 10. Practical compassion for animals and nature

1. Every school curriculum shall include practical instruction and experiential activities designed to foster empathy, ethical treatment, and compassion for animals and nature.

2. Practical animal welfare education shall include—

  (a) humane treatment of domestic and street animals, rescue protocols, and anti-cruelty education;

  (b) field visits to animal shelters, wildlife rescue centers, and nature reserves; and

  (c) prohibition of dissecting or harming live animals for educational demonstration, replacing such exercises with digital 3D virtual simulation models.

### 11. Fifty-fifty ratio between theoretical instruction and practical experiential learning

1. Every educational institution shall structure its academic timetable to achieve a balanced ratio of fifty per cent theoretical instruction and fifty per cent practical experiential learning.

2. Practical experiential learning under sub-section (1) shall comprise—

  (a) hands-on laboratory experimentation and scientific research projects;

  (b) industrial workshops, coding labs, robotics, and design thinking modules;

  (c) vocational trade training, agricultural field practice, and artisanal crafts; and

  (d) sports, physical education, arts, theater, and personality development activities.

### 12. District Skill Development and Entrepreneurship Incubators

1. Every district secondary school cluster and higher education campus shall establish a District Skill Development and Entrepreneurship Incubator.

2. The Incubator established under sub-section (1) shall provide—

  (a) certified vocational skill training aligned with regional employment needs;

  (b) seed funding, mentorship, and legal assistance for student-led startup ventures;

  (c) industry apprenticeship placements and micro-entrepreneurship workshops; and

  (d) financial literacy, digital business management, and patent filing guidance.

### 13. Statutory national R&D expenditure target and funding allocation

1. The Central Government and State Governments shall progressively increase public expenditure on Research and Development to achieve a national target of not less than three per cent of Gross Domestic Product within ten years from the commencement of this Act.

2. Not less than fifty per cent of the national R&D budget allocated under sub-section (1) shall be directed to basic scientific research, medical innovation, agricultural sustainability, clean energy technologies, and higher education university research laboratories.

### 14. Establishment of National Scientific Research Foundation

1. The Central Government shall establish an independent statutory body to be known as the National Scientific Research Foundation to plan, fund, coordinate, and evaluate scientific and academic research across India.

2. The Foundation shall administer a competitive research grant scheme for faculty, independent researchers, and university research centers based strictly on peer-reviewed scientific merit and national development priorities.

### 15. Undergraduate and Postgraduate Research Fellowships

1. Every student enrolled in an undergraduate science, technology, engineering, medical, humanities, or social science degree program shall complete a mandatory research project in their final academic year.

2. The National Scientific Research Foundation shall award monthly stipend-backed Undergraduate and Postgraduate Research Fellowships to not less than five hundred thousand high-performing research students annually to encourage full-time research careers.

### 16. Total ban on commercialization and capitation fees

1. No educational institution, management, trust, society, or individual shall demand, collect, or accept any capitation fee, donation, or unaccounted financial consideration for admitting any student or granting any degree or diploma.

2. Education in all institutions shall be operated strictly on a non-profit basis. Any surplus income generated by an educational institution shall be reinvested exclusively into institutional infrastructure, research laboratories, library resources, or student welfare.

### 17. Establishment of State Education Fee Regulation Commissions

1. Every State Government shall establish an independent statutory State Education Fee Regulation Commission headed by a retired High Court Judge.

2. The Commission shall determine and notify the maximum fee structure chargeable by private self-financed educational institutions for non-subsidized seats, calculating fees strictly on a cost-recovery, non-profit basis after auditing institutional accounts.

3. Any private educational institution that collects fees in excess of the cap notified under sub-section (2) shall be liable to penalties under section 18.

### 18. Penalties for illegal capitation fees and fee gouging

1. Whosoever collects or accepts any capitation fee or donation in violation of section 16, or collects fees in excess of the limit notified under section 17, shall be punishable with rigorous imprisonment for a term which shall not be less than three years but which may extend to ten years, and fine which shall not be less than five times the amount of capitation fee or excess fee collected.

2. In addition to the criminal penalty under sub-section (1), the affiliation or recognition of the offending educational institution shall be subject to mandatory revocation by the competent university or educational board.

### 19. National Education Quality and Integrity Inspectorate

1. The Central Government shall establish an autonomous National Education Quality and Integrity Inspectorate to conduct unannounced academic, infrastructural, and financial audits of educational institutions across India.

2. The Inspectorate shall publish annual district-wise education performance scorecards evaluating learning outcomes, scientific temper implementation, practical learning compliance, R&D outputs, and anti-corruption standards.

### 20. Power to make rules

1. The Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, make rules for carrying out the provisions of this Act.

2. In particular, and without prejudice to the generality of the foregoing power, such rules may provide for all or any of the following matters, namely:—

  (a) the national infrastructure benchmarks and neighborhood school distance standards under section 5;

  (b) the guidelines for National Curriculum Framework for Scientific Temper under section 7;

  (c) the practical ecological, animal welfare, traffic safety, and cleanliness education modules under sections 8, 9, and 10;

  (d) the guidelines for 50:50 theory-practical learning timetables under section 11;

  (e) the operational procedures and research fellowship grant criteria under sections 14 and 15; and

  (f) any other matter which is required to be, or may be, prescribed.

3. Every rule made under this Act shall be laid, as soon as may be after it is made, before each House of Parliament, while it is in session, for a total period of thirty days which may be comprised in one session or in two or more successive sessions, and if, before the expiry of the session immediately following the session or the successive sessions aforesaid, both Houses agree in making any modification in the rule or both Houses agree that the rule should not be made, the rule shall thereafter have effect only in such modified form or be of no effect, as the case may be; so, however, that any such modification or annulment shall be without prejudice to the validity of anything previously done under that rule.

## STATEMENT OF OBJECTS AND REASONS

India's long-term economic prosperity, national security, democratic strength, and global scientific standing depend fundamentally on building a world-class, highly skilled, research-oriented, and constitutionally conscious society. However, the existing educational framework remains constrained by statutory coverage limits, commercialization, out-of-pocket financial barriers, theoretical rote-memorization, under-funded R&D, and a deficit in practical civic and environmental education.

While Article 21A of the Constitution guarantees free and compulsory education for children between the ages of six and fourteen years, pre-primary education (ages 3–6) and secondary, tertiary, technical, and vocational education (ages 14–22) remain un-guaranteed and heavily commercialized. Furthermore, national R&D expenditure remains under-allocated at approximately 0.65 per cent of Gross Domestic Product, limiting the nation's capacity for scientific breakthrough and industrial innovation.

To fulfill the mandate of Article 21A, operationalize the Fundamental Duties of scientific temper (Article 51A(h)) and environmental stewardship (Article 51A(g)), and position India as a global research and skills superpower within ten to fifteen years, this Bill provides for:

1. Guaranteeing universal free education from pre-primary (age 3) through undergraduate degree, technical diploma, or professional vocational training (age 22) in all public and state-assisted institutions.

2. Formulating a National Curriculum Framework focused on empirical scientific temper, secular rationalism, critical inquiry, constitutional ethics, fraternity, anti-discrimination, traffic safety rules, civic cleanliness, and law-abiding citizenship.

3. Mandating field-based practical ecological conservation and compassionate animal welfare education.

4. Enforcing a balanced fifty-fifty ratio between theoretical instruction and practical experiential learning, supported by District Skill Development and Entrepreneurship Incubators.

5. Enacting a statutory target to increase national research and development spending to not less than three per cent of Gross Domestic Product within ten years, establishing the National Scientific Research Foundation, and awarding stipend-backed Undergraduate Research Fellowships.

6. Outlawing the commercialization of education, banning capitation fees, establishing State Education Fee Regulation Commissions, and imposing strict criminal penalties of up to ten years imprisonment for fee gouging.

The Bill seeks to achieve these transformative objectives while respecting the fundamental rights guaranteed under Articles 14, 15, 21, 29, and 30 of the Constitution.

## FINANCIAL MEMORANDUM

1. Clause 4 and Clause 5 provide for expanding public educational infrastructure, universal free tuition, free learning materials, digital devices, uniforms, and daily nutritional meals for students aged three to twenty-two. The additional annual recurring expenditure from the Consolidated Fund of India and State funds is estimated at eighty thousand crore rupees, phased over five financial years.

2. Clause 13 and Clause 14 provide for increasing national R&D expenditure to three per cent of GDP and establishing the National Scientific Research Foundation. The initial non-recurring allocation for establishing the Foundation and digital research networks is estimated at one thousand crore rupees, with a progressive annual recurring research grant allocation of twenty-five thousand crore rupees.

3. Clause 15 provides for awarding five hundred thousand stipend-backed Undergraduate and Postgraduate Research Fellowships annually, incurring an estimated recurring annual expenditure of three thousand crore rupees.

4. Clause 19 provides for establishing the National Education Quality and Integrity Inspectorate, incurring an estimated recurring annual operational expenditure of two hundred crore rupees.

## MEMORANDUM REGARDING DELEGATED LEGISLATION

Clause 20 of the Bill empowers the Central Government to make rules for carrying out the provisions of the Act. The matters with respect to which rules may be made pertain to procedural and administrative details regarding:

1. Infrastructure benchmarks and neighborhood school distance standards under Clause 5;

2. Guidelines for the National Curriculum Framework for Scientific Temper under Clause 7;

3. Practical ecological, animal welfare, traffic safety, and cleanliness education modules under Clauses 8, 9, and 10;

4. Operational guidelines for fifty-fifty theory-practical learning timetables under Clause 11; and

5. Research fellowship grant disbursement criteria under Clauses 14 and 15.

The delegated powers are administrative in nature and strictly bounded by statutory criteria. All rules made under the Act are subject to mandatory parliamentary scrutiny and laying requirements under sub-clause (3) of Clause 20. The delegation of legislative power is, therefore, of a normal and routine character.
