# Indian Law Landscape Research Pack: Universal Free, Scientific & Research-Oriented Education Reform

## 1. Scope and search record

- Matter ID: 006
- Topic and objective: Comprehensive legislative overhaul of the Indian education system to guarantee free, equitable, universal education from early childhood to tertiary/vocational levels; embed constitutional values, scientific temperament, anti-discrimination, animal compassion, ecological conservation, civic responsibility, and research orientation; and establish a world-class R&D ecosystem over a 10-15 year horizon.
- Jurisdiction(s): Union of India and State/UT jurisdictions (Concurrent List Entry 25 and Union List Entries 63–66).
- As-of date: 2026-07-27
- Search terms and synonyms: Right to education, Article 21A, RTE Act 2009, Article 51A(h), scientific temper, National Education Policy (NEP 2020), University Grants Commission (UGC Act 1956), R&D spending, vocational education, skill development, secular education.
- Requested legal question: What is the existing legal, statutory, and constitutional landscape governing education in India, what are its structural gaps regarding universality, scientific temper, R&D funding, and anti-commercialization, and how can a comprehensive central statute reform education across all States and UTs?
- Sources/repositories searched and retrieval dates: Constitution of India (Part III, Part IV, Part IVA, Schedule VII), India Code (indiacode.nic.in), Ministry of Education, UGC, AICTE, Supreme Court of India reports (SCC/SCR).

## 2. Executive finding and reliance limits

The Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act, 2009 (RTE Act) guarantees free education only for ages 6 to 14 (Classes 1 to 8). Early childhood education (ages 3–6) and secondary/higher/vocational education (ages 14–22) remain un-guaranteed and heavily commercialized. Furthermore, while National Education Policy 2020 outlines executive policy goals, it lacks statutory force, binding R&D budget guarantees (India's R&D expenditure remains ~0.65% of GDP), or enforceable statutory mandates for scientific temper, ecological stewardship, and civic practical education. A comprehensive central statute under Concurrent List Entry 25 is required to establish universal free education from pre-primary to higher education, mandate a minimum 3% GDP national research allocation, and enforce a 50:50 practical-experiential curriculum across India.

## 9. Rights, equality, federalism, and public-interest assessment

- Fundamental Rights & Non-Discrimination: Universal free education ensures substantive equality under Article 14 and eliminates class/caste barriers to quality learning.
- Scientific Temper & Secularism: Section 51A(h) demands that public education foster empirical scientific temper, critical inquiry, and secular rationalism, protecting students from unscientific dogmas or superstitious indoctrination.
- Federalism: Education is on the Concurrent List (Entry 25). Central legislation must establish national minimum standards, R&D funding mechanisms, and core curricula while preserving State legislative flexibility for regional languages and local cultural contexts.
