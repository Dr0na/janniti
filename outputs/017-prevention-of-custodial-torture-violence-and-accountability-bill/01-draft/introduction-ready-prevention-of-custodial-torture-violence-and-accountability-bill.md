# THE PREVENTION OF CUSTODIAL TORTURE, VIOLENCE, AND ACCOUNTABILITY BILL, 2026

### A

### BILL

to prohibit and criminalize all forms of custodial torture, cruel, inhuman, or degrading treatment committed by public servants; to establish statutory procedural safeguards during arrest and detention; to reverse the burden of proof in cases of custodial injury or death; to mandate victim compensation and fast-track prosecution; and for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto.

BE it enacted by Parliament in the Seventy-Seventh Year of the Republic of India as follows:—

### 1. Short title, extent and commencement

(1) This Act may be called the Prevention of Custodial Torture, Violence, and Accountability Act, 2026.

(2) It extends to the whole of India.

(3) It shall come into force on such date as the Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, appoint.

### 2. Definitions

In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires,—

(a) "Custodial Injury" means any physical or psychological harm, trauma, or impairment suffered by a person while in police custody or under state detention;

(b) "Custodial Torture" means any act by which severe physical or mental pain or suffering is intentionally inflicted on a person by or at the instigation of or with the consent or acquiescence of a public servant; and

(c) "Public Servant" shall have the meaning assigned to it in the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023.

### 3. Absolute Prohibition of Custodial Torture

(1) Custodial Torture is prohibited under all circumstances, and no exceptional circumstances whatsoever, whether a state of war, threat of war, internal political instability, or any other public emergency, may be invoked as a justification for torture.

(2) No order from a superior officer or public authority may be invoked as a justification for custodial torture.

### 4. Mandatory Custodial Safeguards and Medical Examination

(1) Every person arrested shall be informed immediately of the grounds of arrest and their right to consult a legal practitioner of their choice.

(2) Every arrested person shall be subjected to a mandatory medical examination by a registered medical officer within two hours of arrest, and a copy of the medical report shall be provided to the arrested person.

### 5. Reversal of Burden of Proof in Custodial Injury or Death

In any prosecution under this Act, where a person suffers Custodial Injury or death while in police custody or under state detention, the court shall presume that such injury or death was caused by the custodial public servant, unless the contrary is proved by the defence beyond reasonable doubt.

### 6. Offence of Custodial Torture and Penalties

(1) Whoever commits Custodial Torture shall be punishable with imprisonment for a term which shall not be less than seven years but which may extend to life imprisonment, and with fine.

(2) Where Custodial Torture results in the death of the victim, the public servant responsible shall be punishable with mandatory life imprisonment or death.

(3) In addition to criminal penalties, the court shall order mandatory statutory compensation of not less than twenty-five lakh rupees payable by the State to the victim or surviving family, recoverable from the guilty officers.

### 7. Power to Make Rules

(1) The Central Government may make rules to carry out the provisions of this Act.

(2) Every rule made under this section shall be laid before Parliament.

---

## STATEMENT OF OBJECTS AND REASONS

1. Custodial torture and deaths in custody violate the sacred fundamental right to life and human dignity under Article 21 of the Constitution (*D.K. Basu* judgment).

2. The Prevention of Custodial Torture, Violence, and Accountability Bill, 2026 criminalizes torture with mandatory minimum 7 years to life imprisonment, reverses the burden of proof for custodial injuries, mandates medical exams within 2 hours of arrest, and awards mandatory ₹25 Lakhs victim compensation.

---

## FINANCIAL MEMORANDUM

1. Clause 6 mandates statutory victim compensation.

2. Recurring annual expenditure for compensation funds is estimated at rupees one hundred crores.
