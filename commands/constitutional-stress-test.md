# /constitutional-stress-test

Stress-test the supplied law, bill, constitutional amendment, policy, institution, judgment framework, or legal proposal. If the target is missing, request it. Do not draft a replacement until the stress test identifies the failure mode.

Read `AGENTS.md`, `references/legal-ethics.md`, `references/review-checklist.md`, and the applicable jurisdiction module. For India, also read `references/india-constitutional-research.md` and `knowledge-base/README.md`.

## Test actors and conditions

Assume, separately, that each actor acts lawfully at first and then exploits every ambiguity, delay, secrecy power, appointment process, funding lever, emergency exception, and procedural gap:

1. A government with a large legislative majority.
2. A government with a narrow majority dependent on defections or coercion.
3. A captured or politically compliant enforcement agency.
4. A corrupt or self-protective regulator, auditor, prosecutor, or court administration.
5. A judiciary facing political pressure, conflict, delay, or opaque case allocation.
6. A dominant media owner, platform, advertiser, or coordinated disinformation network.
7. A wealthy private actor using lobbying, procurement, litigation, or ownership to capture policy.
8. A hostile majority targeting a minority, dissident, journalist, protester, whistleblower, or opposition party.
9. A genuine security, disaster, public-health, or financial emergency.
10. An ordinary person with little money, limited literacy, disability, language barriers, or fear of retaliation.

## Required analysis

For every material power, identify the abuse path, trigger, decision-maker, evidence hidden from scrutiny, delay or cost barrier, person harmed, available challenge, and remedy. Test direct and indirect evasion through delegation, outsourcing, technology, secrecy, funding, and serial temporary action.

Rate each finding:

| Rating | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Critical | Enables durable authoritarian control, irreparable rights loss, or unreviewable power. |
| High | Enables selective repression, serious corruption, or material rights deprivation. |
| Medium | Weakens accountability or creates a plausible abuse route. |
| Low | A correctable clarity, implementation, or transparency gap. |

## Required output

1. **Stress-test conclusion:** resilient, resilient with conditions, vulnerable, or critically vulnerable.
2. **Scenario matrix:** actor/condition, exploitation path, right or constitutional principle affected, likelihood, impact, existing check, failure, and rating.
3. **Single-point-of-failure register:** appointment, budget, information, enforcement, emergency, litigation, and media dependencies.
4. **Minimum safeguards:** exact changes needed—objective trigger, independent authorisation, transparent record, time limit, appeal, remedy, audit, anti-evasion clause, or structural separation.
5. **Residual risks:** risks no legal text can eliminate and the democratic monitoring needed to contain them.

Do not accept “independence,” “national security,” “public interest,” “judicial discretion,” or “executive satisfaction” as a safeguard without specifying who verifies it, on what evidence, by when, in public or under what court-controlled confidentiality, and with what remedy.
