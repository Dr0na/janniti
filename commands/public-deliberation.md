# /public-deliberation

Design a Public Participation and Deliberation Plan for the supplied law, amendment, rule, programme, or institutional framework. Read `references/legal-ethics.md`, `references/review-checklist.md`, `templates/public-deliberation-plan.example.json`, and the applicable jurisdiction module.

## Required design

1. Publish a plain-language summary, full draft, rights/equality/fiscal impact note, power map, alternatives, and evidence register before consultation.
2. Set a proportionate consultation period; for urgent action, record urgency evidence and provide an immediate post-action consultation/review route.
3. Provide accessible participation: relevant languages, easy-read/accessible formats, captioning/sign-language where needed, offline/local channels, disability access, low-data options, and assistance without coercion.
4. Identify materially affected groups and provide targeted, safe, non-retaliatory participation routes without treating them as the only public voice.
5. Disclose lobbying, funding, conflicts, meetings, methodology, sampling, moderation rules, and use of automated analysis. Prevent duplicate/fabricated submissions, harassment, doxxing, and organised capture without excluding lawful dissent.
6. For high-impact matters, design representative citizen panels, local hearings, and/or federal/local-government consultation with transparent selection and facilitation safeguards.
7. Publish submissions or reasoned summaries with privacy protection, then issue a response-to-comments register: issue, evidence, decision, reason, draft change, and dissent/remaining disagreement.
8. Provide a challenge/remedy route for inaccessible, manipulated, sham, or unlawfully excluded consultation; set correction, re-consultation, and judicial/legislative referral triggers.
9. Save the plan in the matter's `10-public-deliberation/` folder and run `python3 scripts/validate_public_deliberation.py <plan.json>`.

## Required output

1. Consultation scope, decision stage, and non-negotiable constitutional limits.
2. Publication, evidence, accessibility, and language schedule.
3. Participant/stakeholder and capture-risk map.
4. Deliberation method, moderation, privacy, and safety protocol.
5. Response-to-comments and decision-accountability process.
6. Remedy, re-consultation, and review triggers.

No consultation can legitimise discrimination, removal of fundamental freedoms, or a constitutional abuse of power. Consultation informs democratic decision-making; it does not replace equal rights or judicial remedies.
